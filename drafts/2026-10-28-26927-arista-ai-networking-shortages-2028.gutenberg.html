With Arista Networks approaching its November 3 earnings report, CEO Jayshree Ullal's warning about AI infrastructure supply is in focus: memory, optics and semiconductor parts could remain constrained across the industry through 2028. The forecast appeared in earlier 2026 investor remarks and an August 5 CNBC interview—not a new October interview.

A two-year supply-chain problem

Ullal described shortages in memory, optical components, silicon substrates and chips. Arista responded by increasing advance purchases and signing multiyear supplier commitments, she told CNBC. Some components face lead times of six to twelve months. Her two-year outlook is a management forecast, not a confirmed date when global shortages will end.

The company's second-quarter results put that tension into perspective. Arista generated $3.036 billion in revenue, up 37.7% from a year earlier, and forecast approximately $3.3 billion for its September quarter. The September result is not public yet; management plans to report it November 3.

Why the networking hardware matters

AI clusters cannot achieve their advertised compute capacity without fast, reliable connections between accelerators. Arista's 7060XE7 Ethernet switches target 1.6-terabit ports and up to 102.4 Tbps of system throughput. The company says linear pluggable optics can reduce interconnect power by approximately 60% compared with conventional pluggable designs.

Supply bottlenecks may therefore affect deployment schedules, not just hardware prices. The challenge complements BitcoinVersus.Tech's coverage of large data-center switches, AI chip substrate materials and high-bandwidth optical interconnects. More throughput means little if critical modules cannot arrive on schedule.

Arista's next earnings call will provide a more current test of component availability, lead times and management's mitigation efforts. Any improved supply outlook would be a company-specific update, not proof that the wider industry shortage has ended.
