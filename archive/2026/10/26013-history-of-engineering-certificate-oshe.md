## Gutenberg source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>OSHE is a chronological, document-driven history of engineering, focusing on actual builders, designs, structures, machines and named engineering figures.</strong> The track begins with prehistoric construction and recorded traditions. It introduces the earliest individually named builders without pretending that the first named figure is necessarily the first person to engineer anything.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Scope of the Certificate</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Engineering in this historical curriculum includes tool construction, shelters, water systems, irrigation, ships, bridges, masonry, canals, roads, surveying, machines, mechanics, mining, civil works, industrial systems, electronics, software, aerospace and modern digital infrastructure. The record is global rather than limited to one civilization.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Earliest Engineer: What Can Be Documented?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>No single individual can be proven to be the first human engineer.</strong> Written records appear much later than the earliest constructed objects. Therefore this curriculum starts with three distinct lines of evidence: anonymous physical construction, named figures documented in religious or literary texts, and people independently attested by historical inscriptions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Noah:</strong> <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+6%3A14-22&amp;version=NIV">Genesis 6:14–22</a> names Noah and provides a construction specification for the ark (materials, dimensions, compartments and covering). Noah is included as a named scriptural builder. Independent identification, dating and physical verification of the ark remain separate questions.</li><li><strong>Dhul-Qarnayn:</strong> <a href="https://quran.com/en/al-kahf/96-97">Qur'an 18:96–97</a> recounts a barrier-building project using blocks of iron, heating and molten copper. The source clearly documents a named figure and technique, while scholarly views differ on identity and historicity.</li><li><strong>Imhotep:</strong> the Egyptian official's name and titles survive on contemporaneous <a href="https://www.globalegyptianmuseum.org/record.aspx?id=15183">statue pedestal JE 49889</a> from Djoser's period, approximately the twenty-seventh century BCE. Egyptian antiquities authorities <a href="https://egymonuments.gov.eg/monuments/the-step-pyramid-complex-of-djoser/">associate him with the Step Pyramid</a>, but as the <a href="https://arce.org/resource/imhotep-sage-between-fiction-and-reality/">American Research Center in Egypt</a> explains, the contemporary inscriptions do not explicitly name him as architect of that structure.</li><li><strong>Gudea:</strong> a <a href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/324015">cuneiform votive cone</a> from c. 2090 BCE records Gudea, ruler of Lagash, as having constructed the Eninnu temple. This is an unusually direct example of an attributable ancient building project preserved in writing.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How Every Named Engineer Will Be Documented</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Record the person or culture, the exact original document (book/chapter/verse, inscription, manuscript, archive identifier) and the publication or artifact date.</li><li>Distinguish the date of a source from the date of the person or event it describes.</li><li>Document the reported invention, construction, experimental process or achievement; specify whether a named individual actually performed the work or is merely credited with it.</li><li>Accept scriptural and literary attestations as documentation of a named tradition without requiring artifacts, and explicitly state whether the identity or event has independent corroboration.</li><li>Record archaeological evidence and museum object numbers where available, but never make physical survival a prerequisite for inclusion.</li><li>Attribute alternative identifications, disputed dates and historical interpretations; distinguish legend, retrospective attribution and contemporary inscription.</li><li>Link primary material whenever available and cross-check translations or summaries with credible scholarship; revise entries when improved evidence appears.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Course Sequence and Numbering</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>OSHE.000–099:</strong> early engineering evidence, scriptural and recorded builders, Imhotep and early Nile/Mesopotamian constructions.</li><li><strong>OSHE.100–199:</strong> ancient engineering traditions across Africa, West and South Asia, East Asia, the Mediterranean and the Americas.</li><li><strong>OSHE.200–299:</strong> medieval and early-modern engineers, manuscripts, machines, builders and hydraulic works.</li><li><strong>OSHE.300–399:</strong> civil, mechanical and industrial engineering in the scientific and industrial revolutions.</li><li><strong>OSHE.400–499:</strong> electricity, telecommunications, transport, manufacturing and early computing engineers.</li><li><strong>OSHE.500–999:</strong> aerospace, semiconductors, software, automation, data centers, robotics, modern engineering ethics, source criticism and documented capstones.</li></ul>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Profile Template</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Each lesson centered on a named person will identify: name and variants; location and date range; exact occupation or role established by the evidence; original writing and its approximate date; any construction or artifact; witness and later testimonies; engineering principles involved; scholarly disputes; and linked source documents. A named builder in scripture belongs in the record as a <em>scripturally attested figure</em>, while a contemporary inscription establishes a different evidentiary relationship. Both are legitimate research subjects.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Editorial and Teaching Standard</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Historical attributions must not be confused with modern job titles. We may teach Imhotep among the earliest named engineering figures and explore the tradition crediting him with the Step Pyramid, but we will not state that an inscription proves he designed it. Likewise, identifying Noah in Genesis establishes that the source assigns him a construction task; it does not by itself determine the ark's modern archaeological identification.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
