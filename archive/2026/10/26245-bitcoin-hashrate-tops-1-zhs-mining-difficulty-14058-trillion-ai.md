<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bitcoin's seven-day estimated hashrate has climbed back above <strong>1 zettahash per second (ZH/s)</strong>, or 1,000 exahashes per second, even as publicly traded miners redirect parts of their electricity and facilities toward artificial intelligence. The rebound does not mean AI conversions have stopped. <a href="https://forklog.com/en/bitcoin-hash-rate-surpasses-1-zh-s-for-first-time-since-march/">An October 23 report citing BlocksBridge Consulting</a> confirms the threshold.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Bitcoin's seven-day hashrate estimate is again above 1 ZH/s despite AI infrastructure conversions.</li><li>Mining difficulty rose 5.92% to 140.58 trillion on October 16; the next retarget is only a projection.</li><li>Higher network competition increases the importance of ASIC energy efficiency, measured in J/TH.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Difficulty Reaches 140.58 Trillion</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://mempool.space/mining">mempool.space mining dashboard</a> records Bitcoin's October 16 adjustment from 132.72 trillion to <strong>140.58 trillion</strong>, a 5.92% increase. A separate <a href="https://coinucation.com/indicators/bitcoin-network">October 23 network snapshot</a> reported 1,055 EH/s on a three-day average and projected another increase around October 29. That projection can change; it is <strong>not a completed adjustment</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">AI Conversions Have Not Eliminated Competition</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bitcoin miners have been expanding into GPU hosting and AI data centers, but other operators can deploy new ASICs or bring previously idle machines online. A rebound in total hashrate does not reveal exactly which individual sites added computing power. This contrasts with <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/05/17/bitcoin-hashrate-drops-for-the-first-time-in-6-years-as-miners-pivot-to-ai/">our earlier reporting on the AI-driven hashrate downturn</a> and updates the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitcoin-hashrate-nears-1-zhs-difficulty-higher-hashprice-39/">October 9 near-1 ZH/s snapshot</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What This Means for Power Efficiency Theory</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Mining is measured in hashes, but profitability depends on electricity use. For illustration, <strong>1 ZH/s at 15 J/TH requires approximately 15 GW</strong> of ASIC electricity; at 8.9 J/TH it requires 8.9 GW. Neither number is a measured estimate of Bitcoin's actual network load: real miners use mixed hardware and site overhead adds consumption.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
