## Final Gutenberg Source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Mali and Senegal have connected their national fiber-optic backbones at the Kidira–Diboli border, creating a new cross-border route for data traffic between the two West African countries.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The link was inaugurated on October 5 by Mali’s state-owned SMTD-SA and Sénégal Numérique. <a href="https://gouvernement.ml/cooperation-numerique-et-technologique-les-backbones-du-mali-et-du-senegal-sinterconnectent/">Mali’s government</a> says the connection is part of the West African National Backbone Network and is intended to add redundancy, lower dependence on single routes and improve regional digital resilience. Senegal’s <a href="https://aps.sn/bakel-inauguration-de-linterconnexion-des-backbones-du-senegal-et-du-mali/">APS news agency</a> independently confirmed the inauguration.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>SMTD says its domestic network already spans more than <strong>3,500 kilometers</strong> of fiber across Mali. Connecting that backbone directly to Senegal gives landlocked Mali another path toward international networks reached through Senegal and creates an alternate route if another terrestrial corridor fails. <a href="https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/senegal-numerique-sa_une-m%C3%AAme-fibre-deux-pays-deux-inaugurations-activity-7512979095929049088-fPtH">Sénégal Numérique documented the connection</a> from the Senegalese side of the border.</p>
