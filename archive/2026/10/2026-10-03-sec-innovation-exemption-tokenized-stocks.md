<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has opened a temporary route for qualifying venues to trade tokenized versions of U.S.-listed stocks on public blockchains, creating a new bridge between traditional equities and onchain market infrastructure.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In its <a href="https://twitter.com/SECGov/status/2100571317128888364">official X announcement of the Innovation Exemption</a>, the SEC said it granted temporary, conditional relief to Tokenized Securities Venues, or TSVs, allowing them to use permissioned automated market makers and liquidity pools for tokenized National Market System stocks.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-90-sec-issues-innovation-exemption-facilitate-trading-tokenized-nms-stock-request-comment">SEC’s September 17 order and announcement</a> says the exemption is temporary and conditional. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us-securities-regulator-rolls-out-five-year-exemption-tokenized-stock-trading-2026-09-17/">Reuters reported</a> that the relief runs for five years and also covers qualifying liquidity providers that otherwise could face dealer-registration requirements.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">These are supposed to be stocks, not synthetic look-alikes</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A key distinction is ownership. Under the SEC framework, eligible tokenized NMS stock must preserve the rights and privileges of the traditional security, including voting and dividend rights. Synthetic tokens that merely track a stock’s price do not qualify under this exemption.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Issuers also retain a measure of control. A venue seeking to list a third-party company’s tokenized shares must give the issuer notice and an opportunity to object. That design addresses one of the central tensions in tokenized equities: whether a blockchain representation is actually the stock or simply an instrument referencing it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Wall Street is moving closer to crypto-style market infrastructure</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The change does not instantly turn every public company into an around-the-clock blockchain asset. It creates a regulated experimental pathway where qualifying venues can test a different market structure while meeting conditions around market integrity, issuer rights, trading limits and smart contracts.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That direction connects directly with BitcoinVersus.Tech’s earlier coverage of the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/sec-crypto-custody-rulebook-wall-street-advisers-funds/">SEC’s proposed crypto custody rulebook for Wall Street</a>. It also sits beside the Federal Reserve’s work on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/29/federal-reserve-genius-act-stablecoin-rules/">GENIUS Act stablecoin rules</a>, as U.S. regulators separately define how blockchain-based money and securities can fit inside established financial systems.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The experiment has boundaries</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The exemption is not permanent rulemaking, and it does not eliminate the traditional securities framework. Instead, it gives qualifying market operators a time-limited environment to test tokenized stock trading while the Commission gathers comments and considers longer-term rules.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The practical question now shifts from whether U.S. tokenized equities can receive a regulatory pathway to which venues, issuers and investors actually use it—and whether onchain settlement can deliver meaningful benefits without fragmenting liquidity or weakening the protections attached to conventional shares.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
