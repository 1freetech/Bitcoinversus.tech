<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>SpaceX’s June 5 SEC filing says Google agreed to pay <strong>$920 million per month</strong> from October 2026 through June 2029 for access to roughly <strong>110,000 NVIDIA GPUs</strong>, plus CPUs, memory and related infrastructure.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That means the agreement has entered its full-fee period this month after a reduced-fee capacity ramp through September. The <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026041150/spacexagreementfwp.htm">SEC filing</a> says Google keeps ownership and intellectual-property rights to its models, content and data.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The contract is large, but it is not guaranteed through 2029. If SpaceX missed its September 30 GPU commitment, Google can terminate after a one-month grace period or accept reduced capacity at a proportional price. After December 31, either party can terminate with 90 days’ notice. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacex-signs-cloud-deal-with-google-2026-06-05/">Reuters</a> reported the agreement when it was filed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deal fits SpaceXAI’s rapid buildout. BitcoinVersus.Tech has covered its planned <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/xai-colossus-660000-gb300-gpu-expansion/">660,000-GPU Colossus expansion</a>, its reported <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/finance-spacex-40-billion-nvidia-chips-apollo-ai-infrastructure-debt/">$40 billion NVIDIA financing push</a>, and Google’s separate plan to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/26/google-sending-ai-chips-into-space/">send AI chips into space</a>.</p>
