<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Quanome Technologies says its XDT subsidiary has signed its first enterprise AI-inference customer, with an initial 60-month order carrying a total commitment value above $100 million. The company disclosed the agreement in an October 8 announcement that was furnished to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on October 9.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>XDT’s initial customer order runs for 60 months after the service-ready date.</strong></li><li><strong>Quanome says the total commitment value exceeds $100 million.</strong></li><li><strong>Service is expected to begin in roughly two months, but only if service-start conditions are satisfied.</strong></li><li><strong>Quanome separately ordered 32 additional GPU server units for about $18.8 million.</strong></li><li><strong>The customer was not named.</strong></li><li><strong>The $100 million figure is a contractual commitment value, not revenue already recognized.</strong></li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The primary source is <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996192/000121390026108295/ea030797001ex99-1.htm"><strong>Quanome’s October 8 press release filed with the SEC</strong></a>. It says XDT Infrastructure I LLC entered into a Master Services Agreement and initial order with an enterprise customer for dedicated AI computing capacity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Under the order, XDT is supposed to provide remotely accessible computing capacity for AI inference while XDT manages the underlying infrastructure and day-to-day operation. The agreement starts its 60-month term on the service-ready date rather than on the signing date.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Contract Is Bigger Than the Hardware Order</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Quanome says the initial customer commitment exceeds <strong>$100 million</strong>. Separately, the company entered into a purchase agreement and related order for <strong>32 additional GPU server units costing about $18.8 million</strong>. Those units are expected to go to a U.S. data center, subject to commercial and operational conditions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company had already announced an approximately $18.8 million NVIDIA-powered infrastructure purchase in September as part of the first XDT U.S. AI-infrastructure hub. Its newer XDT website describes the planned platform around dedicated NVIDIA B300 infrastructure, with cluster sizes ranging from 256 to 2,048 GPUs and the first region targeted for Q4 2026.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>BitcoinVersus.Tech recently explained the workload behind this business model in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/artificial-intelligence-training-vs-inference-what-ai-learns-does/"><strong>Training vs. Inference — What AI Learns and What AI Does</strong></a>. Training creates or updates the model; inference is what happens when a deployed model processes new inputs and produces answers, predictions or actions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">XDT Is Selling a Managed Service</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>XDT is not presenting itself as a simple GPU reseller. Its current platform material says XDT intends to own and operate the accelerators, networking and storage while customers consume model endpoints, reserved inference capacity or dedicated clusters.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That approach resembles a private AI cloud: the customer avoids buying and operating the hardware directly, while XDT takes responsibility for the physical systems, facility relationships and ongoing infrastructure operation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>BitcoinVersus.Tech has been tracking the same shift elsewhere. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/qumulusai-616-blackwell-gpus-runpod/"><strong>QumulusAI recently activated 616 Blackwell GPUs for Runpod</strong></a>, another example of companies turning expensive accelerators into externally consumed compute capacity rather than selling the hardware itself.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Service Has Not Started Yet</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The most important limitation in the announcement is timing. Quanome says XDT expects service to begin within approximately two months, but that expectation is subject to applicable service-start conditions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company also says the 32 GPU server units remain subject to delivery and deployment conditions. Its own forward-looking statement says there is no assurance that the servers will arrive on schedule, services will begin on the expected timetable or the order will ultimately generate the anticipated value.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Financial Context Matters</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Quanome’s latest annual filing makes this contract unusually important relative to the company’s existing operating base. For the year ended June 30, 2026, Quanome reported about <strong>$5.9 million of continuing revenue</strong>, primarily from pharmaceutical distribution, and a <strong>$10.1 million net loss from continuing operations</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The same filing says recurring losses, negative operating cash flow and an accumulated deficit created substantial doubt about the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. Quanome said continued operations depend on external financing, including loans, notes and potential equity financing.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That does not invalidate the XDT customer agreement, but it makes execution especially important. A contractual commitment above $100 million is not the same as cash already collected, and the agreement only becomes economically meaningful if the infrastructure is deployed, service begins and the customer continues performing under the contract.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Dedicated Inference Capacity Is Growing</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>As more AI products move from experimentation into production, inference becomes a persistent infrastructure workload. Organizations need predictable accelerator capacity, networking, storage and latency without necessarily wanting to own a complete GPU cluster.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is why networking is becoming just as important as the GPU itself. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/networking-cisco-adds-supermicro-rack-scale-systems-to-nvidia-ai-factory/"><strong>Cisco adding Supermicro rack-scale systems to its NVIDIA AI Factory architecture</strong></a>, showing how servers, switching and accelerated compute are increasingly sold as one integrated stack.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What to Watch Next</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The next useful evidence is operational rather than promotional: delivery of the GPU servers, identification or additional detail around the data-center region, satisfaction of the customer’s service-start conditions and confirmation that billing has begun.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The identity of the enterprise customer also remains undisclosed. Until more contract detail appears, investors and customers cannot independently assess the counterparty, workload scale, termination rights or how much of the headline commitment is guaranteed versus conditional.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Bottom Line</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Quanome has moved XDT from a planned AI-infrastructure project to its first disclosed enterprise customer agreement. The headline is substantial: a five-year order with more than $100 million of stated commitment value, backed by a separate 32-server infrastructure purchase.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The next test is whether the hardware arrives and the service actually starts. Until then, the most precise description is a large conditional customer commitment for an AI-inference platform that is still entering operation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong><em>Editor’s Note:</em></strong> The 60-month term, commitment value above $100 million, approximately two-month expected service-start window and $18.8 million order for 32 GPU servers are company disclosures furnished to the SEC. The customer is unnamed, service-start conditions apply and Quanome says there is no assurance the order will generate the anticipated value.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
