# Nokia and ICEYE Build Sovereign LEO Satellite Network for 2028 Launches

## Final WordPress Gutenberg source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nokia and ICEYE are taking a terrestrial networking problem into orbit: how governments can keep broadband communications operating when ground infrastructure is unavailable, disrupted or outside their control.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On October 1, the companies announced a joint plan to develop secure, sovereign, high-performance broadband systems in low Earth orbit. According to the <a href="https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers">joint announcement</a>, customers would be able to own and control the satellites, ground segment and user terminals rather than depending entirely on a foreign commercial operator. The first communications satellites are expected to launch in 2028.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A satellite network designed to be owned, not merely subscribed to</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The architecture is aimed at governments and public-sector missions spanning defense, border security, emergency services and disaster response. The companies describe the system as complementary to terrestrial and existing satellite networks rather than a wholesale replacement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That distinction matters. A sovereign communications layer is not only about coverage. It is also about who controls the spacecraft, where traffic is routed, which ground systems handle the data, how terminals are provisioned and who can change service policy during a crisis.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://twitter.com/nokia/status/2105553562285601115">Nokia’s launch-day post</a> framed the project around secure broadband links for missions that cannot assume terrestrial connectivity will always be available.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">ICEYE supplies the spacecraft layer; Nokia supplies the network layer</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ICEYE enters the partnership with experience building and operating compact low-Earth-orbit satellite systems, while Nokia brings secure IP, optical, mobile and core networking technology plus orchestration experience. The companies say the combined system will integrate satellites, ground infrastructure and ruggedized end-user terminals.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/nokia-iceye-make-satellite-systems-defence-security-2026-10-01/">Reuters reported</a> that the project puts Nokia and ICEYE into the wider competition around LEO communications, where SpaceX’s Starlink has already demonstrated how a large constellation can extend broadband far beyond conventional terrestrial coverage.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The network extends a broader move from connectivity to orbital infrastructure</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nokia has already pushed cellular technology beyond ordinary terrestrial deployments. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently examined <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/nokia-4g-lte-cellular-network-moon/">Nokia’s 4G/LTE network hardware that reached the Moon</a>, an experiment that compressed radio, routing and core-network functions into a ruggedized space-ready system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ICEYE project moves in a different direction. Instead of placing a local cellular network on another world, it aims to create a sovereign broadband layer around Earth. That means the technical problem expands from one base station or one spacecraft to constellation management, gateway placement, terminal security, routing, capacity allocation and resilient operations across multiple domains.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Low Earth orbit is becoming a computing and networking layer</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Satellite communications is converging with another trend BitcoinVersus.Tech has been tracking: compute moving into orbit. Google is preparing its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/25/google-prepares-first-orbital-ai-chip-test/">first orbital test of Tensor Processing Unit hardware</a>, putting accelerator reliability, thermal management and high-bandwidth links into the space-computing equation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the same time, the competitive landscape is widening beyond a single launch provider or broadband constellation. Our recent look at <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/23/three-companies-that-could-challenge-spacex-over-next-50-years/">companies building pieces of a long-term alternative to SpaceX</a> showed how launch, satellite networks, cloud infrastructure and orbital services can increasingly reinforce one another.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The real test begins when hardware reaches orbit</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The announcement does not yet describe a completed constellation, deployed terminals or operating customer network. The first communications satellites are targeted for 2028, which leaves substantial engineering work between partnership and service: spectrum coordination, spacecraft production, launch integration, ground-station deployment, terminal qualification and network-management software all have to work as one system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What changed this week is the shape of the product. ICEYE is extending its sovereign-satellite model beyond Earth observation, while Nokia is extending its networking stack beyond terrestrial infrastructure. If the companies execute on the 2028 launch target, the result would be a communications system designed around ownership and operational control from the spacecraft down to the terminal rather than around access to someone else’s constellation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
