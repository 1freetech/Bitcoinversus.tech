<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Grand Theft Auto VI is still the gravity well of the gaming calendar, but it is not the only game pulling massive attention. If we treat YouTube as a rough real-time measure of anticipation, three unreleased games stand out immediately behind it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For this ranking, BitcoinVersus.tech used <a href="https://hypetracker.info/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">HypeTracker's daily YouTube-based hype index</a>, which combines trailer views with creator buzz. As of its October 1 measurement, GTA VI sat at roughly 1.1 billion measured views. We then removed titles that have already released; that matters because <a href="https://www.gamesradar.com/games/rpg/the-blood-of-dawnwalker-release-date-september-2026/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">The Blood of Dawnwalker launched in September</a> and therefore no longer qualifies as an upcoming game.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That leaves a clean top three behind GTA Six in the current hype hierarchy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">1. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 — about 280.6 million measured views</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Modern Warfare 4 is the clear number one after GTA Six by this metric. Its measured YouTube footprint is more than three times larger than the next unreleased game in our filtered ranking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That makes sense. Call of Duty does not need to build awareness from zero: it carries one of the largest installed audiences in gaming, a yearly content machine, cross-platform multiplayer, Warzone integration and a global creator ecosystem that can turn every reveal, beta change and weapon-system update into millions of additional views.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The technical challenge is scale. A modern Call of Duty release has to coordinate animation, ballistics, streaming assets, network prediction, matchmaking, anti-cheat, progression and live-service updates while running across millions of different client conditions. The biggest engineering risk is rarely one spectacular feature; it is whether the whole system stays responsive under real launch-day load.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">2. Phantom Blade Zero — about 69.1 million measured views</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Phantom Blade Zero is the most interesting technical outlier in the top three. It does not have Call of Duty's decades of annual releases behind it, yet its combat clips, trailers and creator coverage have built a huge audience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The game is selling speed, visual density and extremely readable melee animation. Its developers have also shown how much rendering technology sits underneath the presentation. In a <a href="https://twitter.com/pbzero_official/status/1957546163957010907" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Phantom Blade Zero technical post</a>, the team highlighted NVIDIA DLSS 4, RTX and scanned assets captured from three ancient towns while rebuilding the in-game setting of Pangzhen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/pbzero_official/status/1957546163957010907","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"x","responsive":true} -->
<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-rich is-provider-x wp-block-embed-x"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters because high-speed sword combat punishes weak animation systems. Hit reactions, camera timing, parry windows, cloth, particles and enemy telegraphs all have to stay visually clear while the scene remains dense enough to look cinematic.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">3. The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake — about 63 million measured views</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A remake of Ocarina of Time does not need a new IP pitch. Its anticipation comes from a different problem: rebuilding one of the most influential 3D games ever made without destroying the pacing, spatial memory and mechanical identity that made the original work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8eMoxo4ipE","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true} -->
<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-video is-provider-youtube wp-block-embed-youtube"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From a development standpoint, that is almost the opposite challenge from Modern Warfare 4. The goal is not maximum systemic expansion. It is selective modernization: rendering, animation, input feel, camera behavior, audio, loading and interface work all have to improve while the underlying adventure still feels recognizably like Ocarina of Time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why GTA Six is still in a different league</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The scale gap remains enormous. GTA VI's measured YouTube footprint is roughly four times Modern Warfare 4's and far beyond the rest of the field. That lines up with what we have already seen in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/gaming-gta-6s-open-world-density-is-a-streaming-and-simulation-problem/">breakdown of GTA 6's open-world density and streaming problem</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rockstar is also trying to make the world itself behave more intelligently. Our earlier look at <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/gaming-gta-6s-wanted-system-shows-how-rockstar-is-coding-smarter-police/">GTA 6's evolving wanted-system logic</a> showed why anticipation is tied to more than graphics: players expect the simulation, AI and world-state systems to feel materially different from the last generation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And the wider 2026 lineup remains crowded with technically ambitious open-world games. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/gaming-crimson-desert-delays-charting-the-unknown-to-october-29/">Crimson Desert's open-world development story</a> is another reminder that the race after GTA Six is increasingly about who can build the most convincing simulation, not merely the biggest map.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The ranking</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>1. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4</strong> — about 280.6 million measured YouTube views and creator buzz.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>2. Phantom Blade Zero</strong> — about 69.1 million.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>3. The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake</strong> — about 63 million.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So after GTA Six, Call of Duty remains the only upcoming game remotely in the same attention tier. Phantom Blade Zero is the breakout challenger, while Ocarina of Time proves that a remake of the right game can still generate new-release levels of hype nearly three decades later.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
