<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>HOPKINS, South Carolina — October 24, 2026:</strong> Westinghouse Electric Company announced an approximately <strong>$1 billion</strong> expansion of its Columbia Fuel Fabrication Facility on October 21. The new manufacturing building will produce low-enriched uranium-plus (LEU+) fuel for commercial nuclear reactors. Construction is underway, with operations targeted for <strong>2030</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul><li>The project is a nuclear <strong>fuel-manufacturing investment</strong>, not a new 1 GW power station.</li><li>Westinghouse targets fuel containing <strong>5%–8% uranium-235</strong>, above typical commercial reactor enrichment.</li><li>The company says LEU+ may allow longer refueling cycles and support uprates at existing nuclear plants; individual reactor changes require technical and regulatory approval.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>What the New Factory Will Make</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>According to <a href="https://info.westinghousenuclear.com/news/westinghouse-announces-landmark-expansion-in-south-carolinato-advance-next-generation-nuclear-fuel">Westinghouse’s announcement</a>, the facility is designed for commercial-scale LEU+ fuel fabrication beside its existing plant, which has operated since 1968. The established site makes uranium fuel pellets, rods and assemblies and employs more than 1,200 people. The new investment expands manufacturing capability; it does not represent 1,000 MW of newly installed electrical generation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Conventional commercial reactor fuel generally contains roughly 3%–5% uranium-235. LEU+ fuel at 5%–8% enrichment could help some operators extend refueling intervals from approximately 18 to 24 months, and may support higher reactor output. These are engineering possibilities, not universal guarantees: reactor design, licensing, fuel qualification and operating limits still determine actual results.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Manufacturing and Licensing Milestones</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://www.sccommerce.com/newsroom/westinghouse-expands-richland-county-operations-advance-next-generation-nuclear-fuel">South Carolina Department of Commerce</a> confirms that construction has begun and the company expects operations in 2030. Nuclear Newswire reports a planned annual output of about 600 metric tons of uranium fuel, subject to regulatory progress. Westinghouse has engaged the Nuclear Regulatory Commission in pre-application discussions; an amended operating license remains a critical milestone.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Westinghouse says the plant will incorporate greater automation and a dry conversion process to manufacture fuel pellets. The company projects approximately $4.9 billion in statewide economic impact over ten years, based on a commissioned study. That projection is not equivalent to committed spending or guaranteed public revenue.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Why It Matters for the Power Grid</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>More nuclear generation from existing facilities would provide another option for meeting rising industrial and data-center demand without requiring an entirely new reactor at every site. However, fuel fabrication is just one part of the supply chain; enriched uranium availability, plant modifications and safety reviews also matter.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
