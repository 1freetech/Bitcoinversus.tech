<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>NASA is testing solid-state batteries designed to survive the Moon’s extreme temperature swings while reducing the thermal hardware needed to keep future lunar equipment powered.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/langley/nasa-develops-batteries-to-power-humanitys-return-to-moon/"><strong>October 14 update</strong></a>, NASA said prototype cells from its SPEARS project operated well from 80°C down to −60°C, continued working with reduced performance at −80°C, and showed no damage after exposure from 100°C down to −170°C in thermal-vacuum testing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>SPEARS replaces the liquid electrolyte used in conventional lithium-ion batteries with solid ceramic- or polymer-based material. NASA says that matters because liquid electrolytes can freeze, evaporate in vacuum, or become a fire risk. The project is targeting rovers, drills, astronaut emergency packs and long-duration instruments.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A <a href="https://science.report/discover/nasa-tests-solid-batteries-for-the-moon-s-deep-cold-107725/"><strong>separate technical summary</strong></a> highlights the main limitation: the test does not show a flight-ready battery that can operate through the coldest lunar conditions. NASA says the Moon’s South Pole can fall to about −200°C. The current cells survive much colder temperatures than they can actively operate in and would still need heaters before use.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The work fits a wider lunar engineering push that includes <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/blue-origin-power-tower-lunar-solar-moon-base/"><strong>surface power systems</strong></a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/how-lunar-landers-survive-the-moon/"><strong>extreme-environment lander design</strong></a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/prologium-rimac-ev-battery-testing/"><strong>solid-state battery development on Earth</strong></a>.</p>
