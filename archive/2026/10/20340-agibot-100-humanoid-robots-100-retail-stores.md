# AGIBOT Deploys 100 Humanoid Robots Across 100 Retail Stores

Live article: https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/agibot-100-humanoid-robots-100-retail-stores/

## Final WordPress Gutenberg Source

```html
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AGIBOT is moving humanoid robots out of controlled demonstrations and into ordinary retail work, deploying 100 X2 robots across 100 stores operated by Chinese cookware company ASD.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://agibot.com/article/231/detail/124.html">September 28 deployment announcement</a>, AGIBOT said the X2 robots are working as in-store sales assistants, greeting shoppers, asking about their needs, explaining products, recommending cookware, guiding customers through stores, and handing conversations to human staff when inventory, promotions, or final purchases require a person.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">One hundred stores is a different test from one demo floor</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A humanoid that performs well on a stage still has to prove it can function around unpredictable people, changing conversations, crowded aisles, store employees, and products that were not arranged specifically for a robot demonstration. AGIBOT says the 100-store rollout is meant to test exactly that kind of day-to-day customer-facing work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deployment fits a broader shift in humanoid robotics from impressive single-task demonstrations toward longer workflows. BitcoinVersus recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/dyna-taku-dyna-2-1-full-workflows-physical-agent/">Dyna’s Taku robot targeting complete workflows instead of isolated tasks</a>, while <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/boston-dynamics-atlas-four-finger-13-dof-hands/">Boston Dynamics redesigned Atlas hands around tool use and dexterous manipulation</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AGIBOT’s official <a href="https://twitter.com/AGIBOTofficial/status/2104932928337948994">X post</a> shows X2 robots joining ASD’s livestream, introducing products and participating in cooking demonstrations alongside people.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/AGIBOTofficial/status/2104932928337948994","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"x","responsive":true} -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The robot is being used as a sales assistant, not a cashier replacement</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AGIBOT describes a division of labor rather than full store automation. X2 can receive customers, ask follow-up questions, explain cookware, and recommend products based on information shoppers provide. Human staff remain responsible for areas such as promotions, inventory information, and completing purchases.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That distinction matters. A retail robot does not have to replace every employee to be operationally useful. A more realistic near-term role is handling repetitive first-contact conversations, demonstrations, directions, and product explanations while people take over cases that require judgment, access to store systems, or accountability.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/agibot-asd-100-x2-retail-stores">Humanoids Daily independently reported</a> the 100-store deployment and the three-day livestream program, while noting that the rollout follows AGIBOT’s expansion into other public-facing environments.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Retail tests a different kind of robotics intelligence</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Factory robots can often rely on repeatable geometry and carefully controlled work cells. Retail is messier. Customers approach from different directions, interrupt one another, ask vague questions, change their minds, and expect a system to understand ordinary speech while remaining physically safe around children, carts, shelving, and employees.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That makes the challenge partly conversational and partly embodied. The robot has to know what to say, where to stand, when to move, when not to move, and when to hand the interaction to a person. The same trend is visible in research hardware such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/eth-zurich-robot-hand-walks-fingertips-fingers-as-legs/">ETH Zurich’s self-supporting robot hand</a>, where physical intelligence increasingly means coordinating perception, movement, recovery, and manipulation rather than optimizing one motion in isolation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The deployment still needs independent operating data</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The 100-store scale is meaningful, but AGIBOT has not publicly supplied detailed independent metrics for uptime, intervention rate, customer satisfaction, sales conversion, failure frequency, maintenance burden, or how often employees must take control of an interaction. Those numbers will matter more than the novelty of seeing a humanoid beside a cookware display.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Even so, the rollout moves the discussion forward. The question is no longer only whether humanoids can perform compelling demonstrations. It is whether fleets can remain useful, safe, understandable, and economically sensible when ordinary customers meet them every day. One hundred stores gives AGIBOT a much larger environment in which to find out.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
```
