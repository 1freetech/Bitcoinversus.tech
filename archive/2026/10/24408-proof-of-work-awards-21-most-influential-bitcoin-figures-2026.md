<p class="has-medium-font-size"><em>The 2026 Proof of Work Awards honor the people whose work has materially moved Bitcoin forward through code, mining, hardware, capital, policy, infrastructure, research, education and real-world adoption.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size"><em>This continues the format of our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/20/proof-of-work-awards-meet-the-10-most-influential-bitcoin-leaders-in-2024-honorable-mention-included/">2024 Proof of Work Awards</a>, but the ranking is rebuilt from scratch for 2026. Influence here is not the same as fame, wealth or endorsement. We weighted documented work from January 1 through October 13, 2026 across protocol development, mining, Lightning, corporate adoption, sovereign strategy, policy, open-source infrastructure and capital markets.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size"><em>Why 21? Bitcoin's fixed supply is 21 million. The descriptions intentionally grow longer as the countdown approaches No. 1.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>21. Alex Leishman — Founder and CEO, River</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Alex Leishman earns the opening spot for pushing Bitcoin deeper into ordinary financial life. In April, River launched a broader banking product that lets eligible users receive paychecks by direct deposit, automatically convert part of them into bitcoin, earn interest on cash that can be paid in bitcoin, and pay bills from cash or bitcoin. River is not a bank, but its 2026 product direction is a serious attempt to make Bitcoin part of day-to-day financial plumbing rather than a separate trading account. <a href="https://river.com/content/banking-with-bitcoin">River announced the banking expansion on April 13</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>20. Dennis Porter — CEO and Co-Founder, Satoshi Action Fund</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Dennis Porter's influence in 2026 is primarily political and infrastructure-focused. Satoshi Action Fund backed the federal Mined in America Act, legislation introduced by Senators Bill Cassidy and Cynthia Lummis that would create a voluntary certification program for domestic mining, encourage U.S. mining-hardware manufacturing, reduce foreign-adversary supply-chain dependence and codify the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. Porter has helped move Bitcoin mining from a niche energy debate toward a broader discussion of industrial policy and national infrastructure. <a href="https://www.cassidy.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cassidy-lummis-introduce-bill-to-boost-u-s-digital-asset-mining-back-president-trumps-strategic-bitcoin-reserve/">The Senate announcement specifically cites Satoshi Action Fund's support</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>19. Luke Dashjr — Bitcoin Developer and Mining Decentralization Advocate</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Luke Dashjr remained one of Bitcoin's most consequential and polarizing technical voices. In August, he separated from OCEAN, resigning as chairman, CTO and director, and announced a new mining venture called CONVOY focused on decentralizing Bitcoin mining. His positions on transaction policy and miner behavior continue to generate intense disagreement, but that debate itself demonstrates his influence: Dashjr remains capable of forcing miners, developers and node operators to confront basic questions about policy, block construction and decentralization. <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/joint-statement-of-ocean-and-luke-dashjr-302864347.html">OCEAN and Dashjr announced the split on August 29</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>18. Gregory Sanders — Bitcoin Developer and BIP Co-Author</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gregory Sanders, known widely in Bitcoin development circles as instagibbs, is one of the authors behind two of 2026's most important covenant proposals. BIP 446 proposes <code>OP_TEMPLATEHASH</code>, while BIP 448 combines that proposal with <code>OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK</code> and <code>OP_INTERNALKEY</code> to enable new Taproot-native transaction constructions. Both remain drafts, which is crucial: this is research and specification work, not activated consensus. But the proposals have become central to serious discussion about safer second layers, Ark, Lightning improvements and the boundaries of future Bitcoin scripting. <a href="https://bips.dev/446/">BIP 446</a> and <a href="https://bips.dev/448/">BIP 448</a> document that work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>17. Steven Roose — CEO, Second; Ark and Bitcoin Protocol Developer</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Steven Roose combines protocol research with shipping software. He co-authored BIPs 446 and 448, but in 2026 his Ark work moved beyond proposals. Second's Bark implementation reached Bitcoin mainnet in June, giving developers and users a working Ark system for fast, self-custodial off-chain payments. In August, Roose also launched a signet demonstration of Bark running against <code>OP_TEMPLATEHASH</code>, creating a real prototype for how proposed covenant functionality could simplify Ark. That ability to connect a consensus proposal to running second-layer software gives Roose an unusually practical role in the covenant debate. <a href="https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/08/21/">Bitcoin Optech documented the OP_TEMPLATEHASH demonstration</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>16. Antoine Poinsot — Bitcoin Developer and Protocol Researcher</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Antoine Poinsot spent 2026 working close to Bitcoin's consensus layer. Together with Sanders and Roose, he co-authored BIP 446 and BIP 448, proposals aimed at adding narrowly scoped Taproot functionality for transaction commitments, signature verification and internal-key access. Their stated motivation is not to turn Bitcoin into a general-purpose smart-contract chain, but to make established second-layer designs less interactive and more efficient. That conservative engineering framing matters because any consensus change must clear an exceptionally high bar. Poinsot's influence comes from helping define one of the year's most serious technical pathways for expanding Bitcoin functionality while preserving the base layer's deliberately limited design.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>15. Peter Todd — Bitcoin Core Contributor and Slipstream Lead Maintainer</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Peter Todd's 2026 work connected protocol expertise directly to a real security emergency. The MARA Foundation brought the longtime Bitcoin Core contributor in as lead maintainer of Slipstream, MARA's private mempool service for direct transaction submission to its mining pool. According to the Foundation, Slipstream helped users move more than 10,000 BTC out of affected Coldcard multisig wallets during the July 2026 firmware exploit. That episode illustrated why alternative transaction-relay paths and experienced Bitcoin engineers matter during abnormal network conditions. <a href="https://foundation.mara.com/articles/peter-todd-joins-mara-foundation-slipstream">MARA Foundation announced Todd's new role and described Slipstream's use during the incident</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>14. Skot — Creator of Bitaxe and 256 Foundation Board Member</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Skot remains one of the clearest symbols of open-source Bitcoin mining. Bitaxe helped prove that modern ASIC mining can be accessible, inspectable and hackable rather than confined to sealed industrial boxes. In 2026, the broader 256 Foundation ecosystem pushed that idea into a more complete open stack: hashboards, control boards, firmware, pool software and fleet management. At Bitcoin 2026, the DOOMAXE demonstration combined Bitaxe with Libre Board, Mujina, Hydrapool, a Bitcoin node and Proto Fleet in one open reference system. The community then voted the 256 Foundation the winner of MARA Foundation's inaugural $100,000 grant. That is meaningful proof that open-source mining has matured from a hobbyist niche into a recognized decentralization effort. <a href="https://www.256foundation.org/newsroom/mara-foundation-tier1-supporter">The 256 Foundation documented the grant and open mining stack</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>13. Robin Linus Woll — Creator of BitVM</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Robin Linus Woll remains one of Bitcoin's most important second-layer researchers because BitVM attacks a fundamental problem: how can complex computation be verified against Bitcoin without turning Bitcoin itself into a general-purpose execution layer? At USENIX Security 2026, Woll and collaborators presented BitVM2, including a light-client-based Bitcoin bridge design with a 1-of-n setup assumption, permissionless challenges and a production-level implementation. The team reported that a complete challenge verification had been executed on Bitcoin mainnet. This does not make trustless bridges a solved problem, but it moves BitVM from a provocative 2023 idea toward testable infrastructure with concrete security assumptions. <a href="https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/woll">USENIX published the paper and implementation claims</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>12. Fred Thiel — Chairman and CEO, MARA</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Fred Thiel's influence spans hashrate, energy, treasury management and open-source funding. MARA reported 70.3 EH/s of energized hashrate in the second quarter of 2026, mined 2,422 BTC during the quarter and held 35,577 BTC at June 30. At the same time, the company expanded a power-and-compute portfolio that it expects to reach roughly 4.8 GW if pending transactions close. The more unusual move was institutional: MARA launched the MARA Foundation at Bitcoin 2026 to fund Bitcoin security research, quantum-resistance work, open-source development, self-custody infrastructure, policy and education. Its first $100,000 community-selected contribution went to the 256 Foundation. Thiel therefore influenced both the industrial side of Proof of Work and the public-good infrastructure surrounding it. <a href="https://www.mara.com/posts/q2-2026-mara-earnings-call">MARA's Q2 update provides the operating figures</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>11. Elizabeth Stark — Co-Founder and CEO, Lightning Labs</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Elizabeth Stark's Lightning Labs delivered two major pieces of Bitcoin payment infrastructure in 2026. LND 0.21 brought onion messaging, production-ready simple Taproot channels, RBF cooperative closes and continued database work. Weeks later, Taproot Assets v0.8 arrived alongside the first public Taproot Assets SDK, making it easier for developers to build applications that issue and move assets over Lightning while anchoring them to Bitcoin. Lightning Labs describes Taproot Assets as the first multi-asset Lightning protocol on mainnet. Whether stablecoins ultimately become a major Lightning use case remains an open market question, but Stark's organization is clearly widening Lightning's scope beyond BTC-only payments while continuing to harden the underlying node software. <a href="https://lightning.engineering/posts/2026-06-11-lnd-0.21-launch/">LND 0.21</a> and <a href="https://lightning.engineering/posts/2026-06-23-tapd-0.8-launch/">Taproot Assets v0.8</a> were both released in June.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>10. Simon Gerovich — CEO, Metaplanet</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Simon Gerovich turned Metaplanet into the clearest international replication of the public-company Bitcoin treasury model. The company's own tracker shows holdings rising from 35,102 BTC at the end of 2025 to 40,177 BTC on March 31, 43,000 BTC on June 30 and 44,000 BTC on September 30. That scale makes Metaplanet one of the largest public corporate holders of Bitcoin in the world and, more importantly, proves that Bitcoin-centric treasury structures are not confined to U.S. capital markets. Gerovich has had to adapt the model to Japanese equity markets, yen financing and a different shareholder base. The result is a global test of whether Bitcoin-per-share accumulation can become a durable corporate strategy across jurisdictions. <a href="https://treasury.metaplanet.jp/">Metaplanet publishes the acquisition history in its Bitcoin Strategy Tracker</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>9. Jack Mallers — Founder and CEO, Strike</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Jack Mallers spent 2026 pushing Bitcoin financial services beyond simple brokerage. Strike launched a Bitcoin-backed line of credit in March, entered New York days later and continued serving users across more than 100 countries. The company also expanded engineering around security, smart order routing and software automation. Mallers' significance comes from his attempt to build a Bitcoin-native financial institution where customers can buy, hold, send, borrow against and increasingly live from Bitcoin without treating it as a casino asset. That is a different adoption path from ETFs or corporate treasuries: Strike is trying to make Bitcoin a usable financial base layer for individuals and businesses. <a href="https://strike.me/gb/en/about/">Strike's company timeline records the 2026 line-of-credit and New York launches</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>8. King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck — King of Bhutan</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bhutan remains the world's most unusual sovereign Bitcoin experiment because its position was built substantially through hydroelectric mining rather than an open-market treasury purchase. King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck connected that accumulated Bitcoin to a long-term national development strategy through Gelephu Mindfulness City. Bhutan had pledged up to 10,000 BTC toward the city's development, and in 2026 GMC appointed 3iQ to manage an undisclosed portion of its Bitcoin treasury under a market-neutral mandate. The country's own pledge frames the asset as surplus renewable energy transformed into long-term national value for jobs, technology skills and financial resilience. This is not merely a government holding seized coins. It is a sovereign strategy that links energy, mining, Bitcoin reserves and physical economic development. <a href="https://gmc.bt/pledge">Gelephu Mindfulness City publishes the Bitcoin pledge and its rationale</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>7. Paolo Ardoino — CEO, Tether</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Paolo Ardoino sits at an unusual intersection of stablecoins, Bitcoin capital and Bitcoin infrastructure. In May, Tether acquired SoftBank's stake in Twenty One Capital and became the controlling shareholder of a company holding more than 43,500 BTC. Twenty One is not pitched as a passive treasury shell: its stated vision spans mining, treasury management, capital markets and financial services. At the same time, Tether's USDT strategy increasingly intersects with Bitcoin through Taproot Assets and Lightning infrastructure. That means Ardoino is helping allocate enormous capital toward Bitcoin while also participating in efforts to make Bitcoin rails useful for assets other than BTC. The combination gives him influence over both balance-sheet demand and payment-network experimentation. <a href="https://tether.io/news/tether-designates-independent-director-to-twenty-one-capitals-board/">Tether described its control of Twenty One and the company's 43,500-plus BTC position in June</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>6. Cynthia Lummis — U.S. Senator from Wyoming</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Cynthia Lummis remains the most persistent Bitcoin-specific policymaker in the U.S. Senate. Her 2026 influence came from connecting Bitcoin policy to physical infrastructure. The Mined in America Act, introduced with Senator Bill Cassidy on March 30, would create a voluntary certification program for U.S. cryptocurrency miners and mining pools, encourage domestic manufacturing of secure and energy-efficient mining hardware, reduce dependence on foreign-adversary equipment, use existing federal energy and rural programs to support qualifying projects and codify the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve inside Treasury.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That framing matters. Bitcoin policy in Washington has historically centered on exchanges, taxes, securities law and consumer protection. Lummis is increasingly forcing policymakers to consider ASIC supply chains, power generation, mining pools and strategic reserves as one connected industrial system. Whether the bill becomes law is separate from its influence on the debate. <a href="https://www.cassidy.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cassidy-lummis-introduce-bill-to-boost-u-s-digital-asset-mining-back-president-trumps-strategic-bitcoin-reserve/">The Senate release outlines the bill's five main provisions</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>5. Jihan Wu — Chairman and CEO, Bitdeer</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Jihan Wu returned to the center of the ASIC race in 2026. Bitdeer launched the SEALMINER A4 family in April around its internally developed SEAL04 chip. The flagship A4 Ultra Hydro is rated at 886 TH/s and 9.45 J/TH, crossing below the 10 J/TH threshold in the company's published specifications. Bitdeer also reported self-mining hashrate above 65 EH/s during the year, giving it the rare combination of chip design, machine manufacturing, mining operations and global power infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That combination matters because ASIC competition is itself a decentralization issue. A network secured by Proof of Work depends on specialized chips, and the smaller the number of companies capable of building cutting-edge SHA-256 hardware, the more concentrated the supply chain becomes. Wu previously helped create Bitmain, the company that defined much of Bitcoin's industrial-mining era. His work at Bitdeer now gives the market another vertically integrated hardware competitor. BitcoinVersus.Tech's own <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/top-10-most-efficient-bitcoin-mining-machines-2026-j-th/">2026 efficiency ranking</a> shows how intensely that machine race is accelerating.</p>

<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>4. Jack Dorsey — Block Head and Co-Founder, Block</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Jack Dorsey's Bitcoin influence comes from building across several layers at once. Block operates Bitkey for self-custody, Proto for mining, Cash App for Bitcoin access and Spiral for open-source development. In 2026, Proto Fleet became one of the most interesting pieces of that stack: a free, open-source, self-hosted mining-management system designed to pair devices, collect telemetry and manage heterogeneous fleets without forcing operators into a closed vendor platform.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The software supports miners and firmware from multiple manufacturers, and its architecture explicitly builds on the 256 Foundation's open-source <code>asic-rs</code> work. That matters because industrial mining does not only depend on chips. It depends on fleet management, firmware, telemetry, repair workflows and the software that decides how thousands of machines are operated. Dorsey's strategy is to make more of that stack open and auditable while also building physical mining hardware and consumer self-custody tools. Most wealthy Bitcoin advocates buy the asset. Dorsey continues trying to build the infrastructure around it. <a href="https://github.com/block/proto-fleet">Proto Fleet is developed publicly on GitHub</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>3. Larry Fink — Chairman and CEO, BlackRock</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Larry Fink's influence is almost the mirror image of Dorsey's. He does not write Bitcoin software or manufacture ASICs. BlackRock instead built one of the largest bridges ever created between Bitcoin and institutional capital. By September 24, 2026, BlackRock reported about $67.3 billion in net assets inside the iShares Bitcoin Trust, IBIT. The ETF trades on Nasdaq and gives brokerage and institutional customers Bitcoin price exposure through familiar financial infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That distinction must be stated clearly: ETF ownership is not self-custody. Investors do not hold their own Bitcoin keys, and an ETF cannot reproduce the censorship resistance of direct ownership. But the Proof of Work Awards measure influence, not ideological purity. Fink helped make Bitcoin a standard allocation question for financial advisers, institutional portfolios and mainstream brokerage customers. The scale of IBIT has consequences for Bitcoin liquidity, ownership concentration, regulation and public legitimacy. Wall Street once debated whether Bitcoin belonged inside the financial system. Under Fink, the world's largest asset manager distributes it. <a href="https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/products/333011/fund">BlackRock publishes IBIT's current fund statistics</a>.</p>

<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>2. Donald Trump — President of the United States</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Donald Trump ranks second because no individual exercises more direct government power over the legal and institutional environment surrounding Bitcoin in the United States. This ranking measures influence, not political endorsement. The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve was established by executive action in 2025, but its consequences continued into 2026 as lawmakers moved to codify it and connect it to domestic mining and hardware policy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Trump added another major policy signal on May 19, 2026 with Executive Order 14405. The order directs federal financial regulators to review rules, supervisory practices and payment-access frameworks with the stated goal of allowing digital assets and fintech firms to integrate more directly with traditional financial services and payment systems. Separately, the Mined in America Act introduced in March explicitly tied domestic mining, hardware manufacturing and the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve together.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Whether those policies ultimately help Bitcoin, create new risks, or face legislative and legal limits is a matter for continued scrutiny. Their influence, however, is difficult to dispute. The United States controls the world's deepest capital markets, major mining capacity and powerful financial regulators. When the presidency treats Bitcoin as a reserve asset and digital-asset infrastructure as part of national financial policy, the debate changes far beyond Washington. <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/05/integrating-financial-technology-innovation-into-regulatory-frameworks/">The White House published Executive Order 14405 on May 19</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"large"} -->
<p class="has-large-font-size"><em>1. Michael Saylor — Founder and Executive Chairman, Strategy</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the second Proof of Work Awards cycle, Michael Saylor takes the top position. The reason is not simply his visibility. His corporate Bitcoin strategy has become an entire capital-markets category.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On October 12, Strategy disclosed the purchase of another 250 BTC. As of October 11, the company held <strong>848,250 BTC</strong> acquired for an aggregate cost of roughly $64 billion. That is more than 4% of Bitcoin's fixed 21 million supply. Strategy has also spent 2026 developing a broader capital structure around Bitcoin through preferred securities, common-equity issuance, dollar reserves, repurchases and what the company calls digital credit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The model has also endured a real stress test. Bitcoin weakness in 2026 pushed Strategy's valuation below the market value of its Bitcoin holdings for a period, and the company authorized limited Bitcoin sales within a broader capital framework. That is important because influence is not the same as uninterrupted success. Saylor's model now has enough scale that its strengths, financing costs, dilution risks, preferred-stock obligations and treasury decisions have become questions for the entire corporate Bitcoin sector.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>More important than the number of coins is imitation. Companies in multiple countries now compete to increase Bitcoin per share, finance BTC accumulation and describe themselves as Bitcoin treasury companies. Metaplanet, Strive, Twenty One and others are testing variations of an architecture that Strategy made globally visible. Saylor did not invent corporate treasury management, and he did not create Bitcoin. What he did was make Bitcoin the organizing principle of a public company's balance sheet and then scale that idea until global capital markets had to take it seriously.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus.Tech has continued tracking his evolving thesis, including his recent argument that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/michael-saylor-ai-agents-digital-money-bitcoin/">AI agents will need digital money</a>. For 2026, no single individual has done more to make Bitcoin a central variable in corporate finance. That makes Michael Saylor the <strong>2026 Proof of Work Awards Most Influential Bitcoin Figure of the Year</strong>. <a href="https://www.strategy.com/press/strategy-acquires-250-btc-and-repurchases-192-million-of-strc_10-12-2026">Strategy's October 12 disclosure confirms the 848,250 BTC position</a>.</p>
