<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>BitFuFu and Finloop have launched HASH, a tokenized note designed to give eligible investors Bitcoin-denominated economic exposure to professionally managed Bitcoin mining without requiring them to own or operate ASIC miners.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to BitFuFu’s <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1921158/000121390026109550/ea030832401ex99-1.htm">October 15 SEC filing</a>, the underlying hashrate is provided as a service and contributes to actual Bitcoin mining activity. The resulting mining output forms the economic basis for HASH, while profit distributions are recorded on-chain. Returns remain denominated in BTC rather than being converted into dollars during the investment period.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That distinction matters. HASH is <strong>not a new Bitcoin-native asset</strong>, does not change Bitcoin’s consensus rules, and does not represent direct ownership of mining machines. It is a financial instrument tied to the economics generated by mining infrastructure operated by BitFuFu.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Mining Exposure Without Running the Hardware</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Traditional Bitcoin mining requires machines, electrical infrastructure, cooling, network connectivity, pool configuration, maintenance and continuous attention to operating efficiency. HASH moves the investor-facing layer away from that physical stack. BitFuFu continues operating or sourcing the hashrate, while the note packages the resulting mining economics into an on-chain product.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Legal Restrictions Are Part of the Product</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitFuFu says HASH is available only to certain eligible investors and is subject to jurisdictional, investor-qualification, KYC and anti-money-laundering requirements. The company’s filing also says the note has not been registered under the U.S. Securities Act and may not generally be offered or sold in the United States or to U.S. persons except through applicable exemptions or Regulation S.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://cryptobriefing.com/bitfufu-finloop-hash-tokenized-note-btc-mining/">Crypto Briefing</a> reports that the product is structured as a 12-month note with planned deployment across Ethereum and Solana. Those chains provide the tokenization layer; the economic source remains Bitcoin mining rather than proof-of-stake rewards from either network.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Mining Risks Do Not Disappear</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tokenization can simplify access, but it does not remove the underlying mining variables. Mining output can still change with network difficulty, machine uptime, electricity cost, pool performance, transaction fees and Bitcoin’s market price. BitcoinVersus.Tech has been tracking those same operating pressures through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/canaan-44-btc-september-power-cost/">Canaan’s September production and power-cost data</a>, the broader <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitcoin-miner-revenue-rebounded-78-percent-btc-price-hashprice-fees/">rebound in Bitcoin miner revenue</a>, and our explainer on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/bitcoin-mining-it-what-is-stratum-asic-miners-pools/">how Stratum connects ASIC miners to mining pools</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The result is a new layer between capital and physical mining infrastructure: investors can gain exposure to mining-linked BTC returns while professional operators handle the machines. Whether that structure produces attractive results will still depend on the economics of the underlying hashrate.</p>
