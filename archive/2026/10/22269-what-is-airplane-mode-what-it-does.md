<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Airplane Mode is a quick way to stop your phone from using its normal cellular connection.</strong> That little airplane icon does not shut your phone down. It simply changes which wireless connections the phone is allowed to use.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That means you can still take pictures, read downloaded books, play offline games, listen to saved music, write notes, or watch something you downloaded before the flight.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Is It Called Airplane Mode?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The setting became common because phones normally try to communicate with cellular towers. While flying, passengers are asked to disable normal cellular transmission or use Airplane Mode.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentid/1032206"><strong>Federal Aviation Administration</strong></a> tells aircraft operators to have passengers turn off cellular functions or place cellular-capable devices in Airplane Mode while airborne in U.S. airspace.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Does It Turn Off?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The big one is <strong>cellular service</strong>. Your phone stops talking to the normal mobile network, so regular cellular calls, texts, and mobile data stop working until that connection is turned back on.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Depending on the phone, Airplane Mode may also switch off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth when you first turn it on. But modern phones usually let you turn Wi-Fi or Bluetooth back on while leaving Airplane Mode active.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>So Wi-Fi Can Still Work?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Yes. If the airline allows Wi-Fi, you can turn Airplane Mode on and then turn Wi-Fi back on. That is how people use in-flight internet without reconnecting their phone to ordinary cellular towers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bluetooth can also usually be turned back on, which is why wireless headphones often still work during a flight. Apple, for example, explains that Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can be used while Airplane Mode is active when the airline permits them.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Your Phone Is Still a Phone-Sized Computer</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Airplane Mode only changes wireless connections. It does not erase your apps or disable the rest of the device. Think of it like closing a few doors instead of shutting down the entire house.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Your camera still works. Your calculator still works. Downloads still work. Most offline games still work. Anything that does not need an internet connection can keep running normally.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Airplane Mode Can Be Useful on the Ground Too</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>You do not have to be on an airplane to use it. Airplane Mode can be handy when you want a quick break from calls and cellular data, when you are somewhere with very poor cellular reception, or when you simply want the phone to stop trying to connect for a while.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>It is also a fast way to reset wireless connections: some people briefly turn Airplane Mode on and off when a phone is having trouble reconnecting to a mobile network.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Does Airplane Mode Make Your Phone Invisible?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>No. Airplane Mode is not a privacy cloak. If you turn Wi-Fi or Bluetooth back on, those radios are active again. Apps and the phone itself can also keep doing things that do not require a live cellular connection.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>So the simple rule is: <strong>Airplane Mode mainly controls wireless connections. It does not turn the whole phone off.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Easy Way to Remember It</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Airplane Mode = cellular connection off, phone still on.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>You can still use offline apps, pictures, music and games. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can often be turned back on separately when allowed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
