<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CoreWeave is expanding into India with plans for 240 megawatts of AI data-center capacity at AdaniConneX’s Taloja campus in Navi Mumbai. The project is planned across three 80 MW buildings and is expected to use NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform for AI training, inference, reasoning, and agentic workloads.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CoreWeave also says it will open an office in India and hire locally. In its <a href="https://twitter.com/CoreWeave/status/2108243477444358640">original X post</a>, the company highlighted CEO Michael Intrator’s discussion of the expansion and the need to put AI compute closer to customers around the world.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:shortcode -->[tweet https://twitter.com/CoreWeave/status/2108243477444358640]<!-- /wp:shortcode -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Three 80 MW Buildings in Navi Mumbai</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The planned deployment totals 240 MW, with each building accounting for 80 MW. CoreWeave says the site will support the NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform, which places the project squarely in the next generation of dense AI infrastructure rather than conventional enterprise hosting.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That matters because AI campuses are increasingly constrained by power, cooling, networking, and construction timelines as much as by GPU supply. BitcoinVersus recently covered how <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/senate-probe-questions-ai-data-center-costs-and-jobs/">lawmakers are questioning the costs and local impacts of AI data centers</a>, while CoreWeave’s own stack is already moving toward <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/coreweave-forge-vera-rubin-production-ai/">Vera Rubin production infrastructure</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:shortcode -->[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MkMQj-Q0bBo]<!-- /wp:shortcode -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Why India Matters</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>India gives CoreWeave access to a large base of developers, enterprises, and AI demand while extending its infrastructure footprint in Asia. The company’s announcement says the local office and hiring plan are intended to support customers and partners directly, rather than treating the region only as remote capacity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The move also fits a broader global race to place high-density AI infrastructure closer to fast-growing markets. Earlier international projects, including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/09/21/nvidia-and-openai-partner-with-uae-on-massive-ai-data-center-2/">NVIDIA-linked AI data-center expansion in the UAE</a>, show how geography, energy, and access to advanced accelerators are becoming strategic decisions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>What Is Confirmed and What Is Planned</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The collaboration and 240 MW plan are announced. The buildings are not yet described as operating capacity, and the Vera Rubin deployment remains a future installation. That distinction matters: announced megawatts should not be counted as live compute until the facilities are energized and systems are installed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>SmartNews Takeaway</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CoreWeave’s India move is another sign that the AI infrastructure race is becoming global at the campus level. The headline number is 240 MW, but the more important test will be how quickly the company converts that planned capacity into operating Vera Rubin systems with reliable power, cooling, and network connectivity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
