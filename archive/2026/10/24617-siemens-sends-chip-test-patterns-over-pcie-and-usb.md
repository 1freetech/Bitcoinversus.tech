<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Siemens has launched Tessent Silicon Executable, a new semiconductor-test tool that can deliver deterministic test patterns through high-speed interfaces such as PCIe and USB.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-tessent-silicon-executable/">October 13 announcement</a>, Siemens said the software extends its Tessent workflow from design-for-test insertion and pattern generation into direct execution on silicon. The goal is to let the same test content move across automated test equipment, system-level test, bench and in-system environments without repeated pattern conversion.</p>

Failure data can then flow back into existing Tessent diagnosis tools.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The change matters as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/lisa-su-national-medal-science-chiplets-exascale-computing/">chiplet-based processors</a> grow more complex, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/hardware-hpe-proliant-gen13-256-core-amd-epyc-venice-pcie-6-ai-servers/">PCIe 6 enters AI servers</a>, and new silicon still has to move through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/bolt-graphics-zeus-test-chip-silicon-bringup/">bring-up and validation</a> efficiently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> chip testing is becoming a bandwidth problem. Reusing the interfaces already built into a device could reduce test overhead and speed debugging as AI and data-center chips add more cores, chiplets and test data.</p>
