# New Photonics Specifications Target AI Data Center Manufacturing

<!-- Canonical Gutenberg content follows; do not replace with rendered HTML. -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>October 20, 2026 — Sunnyvale, California.</strong> The <a href="https://www.advanced-photonics.org/">Advanced Photonics Coalition</a> (APC) has released five specifications and recommended-practice documents aimed at making optical-networking hardware easier to manufacture at scale. Developed with participation from 60 member organizations, the portfolio addresses co-packaged optics, silicon photonics manufacturing, four-core multicore fiber, and system design that can support optical circuit switching.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The release targets a growing engineering challenge for AI data centers: moving data among accelerators and switches at greater bandwidth without letting interconnect power, physical space, testing complexity and manufacturing costs grow unchecked. <strong>APC describes lower production costs as an objective, not a measured industry-wide result.</strong> The announcement does not provide audited cost-per-bit savings, production yields or commercial deployment totals. <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/advanced-photonics-coalition-announces-portfolio-of-specifications-and-best-practice-documents-to-accelerate-high-volume-manufacturing-of-optical-networking-gear-302913234.html">Read APC's October 20 primary announcement.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>APC's initial portfolio includes <strong>five</strong> published documents covering optical packaging, production testing, physical fiber interfaces and system integration.</li><li>Common interfaces and test methods could improve repeatability and let manufacturers source compatible components from more suppliers.</li><li>Four-core multicore fiber could increase spatial channel density, while co-packaged optics could reduce the electrical reach between a switching ASIC and its optical engines.</li><li>A specification is <strong>not</strong> proof of lower commercial costs, interoperability across all vendors or field reliability.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What The Five Publications Cover</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>System Level Architecture Overview for Co-Packaged Optics:</strong> a reference framework for fitting optical engines, lasers, fiber connections and switching silicon into a package and system.</li><li><strong>Co-Packaged Optics Informative Outlook and Recommended Practices:</strong> guidance on integration, operations, serviceability and possible support for optical circuit switching.</li><li><strong>Silicon Photonics for Co-Packaged Optics Recommended Test Practices:</strong> an approach to testing optical and electrical performance during successive manufacturing stages.</li><li><strong>4-Core Multicore Fiber Cable, Connector and Fiber Array Unit Specification:</strong> common expectations for four-core fiber, its connectors and the optical attachment interface.</li><li><strong>Silicon Photonics Manufacturing Specification:</strong> shared terminology and interfaces for the manufacturing and assembly of photonic integrated circuits.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>APC's <a href="https://www.advanced-photonics.org/document-request">document request page</a> offers the publications free after registration. The documents mix specifications, informative guidance and reference architectures; these do not all carry the same compliance status. Technical buyers should examine the normative provisions and conformance methods individually rather than treating the entire portfolio as a certification.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How Standardization Could Reduce Costs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Photonics assembly requires exceptionally precise alignment between optical chips, fiber-array units, lasers and detectors. Manufacturers may otherwise design customized fixtures, calibration routines, connector geometries and test recipes for each customer. Shared interfaces and measurement conventions offer a route to reusing production tooling, reducing engineering redesign, qualifying alternative suppliers and comparing component performance more consistently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The economic mechanism is plausible: higher-volume common parts, reusable test procedures and multiple sourcing can spread tooling and engineering costs over more units. But the outcome still depends on wafer yield, fiber-attachment yield, automated test time, optical power, thermal performance, supplier maturity and maintenance costs. Manufacturers may initially incur extra qualification expenses as they transition to new specifications.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">More Bandwidth In Less Space</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Co-packaged optics (CPO)</strong> moves optical engines near switching silicon rather than relying exclusively on pluggable transceivers connected through relatively long high-speed electrical traces. Shorter electrical paths can support denser optical I/O and potentially lower electrical signaling power. That potential comes with packaging, serviceability, thermal and reliability trade-offs, particularly where optics and hot electronic silicon share a tightly coupled assembly.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Multicore fiber</strong> places several independent light-guiding cores within one cladding. APC's four-core initiative seeks common cable, connector and fiber-array interfaces so denser optical links can be assembled consistently. Four cores do not automatically mean four times the usable network bandwidth: transceiver design, crosstalk, connector loss, routing and signal processing all affect the deployed result. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously explored <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/corning-four-core-fiber-contour-form-ai-networks/">Corning's four-core fiber approach</a> and ranked <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/top-7-fiber-optic-technologies-in-2026/">seven key optical networking technologies</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Where Optical Circuit Switching Fits</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Optical circuit switching (OCS) can reconfigure paths of light directly, potentially easing bandwidth bottlenecks between groups of AI accelerators. APC says its work includes support for different OCS approaches, including reliability and configuration requirements for rack-level deployment. That does not mean its new documents establish a universally compatible OCS product or prove one switching architecture is superior for every AI workload.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AI networks also have competing requirements for fast path reconfiguration, packet routing, fault recovery and manageability. Industry experiments will need to demonstrate how optical switching interacts with existing electronic fabrics under real cluster traffic. For context, see BitcoinVersus.Tech's earlier report on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/salience-labs-125m-ai-optical-switches/">Salience Labs and optical switching</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Still Needs To Be Demonstrated</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The meaningful next milestones are third-party conformance testing, cross-vendor mating and electrical/optical interoperability, production-line yield, field replacement practices and published total-cost-of-ownership comparisons. Useful measures include dollars per delivered Gb/s, watts per Tb/s, manufacturing yield, test time per optical channel, insertion loss, failure rate and mean time to repair. The same metrics should be compared at a defined deployment scale and operating temperature.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
