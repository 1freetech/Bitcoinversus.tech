<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Accelsius said Monday that it has upgraded its Springdale facility in Austin, Texas, to <strong>multi-megawatt power</strong>, giving the company a larger test bed for two-phase, direct-to-chip cooling systems aimed at future AI racks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to the company’s <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261012173285/en/">October 12 announcement</a>, the lab can test a single rack at megawatt-plus loads or multiple racks at today’s highest densities. Accelsius also plans to add <strong>18,000 square feet</strong> to the site in January 2027.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The expansion arrives as AI infrastructure vendors push toward much denser systems. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/delta-800-vdc-3mw-cooling-ocp-2026/">Delta’s 3 MW cooling platform</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/data-centers-quantumscape-solid-state-battery-1mw-ai-racks-powerblock/">1 MW-class AI rack power</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/data-centers-airtrunk-1-billion-tok1-japan-liquid-cooling-ai/">liquid-cooled AI data center expansion</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Accelsius says its <a href="https://accelsius.com/solutions/">NeuCool platform</a> uses a two-phase dielectric refrigerant loop to remove heat directly from chips. The company also disclosed a forthcoming <strong>375 kW-capable</strong> multi-rack unit beyond its existing MR250 product.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company cited July benchmark results showing NeuCool produced NVIDIA B200 junction temperatures 9°C lower than single-phase direct-to-chip cooling under warm-water conditions. That performance claim comes from Accelsius and has not been independently retested by BitcoinVersus.Tech.</p>
