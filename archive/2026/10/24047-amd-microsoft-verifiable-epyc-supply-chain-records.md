<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2026/advancing-semiconductor-supply-chain-assurance.html">AMD disclosed on October 12, 2026</a>, that it will demonstrate a semiconductor-provenance proof of concept with Microsoft at the 2026 OCP Global Summit. The demo uses a 6th Generation AMD EPYC processor and is designed to let a relying party independently check whether manufacturing records are authentic and bound to the processor being evaluated.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Confirmed:</strong> AMD says it creates a hardware bill of materials (HBOM) across key manufacturing stages and cryptographically binds it to the finished SoC. Microsoft Artifact Signing signs the provenance statement; Microsoft Signing Transparency records it using the <a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9943.html">IETF SCITT architecture</a>. A validator can then check the signature, device binding, ledger receipt and issuer policy.</p>

<p><strong>Claim:</strong> AMD says the workflow could give cloud customers stronger evidence that workloads are running on the expected hardware. That is a company claim, not an independently measured outcome. AMD also explicitly says this is a proof of concept and should not be read as a product commitment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The 2026 system extends AMD’s earlier device-provenance work by joining provenance, signing, transparency and validation in one flow. The <a href="https://www.opencompute.org/events/ocp-summit/2026-ocp-global-summit/">OCP Global Summit runs October 12–15 in San Jose</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What is not known:</strong> AMD has not announced commercial availability, pricing or a deployment schedule.</p>
