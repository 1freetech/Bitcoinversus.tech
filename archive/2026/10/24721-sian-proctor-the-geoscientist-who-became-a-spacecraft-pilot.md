<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sian Proctor spent decades building a career across geology, teaching, exploration, aviation and science communication before one of her oldest ambitions finally came together in orbit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Proctor is a geoscientist, educator, artist and astronaut. According to <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/people/dr-sian-proctor/">NASA</a>, she began teaching geology, sustainability and planetary science at South Mountain Community College in 1999. Her work later expanded far beyond the classroom into field expeditions, disaster education, aviation and simulated space missions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">She Almost Became a NASA Astronaut</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In 2009, Proctor applied to NASA's astronaut program and advanced to the final 47 candidates from a pool of roughly 3,500 applicants. NASA ultimately selected nine astronauts, and Proctor was not one of them.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That rejection did not end the path. She kept building the résumé. She earned a pilot's license, became scuba certified, participated in analog astronaut missions that simulated living on the Moon and Mars, worked as a NOAA Teacher at Sea, conducted Arctic field work through PolarTREC and spent two months at sea with the JOIDES Resolution climate-research expedition.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Then a Different Door Opened</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In 2021, Inspiration4 created a route to space that did not run through the traditional government astronaut corps. Proctor won the mission's Prosperity seat through an entrepreneurship competition built around her art and her Space2inspire project.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Her years of exploration and flight preparation mattered. She was assigned the role of mission pilot aboard SpaceX's Crew Dragon Resilience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Proctor has continued to connect her own career with the science-fiction, art and exploration that shaped her early imagination.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On September 15, 2021, Inspiration4 launched into orbit. Proctor became the first Black woman to pilot a spacecraft and helped fly the first orbital mission crewed entirely by private citizens. The mission spent nearly three days in orbit before returning to Earth.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The achievement was the result of much more than winning a seat. <a href="https://www.stjude.org/inspire/series/selfless/as-a-child-sian-proctor-dreamed-of-becoming-an-astronaut.html">St. Jude</a>, whose fundraising mission was central to Inspiration4, documented how Proctor had continued preparing for space for years after NASA's 2009 rejection.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A Career Built Across Fields</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What makes Proctor's career compelling is not simply that she became an astronaut. Geology led into education. Education led into field exploration and science communication. Aviation and analog missions kept the spaceflight goal alive. Art and entrepreneurship eventually helped create the opportunity that NASA had not provided.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Her story fits alongside other remarkable career journeys such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/shawna-pandya-the-doctor-training-for-space/">Shawna Pandya's path through medicine and space research</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/toyohiro-akiyama-the-journalist-who-reported-from-space/">Toyohiro Akiyama's transition from journalism to spaceflight, farming and teaching</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-navy-seal-to-doctor-to-astronaut/">Jonny Kim's journey from Navy SEAL to doctor to astronaut</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Proctor's career is a reminder that a professional life does not always move in a straight line. Sometimes the skills collected across seemingly separate fields eventually converge around one opportunity.</p>
