<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>U.S. natural-gas production is forecast to set new annual records in 2026 and 2027, as liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports expand and power demand rises.</strong> The <a href="https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/marketreview/natgas.php">Energy Information Administration’s October 6 outlook</a>, highlighted in <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-energy-firms-produce-record-natgas-2026-global-demand-soars-2026-10-27/">October 27 reporting</a>, forecasts dry-gas output of <strong>112.20 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) in 2026</strong> and <strong>116.13 Bcf/d in 2027</strong>, up from 107.64 Bcf/d in 2025. These are projections, not completed production totals.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Projected dry-gas output grows about 4.2% in 2026 and 3.5% in 2027.</li><li>EIA forecasts U.S. LNG exports of 17.6 Bcf/d in 2026 and 18.6 Bcf/d in 2027.</li><li>Natural gas remains a major source of American electricity, but gas production volume is not electrical capacity.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Gas drilling and LNG demand</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Growth is concentrated in the Permian Basin and Haynesville, where new production and infrastructure support supply. International demand matters: the <a href="https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/marketreview/natgas.php">EIA forecasts</a> higher LNG shipments as export plants move gas overseas. The export volumes are forecasts, not proof of shipments already delivered. BitcoinVersus.Tech has previously reported on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/26/china-halts-liquified-natural-gas-purchases-from-the-united-states-amid-trade-tensions/">U.S.–China LNG trade disruptions</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What it means for electricity and data centers</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gas supplies fuel to turbines and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/data-centers-enerflex-450mw-behind-meter-gas-power/">on-site data-center generators</a>. EIA projects its share of U.S. electricity generation at about 40% in 2026 and 39% in 2027. Separately, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-us-power-demand-record-highs-2026-2027-ai-crypto-data-centers/">electricity demand is growing</a> as AI facilities and manufacturers expand. Rising gas production therefore supports energy supply, but cannot directly establish the megawatts available to any specific campus.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The physical distinction also matters for <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-what-is-capacity-factor-why-100mw-does-not-mean-100mw-all-year/">power-plant capacity factors</a>: fuel production is measured in volume per day, while an electrical plant is rated in watts and produces energy in watt-hours. Costs, pipeline access, generator efficiency and grid interconnection still determine how much usable electricity reaches consumers. EIA's forecast does not guarantee lower household power bills.</p>
