<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Robert Rosenthal spent years parachuting into remote wildfires. Then he traded a smokejumper helmet for dental loupes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rosenthal worked as a smokejumper at the North Cascades Smokejumper Base in Winthrop, Washington, from 2010 to 2017. That puts his first career directly inside the world we ranked among the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">toughest jobs in 2026</a>: parachuting into remote terrain, carrying heavy equipment and attacking wildfires before they grow.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What makes his résumé stand out is what he was doing between fire seasons. According to the <a href="https://dental.washington.edu/ride-story/">University of Washington School of Dentistry</a>, Rosenthal repeatedly returned to school while smokejumping and eventually chose dentistry, a profession already familiar through his father and his late sister.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div style="position:relative;padding-bottom:56.25%;height:0;overflow:hidden;max-width:100%">[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGaim3JcHdQ]</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>The University of Washington’s RIDE program explains the rural dental-training pathway Rosenthal followed.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He graduated from the University of Washington School of Dentistry in 2021. His current practice biography at <a href="https://sawtoothdentalcare.com/dentists/">Sawtooth Dental Care</a> says he graduated with distinctions, received the Dennis P. Duskin Inspirational Award from his classmates and was inducted into the Omicron Kappa Upsilon dental honor society.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After graduation, Rosenthal worked as a community-health dentist in Brewster before practicing in Twisp, near the same North Cascades communities where his smokejumper career had taken shape.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The transition is striking because neither profession is a side hobby. Smokejumping requires years of wildfire experience, parachute training, physical conditioning and judgment under dangerous conditions. Dentistry requires a doctoral degree, clinical training, licensure and precision measured in millimeters.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is the kind of combination this Career series is looking for. Rosenthal belongs beside saturation diver <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/chris-lemons-saturation-diver-survived-seafloor/">Chris Lemons</a> and veterinarian-mountaineer <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/ed-viesturs-veterinarian-high-altitude-mountaineer/">Ed Viesturs</a>: people whose résumés cross disciplines that each take years to master on their own.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rosenthal’s career also has a useful through-line. Smokejumping brought him into small Western communities where specialized services can be difficult to reach. Dentistry ultimately brought him back to serve one of those communities in a completely different way.</p>
