## Final Gutenberg Source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus.Tech’s latest <a href="https://x.com/bitcoinversus_/status/2111022048025985132?s=46">Power Efficiency Theory post</a> places the <strong>Bitcoin Mining Singularity</strong> near 2046 if Bitcoin ASIC efficiency continues improving from today’s single-digit joules-per-terahash frontier. The date is plausible as a scenario, but it should not be presented as a forecast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The current reference point is the Bitmain S23 XP Hyd., rated at <strong>600 TH/s, 5,340 W and 8.9 J/TH</strong>. That is still a manufacturer-rated frontier rather than a broad field measurement from deployed fleets. BitcoinVersus.Tech’s earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/bitcoin-mining-singularity-update-8-3-percent-scenario/">8.3% scenario update</a> annualized the recent move from 9.5 to 8.9 J/TH. For this report, <strong>AGR</strong> means the annual efficiency-improvement rate: the percentage by which J/TH falls each year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The first correction is mathematical</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A flat 8.3% annual reduction does <strong>not</strong> reach 1 J/TH in 2046. Starting at 8.9 J/TH in 2026, the constant model is:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>J(y) = 8.9 × 0.917<sup>(y−2026)</sup></strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That produces about <strong>1.57 J/TH in 2046</strong> and first falls below 1 J/TH around <strong>2052</strong>. The light-blue 2046 path therefore has to represent an <strong>accelerating</strong> improvement rate, not a constant 8.3% line.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What has to happen for 2046 to work</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>An illustrative acceleration model can make 2046 mathematically consistent. Start with an 8.3% improvement in 2026 and increase that annual improvement rate by only <strong>0.25 percentage point per year</strong>. Under that rule, the annual rate reaches roughly 13% by the mid-2040s and the model crosses 1 J/TH in 2046.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is where Ray Kurzweil’s <a href="https://www.writingsbyraykurzweil.com/the-law-of-accelerating-returns">Law of Accelerating Returns</a> is useful as a hypothesis. Kurzweil argues that more capable technologies help create the next generation of technologies, creating positive feedback. That idea can justify testing an accelerating curve. It does <strong>not</strong> prove that SHA-256 ASIC efficiency will accelerate, and it should not be treated as a physical law governing mining hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The countermodel is Diminishing Efficiency Returns</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Power Efficiency Theory also needs the opposite case. As ASICs become more efficient, each additional reduction in J/TH may become harder to obtain because of transistor scaling, leakage, interconnect losses, voltage limits, power delivery, thermal density, packaging, cooling and economics. BitcoinVersus.Tech calls that pressure <strong>Diminishing Efficiency Returns</strong>. When technical limits and economic cost increasingly resist further gains, the model enters <strong>Thermoeconomic Capitulation</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The alternative chart therefore lets the 8.3% AGR decay instead of accelerate. In the mild case, the annual improvement rate shrinks by 2% relative to the prior year. That delays the 1 J/TH crossing to about <strong>2062</strong>. In the stronger case, the annual improvement rate shrinks by 5% relatively each year. That curve never reaches 1 J/TH; mathematically it approaches an efficiency floor near <strong>1.63 J/TH</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why the acceleration case is not unreasonable — and not enough</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Computing history gives some support for testing continued nonlinear efficiency improvement. A 2025 peer-reviewed reassessment of <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10586-024-04767-y">Koomey’s Law</a> found that high-performance-computing energy efficiency continued to improve exponentially from 2008 through 2023, but more slowly than the earlier historical trend: maximum efficiency doubled roughly every 2.29 years instead of every 1.57 years. That is evidence for both sides of this report. Efficiency can keep compounding for long periods, while the rate itself can slow.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitcoin ASICs are not supercomputers, and SHA-256 is a specialized workload. The comparison cannot establish a Bitcoin-mining date. It simply shows why both acceleration and deceleration deserve to be modeled rather than selecting one curve because it produces the preferred year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Where this sits inside Power Efficiency Theory</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The original <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/06/02/the-bitcoin-mining-singularity-the-future-of-bitcoin-mining-machine-hashrate-and-asic-chip-efficiency/">Bitcoin Mining Singularity</a> defines 1 J/TH as a research milestone for mining-machine energy efficiency. It is not a claim that 1 J/TH is the absolute thermodynamic limit of computation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Power Efficiency Theory</strong> separates the <strong>Rate of Compute</strong> from the <strong>Rate of Energy Efficiency</strong>. Compute can rise through machine hashrate or network hashrate, while energy efficiency improves as J/TH falls. Their compounded progression can then be measured with the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/05/power-efficiency-index-explained/">Power Efficiency Index</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Within PEI, <strong>Physical Price</strong> is the observed market-price baseline selected for a measurement window. <strong>Relative Price</strong> is the model-implied price after applying compounded compute and efficiency progression. <strong>Valuation Divergence</strong> is the gap between that Relative Price and Actual Price. Those valuation terms do <strong>not</strong> determine when 1 J/TH arrives. They belong to the valuation layer of Power Efficiency Theory, while the Singularity curves belong to the engineering layer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The skeptical conclusion</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>2046 is a defensible scenario, not a defensible certainty.</strong> A fixed 8.3% AGR does not get there; it points closer to 2052. A modestly accelerating AGR can get there. A decaying AGR can push the milestone into the 2060s or prevent the model from reaching 1 J/TH at all.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most useful prediction is therefore not the year itself. It is the test: every new leading ASIC generation can be plotted against the acceleration curve and the Capitulation Curve. If realized efficiency gains begin widening again, the 2046 case strengthens. If gains continue shrinking, Thermoeconomic Capitulation becomes the better description.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Bitcoin Mining Singularity should move when the data moves.</p>
