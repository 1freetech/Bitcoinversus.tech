<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Video games can cut electricity use without always looking worse.</strong> Microsoft’s <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/sustainability/case-studies/case-studies-halo"><strong>Halo Infinite energy-efficiency case study</strong></a> says the team reduced the resolution of a blurred pause-menu background to 1080p and measured about a 15% reduction in power use, with total AC power falling to roughly 165 watts in that test state. Microsoft says the change did not negatively affect the player experience.</p>

<p><em>Players have long noticed that different games and rendering modes can produce different console power draw, even on the same hardware.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The larger lesson is that gaming energy use is partly software-defined. Menu rendering, frame-rate targets, resolution, idle behavior and unnecessary background work all affect how hard the CPU and GPU must run. That makes energy efficiency another optimization target alongside technologies such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/gaming-gears-of-war-e-day-advanced-shader-delivery-95-percent/"><strong>faster shader delivery</strong></a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/gaming-sony-brings-qssr-ai-upscaling-to-the-standard-ps5/"><strong>AI upscaling</strong></a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/gaming-xbox-labs-emerging-game-technology/"><strong>new game-development tooling</strong></a>.</p>
