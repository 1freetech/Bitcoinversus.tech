## Final Gutenberg Source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chamath Palihapitiya predicts Bitcoin will eventually be hacked and lose all value. In <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kAwG9E1W_ME">Moonshots episode 304</a>, released October 21, 2026, and recirculated by <a href="https://twitter.com/coinbureau/status/2114069420826108326">Coin Bureau on October 24</a>, he linked that forecast to AI-assisted cryptographic attacks. The security question deserves scrutiny; the price outcome remains speculative.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The interview describes a hypothetical attack, rather than a reported Bitcoin breach.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Quantum attacks on public keys differ from attacks on SHA-256 hashing.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Defenses require development and adoption; a zero price is a prediction.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Chamath Said</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Palihapitiya said, “eventually it gets hacked and then it goes to zero.” He imagined targeting coins attributed to Satoshi Nakamoto, stealing them, and shorting Bitcoin before publicizing the attack. He also referenced breaking SHA-256. The segment provides no working exploit, computing-resource estimate or attack date.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Technical Distinction</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitcoin uses <a href="https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html">digital signatures to authorize spending</a>, including ECDSA and <a href="https://bips.dev/340/">Schnorr signatures</a>. A sufficiently capable quantum computer could use Shor’s algorithm to recover private keys from exposed public keys. That differs from defeating SHA-256, used in mining. Wallet theft also differs from breaking network consensus. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/can-quantum-computers-hack-bitcoin/">Bitcoin quantum-security explainer</a> examines these separate risks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AI can assist cryptographic research: the <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09582">ECDSA.Fail paper</a>, covered in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/bitcoin-security-ai-agents-quantum-attack-benchmark-1151-logical-qubits/">earlier benchmark report</a>, improved a quantum-circuit component. That research does not demonstrate Bitcoin key theft. <a href="https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/quantum-computers-may-put-internet-traffic-risk-nist-safeguarding-computers-new">NIST said in July</a> that existing quantum computers were too small and unstable to threaten cryptography. AI progress alone supplies neither a demonstrated SHA-256 break nor the necessary quantum hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Can Bitcoin Adapt?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There are concrete proposals. Draft <a href="https://bips.dev/360/">BIP 360</a> would introduce Pay-to-Merkle-Root outputs to reduce long-exposure public-key risk. It does not provide complete protection against attacks during transaction confirmation and is not activated. <a href="https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography">NIST’s post-quantum signature standards</a> show alternatives exist within <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/what-is-post-quantum-cryptography/">post-quantum cryptography</a>; adopting them in Bitcoin would require separate engineering and consensus work.</p>

Migration raises difficult questions about vulnerable old coins, wallet support and deployment time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Assessment:</strong> Chamath identifies a consequential risk worth preparing for. A successful large-scale theft could damage confidence and prices. Whether Bitcoin reaches zero would also depend on the attack’s scope, the network’s response and continued demand. The interview establishes neither that outcome nor its inevitability.</p>
