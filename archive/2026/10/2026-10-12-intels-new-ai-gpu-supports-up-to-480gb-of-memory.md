<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Intel has given its Crescent Island data-center accelerator a product name: <strong>Intel GPU D-Series</strong>. The company used the October 12, 2026 Open Compute Project Global Summit to publish the clearest performance figures yet for the inference-focused card.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/artificial-intelligence/intel-accelerates-open-infrastructure-agentic-ai-era.html">Intel’s official announcement</a>, GPU D-Series is built on Xe3P and combines up to <strong>480GB of LPDDR5X</strong>, <strong>1.5TB/s</strong> of memory bandwidth and <strong>1.3 petaflops of FP8 compute</strong> inside a <strong>350-watt</strong> air-cooled PCIe design. Intel’s own branded card is specified with 160GB; the 480GB ceiling applies to partner-built configurations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Intel says one card can support models up to 300 billion parameters, while an eight-GPU system can move beyond one trillion parameters. That places the product squarely in the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/artificial-intelligence-training-vs-inference-what-ai-learns-does/">AI inference</a> market rather than positioning it as a maximum-throughput training accelerator.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The unusual part is memory. Instead of HBM, Intel is leaning on high-capacity LPDDR5X. <a href="https://www.servethehome.com/intels-crescent-island-ai-server-gpu-gets-a-name-and-specs-meet-the-intel-gpu-d-series/">ServeTheHome’s technical breakdown</a> reports the first D270 card with 32 Xe cores, 256 XMX engines, PCIe Gen5 x16 and the same 350W target. The tradeoff is straightforward: LPDDR5X offers far more capacity at this power envelope, while HBM remains much faster for bandwidth-heavy workloads.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The launch also fits Intel’s broader OCP message that AI work should be <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/intel-ai-prompts-right-chip-ocp-2026/">routed to the processor best suited to each task</a>. That matters because lower-cost inference can increase total utilization and ultimately <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/artificial-intelligence-cheaper-ai-could-drive-data-center-power-demand-even-higher/">push data-center power demand higher</a>, even when individual accelerators become more efficient.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Intel says customer sampling is underway. The company has not yet published independent real-world benchmarks or a broad retail availability date, so the 1.3-PFLOPS figure and model-capacity claims should be treated as vendor specifications until production systems can be tested.</p>
