<p><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/14/nvidia-to-manufacture-500-billion-in-ai-supercomputers-across-texas-and-arizona/">BitcoinVersus.Tech reported in April 2025</a> on NVIDIA’s plan to support up to $500 billion of U.S.-made AI infrastructure. One part of that pledge is now operating in Fort Worth, Texas.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/wistron-manufacturing-texas/">NVIDIA says</a> Wistron opened a 324,000-square-foot factory on July 21, 2026 that is already producing GB300 Grace Blackwell Ultra hardware and is preparing to build Vera Rubin systems. NVIDIA says the $700 million project has created more than 500 jobs, with a target of 1,000 by the end of 2026.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.wistron.com/en/Newsroom/2026-07-22">Wistron says</a> the facility is its first U.S. manufacturing plant and the site where the first NVIDIA GB300 Grace Blackwell Ultra Superchip was built and mass-produced in the United States. NVIDIA says the plant is scaling toward tens of thousands of boards per month.</p>

<p>The follow-up is different from BitcoinVersus.Tech’s recent report on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/nvidia-robots-assemble-gb300-ai-hardware/">robots assembling GB300 hardware</a>: that story focused on experimental automation, while this one tracks the physical factory NVIDIA’s 2025 U.S. manufacturing plan helped bring online. It also fits the broader <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/nvidia-leads-2-4-billion-ai-compute-pledge-for-u-s-science/">U.S. AI infrastructure buildout</a>.</p>
