<p><a href="https://ir.bitdeer.com/news-releases/news-release-details/bitdeer-ai-expands-malaysia-campus-new-60mw-a103-facility">Bitdeer says it has secured another 60 MW of AI data-center capacity in Cyberjaya, Malaysia</a>, pushing its global secured AI Cloud capacity to about <strong>333.5 MW</strong> across Malaysia, Norway and the United States.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The new A103 facility sits beside Bitdeer AI’s existing A101 and A102 buildings. It is covered by an <strong>8-year data-center services agreement</strong>, is designed for liquid-cooled rack-scale NVIDIA systems including <strong>Vera Rubin</strong>, and is expected to energize in the first quarter of 2028. Together, the three Cyberjaya buildings would represent about <strong>71.5 MW of critical IT load</strong>.</p>

<p><strong>Important distinction:</strong> “secured capacity” does not mean sold capacity. Bitdeer says <strong>no offtake commitments</strong> have yet been signed for A103. Its quoted AI Cloud pipeline of more than <strong>$10 billion</strong> is a management estimate of opportunities under discussion, not backlog or guaranteed revenue. <a href="https://www.securities.io/bitdeer-ai-secures-60mw-a103-facility-under-8-year-agreement/">Independent coverage</a> highlights the same gap.</p>

<p>The mining angle matters because Bitdeer is still one of the world’s largest Bitcoin miners. The same skills used to run high-density ASIC sites—power procurement, substations, cooling, networking and 24/7 operations—are increasingly being redirected toward GPU infrastructure.</p>
