<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Banking regulators in the United States and Europe are increasing scrutiny of how financial institutions use advanced and agentic artificial intelligence, focusing on cybersecurity, outside technology providers and whether increasingly autonomous systems can be controlled when something goes wrong.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/banking-watchdogs-ramp-up-ai-scrutiny-stability-fears-mount-sources-say-2026-10-13/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reuters reported October 13</a>, citing six people familiar with the matter, that U.S. regulators including the Federal Reserve are asking banks about advanced AI models. Questions reportedly include the use of foreign-developed technology, AI agents in cyber defense and whether institutions can stop autonomous systems that behave unexpectedly.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The concern overlaps with a broader financial-stability debate. BitcoinVersus.Tech reported that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/imf-ai-market-correction-global-financial-stability/">the IMF sees a sharp AI-market correction as a possible channel for wider financial stress</a>, showing that regulators are now examining both market exposure and operational risk.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The regulatory framework is still catching up. In its <a href="https://occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-29.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">updated model-risk guidance</a>, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency explicitly said generative and agentic AI are outside the scope of the revised traditional model framework. The OCC also said federal banking agencies plan a separate request for information focused on AI use.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One practical problem is autonomy itself. BitcoinVersus.Tech has covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/28/nvidia-adds-a-hardware-watchdog-for-autonomous-ai-agents/">NVIDIA’s hardware-level watchdog approach for autonomous AI agents</a>, an example of the kind of technical control regulators may increasingly expect institutions to understand and test.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The same control question appears in AI safety research. BitcoinVersus.Tech reported that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/goodfires-inside-out-ai-monitors-watch-for-rogue-agents-before-they-act/">Goodfire is developing internal monitors designed to detect rogue-agent behavior before actions are executed</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No new U.S. banking rule was announced in the Reuters report. The current shift is increased supervisory questioning and preparation for possible future guidance. That distinction matters: regulators are gathering information about agentic AI risk now, while the formal rules governing these systems remain unsettled.</p>
