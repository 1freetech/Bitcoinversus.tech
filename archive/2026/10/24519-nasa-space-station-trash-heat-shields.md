<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>NASA is using a disposable cargo spacecraft’s final plunge through Earth’s atmosphere to test heat shields for future Moon and Mars missions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ames/nasa-demonstrates-next-generation-heat-shield-technologies/">KREPE-3 experiment</a> packed 12 instrumented capsules inside Northrop Grumman’s departing Cygnus XL spacecraft. As Cygnus headed for destructive reentry, the capsules were designed to eject and transmit temperature, pressure, motion, magnetic-field and light data while their thermal-protection systems faced real hypersonic heating.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/NASAAdmin/status/2016160416859111715]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman highlighted Ames heat-shield development and arc-jet testing earlier this year. The post is broader heat-shield context, not a KREPE-3 results announcement.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The hardware mix is unusually diverse. One capsule carries a <strong>3D-printed heat shield</strong> developed by NASA Johnson and Oak Ridge National Laboratory. Others test stitched carbon-and-phenolic materials called MERINO, a deployable umbrella-like shield, and a capsule shape based on the aeroshell planned for NASA’s Dragonfly mission to Titan.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>BPS.space tours NASA Ames and shows how arc-jet facilities reproduce the extreme heating spacecraft encounter during atmospheric entry.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The University of Kentucky hosted a <a href="https://engr.uky.edu/events/space-capsule-re-entry-data-watch-party">reentry data watch party on October 12</a>. As of October 13, NASA’s public KREPE-3 article has not yet reported which experimental shield performed best.</p>
