<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>A QR code is a square pattern that lets a phone quickly read information.</strong> Most of the time, scanning one opens a website, menu, ticket, payment page, or app.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>You have probably seen them on restaurant tables, posters, packages, parking meters, event tickets, and TV screens. The little black-and-white square is basically a shortcut between the physical world and something digital.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Does “QR” Mean?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>QR means “Quick Response.”</strong> The name came from the original goal: make a code that machines could read quickly.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>DENSO WAVE introduced the QR Code in 1994. It was first used in the automotive industry before spreading into everyday life. The company later made the standard widely available, helping QR codes become common around the world.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Does Your Phone Read It?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Your camera sees the dark and light squares as a pattern. Software in the phone turns that pattern back into information.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>If the QR code contains a web address, your phone usually shows you a link you can tap. That is why scanning a code can take you straight to a menu or website without typing anything.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The large squares near the corners help the phone recognize that it is looking at a QR code and figure out which way the code is facing.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Not Just Use a Normal Barcode?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A normal barcode is mostly a row of lines. A QR code uses a square area, so it can hold more information in a small space.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That makes QR codes useful for more than product numbers. They can point to websites, store contact details, help connect to Wi-Fi, open digital tickets, or share other small pieces of information.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Did QR Codes Become So Popular?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Because nearly everyone now carries a camera-equipped smartphone. You no longer need a special scanner for everyday QR codes.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Open your camera, point it at the code, and the phone usually recognizes it automatically. That simplicity turned QR codes into an easy way to move people from a sign, package, or screen to something online.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>One Easy Safety Tip</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A QR code can hide where a link goes until you scan it. So take one second to look at the web address your phone shows before opening it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The Federal Trade Commission warns that scammers sometimes place fake QR codes over real ones, especially in places like parking meters. If the link looks strange or misspelled, do not open it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Easy Way to Remember It</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>A QR code is a tiny square shortcut.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Your phone looks at the pattern, reads the information inside it, and usually gives you a quick way to open a link or perform a simple action.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is really the whole idea.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
