<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nasdaq-listed <strong>iPower Inc. (IPW)</strong>, historically an e-commerce and supply-chain company, disclosed <strong>15.11524045 Bitcoin</strong> in its <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830072/000168316826007908/ipower_s1.htm">October 15, 2026 SEC Form S-1</a>. The holding carried an October 13 indicative value of <strong>$1,255,910.13</strong>. This is an updated balance-sheet disclosure, <strong>not a new October Bitcoin purchase</strong>: the coins were bought in December 2025 for approximately $1.325 million.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><li><strong>15.11524045 BTC:</strong> Worth about $1.256 million using iPower's October 13 valuation.</li><li><strong>$4.82 million:</strong> Additional cash within the same controlled collateral account.</li><li><strong>$6.07 million:</strong> Combined gross account value; not unrestricted liquidity.</li><li><strong>AI infrastructure:</strong> Management now prioritizes financing, equipment leasing and compute services over additional passive crypto accumulation.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why The Bitcoin Is Restricted</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>iPower says substantially all assets in the controlled account, including Bitcoin and cash proceeds, are <strong>pledged to a collateral agent</strong> for the company's convertible-note holders. The agent controls the blocked custody account. Selling Bitcoin there would change the form of the collateral, <strong>not automatically release the proceeds</strong>. Investors therefore should not interpret the $6.07 million gross balance as money freely available to common shareholders.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company previously bought Ethereum and a yield-bearing AI-infrastructure-related digital instrument, but reported selling those positions in August 2026. Its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/08/02/microstrategy-plans-2-billion-equity-offering-to-purchase-bitcoin/">corporate treasury financing context</a> and the earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/12/16/boyaa-interactive-bets-big-on-bitcoin-with-100-million-investment-plan/">2023 Bitcoin treasury adoption</a> illustrate how different businesses have used digital assets in fundamentally different ways.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">An AI Infrastructure Strategy Takes Priority</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On October 15, iPower separately announced a <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830072/000168316826007863/ipower_ex9901.htm">customer-signed, non-binding letter of intent</a> for AI equipment operations and management. The proposed project starts with <strong>48 computing units</strong> and contemplates <strong>396 units during 2027</strong>. At the illustrated $4,608 monthly fee per unit, a fully deployed fleet could represent <strong>up to $21.9 million in annualized service fees</strong>. This is a hypothetical full-buildout figure, <strong>not contracted revenue or recognized sales</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Investors Should Watch Next</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On <strong>October 22</strong>, the company also <a href="https://rss.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/22/3386656/0/en/ipower-inc-announces-1-for-12-reverse-stock-split.html">announced a 1-for-12 reverse stock split</a> scheduled to take effect October 26 to help address Nasdaq minimum-bid compliance. A reverse split does not itself increase total company value. Investors should distinguish that capital-market action, iPower's pledged Bitcoin, and its still-proposed AI contract.</p>
