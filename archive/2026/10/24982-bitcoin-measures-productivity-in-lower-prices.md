<p><strong>Bitcoin Verse of the Day — October 14, 2026</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p>“As long as it stays decentralized and secure, it is measuring the productivity flowing to society in the form of lower prices.”</p><cite>Jeff Booth</cite></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Jeff Booth made this point during <em>BTC157: Personal AI Models and Bitcoin</em> with Preston Pysh. I verified the wording against the <a href="https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/bitcoin-fundamentals/personal-ai-models-and-bitcoin-jeff-booth/">official Investor’s Podcast Network transcript</a>, where the passage appears at about <strong>26:12</strong>. An <a href="https://podscripts.co/podcasts/we-study-billionaires-the-investors-podcast-network/btc157-personal-ai-models-and-bitcoin-w-jeff-booth-bitcoin-podcast">independent transcript</a> records the same passage at about <strong>31:02</strong>; the time difference comes from platform-specific intro and ad timing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Booth’s argument is that productivity should normally make useful things cheaper over time. In his view, Bitcoin offers a monetary reference point that cannot be expanded at will, so technological gains can show up as falling prices rather than being obscured by monetary expansion. That is an argument about how prices behave under a fixed monetary supply, not a guarantee that every individual product becomes cheaper on every timeline.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The idea extends themes already explored by BitcoinVersus in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/the-future-of-money-is-programmable/">programmable money</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/bitcoin-separates-money-from-state/">the separation of money and state</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/anyone-should-be-able-to-send-bitcoin/">permissionless Bitcoin payments</a>. Booth’s version adds a different lens: if technology keeps increasing output per unit of work, what should honest money allow society to see in prices?</p>
