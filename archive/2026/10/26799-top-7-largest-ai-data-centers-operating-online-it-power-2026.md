<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The world's largest <em>operating</em> AI data-center campus is not necessarily the one with the most ambitious announced expansion. As of <strong>October 27, 2026</strong>, the <a href="https://epoch.ai/graphs/largest-ai-data-centers-by-power-capacity">Epoch AI frontier data-center register</a> estimates that Amazon's New Carlisle campus has 1,037 megawatts of AI IT capacity already online, ahead of Colossus 2 and Fairwater Atlanta. This countdown ranks the seven largest <strong>tracked, operating AI campuses</strong> by that measured-in-megawatts <em>capacity estimate</em> rather than planned gigawatts, corporate spending or sites that have yet to open.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What “actually operating” means here:</strong> The rankings use Epoch's <em>modeled IT power online</em>, not instantaneous facility electricity use. Epoch infers the amount of compute power likely available to run using satellite imagery, permits, hardware-efficiency assumptions, company statements and cooling infrastructure. Actual electrical draw fluctuates with server utilization and requires meter data. The dataset covers about 95 prominent frontier AI sites, <strong>not every data center in the world</strong>. Site overhead and power usage effectiveness (PUE) are also separate from the IT-power metric. The differences from <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/top-7-biggest-ai-data-center-megaprojects-planned-power-2026/">planned end-state megaproject rankings</a> are the entire point of this comparison.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How much power do these seven operating campuses represent?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Together, the seven sites represent approximately <strong>4,406 MW (4.406 GW)</strong> of modeled online AI IT capacity. If that entire capacity drew its rated power continuously for 8,760 hours, the hypothetical annual electricity use would be <strong>38.6 TWh</strong> before data-center support equipment and electrical losses. That is <strong>an illustrative upper-load scenario</strong>, not a statement about actual 2026 consumption. At real workloads, scheduled maintenance and changing utilization, electricity use can differ substantially.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The DayOne discrepancy deserves particular scrutiny: its own marketing states over <strong>478 MW operational across Nusajaya and Kempas combined</strong>, while Epoch's separate modeled site estimates total approximately <strong>895 MW</strong>. The difference is unresolved. Reporting it explicitly is more defensible than assuming either data source is a verified meter reading. The same caution applies to the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/top-7-biggest-nuclear-power-deals-ai-boom-2026/">nuclear power agreements</a> meant to secure future electricity: contracted power and built generation are not the same as operating AI servers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A functional AI supercluster requires more than powered buildings: high-voltage interconnection, distributed computing racks, memory, networking, liquid cooling, firm power, and applications that can actually use the hardware. Performance per watt at the chip, at the rack and at the facility can diverge, as our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/asic-jth-not-site-jth-bitcoin-mining-efficiency/">equipment-versus-site efficiency</a> research explains in the related world of Bitcoin mining. The underlying principle applies to both industries: neither nameplate capacity nor approved expansion proves delivered computation.</p>
