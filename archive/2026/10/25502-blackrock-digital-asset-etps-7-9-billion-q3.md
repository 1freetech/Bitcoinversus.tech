<p>BlackRock’s <a href="https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/newsroom/media/press-releases/blackrock-reports-third-quarter-2026">official Q3 results</a> confirm the figure more precisely: <strong>$7.907 billion</strong> of net flows into digital assets and <strong>$79.632 billion</strong> in digital-asset AUM. The category represented about 1% of BlackRock’s total AUM, while the firm ended the quarter with <strong>$15.5 trillion</strong> overall and <strong>$205 billion</strong> in total quarterly net inflows.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The reported digital-asset AUM rose from about <strong>$48.8 billion</strong> at the start of the quarter to $79.6 billion at the end—roughly a <strong>63% increase</strong>. Because the AUM gain was far larger than the $7.9 billion of new money, market appreciation accounted for a substantial share of the growth. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/blackrock-profit-jumps-resilient-markets-boost-fees-2026-10-14/">Reuters</a> separately reported that BlackRock’s broader iShares ETF business took in $134.5 billion during the quarter.</p>
