# Meta Reserves Up to 1 GW of Solar Power Beamed From Space

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Meta and Virginia-based Overview Energy announced on April 27, 2026, that Meta had reserved early access to up to 1 gigawatt of proposed space-based solar power for its AI infrastructure.</strong> The <a href="https://www.overviewenergy.com/updates/overview-and-meta">company announcement</a> calls the deal a <em>capacity reservation agreement</em>, not confirmation that satellites are supplying electricity or that a gigawatt has been purchased and delivered.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>The agreement reserves access to as much as 1 GW of future capacity, with financial terms undisclosed.</li><li>Overview aims to test orbital power beaming in 2028 and begin commercial grid delivery in 2030.</li><li>The proposed system would direct low-intensity near-infrared light toward existing ground-based solar farms.</li><li>No commercial orbit-to-grid system at this scale has been demonstrated under the agreement.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">How Solar Power From Space Would Work</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Overview proposes placing large solar-energy collectors in geosynchronous orbit, approximately 22,000 miles above Earth. Instead of sending electricity through a cable, satellites would convert collected power into a directed near-infrared light beam. Existing terrestrial photovoltaic sites would receive that light and convert it into grid electricity, potentially operating after sunset.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>This <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2026/04/powering-ai-strengthening-the-grid-space-solar-energy-and-long-duration-storage/">official Meta explanation</a> includes a short visualization of the proposed satellite-to-solar-farm process. It is a conceptual animation, not footage of an operational orbital power station.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why the 1 GW Number Needs Context</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A gigawatt is 1,000 megawatts of power capacity, but a reservation for future capacity is not the same as continuously delivering 1 GW. Commercial viability still depends on launch economics, satellite maintenance, transmission losses, receiver performance, orbital operations and regulatory approvals. Overview says it has previously beamed power from a moving aircraft; that does not establish performance from orbit.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Comes Next</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The key milestones are the proposed 2028 orbital demonstration and successful electrical delivery to the grid, which both partners target for 2030. A <a href="https://x.com/polymarketmoney/status/2048749846119801233">Polymarket Money social post</a> also circulated the agreement, but <strong>Meta and Overview's published statements</strong> provide the authoritative terms. Until large-scale tests succeed, the 1 GW figure remains a forward-looking capacity target.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
