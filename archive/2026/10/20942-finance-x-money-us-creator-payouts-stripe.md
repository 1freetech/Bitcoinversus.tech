<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>X is pulling creator income deeper inside its own financial stack.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Beginning September 2, U.S. payouts from X’s Original Content Rewards program and creator subscriptions moved from Stripe into X Money, the platform’s new payments service. The change is more than a back-end processor swap: creators in the United States now need X Money to receive those earnings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">X is turning creator payouts into an X Money on-ramp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>X announced the change in a <a href="https://twitter.com/XCreators/status/2094959821782983076">September 2 post from its Creators account</a>, telling U.S. creators that payouts for Original Content Rewards and subscriptions would be delivered through X Money and become available as soon as they are sent.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/XCreators/status/2094959821782983076","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"x","responsive":true} -->

</div><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>X tells U.S. creators that Original Content Rewards and subscription payouts are moving directly into X Money.</em></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That creates a direct bridge between earning on the social platform and holding, spending or moving money inside the company’s financial product. Instead of X calculating creator earnings while a separate processor handles distribution, the payout itself becomes a reason to open and keep an X Money account.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">U.S. creators are required to use the new route</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>X’s <a href="https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards">Original Content Rewards documentation</a> now states that U.S. users receive payouts through X Money, while creators outside the United States continue connecting a Stripe payout account.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The geographic split matters. This is not simply another optional withdrawal method sitting beside Stripe for U.S. users. It makes X Money part of the required infrastructure for participating in X’s creator economy in the company’s home market.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The business model is bigger than faster payouts</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/x-shifts-us-creator-payouts-from-stripe-to-x-money/">TechCrunch reported</a> that X confirmed the change is mandatory for U.S. creators. The publication also noted that X Money includes banking-style features such as instant payments, a payment card, ATM access and interest-bearing account options, while the underlying accounts are held at FDIC-insured Cross River Bank.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That turns creator payouts into a customer-acquisition engine. A creator who might never have opened X Money voluntarily now has a recurring reason to use it, and every payout creates another opportunity for X to keep funds inside its broader payments ecosystem.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">X is replacing one creator program while changing the payment rail</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The payout migration arrives as X retires its older Creator Revenue Sharing program and shifts eligible users toward Original Content Rewards, which places more emphasis on original material rather than the previous engagement-driven model.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So two changes are happening at once: X is changing what kind of content earns money and changing where that money lands. That gives the company more control over both sides of the creator transaction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Creator platforms increasingly want to own the money movement too</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The strategy fits a broader shift in financial infrastructure. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-stripe-makes-open-usd-the-default-stablecoin-across-its-payment-stack/">Stripe moving Open USD deeper into its payments stack</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-citi-coinbase-stablecoin-payments-bank-rails/">Citi and Coinbase putting stablecoin payments inside bank rails</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-uk-banks-live-sterling-tokenized-deposits/">UK banks moving live sterling deposits across tokenized rails</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The common thread is control of the settlement layer. Platforms that once depended entirely on outside financial processors increasingly want a direct role in how money is stored, routed and accessed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">For X, creator income doubles as financial distribution</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Creator payouts give X Money something most new financial products struggle to acquire: a built-in stream of recurring deposits tied to an existing user base.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If X can make those earnings immediately usable inside the same app where they were generated, the company moves one step closer to its long-running “everything app” ambition. The creator economy becomes not just content supply, but distribution for a financial product.</p>
