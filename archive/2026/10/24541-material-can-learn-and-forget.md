<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>UC San Diego researchers say a quantum material can be pushed into states that make a tiny electronic device behave more like a learning neuron.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In a <a href="https://today.ucsd.edu/story/jamming-flow-to-enable-memory-in-quantum-materials">study highlighted October 13</a>, the team watched a vanadium-dioxide neuristor while electricity flowed through it using coherent X-ray diffraction. A neuristor is an electronic device designed to imitate some behavior of biological neurons.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/UCSDJacobs/status/1562559713761366017]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>UC San Diego’s Jacobs School has previously highlighted the wider push to scale neuromorphic computing for more energy-efficient AI hardware.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The researchers found that repeated above-threshold electrical pulses reorganized nanoscale domains into a jammed, glass-like state. Once jammed, the device switched more predictably. Lower-current pulses could erase that short-term structural memory and restore more random switching. The peer-reviewed <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76844-2">Nature Communications paper</a> describes this as a possible route toward learning directly inside hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VTKcsNrqdqA]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>The Royal Institution explains why neuromorphic computing is being explored as a lower-power alternative to conventional computing architectures.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is not a material that “thinks” like a person. The learning and forgetting refer to controllable physical switching behavior inside the device. But that distinction is exactly why the result matters: future AI hardware may gain useful memory from the physics of the material itself instead of constantly moving data between separate processors and memory. That direction complements BitcoinVersus.Tech’s coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/ubitium-14-million-physical-ai-processor/">new physical-AI processors</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/efficient-computer-raises-97-million-to-scale-dataflow-chips/">lower-power dataflow chips</a>, and the rising <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/artificial-intelligence-cheaper-ai-could-drive-data-center-power-demand-even-higher/">power demand behind cheaper AI</a>.</p>
