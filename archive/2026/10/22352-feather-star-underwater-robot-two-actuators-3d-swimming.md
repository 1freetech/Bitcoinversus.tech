<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A soft underwater robot from North Carolina State University can rise, sink, hover, swim forward or backward and spin around its own axis with only <strong>two pneumatic actuators</strong>. The trick is not adding more motors. It is making the robot's body do more of the work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The researchers took inspiration from <strong>feather stars</strong>, marine animals whose many flexible arms let them maneuver through water in unusually versatile ways. Their new swimmer turns that idea into a four-wing robot whose structure can switch between several kinds of motion depending on how two simple air-powered inputs are timed. The work was published October 7 in <a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aeg9211">Science Advances</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">One robot, three swimming styles</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The robot has four elastic wings extending from a central body. Those wings can bend and then snap back into shape. By changing which of the two <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/robotics-humanoid-actuators-40-60-percent-hardware-cost-supply-chain/">actuators</a> fires, and how quickly it fires, the same body behaves in three very different ways.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Jellyfish mode:</strong> all four wings flap together, allowing the robot to climb, descend or hover.</li><li><strong>Fish mode:</strong> one side flutters like a tail fin, pushing the robot forward or backward.</li><li><strong>Rotor mode:</strong> alternating the two inputs makes the robot rotate, so it can reorient itself underwater.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Combining those modes gives the machine full three-dimensional maneuverability. NC State professor Jie Yin said a conventional design might use at least six actuators to achieve comparable motion. Here, the geometry and flexibility of the robot replace much of that extra hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The clever part is the body</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The researchers call the approach <strong>mechanical intelligence</strong>. Instead of asking software and a large collection of motors to control every possible movement, the physical structure naturally turns a small number of control signals into more complicated motion.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That idea also shows up elsewhere in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/thread-thin-fibermotor-lifts-chocolate-bar-soft-robotics/">soft robotics</a>: flexible materials, stored elastic energy and carefully chosen shapes can sometimes replace gears, joints or bulky drive systems. It is a different philosophy from simply adding more computing power and more hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In testing, the feather-star robot reached a maximum swimming speed of about <strong>1.64 body lengths per second</strong> and a peak rotation speed of <strong>90 degrees per second</strong>. Researchers also studied the vortices created around the wings to understand why each motion mode produces a different kind of thrust.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">It can do more than swim around a tank</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The team attached a camera to demonstrate underwater exploration, and the robots were also able to lift objects either alone or while working together. That makes the project more than a movement demo: a compact swimmer with fewer actuators could eventually carry sensors or tools into spaces where a larger rigid robot would be awkward.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The researchers are not calling it finished. One obvious next step is a fully wireless version. The current work is more interesting as a proof of design: sometimes a smarter mechanical shape can simplify the controls before software ever gets involved. That same principle matters for everything from small aquatic machines to future <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/osrec-006-robot-feedback-control-p-pi-pid-feedforward-trajectory-tracking-gain-tuning/">robot control systems</a>.</p>
