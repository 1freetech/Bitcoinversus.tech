<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>TSMC has moved its possible Texas expansion beyond the rumor stage. Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei said on October 15 that the company has already sent teams to Texas to evaluate conditions for additional U.S. manufacturing capacity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The update matters because BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/semiconductors-tsmc-weighs-texas-expansion-as-amd-pushes-for-more-wafer-capacity/">TSMC weighing a Texas expansion</a> when the idea was still based on people familiar with the matter. Today, management itself confirmed that Texas is under active evaluation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/tsmc-expected-report-sixth-straight-quarter-record-profit-thanks-ai-boom-2026-10-14/">Reuters reported</a> that TSMC is evaluating Texas as it looks for more U.S. capacity to serve major customers. Wei also confirmed discussions tied to the proposed Terafab project associated with Elon Musk, while making one condition clear: if TSMC participates in a wafer fab, TSMC would operate it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The distinction is important. TSMC has not announced a Texas fab, selected a site, disclosed a construction schedule or committed a final investment figure. <a href="https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/10/15/news-tsmc-sends-team-to-assess-texas-fab-expansion-confirms-terafab-talks-but-insists-on-operating-control/">TrendForce reported</a> that Wei said assessment teams are already on the ground, but the project remains under evaluation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>TSMC’s official Arizona video shows the kind of advanced manufacturing ecosystem the company is already building in the United States.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Texas would add a second major U.S. manufacturing region alongside Arizona. That could matter as AI-chip demand keeps pressure on advanced wafer capacity and packaging. TSMC’s U.S. expansion is also pulling more suppliers into the region. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/fujifilm-cmp-slurry-arizona/">Fujifilm adding CMP slurry capacity in Arizona</a>, a reminder that fabs depend on a much larger materials and equipment network.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Advanced packaging is another constraint. BitcoinVersus.Tech also covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/amkor-arizona-advanced-packaging-12-billion/">Amkor’s expanded Arizona packaging plan</a>. A Texas manufacturing cluster would therefore need more than cleanrooms and lithography tools; it would require power, water, specialty chemicals, packaging, testing, logistics and a trained semiconductor workforce.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The timing is notable because TSMC just posted another record quarter, with AI-related demand continuing to drive advanced-node production. Strong demand gives the company a reason to study additional capacity, but evaluating Texas does not mean construction is approved.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The new fact today is narrower and more concrete: TSMC has confirmed that Texas is being actively assessed, and its CEO has confirmed Terafab discussions while insisting that TSMC retain operational control over any fab it joins. That moves the story from industry reporting into direct management confirmation.</p>
