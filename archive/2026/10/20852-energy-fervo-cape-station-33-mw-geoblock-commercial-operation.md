<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Fervo Energy’s first 33 MW GeoBlock at Cape Station in Utah has reached commercial operation, moving enhanced geothermal from a grid-synchronization milestone into contracted electricity sales just six days after the plant first began exporting power.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>According to <a href="https://fervoenergy.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fervo-energy-declares-commercial-operation-cape-station-ahead">Fervo’s October 1 commercial-operation announcement</a>, the first Cape Station GeoBlock synchronized to the grid on September 24 and reached its contractual commercial operation date on September 30, one day ahead of schedule. Fervo says the unit is producing 33 MW net and is now generating revenue under its power-purchase agreement.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The milestone is a direct follow-through from the first-power event BitcoinVersus.Tech covered in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/fervos-cape-station-starts-sending-geothermal-power-to-the-grid/">Fervo’s Cape Station Starts Sending Geothermal Power to the Grid</a>. First power proved that the plant could synchronize and export electricity; commercial operation means the first standardized block has now crossed the contractual performance gate required to sell power under its agreement.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Twenty-three months from construction to commercial operation</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/worlds-first-enhanced-geothermal-power-plant-completed-in-just-23-months/">TechCrunch reports that the first block took 23 months from construction to commercial operation</a>, a development speed that matters because power-hungry data centers increasingly need generation that can arrive on a timetable closer to the facilities consuming it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Fervo’s model combines horizontal drilling and subsurface stimulation techniques adapted from the oil and gas industry with geothermal heat extraction. Unlike conventional geothermal projects that depend on naturally occurring hot-water reservoirs near the surface, enhanced geothermal systems are designed to create usable circulation pathways through hot rock at greater depth.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company marked the earlier grid milestone in <a href="https://twitter.com/fervoenergy/status/2103146366017151234">its official Cape Station first-power post on X</a>, showing the project transitioning from construction into electricity production.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A modular power plant built in blocks</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Cape Station Phase I is planned as roughly 100 MW across three 33 MW GeoBlocks. Fervo says commissioning is continuing on the other two units, which are expected to reach contractual commercial operation by January 1, 2027. An additional 400 MW phase is already under construction for a planned 2028 commercial-operation date.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That block-by-block deployment model resembles the way large computing campuses expand capacity in stages. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently examined the same speed-to-power pressure from the load side in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/xai-builds-massive-megapack-battery-behind-colossus-2/">xAI’s large Megapack battery deployment behind Colossus 2</a>, where energy infrastructure is being assembled alongside rapidly growing AI compute.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Geothermal brings a different operating profile from batteries, solar or wind because the resource can provide continuous generation rather than shifting or intermittently producing energy. That makes construction speed and repeatability especially important if enhanced geothermal is going to become a meaningful supply option for round-the-clock industrial and data-center loads.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The next test is repeatability</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The first GeoBlock proves one commercial unit can cross the contractual finish line. The larger engineering question is whether the same drilling, completion and surface-plant process can be repeated across the remaining blocks without losing the schedule advantages demonstrated by the first unit.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That question is increasingly important as utilities negotiate how enormous new computing loads are connected to the grid. BitcoinVersus.Tech’s coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/google-michigan-data-center-dte-power-deal-ratepayer-protections/">Google’s Michigan data-center power agreement and ratepayer protections</a> showed how generation, transmission and large-load economics are becoming central to data-center development.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Fervo says future GeoBlocks could be completed even faster as the company carries lessons from Cape Station into later builds. If the remaining units reproduce the first block’s performance and schedule, enhanced geothermal would gain something power markets value almost as much as raw capacity: a repeatable construction timeline.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
