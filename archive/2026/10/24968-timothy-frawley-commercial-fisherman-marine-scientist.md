<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Timothy Frawley built his career from both sides of the fishing industry: first on working waterfronts, then inside marine science.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Commercial fishing ranked among our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">Top 10 Toughest Jobs in the World in 2026</a>. Frawley is a strong Career Journalist example because he later paired that physically demanding background with doctoral-level research.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to the <a href="https://umaine.edu/leslie-lab/people/tim-frawley/">University of Maine</a>, Frawley was born near Casco Bay and worked in commercial fisheries in Maine, Alaska, California and Baja California Sur. He later earned a PhD from Stanford University’s Hopkins Marine Station in 2019 and moved into research spanning fisheries, climate adaptation, marine ecology and resource governance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That combination matters because Frawley’s science is rooted in the same communities he once worked inside. His research has examined the collapse of Mexico’s jumbo-squid fishery, West Coast albacore fleets and the ways small fishing businesses adapt when ocean conditions and species distributions shift.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[vimeo 314565974]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>This fisheries webinar looks at how fishing communities respond when major fish resources move or decline.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His work is still active. A <a href="https://caseagrant.ucsd.edu/our-work/research-projects/pathways-resilience-advancing-community-engaged-fisheries-research-and">2026 California Sea Grant project</a> lists Frawley as principal investigator on a Monterey Bay effort designed to help fishing communities plan for environmental, regulatory and economic change.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That puts his résumé in the same spirit as former EOD technician and ultramarathoner <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/aaron-hale-eod-technician-ultramarathoner/">Aaron Hale</a> and smokejumper-turned-dentist <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/robert-rosenthal-smokejumper-dentist/">Robert Rosenthal</a>: difficult work in one world followed by mastery in another.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Frawley’s career is unusual for a simple reason. He did not just study fishermen from a distance. He worked the decks first, then learned the science well enough to help study how those same industries survive a changing ocean.</p>
