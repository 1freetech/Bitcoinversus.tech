<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Power Mining has unveiled Bitcube</strong>, a palm-sized Bitcoin miner using Canaan's Avalon A3197 ASIC chip, with an advertised maximum <strong>777 GH/s</strong> (0.777 TH/s). Announced October 21, 2026, it combines USB-C power, Wi-Fi and a 2.73-inch touchscreen in a small desktop enclosure. The manufacturer is accepting preorders; the advertised performance and Christmas delivery target are not independently confirmed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Turbo mode is rated up to 777 GH/s; Quiet mode is approximately 550 GH/s.</li><li>Manufacturer-listed 20–25 W wall power implies roughly 25.7–32.2 J/TH at maximum hashrate, not the advertised 20 J/TH chip figure.</li><li>Bitcube remains a preorder; solo mining at this scale offers very low block-winning odds.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Canaan Chip Moves Into a Desktop Miner</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Power Mining's <a href="https://powermining.io/products/bitcube">official specifications</a> list a 74 × 74 × 80 mm case, a 2.73-inch touch display, an ESP32-S3 controller and a 50 mm fan. The €117.50 product is offered for preorder, with shipment targeted before Christmas. In its <a href="https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/950982054/power-mining-partners-with-canaan-to-bring-home-bitcoin-mining-device-to-mass-audiences">October 21 release</a>, the Latvian company said direct access to Canaan chips reduces the need to salvage ASICs from larger mining machines.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Wall Power Changes the Efficiency Calculation</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The product page lists <strong>20 J/TH chip efficiency</strong> but <strong>20–25 W total device consumption</strong>. Divide that wall power by 0.777 TH/s and the complete machine would use roughly <strong>25.7–32.2 J/TH</strong> at full advertised speed. At $0.15/kWh, continuous operation would cost approximately <strong>$2.16–$2.70 per 30 days</strong>, excluding purchase cost. These are calculations from advertised inputs, not measured results.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Solo Mining Is a Learning Experience</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>At a hypothetical 1 ZH/s network hashrate, 0.777 TH/s would imply a statistical average wait of roughly <strong>24,500 years</strong> for a solo block. An actual winner could emerge sooner—or never. BitcoinVersus.Tech documented <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/braiins-solo-bitcoin-block-966351-38-day-gap/">another verified solo-mining block</a>, and covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/25/pierre-rochard-highlights-bitaxe-gamma-for-bitcoin-solo-mining/">Bitaxe home-mining hardware in 2024</a>. Small wins are possible but not representative of ordinary returns.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
