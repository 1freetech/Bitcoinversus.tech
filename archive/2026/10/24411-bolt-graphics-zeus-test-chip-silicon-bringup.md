<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bolt Graphics says its Zeus GPU project has moved from emulation into working silicon.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Sunnyvale semiconductor startup announced October 13 that it has completed <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bolt-graphics-achieves-silicon-bring-up-of-zeus-test-chip-opens-taipei-office-302906384.html">silicon bring-up of a 12nm TSMC Zeus test chip</a>. The device is not the final commercial GPU. It is a validation chip used to prove key parts of the architecture before Bolt moves toward production silicon planned on TSMC’s N5P process.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bolt says the 6 mm² A0 test chip was powered on and brought up within 24 hours with no functional issues, then tested across a temperature range of -40°C to 85°C. The company reports power consumption of 1.4 watts for the chip compared with 35 watts for the FPGA platform it previously used for prototyping. Those are company-reported engineering results, not independent benchmark results.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The unusual part of Zeus is its focus on physics simulation as well as graphics. Bolt says the test chip includes dedicated hardware for electromagnetic simulation and path tracing, allowing parts of Maxwell’s equations to execute directly in fixed-function hardware. The company argues this could accelerate full-product simulation for areas such as silicon photonics, materials research, optics and robotics.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That simulation angle connects to a broader change in compute design. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/amd-rocm-sifive-risc-v-datacenter-server/">RISC-V moving deeper into data-center AI systems</a>. Zeus similarly combines specialized acceleration with general-purpose processing rather than treating a GPU as only a graphics device.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bolt also points to silicon photonics as a target workload. That market is expanding quickly as AI systems push optical links closer to compute. BitcoinVersus.Tech has tracked that shift through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/smart-photonics-globalfoundries-inp-silicon-photonics-foundry/">new foundry work combining indium phosphide with silicon photonics</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The production schedule is still aggressive. Bolt says developer access is planned for the first quarter of 2027, followed by initial Early Access Program hardware in late 2027 and volume production of PCIe cards and servers in 2028. The company has also opened a Taipei office to work more closely with manufacturing partners and suppliers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The move to N5P will put Bolt into the same advanced-node supply chain that serves many established chip designers. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently reported that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/semiconductors-tsmc-hits-46-71b-record-quarter-as-ai-chip-demand-surges/">TSMC posted a record quarter as AI chip demand surged</a>, highlighting how competitive access to leading-edge capacity remains.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For now, the important milestone is narrower than Bolt’s performance claims. Zeus is no longer only an FPGA model or architecture presentation: at least part of the design has now been fabricated, powered on and tested in silicon. The harder proof will come when production chips, software and independent benchmarks arrive.</p>
