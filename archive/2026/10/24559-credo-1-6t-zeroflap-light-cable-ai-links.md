<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Credo is pushing optical links deeper into the AI data center with a new 1.6 Tb/s active light cable designed to reach across an entire row of equipment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an <a href="https://investors.credosemi.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Credo-Unveils-1-6T-ZeroFlap-Active-Light-Cable-Industrys-First-Row-Scale-Wide-Parallel-Optical-Interconnect-for-AI-Data-Centers/default.aspx">October 12 announcement</a>, Credo said its 1.6T ZeroFlap Active Light Cable uses arrays of micro-emitters and photodetectors instead of pushing a small number of optical channels to extreme speeds. The company is targeting up to <strong>50 meters of reach</strong> while drawing <strong>12 watts per cable end</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/stockedgeN/status/2043796885735956990]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>This earlier Credo optics post covers the company’s acquisition of DustPhotonics, background that helps explain its broader push into silicon photonics. It is separate from the new cable announcement.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Credo says the cable is about <strong>30% lower power</strong> and <strong>10 times lighter</strong> than comparable active electrical cables, with built-in channel redundancy intended to keep traffic flowing if one optical path degrades. An independent <a href="https://www.datacenter-intelligence.com/news/credo-ai-data-centre-optics-workstream/">Data Center Intelligence report</a> also notes that Credo and several optical suppliers are now working through the Open Compute Project to standardize this wider, parallel micro-emitter approach.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fjQ3Yorw-Ps]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>UC Santa Barbara professor John Bowers explains why silicon photonics and optical interconnects are becoming central to scaling AI systems.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The important limitation is timing: the ZeroFlap active light cable is <strong>planned to ship in 2027</strong>, so these are product targets rather than field results from broad deployment. Still, the direction lines up with BitcoinVersus.Tech’s coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/voyant-photonics-ai-optical-switching/">optical switching</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/lumentum-says-ai-optics-are-sold-out-through-early-2029/">tight AI-optics supply</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/networking-lightmatter-passage-l20-bidirectional-optics-half-ai-fiber/">Lightmatter’s bidirectional optical links</a>: copper is still dominant at short reach, but optics keeps moving closer to the rack.</p>
