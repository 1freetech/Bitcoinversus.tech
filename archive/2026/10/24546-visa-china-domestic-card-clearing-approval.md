<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Visa received approval from the People’s Bank of China on October 13, 2026 to begin formal preparations for a domestic bank-card clearing institution in China.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The distinction matters: this is <strong>not yet an operating license</strong>. Visa’s own <a href="https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22846.html">announcement</a> says the approval covers its “Application to Prepare.” Reuters reports that Visa Information Technology (Beijing) has one year to complete the preparatory stage before applying to open, with Chinese regulators reviewing the business again before a clearing license can be issued.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The political context is hard to separate from the fintech story, but it should not be overstated. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/china/visa-gets-approval-prepare-domestic-business-china-2026-10-13/">Reuters</a> notes the decision follows recent U.S.-China talks over market access. Visa CEO Ryan McInerney explicitly thanked leaders of both countries for pursuing a more stable economic relationship.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Visa eventually receives a full license, it would join American Express and Mastercard in China’s domestic clearing market. For BitcoinVersus.Tech, the bigger theme is the continuing convergence between traditional card networks and newer financial rails already visible in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-stripe-makes-open-usd-the-default-stablecoin-across-its-payment-stack/">stablecoin payments</a>, app-based finance such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/finance-gcash-mynt-53b-ipo-retail-access/">GCash</a>, and earlier efforts by <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/10/23/us-banks-explore-joint-stablecoin-initiative-to-modernize-payments/">U.S. banks to modernize payment infrastructure</a>.</p>
