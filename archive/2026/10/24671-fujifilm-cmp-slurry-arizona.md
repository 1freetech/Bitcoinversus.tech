<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Fujifilm is putting another <strong>$18 million</strong> into semiconductor materials production in Mesa, Arizona. The company said on October 14 that it will expand copper-interconnect CMP slurry capacity and add a flexible production line for several other polishing chemistries used across advanced chip manufacturing. <a href="https://newsroom-us.fujifilm.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Fujifilm-to-Expand-CMP-Slurry-Production-Capacity-in-the-United-States/default.aspx">Fujifilm’s announcement</a> says the new capacity is scheduled to begin operating in 2027.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CMP, or chemical mechanical planarization, is the process that flattens wafer surfaces between manufacturing steps. Fujifilm says the Mesa expansion will support front-end, middle-of-line, back-end and <strong>advanced packaging</strong> applications, including hybrid bonding. That connects directly with the packaging bottlenecks highlighted in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/semicon-west-2026-ai-chip-manufacturing-packaging/">SEMICON West coverage</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The expansion is small compared with a new fab, but the materials are essential to the fab itself. <a href="https://www.bizjournals.com/phoenix/news/2026/10/13/fujifilm-to-invest-18-m-in-mesa-expansion.html">Phoenix Business Journal</a> reports the Mesa site already anchors Fujifilm’s U.S. semiconductor-materials footprint. More local slurry capacity matters as U.S. fabs add <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/semiconductors-globalfoundries-tsmc-2-billion-silicon-interposer-new-york-ai-packaging/">advanced packaging capacity</a> and continue building around <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/semiconductors-what-is-300mm-wafer-12-inch-silicon/">300 mm wafer manufacturing</a>.</p>
