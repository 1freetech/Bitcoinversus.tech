<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A Chicago startup is developing printable materials designed to turn one of a data center’s biggest waste products back into electricity: heat.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Captis Materials is working with the U.S. Department of Energy’s Chain Reaction Innovations program at Argonne National Laboratory on <a href="https://chainreaction.anl.gov/recapturing-data-center-waste-heat-with-low-cost-printable-thermoelectric-generators/">low-cost thermoelectric generators</a> for data centers. The company’s approach uses metal-free p- and n-type semiconductor materials that can be printed at room temperature onto plastic substrates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/te_materials/status/1817807266470338707]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Thermoelectric materials generate voltage from a temperature difference. This research post provides broader materials-science context; it is not a Captis announcement.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The interesting target is low-grade heat. Captis says its technology can operate below 70 °C, a range that is often too cool for conventional heat engines. Its <a href="https://www.captismaterials.com/datacenters">data-center program</a> is aimed at modular solid-state devices with no moving parts that could sit alongside cooling infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K-56cKeK_ss]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>PyroDelta demonstrates a different thermoelectric generator built for data-center liquid cooling, showing how the same heat-to-electricity principle can be integrated around cooling loops.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Argonne says a hypothetical 5%-efficient Captis device could save more than $30 million per year at a 1 GW data center and potentially pay back in under five years. Those numbers are projections, not published commercial results. Captis is still seeking data-center pilot partners.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The idea extends the waste-heat problem BitcoinVersus.Tech has explored through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/can-data-center-waste-heat-warm-homes-or-nah/">district heating</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/osdcec-008-data-center-water-systems-cooling-loops-towers-water-quality-blowdown-wue/">data-center cooling loops</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/bitcoin-miner-dried-laundry-heated-home-hash-to-heat/">Bitcoin mining heat reuse</a>. Instead of exporting the heat, Captis wants to convert part of it back into the thing data centers value most: electricity.</p>
