<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Microsoft has withdrawn as the anchor customer for Nscale’s planned <strong>50-megawatt AI data center</strong> in Loughton, Essex, after delays reportedly pushed its electricity-grid connection into the 2030s. The October 19 confirmation concerns one UK location—not the companies’ broader partnership—and shows why securing a power connection can matter as much as acquiring GPUs.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Site Was Designed for 23,040 GPUs</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-uk-ai-infrastructure-announcement">Nscale’s September 16, 2025 announcement</a> described a 50 MW campus expandable to 90 MW, with <strong>23,040 Nvidia GB300 GPUs</strong> expected in the first quarter of 2027 to support Microsoft Azure services. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/microsoft-pulls-out-uk-data-center-deal-with-nscale-telegraph-reports-2026-10-18/">Reuters reported</a> that grid officials now expect sufficient power only in the 2030s. Microsoft said its wider cooperation with Nscale continues, while Nscale said it is examining alternative energy supplies and other UK sites.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Bitcoin Mining Has to Do With It</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Nscale grew out of Arkon Energy, which developed Bitcoin mining infrastructure. Its move toward AI computing illustrates how expertise in electrical systems, cooling, land and industrial data-center development can transfer between industries. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/24/bitcoin-mining-spinout-nscale-new-york-ipo/">Nscale’s proposed New York IPO</a> and its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/nscale-raises-3-36-billion-for-ai-infrastructure-push/">$3.36 billion capital raise</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Yet business models differ. Some Bitcoin mines can curtail electrical load when power is constrained, while an AI cloud customer may require contractually specified availability and a firm delivery date. Read our analysis of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/pjm-grid-emergency-bitcoin-mining-flexible-load/">Bitcoin mining as a flexible grid resource</a> and our explainer on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/can-a-data-center-run-entirely-off-grid-or-nah/">off-grid data centers</a> for more on those engineering trade-offs.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The UK Connection Queue</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>British regulator <a href="https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/ofgem-acts-free-grid-capacity-tackling-speculative-data-centre-projects">Ofgem said in July</a> that data-center applications accounted for about <strong>73 GW</strong> in the electricity-connection queue. That figure describes requested future capacity, not operating facilities. Ofgem proposed commitment fees and project milestones to reduce speculative requests that can slow projects ready to connect.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Happens Next?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The unresolved question is whether Nscale can secure dependable alternative power at Loughton—or find another anchor tenant. Neither outcome is confirmed. The immediate lesson is that planned GPU capacity and contracted computing capacity are not the same thing as electricity delivered to a working data center.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Microsoft exited the Loughton site agreement, not the wider Nscale relationship.</li><li>The original project envisioned 50 MW of capacity and 23,040 GPUs.</li><li>Grid-connection availability remains a key AI infrastructure constraint.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->
