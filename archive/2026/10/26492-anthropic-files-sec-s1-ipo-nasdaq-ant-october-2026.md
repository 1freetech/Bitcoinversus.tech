## Final Gutenberg source

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Anthropic, the San Francisco company behind Claude, publicly filed its <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm">Form S-1 registration statement</a> with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26, 2026. Its <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec">official announcement</a> confirms a proposed initial public offering of Class A shares and an application to trade on Nasdaq under the symbol ANT. No offer price, share count or first trading date is established.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list --><ul><li>Anthropic's October 26 public S-1 proposes Nasdaq ticker <strong>ANT</strong>, but pricing and timing are open.</li><li>First-half 2026 revenue reached <strong>$16.286 billion</strong>, roughly 14 times a year earlier.</li><li>The <strong>$80.748 billion</strong> net loss contains substantial noncash valuation effects.</li><li>AI compute costs, shareholder rights and long-term financing remain central questions.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>What the Filing Shows</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The filing makes Anthropic's finances easier to evaluate while the company prepares for public-market scrutiny. It does not mean shares are available to purchase: the registration statement has not yet become effective.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Anthropic reported $16.286 billion in revenue for the six months ended June 30, 2026, compared with $1.163 billion a year earlier—about 14 times as much. Yet its operating loss was $693 million, versus $2.135 billion in the comparable 2025 period. The revenue growth is substantial, but it should not be confused with sustained profitability.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Why the $80.7 Billion Net Loss Needs Context</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The filing also reports an $80.748 billion net loss for that 2026 half-year. Importantly, $72.388 billion arose from changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs. That accounting charge is different from cash spent operating AI models or building data centers. Our earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/anthropic-revenue-12x-compute-costs-7-3-billion-ai-profitability/">analysis of Anthropic's revenue and computing costs</a> helps explain the underlying operating economics.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>What Investors Will Watch</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Anthropic's application could give public investors exposure to a major frontier-model developer, but it will also invite close examination of capital demands and governance. The filing describes a public-benefit corporation with a Long-Term Benefit Trust empowered to select a majority of directors and a founder-controlled voting class.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The infrastructure question is particularly important. Our previous reporting detailed <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/29/anthropic-locks-in-518-billion-of-ai-infrastructure-commitments/">Anthropic's extensive compute commitments</a>, a <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/broadcom-anthropic-42-billion-ai-infrastructure-financing/">potential $42 billion Broadcom financing arrangement</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/terawulf-anthropic-20-year-data-center-lease/">its long-term data-center lease relationships</a>. Revenue must eventually support hardware, electricity, networking and software costs at scale.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
