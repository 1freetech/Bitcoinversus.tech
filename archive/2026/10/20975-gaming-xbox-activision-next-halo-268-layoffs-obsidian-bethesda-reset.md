<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Microsoft is collapsing more of Xbox’s studio structure into a smaller number of operating groups — and the biggest symbolic change is Halo moving under Activision for its next game.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On September 22, Xbox announced another 268 role eliminations across Halo Studios, other first-party teams, and Xbox Game Studios management and central functions. At the same time, Microsoft reassigned several of its most important franchises and studios under Activision, Bethesda, King, and a newly combined Playground/Turn 10 organization.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Activision is taking the next Halo</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Xbox’s <a href="https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/09/22/continuing-our-reset/">September 22 restructuring memo</a>, Matt Booty said Activision will expand its remit to include World’s Edge and Rare and will develop the next Halo title through a new purpose-built team separate from Call of Duty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A smaller Halo Studios group will continue supporting existing Halo games and the community. That means the franchise is not being abandoned, but the center of gravity for its next major game is moving away from the studio structure historically associated with it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Bethesda is taking Obsidian</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bethesda will now oversee Obsidian Entertainment, which Xbox says will continue its current work, including Grounded and a new Fallout project being developed with Bethesda Game Studios.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The logic is easy to see. Obsidian already has deep role-playing-game DNA, and moving it closer to Bethesda consolidates two groups that work in adjacent genres and share one of Microsoft’s most valuable RPG franchises.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">King, Playground, and Turn 10 are being consolidated too</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>King will expand its remit to Microsoft Casual Games, combining two large-scale casual and live-operations organizations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Playground Games and Turn 10 Studios will become one studio focused on Forza and Fable. The two teams have worked together for years, but the change turns that long-standing collaboration into one formal organization.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The reset is also shrinking Xbox</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The structural changes cannot be separated from the layoffs. Xbox says the latest 268 cuts bring the company roughly three-quarters of the way through the restructuring it began earlier this year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/entertainment/video-games/2026/09/22/halo-move-under-call-duty-publisher-xbox-announces-layoffs/">The Washington Post reported</a> that the changes affect Halo Studios, other first-party developers, and management, while also shifting Halo development responsibility to Activision and Obsidian under Bethesda.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Xbox also confirmed that Compulsion Games and Double Fine returned to management with their intellectual property and back catalogs, while Undead Labs transitioned under similar terms with a new publisher. Proposed deals for Ninja Theory fell through, leaving Microsoft to begin consultation on a possible closure while exploring alternatives. Arkane remains under consultation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Microsoft is turning acquisitions into operating groups</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deeper strategy is becoming clearer. Microsoft spent years acquiring studios and publishers. The current phase is about deciding which of those organizations should actually operate together.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Activision becomes a broader publishing and development center. Bethesda becomes a larger RPG organization. King becomes a casual-games hub. Playground and Turn 10 become a combined racing-and-Fable studio.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That can reduce duplicated management and align development expertise, but it also concentrates creative decision-making inside fewer corporate groups.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Halo is the most important test</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Halo carries more symbolic weight than any other move because it was once the franchise that defined Xbox hardware itself.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Activision can build a stronger development pipeline around Halo without turning it into a Call of Duty imitation, Microsoft could prove that its large-publisher structure can revive underperforming franchises. If the transition creates another layer of disruption, it will reinforce criticism that Xbox’s acquisition strategy produced organizational complexity faster than creative momentum.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The next Xbox may be defined by fewer silos</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Microsoft’s latest reset is not simply about cutting headcount. It is an attempt to turn a sprawling collection of acquired studios into a smaller number of operating systems for game development.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The risk is that efficiency comes at the expense of studio identity. The opportunity is that Activision, Bethesda, King, and the combined Playground/Turn 10 organization gain enough scale and focus to ship better games more consistently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Halo will be the clearest signal of which outcome Microsoft gets.</p>
