<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Olenox Industries (NASDAQ: OLOX) plans to expand its Bitcoin mining fleet from approximately <strong>9,700 to 20,000 machines</strong> while targeting electricity at <strong>1.5¢/kWh</strong>—down from roughly 6¢ under its current arrangements. The October 22 announcement outlines a natural-gas-to-power-to-compute strategy, but neither the lower rate nor the expanded fleet has been achieved.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Olenox targets 20,000 miners and 75 MW of generation capacity, neither of which is currently deployed.</li><li>Its 1.5¢/kWh target implies a 75% electricity-rate reduction, not a 75% profit increase.</li><li>Financing, gas supply, generator deployment and site economics remain decisive.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Three Phases From Gas to Computing</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://ir.olenox.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/465/olenox-unveils-three-phase-plan-to-power-and-expand-bitcoin">company's October 22 statement</a> describes three steps: establish lower-cost onsite power; potentially relocate eligible Texas miners; then purchase and deploy additional machines. Olenox is evaluating its Wildboy gas platform in British Columbia and its Pecos-area Texas assets. Reaching the contemplated mining capacity requires adding enough generators to reach <strong>75 MW</strong>; that capacity is not presently available.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What 1.5¢ Electricity Would Change</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>For a hypothetical 3 kW ASIC running continuously, electricity alone costs <strong>$4.32 per day at 6¢/kWh</strong>, versus <strong>$1.08 at 1.5¢</strong>—a potential $3.24 daily saving. Generator purchase, natural-gas delivery, maintenance, cooling and financing can narrow the actual advantage. The <a href="https://ir.olenox.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/463/olenox-expands-energy-platform-with-completion-of-wildboy">October 20 acquisition announcement</a> describes a registered 100 MW Texas power-development platform, not 100 MW of operating generation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Existing Miners Still Face Operational Limits</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://ir.olenox.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/464/olenox-industries-reports-september-bitcoin-production-as">Olenox reported</a> <strong>15.03 BTC earned in September</strong>, an average 1.04 EH/s operational hashrate and 68% uptime, citing curtailment and heat-related underclocking. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously documented the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/23/olenox-s21-bitcoin-miners-texas-heat/">Texas S21 heat-testing challenges</a> and explored <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/28/texas-natural-gas-producers-boost-profits-with-bitcoin-mining/">natural-gas-powered mining economics in 2024</a>. A lower fuel-based electricity rate would not eliminate equipment or operational constraints.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
