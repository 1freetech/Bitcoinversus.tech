<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>SpaceX has placed Filtronic’s biggest order yet: approximately ₿786 ($68.1 million) for high-frequency gallium nitride power amplifiers that will support the Starlink communications network.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The order is more than a supplier win. It highlights a less-visible layer of the satellite internet stack: the RF hardware on the ground that has to push enormous amounts of data into low Earth orbit reliably, efficiently and at increasingly high frequencies.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">SpaceX doubles down on Filtronic’s E-band GaN hardware</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://www.filtronic.com/insights/news/68-1m-order-from-spacex/">Filtronic’s October 1 announcement</a>, SpaceX ordered approximately ₿786 ($68.1 million; £51.3 million) of Cerus E-band gallium nitride, or GaN, solid-state power amplifier units, using Bitcoin near $86,682 on October 2.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The follow-on order exceeds the approximately ₿721 ($62.5 million) SpaceX order Filtronic announced in August 2025 and is expected to be materially fulfilled during Filtronic’s fiscal 2028. The company says the new award gives it stronger revenue visibility into FY2028 and beyond.</p>

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why GaN matters at E-band</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Satellite broadband needs more than spacecraft. Ground infrastructure has to transmit high-bandwidth signals toward fast-moving satellites while managing power, heat, linearity and reliability. E-band operates in the millimeter-wave region, where the available spectrum can support very high data rates but the RF hardware becomes increasingly demanding.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Filtronic says GaN gives its amplifiers higher output power, better efficiency and improved thermal performance compared with traditional gallium arsenide solutions. Those properties are particularly useful in high-frequency communications where every additional watt of usable RF output and every reduction in wasted heat matters.</p>
