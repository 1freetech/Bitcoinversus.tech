<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The <a href="https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-5-billion-loan-boost-nuclear-power-and-help-lower-costs">U.S. Department of Energy</a> announced on October 27, 2026, a conditional loan commitment of up to $5 billion for <a href="https://investors.constellationenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/constellation-secures-conditional-doe-loan-commitment-5-billion">Constellation Energy</a> to modernize existing nuclear plants in Illinois, Maryland and Pennsylvania.</strong> Proposed power uprates could add nearly 1.1 gigawatts of generating capacity, enough electricity for an estimated 930,000 homes. The money has not yet been disbursed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Up to $5 billion is conditionally committed; it is not a closed or funded loan.</li><li>Eight existing nuclear facilities are eligible for potential uprates.</li><li>Any reduction in electricity prices remains projected, not an observed customer saving.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Eight plants across three states</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-5-billion-loan-boost-nuclear-power-and-help-lower-costs">DOE announcement</a> names Braidwood, Byron, Clinton, Dresden, LaSalle and Quad Cities in Illinois, Calvert Cliffs in Maryland and Limerick in Pennsylvania. An uprate increases a reactor station's output using improvements such as turbine or generating-equipment upgrades; it does not necessarily mean building another reactor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The financing could help Constellation borrow at lower cost, but each project must satisfy technical, environmental, legal and financial requirements before funds are released. Constellation also says the agreement does not constitute a final decision to proceed at every site. DOE estimates more than 10,000 project-related jobs; that figure is a projection.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why nuclear capacity matters for AI demand</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The plan overlaps with investment backed by Constellation's earlier customer agreements, including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-google-constellation-3590mw-pjm-890mw-new-nuclear/">Google's 890 MW nuclear capacity arrangement</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/energy-amazon-calvert-cliffs-190mw-nuclear-uprate/">Amazon's 190 MW Calvert Cliffs uprate</a>. Their megawatts should not be added to DOE's nearly 1.1 GW as if they were separate projects. Large <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-us-power-demand-record-highs-2026-2027-ai-crypto-data-centers/">AI and Bitcoin mining power demand</a> is part of the broader pressure on electricity systems.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>DOE says greater supply could improve reliability in the PJM and MISO regions and help restrain prices. Those outcomes depend on project completion, grid conditions and market prices; <strong>no specific household bill reduction has been demonstrated</strong>. For context, BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/u-s-plans-4-2-billion-vistra-loan-to-boost-nuclear-output/">separate $4.2 billion Vistra commitment</a>. Both remain distinct from operating nuclear output already online.</p>
