<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>On <strong>October 22, 2026</strong>, <a href="https://transalta.com/newsroom/transalta-advances-portfolio-rotation-through-sale-of-minority-interest-in-oklahoma-wind-portfolio-for-us-305-million-and-closes-acquisition-of-colorado-gas-assets-for-us-1-billion-2026-10-22">TransAlta confirmed</a> it completed the <strong>US$1 billion</strong> acquisition of two natural-gas peaking power plants near Denver, Colorado, from Blackstone-affiliated sellers. Together, <strong>Mountain Peak Power and Canyon Peak Power total 318 megawatts (MW)</strong> of installed generating capacity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul><li>TransAlta acquired <strong>162 MW Mountain Peak Power</strong> and <strong>156 MW Canyon Peak Power</strong>.</li><li>The US$1 billion transaction value includes <strong>US$750 million in assumed project debt</strong>, not US$1 billion in cash.</li><li>Both plants are operating and supported by long-term capacity agreements with Colorado electric cooperatives.</li><li>Forecast annual earnings are estimates, not realized results.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Two Colorado Plants, Twelve Gas Turbines</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In <strong>Keenesburg</strong>, <a href="https://transalta.com/our-operations/facilities/mountain-peak-power">Mountain Peak</a> has operated since September 2025 and uses six GE LM2500XPRESS gas turbines. Its 30-year tolling agreement is with <a href="https://www.unitedpower.com/united-power-celebrates-mountain-peak-power-plant-ribbon-cutting">United Power</a>. In <strong>Bennett</strong>, <a href="https://transalta.com/our-operations/facilities/canyon-peak-power">Canyon Peak</a> achieved commercial operation in the third quarter of 2026; its six comparable turbines are contracted to CORE Electric Cooperative for 25 years.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Peaking Capacity Matters</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Unlike continuously operated baseload units, <strong>peaking plants</strong> can be dispatched when grid demand or electricity prices rise. These gas turbines complement other generation but still emit carbon dioxide and require fuel. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-clean-vs-renewable-energy-difference-explained/">clean-versus-renewable energy explainer</a> clarifies that distinction.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A $1 Billion Portfolio Move</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The price comprised roughly <strong>US$250 million of cash consideration</strong> and <strong>US$750 million of assumed secured debt</strong>. TransAlta's <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144800/000119312526255694/d131521dfwp.htm">SEC-filed presentation</a> projected around <strong>US$80 million in annual adjusted EBITDA</strong> and US$33 million in free cash flow, figures that are forward-looking rather than guaranteed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The closure coincided with the company's <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/24/ares-transalta-305-million-500mw-oklahoma-wind-portfolio/">US$305 million sale of a 49% Oklahoma wind interest</a>. Unlike <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/applied-digital-15-year-1-2gw-north-dakota-power-deal/">a planned 1.2 GW North Dakota natural-gas project</a>, both Colorado facilities are already in service. Their contracted capacity and long-term performance—not projected headline returns alone—will determine the acquisition's results.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
