<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong><a href="https://www.ferveret.com/data-center">Ferveret</a> has deployed its rack-mounted immersion-cooling technology in high-performance computing research at <a href="https://news.mit.edu/2026/nuclear-inspired-cooling-system-ferveret-could-make-data-centers-more-sustainable-0610">MIT</a>, according to <a href="https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ferverets-in-rack-immersion-cooling-deployed-on-hpc-infrastructure-at-mit/">October 27, 2026 reporting</a>.</strong> Researchers are using the infrastructure for AI-driven investigations of materials for next-generation nuclear systems. Ferveret says combined thermal and power-control improvements could deliver <strong>up to 35% more usable compute under the same power budget</strong>; that is a company claim, not an independently published MIT campus-wide result.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>MIT researchers are using Ferveret-cooled computing for demanding nuclear-materials research.</li><li>Ferveret previously reported approximately 15% better server-level compute efficiency versus advanced liquid cooling in collaborative testing.</li><li>The larger 35% figure combines cooling and control benefits; facility-scale savings still require independent validation.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A smaller kind of immersion cooling</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Ferveret’s <a href="https://www.ferveret.com/technology">Adaptive Phase Cooling</a> immerses a server in dielectric fluid inside a compact enclosure. Tiny bubbles form at hot surfaces and collapse back into cooled liquid, moving heat without a conventional shared immersion tank. The company sells the modular <strong>Lambert 4U unit</strong> for integration into existing data-center racks, a potentially useful retrofit compared with rebuilding entire rooms.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://news.mit.edu/2026/nuclear-inspired-cooling-system-ferveret-could-make-data-centers-more-sustainable-0610">MIT’s June explanation</a>, the cooling approach emerged from heat-transfer research associated with nuclear reactors. Ferveret co-founders Reza Azizian and Matteo Bucci have both worked at MIT. Its latest research deployment supports analysis of difficult X-ray images from nuclear materials—not commercial power-generation output.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What capacity planners should measure</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A cooling upgrade can reduce temperature-related GPU throttling, fan consumption and thermal constraints, but it does not create additional utility megawatts. Operators still must measure <strong>actual work per watt</strong>, power utilization, rack weight, fluid serviceability, coolant safety and heat rejection. That connects directly to BitcoinVersus.Tech’s <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/compute-per-joule-bitcoin-mining-supercomputers-singularity-power-efficiency-theory/">compute-per-joule research</a> and its guide to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/top-7-data-center-cooling-technologies-in-2026/">data-center cooling technologies</a>.</p>

Before planners count that gain, sustained workload benchmarks and measured facility power are essential.</p>
