<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This countdown ranks <strong>road-car internal-combustion engines by engine-only peak power</strong>. Electric-motor output does not count. Track-only cars are excluded. Factory-supported high-output fuels such as E85 do count when the manufacturer explicitly rates the engine for them. Power density—horsepower per liter—is included as a second metric because displacement matters. That keeps a hybrid car’s electric assistance separate from the combustion engine itself; for context, see our explainer on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/what-is-a-hybrid-car/">what a hybrid car actually is</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">7. Dodge Challenger SRT Demon 170 — 1,025 hp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Dodge’s 6.2-liter supercharged HEMI V8 makes <strong>1,025 hp at 6,500 rpm</strong> and <strong>945 lb-ft at 4,200 rpm</strong> on E85. That works out to roughly <strong>165 hp per liter</strong>. Dodge also rates it at 900 hp on premium gasoline, making the ethanol content part of the factory power strategy. <a href="https://www.dodge.com/news/challenger-srt-demon-170-edition.html">Dodge’s technical release</a> documents the full output.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">6. Zenvo Aurora V12 — 1,250 bhp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Zenvo’s Aurora uses a <strong>6.6-liter quad-turbo V12</strong> developed with MAHLE Powertrain. The combustion engine alone is rated at <strong>1,250 bhp</strong> and can rev to 9,800 rpm, or about <strong>189 bhp per liter</strong>. Hybrid motors can raise total vehicle output to 1,850 bhp, but they are excluded here. <a href="https://zenvoautomotive.com/news/zenvo-automotive-the-second-chapter/">Zenvo identifies it as the most powerful V12 fitted to a road car</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">5. Koenigsegg Gemera HV8 — 1,500 hp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Gemera’s compact <strong>5.0-liter twin-turbo Hot V8</strong> produces <strong>1,500 hp and 1,500 Nm</strong> before the electric motor contributes anything. That is a striking <strong>300 hp per liter</strong>. Koenigsegg’s own specification separates the HV8 from the 800-hp Dark Matter electric motor, which is why the engine can be ranked cleanly here. <a href="https://www.koenigsegg.com/technical-specifications-gemera-2023">Koenigsegg lists the engine and motor outputs independently</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">4. Bugatti Chiron Super Sport W16 — 1,600 PS</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bugatti’s <strong>8.0-liter quad-turbo W16</strong> reaches <strong>1,600 PS</strong>, roughly 1,578 mechanical horsepower, with <strong>1,600 Nm</strong> of torque. That is about <strong>197 hp per liter</strong>. Its specific output is lower than the smaller engines above it, but the W16 combines huge displacement with four turbochargers and a very broad torque plateau. <a href="https://newsroom.bugatti.com/en/press-releases/the-bugatti-chiron-super-sport-the-quintessence-of-luxury-and-speed">Bugatti details the 1,600-PS engine here</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">3. Koenigsegg Jesko V8 — 1,600 hp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Jesko’s <strong>5.0-liter twin-turbo V8</strong> makes 1,280 hp on gasoline and <strong>1,600 hp on E85</strong>, plus up to <strong>1,500 Nm</strong> of torque. Its <strong>320 hp per liter</strong> is the highest specific output in this countdown. Koenigsegg also gives the engine an 8,500-rpm redline and an unusually light 12.5-kg flat-plane crankshaft. <a href="https://www.koenigsegg.com/technical-specifications-jesko-absolut">The factory specification lists both fuel-dependent outputs</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">2. SSC Tuatara V8 — 1,750 hp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>SSC’s road-legal Tuatara uses a bespoke <strong>5.9-liter twin-turbo flat-plane V8</strong> rated at <strong>1,750 hp on E85</strong> and 1,350 hp on 91-octane gasoline. That is about <strong>297 hp per liter</strong>. SSC’s non-road-legal Aggressor can go higher, but it is excluded by this ranking’s road-car rule. <a href="https://www.sscnorthamerica.com/model/tuatara">SSC’s current Tuatara specification confirms the 1,750-hp road-car figure</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">1. Hennessey Venom F5 Evolution V8 — 2,031 bhp</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hennessey’s revised <strong>6.6-liter twin-turbo “Fury” V8</strong> takes No. 1 with <strong>2,031 bhp at 8,000 rpm</strong> and <strong>1,445 lb-ft at 5,200 rpm</strong> on E85. That is about <strong>308 bhp per liter</strong>. Hennessey says the Evolution is the world’s most powerful 100% internal-combustion road car, and its engine output is more than double the Demon 170’s. <a href="https://www.hennesseyspecialvehicles.com/news/hennessey-finalizes-testing-of-2031-hp-venom-f5-evolution-hypercar/">Hennessey published the final validated figures in 2025</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most interesting result is not just the 2,031-bhp winner. The Jesko leads this group in <strong>power density</strong>, while the Chiron shows what enormous displacement and sustained torque can do. Hybridization changes the comparison again, as seen in the Gemera and Aurora; the same transition is reshaping the broader auto industry alongside <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/prologium-rimac-ev-battery-testing/">new EV battery systems</a> and the increasingly electrified vehicles appearing at events such as the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/paris-motor-show-2026-20-chinese-car-brands-byd-europe/">2026 Paris Motor Show</a>.</p>
