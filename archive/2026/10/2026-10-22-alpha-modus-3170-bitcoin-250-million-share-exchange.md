<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nasdaq-listed <strong>Alpha Modus Holdings (AMOD)</strong> says its wholly owned subsidiary, AMOD Tech Pte. Ltd., holds <strong>3,170 Bitcoin</strong> worth more than <strong>$250 million</strong> using an October 20, 2026 reference price. The company reiterated the position in an <a href="https://alphamodus.com/press-room/alpha-modus-highlights-bitcoin-holdings-with-value-exceeding-250-million-potential-225-million-in-additional-cash-proceeds-aria-expansion-targeting-over-20000-retail-locations-and-significant-federal-patent-litigation-milestones">October 21 announcement</a>, but the coins were transferred to the subsidiary on <strong>September 30</strong>—not purchased for cash this week.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><li><strong>3,170 BTC:</strong> Delivered to the company's Singapore subsidiary in a completed September 30 exchange.</li><li><strong>51.6 million shares:</strong> Issued to participating investors, alongside warrants for another 51.6 million shares.</li><li><strong>More than $250 million:</strong> A dated gross Bitcoin market valuation, not realized cash proceeds.</li><li><strong>Potential $225 million:</strong> Additional proceeds only if the accompanying warrants are exercised for cash.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How The Bitcoin Transaction Worked</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Alpha Modus issued <strong>51,621,560 Class A common shares</strong> and warrants for the same number of additional shares to non-U.S. investors. Those investors supplied 3,170 BTC as consideration. The company's <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1862463/000149315226045296/form8-k.htm">October 1 SEC Form 8-K</a> confirms the September 30 closing and delivery of Bitcoin to its subsidiary's custody and control. That structure is a <em>private investment in public equity (PIPE)</em> transaction—not an ordinary open-market Bitcoin buy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company used <strong>$82,525.80 per BTC</strong> on October 20 to calculate the more-than-$250-million value, versus an original agreed transaction valuation near $225 million. Both are snapshots: the holdings' dollar value changes with Bitcoin's market price.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Shareholders Should Watch</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The warrants carry a <strong>$4.36 exercise price</strong>. Full cash exercise could generate approximately <strong>$225 million</strong> in gross proceeds, but that cash has not been received simply because warrants were issued. The original share issuance materially expanded the share count, and exercise of warrants would increase it further. Share dilution, trading liquidity, custody risks and Bitcoin volatility matter alongside the asset headline.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deal also followed the company's effort to address a Nasdaq stockholders' equity shortfall. Readers comparing corporate holdings can consult our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/18/top-7-largest-public-company-bitcoin-treasuries-2026/">October public-company Bitcoin treasury ranking</a>, our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/robinhood-25-million-bitcoin-balance-sheet/">Robinhood corporate allocation report</a>, and the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/mara-says-1292-btc-transfer-was-a-loan-return-not-a-buy/">MARA transfer-versus-purchase explainer</a>. They illustrate why Bitcoin custody, ownership and how the coins were acquired must be distinguished.</p>
