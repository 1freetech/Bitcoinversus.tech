<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>HEX and PulseChain founder <strong>Richard Heart</strong>, whose legal name is Richard James Schueler, was reportedly detained in Istanbul on October 14 during an operation involving Turkey’s National Intelligence Organization and local police.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://tr.euronews.com/2026/10/14/kirmizi-bultenle-aranan-kriptopara-balinasi-istanbulda-yakalandi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Euronews Turkey</strong></a> reports that Schueler was taken into custody in Beşiktaş. Its itemized account says officers seized about <strong>$600,000</strong>, €2,600, ₺50,000, 24 watches, gold jewelry, 12 phones, computers, storage devices, three pistols, four long guns, seven magazines and 1,360 rounds of ammunition.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The numbers are not fully settled. Other Turkish reports cited by international outlets describe roughly <strong>$4 million</strong> in cash and 64 watches. BitcoinVersus.Tech is treating the seizure inventory as <strong>reported, not independently confirmed</strong>, until Turkish authorities publish a formal statement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Heart had been sought by Finland through an Interpol Red Notice connected to alleged aggravated tax fraud and an assault investigation. A Red Notice is a request for countries to locate and provisionally arrest a wanted person; it is not itself an international arrest warrant. No extradition to Finland has been confirmed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Istanbul matter is separate from Heart’s former U.S. SEC case. A federal judge dismissed that lawsuit in February 2025 on jurisdictional grounds, and the SEC later declined to amend it. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/us-judge-dismisses-sec-fraud-lawsuit-against-hex-crypto-founder-2025-03-01/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Reuters</strong></a> reported that dismissal at the time.</p>
