# Gutenberg source

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Core Scientific <strong>completed its acquisition of Polaris DS on August 13, 2026</strong>, paying approximately <strong>$444.3 million</strong> for a business holding access to about <strong>440 MW of existing grid-connected power</strong> in Muskogee, Oklahoma. The deal, <a href="https://investors.corescientific.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/142/core-scientific-completes-acquisition-of-polaris-ds-advancing-planned-muskogee-expansion-to-1-5-gw-of-gross-power">announced August 14</a>, highlights how electricity infrastructure developed for Bitcoin mining can become strategically valuable for AI data centers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>The acquisition closed on August 13; it is not merely a proposed merger.</li><li>Polaris held about 440 MW of power already in service under Oklahoma Gas &amp; Electric agreements.</li><li>Core Scientific targets 1.5 GW of gross campus power, but the full expansion remains planned.</li><li>The $444.3 million purchase may increase by another $40 million if a specific extra-power condition is met.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the Acquisition Includes</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company’s <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839341/000183934126000018/core-20260813.htm">August 14 Form 8-K</a> confirms that the transaction closed August 13. The filing identifies approximately $444.3 million in cash consideration, subject to post-closing adjustments. It also permits a further $40 million payment if an additional 40 MW of firm capacity becomes available before December 31, 2026.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The acquired infrastructure includes power arrangements and an adjacent approximately 40-acre site described in <a href="https://investors.corescientific.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/135/core-scientific-plans-expansion-to-1-5-gigawatts-of-gross-power-at-muskogee-oklahoma-campus">Core Scientific’s original May proposal</a>. Connected capacity is not interchangeable with installed GPU capacity: substations, buildings, cooling, networking and customer delivery still require execution.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Mining Power Becomes AI Infrastructure</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Polaris had been using the electricity for Bitcoin mining. Core Scientific intends to expand high-density colocation instead, illustrating the trend tracked in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/09/02/coreweave-core-scientific-deal-key-indicator-ai-boosts-profitability-for-bitcoin-miners/">earlier CoreWeave–Core Scientific coverage</a>. For context, our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitcoin-miners-unplugged-75-ehs/">75 EH/s mining capacity analysis</a> explores how retired or relocated SHA-256 fleets can leave valuable power-ready sites behind.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Core Scientific says it aims for <strong>1.5 GW gross power and approximately 1.0 GW leasable power</strong> at Muskogee through utility connections and behind-the-meter solutions. Its next roughly 82 MW of customer delivery is projected for the second half of 2027. Those are forward-looking targets, not delivered capacity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The financial logic centers on scarce, energized real estate. Yet converting mining sites into AI campuses also carries construction, financing and customer concentration risks, which we examined in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/ai-selloff-hits-bitcoin-mining-stocks-harder-than-bitcoin/">report on mining-stock exposure to the AI market</a>. The completed Polaris transaction establishes ownership and power access; its ultimate return still depends on converting those assets into reliable, revenue-producing data centers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
