<p><a href="https://shop.bitmain.com/product/detail?pid=00020260826005834776G6rGvY730671"><strong>Bitmain</strong></a> is preparing to ship one of its densest current Bitcoin mining machines: the <strong>Antminer S23e U2H</strong>, a rack-mount hydro ASIC rated at <strong>865 TH/s</strong>, <strong>8,650 W</strong>, and <strong>10 J/TH</strong>. Bitmain’s current store listing shows the November 2026 futures batch as <strong>sold out</strong> at <strong>₿0.11342 ($9,515)</strong>, or $11 per terahash.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the dedicated mining-hardware story the current rotation needed. The S23e U2H is not an air-cooled shelf miner or a generic crypto server. It is a <strong>SHA-256 Bitcoin ASIC</strong> built in a standard <strong>2U server chassis</strong>, designed to slide directly into high-density hydro-cooled racks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">865 TH/s in a Standard 2U Chassis</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitmain’s official specifications list the S23e U2H at 865 TH/s with ±3% hashrate variation and 10 J/TH typical wall efficiency at a 35°C coolant condition. The server measures roughly <strong>600 × 482.6 × 86 mm</strong>, which puts it in standard 19-inch rack territory rather than the freestanding chassis shape familiar from older Antminers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That form factor changes how operators think about density. The question is no longer only how many ASICs fit in a container; it is how much <strong>hashrate, power, coolant flow, network connectivity, and service access</strong> can be packed into conventional rack infrastructure without creating a thermal or electrical bottleneck.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">The Price Was Cut in Half</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When Bitmain <a href="https://bitmainofficial.com/news-detail/bitmain-launches-antminer-s23e-u2h-432/"><strong>launched the S23e U2H in January 2026</strong></a>, the company priced it at <strong>$22/TH</strong>, or about <strong>₿0.22684 ($19,030)</strong> for an 865 TH/s unit. At WDMS 2026, Bitmain later announced a limited-time <strong>50% promotional price of $11/TH</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitmain said that promotion ended September 27, 2026. The current official store page still displays the <strong>$11/TH, $9,515-per-unit</strong> figure, but the batch is marked sold out. That means the visible price should be treated as the manufacturer’s listed futures price for that allocation, not proof that a buyer can still place a new order today.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Efficiency Is Good, but Density Is the Bigger Story</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At <strong>10 J/TH</strong>, the S23e U2H is not Bitmain’s efficiency leader. The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/bitmains-8-9-j-th-s23-xp-hydro-nears-november-shipments/"><strong>S23 XP Hydro reaches 8.9 J/TH</strong></a> at 600 TH/s. The tradeoff is density: the U2H pushes much more hashrate through a rack-native 2U chassis while drawing 8.65 kW per server.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is why <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/bitcoin-mining-hardware-nameplate-wall-facility-joules-per-terahash/"><strong>wall efficiency and facility efficiency should not be treated as the same number</strong></a>. A dense hydro rack still needs pumps, heat rejection, manifolds, power distribution, and network infrastructure. The ASIC’s 10 J/TH nameplate tells only part of the facility story.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">The Hydro Loop Is Not Optional Infrastructure</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The S23e U2H requires three-phase <strong>380–415 V</strong> input power, a coolant inlet between <strong>20°C and 50°C</strong>, roughly <strong>16–20 liters per minute</strong> of coolant flow, and coolant pressure of no more than <strong>3.5 bar</strong>. Bitmain supports antifreeze, pure water, or deionized water within specified pH ranges.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Those numbers make this an infrastructure purchase as much as a miner purchase. Operators need the electrical distribution, rack plumbing, coolant chemistry, leak control, heat rejection, and maintenance procedures to support a high-density liquid loop before the ASIC ever starts hashing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Bitmain Is Selling the Miner as Part of a System</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitmain has paired the S23e U2H with its ANTSPACE hydro containers, ANTRACK rack systems, and more recently <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/bitmain-ant-cold-storage-8gj-water-scarce-bitcoin-mining/"><strong>ANT COLD STORAGE thermal infrastructure</strong></a>. The strategy is clear: Bitmain increasingly wants to sell miners together with the physical system required to power and cool them.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That mirrors the wider direction of industrial Bitcoin mining. As ASIC power density rises, the competitive unit is becoming less like a standalone miner and more like a tightly integrated rack, coolant loop, power train, network, and control system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">November Is the Next Proof Point</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitmain’s store lists shipping from <strong>November 2026</strong>, while its promotional materials described a broader <strong>November 2026–January 2027</strong> delivery window. Those are manufacturer delivery targets, not evidence that production units have already reached operating sites.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next meaningful data will come from field deployments: accepted hashrate, wall power, coolant temperature delta, pump requirements, failure rates, uptime, and whether operators can maintain the advertised density without giving back too much efficiency at the facility level.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">The Takeaway</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The S23e U2H shows where large-scale Bitcoin ASIC design is going: <strong>rack-native, liquid-cooled, extremely dense, and increasingly sold as part of a complete infrastructure stack</strong>. Its 10 J/TH efficiency is strong, but the more important engineering story is 865 TH/s inside a 2U chassis.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For miners, that creates a new optimization problem. The winning machine is not automatically the ASIC with the lowest J/TH. It may be the system that delivers the best combination of silicon efficiency, rack density, cooling overhead, electrical utilization, maintainability, and acquisition cost.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
