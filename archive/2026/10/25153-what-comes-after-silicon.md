<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Two years after researchers demonstrated a functional graphene semiconductor, the answer to “what replaces silicon?” looks less like a single material and more like a <strong>hybrid architecture</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus.Tech first covered the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/01/28/smarter-together-u-s-china-breakthrough-in-graphene-semiconductor-technology/">U.S.-China graphene semiconductor breakthrough</a> in January 2024. Graphene is still in the race. But IBM’s new 7Å NanoStack work, commercial progress in photonic interconnects and rapid advances in other two-dimensional materials now suggest the future chip will combine several technologies rather than crown one universal replacement for silicon.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">IBM’s 7Å NanoStack changes the question</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IBM Research unveiled its first <a href="https://research.ibm.com/blog/sub-1nm-node-chips">0.7 nm, or 7-angstrom, NanoStack technology</a> in June 2026. NanoStack places nanosheet transistors vertically instead of relying only on shrinking them across a flat surface. IBM projects roughly <strong>50% higher performance at the same power or about 70% lower power at the same performance</strong> versus its 2 nm reference, while nearly doubling transistor density.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The “7Å” label should not be read as one universal 0.7-nm physical dimension inside the finished chip. Modern process-node names describe a technology generation. More important than the label is the architecture: IBM is moving transistor scaling into the <strong>Z axis</strong>, and says individual stacked layers can eventually use different channel materials.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The follow-through is now visible. On <strong>October 13, 2026</strong>, IBM announced an early research-and-development process design kit for 7Å NanoStack planned for the first quarter of 2027. On <strong>October 14</strong>, IBM and Siemens announced joint work on the design, simulation, lithography and manufacturing tools needed to push NanoStack toward future production. That does not make 7Å a shipping mass-market node yet, but it moves the architecture from a device demonstration toward an ecosystem that chip designers and manufacturing-tool companies can begin preparing for.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Graphene is still an option</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Graphene’s historical weakness was its lack of a useful intrinsic bandgap. The 2024 Georgia Tech-led work changed that discussion by demonstrating semiconducting epigraphene with a <strong>0.6 eV bandgap</strong> and room-temperature mobility above 5,000 cm²/V·s. That is why graphene should not be written off simply because silicon has continued scaling.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/ChinaScience/status/1743193114851074529]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>China Science highlighted the Georgia Tech/Tianjin University graphene-semiconductor result when the Nature paper was published in 2024.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But graphene now looks more likely to become <strong>one material inside a heterogeneous device</strong> than the sole replacement for silicon CMOS. A 2026 wafer-scale transistor demonstration even used an atomically thin graphene edge as the gate for a vertical MoS₂ device—showing how graphene and another 2D semiconductor can work together instead of competing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">MoS₂ may be the stronger transistor-channel candidate</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the transistor channel itself, molybdenum disulfide and related transition-metal dichalcogenides may currently have the stronger scaling case. A September 2026 <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77186-9">Nature Communications study</a> demonstrated monolayer MoS₂ transistors with physical channel lengths below 5 nm, on/off ratios above 10⁶ and a CMOS-compatible process across a 4-inch wafer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That fits with another trend we have been following in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/coal-nanodots-help-build-2d-transistors/">2D transistor fabrication</a>: silicon does not have to disappear for atomically thin materials to become strategically important. They can be inserted into selected transistor layers where electrostatics or channel thickness become limiting.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Photonics is likely to arrive first around the transistor</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Photonics is further along commercially, but mainly as the <strong>communication fabric between chips</strong>. AI and high-performance computing systems increasingly lose power and performance moving data between processors, memory and switches. That makes optical links an immediate engineering target even while the processors themselves remain electronic.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is consistent with our recent coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/georgia-tech-shrinks-silicon-photonic-bends/">Georgia Tech’s silicon-photonic scaling work</a>. Photonics does not need to replace the transistor to become essential. It can replace increasingly expensive electrical links around the compute die first.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The most likely architecture</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:table -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Technology</th><th>Most likely role</th><th>Near-term outlook</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>3D silicon CMOS / NanoStack</td><td>Core logic</td><td>Highest</td></tr><tr><td>Silicon photonics / CPO</td><td>Chip-to-chip and rack-scale communication</td><td>Already entering systems</td></tr><tr><td>MoS₂ and other 2D semiconductors</td><td>Future ultra-thin transistor channels</td><td>Strong research trajectory</td></tr><tr><td>Graphene</td><td>Specialized channels, gates, interconnects, RF and heterogeneous layers</td><td>Still viable, but unlikely to replace everything</td></tr><tr><td>Fully photonic logic</td><td>Specialized accelerators</td><td>Longer-term</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most probable post-silicon chip is therefore not really “post-silicon.” It is a <strong>3D heterogeneous semiconductor</strong>: vertically stacked CMOS logic, specialized 2D-material layers where they outperform silicon, advanced memory and packaging, and optical links carrying data between dense compute blocks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IBM’s NanoStack strengthens that forecast because it makes material diversity part of the architecture itself. Graphene remains relevant. Photonics may become unavoidable. MoS₂ may win some future transistor layers. But the architecture most likely to dominate is the one that combines them.</p>
