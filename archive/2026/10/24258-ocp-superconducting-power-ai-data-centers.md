<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Open Compute Project is evaluating whether <strong>high-temperature superconductors</strong> could become part of future AI data-center power systems as rack and facility loads move into the multi-megawatt range.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>OCP’s <a href="https://www.opencompute.org/projects/future-technologies-initiative/high-temperature-superconducting-power-infrastructure/"><strong>High-Temperature Superconducting Power Infrastructure workstream</strong></a> is holding an October 13 workshop at the 2026 OCP Global Summit. It is studying grid-to-rack integration, cryogenics, redundancy, fault behavior, protection, fire safety, standards, and how HTS compares with copper and aluminum.</p>

OCP’s <a href="https://www.opencompute.org/events/future-tech-symposium/ocp-future-technologies-symposium/past-symposium-events/"><strong>symposium archive</strong></a> also lists a sustainability presentation on reducing material intensity and lifecycle emissions with superconducting distribution.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This work sits beside OCP’s broader push toward denser electrical infrastructure. BitcoinVersus.Tech has recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/ocp-ai-racks-past-1mw-800vdc-1500vdc/">OCP’s path beyond 1 MW racks</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/rittal-bosch-800v-power-ai-racks/">Rittal and Bosch’s 800 V AI-rack work</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/delta-800-vdc-3mw-cooling-ocp-2026/">Delta’s 800 VDC and 3 MW cooling systems</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The key point is that OCP is still evaluating where HTS makes technical and economic sense. This is not evidence that superconducting distribution is ready to replace conventional data-center power systems today. The workstream’s stated goal is to identify viable use cases, candidate architectures and a path toward validation and standardization.</p>
