Franklin Templeton's October 22 SEC prospectus filing identifies two new exchange-traded funds designed to combine U.S. stocks with Bitcoin exposure purchased through reinvested corporate dividends. The Franklin US Equity Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF (FBEQ) and Franklin US Innovation Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF (FIBT) list October 27, 2026 as their registration effective date, but that is not proof that trading has begun.

Their design starts with approximately 95% U.S. equities and 5% Bitcoin exposure. Instead of reinvesting stock dividends back into shares, the underlying indexes direct regular and special dividend proceeds toward Bitcoin exposure. The arrangement would let a traditional stock portfolio gradually accumulate a secondary Bitcoin allocation, subject to strict index rebalancing rules.

Two ETFs, two equity benchmarks

FBEQ follows the VettaFi US Large-Cap 500 Bitcoin DRIP Index, reflecting major U.S. companies, with a 0.09% annual expense ratio. FIBT tracks the VettaFi US Innovation 100 Bitcoin DRIP Index, built around leading Nasdaq-listed innovators, and lists 0.19% annual expenses. Both are index-tracking products, not direct ownership of coins in investors' personal wallets.

The prospectuses specify a 5% Bitcoin ceiling at quarterly rebalances: weights above 5% reset to 4.5%. A separate 20% threshold triggers another reset between quarters. To reproduce their target exposure, the funds may use Bitcoin-backed ETPs, derivatives or depositary receipts. Thus dividend reinvestment does not necessarily entail buying spot Bitcoin directly for every distribution.

Registration is not a first trading day

The related Form 8-A registers the shares for Nasdaq, while the prospectus expressly states the SEC has neither approved nor disapproved the investments. No independently confirmed first trading date or live volume was established in the records reviewed. Market risk, tracking differences, trading spreads and fees remain relevant.

The latest structures extend Franklin's earlier crypto index ETF initiative. They could connect traditional equity dividends with Bitcoin demand over time, but the size of any resulting purchases will depend on actual fund assets, dividends, implementation and investor uptake.
