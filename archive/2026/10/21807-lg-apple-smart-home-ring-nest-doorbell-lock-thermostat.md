<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Apple’s long-delayed smart-home push may be much bigger than a new hub.</strong> Bloomberg reports that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/lg-builds-its-first-u-s-chiller-factory-for-ai-data-centers/">LG Electronics</a> has worked with Apple on a full accessory lineup that includes a video doorbell, smart deadbolt lock, thermostat, indoor and outdoor security cameras, a floodlight camera and a temperature sensor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The goal is straightforward: give Apple a deeper hardware stack for the home without forcing it to manufacture and support every accessory itself. If the reported lineup ships as planned, Apple would finally have a more direct ecosystem answer to Amazon’s Ring devices and Google’s Nest hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Bloomberg Says Apple and LG Co-Developed the Lineup</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/apple-s-smart-home-push-includes-doorbell-lock-thermostat-codeveloped-with-lg">Bloomberg reports</a> that Apple and LG co-developed the accessories, but the devices are expected to be built, branded and supported by LG. That is an important distinction: this appears to be an ecosystem partnership, not a conventional Apple-branded accessory launch.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The reported product list includes a wired video doorbell, thermostat, smart deadbolt lock, temperature sensor, indoor security camera, outdoor security camera and floodlight camera. Bloomberg says the products are designed to work with Apple’s upcoming smart-home hub and broader home ecosystem.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The FCC Filing Confirms at Least One LG Device Is Real</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The strongest public regulatory evidence so far is the lock. An <a href="https://fccid.io/2BO3LTMCDD15">FCC equipment-authorization record</a> dated September 23 lists an <strong>“LG Smart Deadbolt Lock”</strong> from LG Electronics under FCC ID 2BO3L-TMCDD15.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The filing shows radios operating around 8 GHz for ultra-wideband, 13.56 MHz for near-field communication and the 2.4 GHz band. That combination fits a modern smart-lock design built for close-range credentials, phone-based access and automatic proximity features. Apple has already been expanding access to the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/09/25/apple-opens-iphone-nfc-chip-to-third-parties-paving-way-for-bitcoin-payments/">iPhone’s NFC hardware</a>, making digital-key-style integrations a natural extension of the platform.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Just as important, the FCC record names LG — not Apple. That supports Bloomberg’s description of a second-party arrangement in which Apple can shape the ecosystem while LG handles the visible product brand, manufacturing and support.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Matter and Thread Could Be the Glue</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The smart-home market has historically been fragmented by incompatible apps, hubs and wireless standards. Matter and Thread are intended to reduce that friction by giving devices a more common interoperability layer and low-power mesh networking path.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters for Apple because the company does not need every accessory to carry an Apple logo if the devices can still feel native inside the Home app, Siri and the new hub. A well-integrated LG doorbell, lock or thermostat could behave like an Apple accessory even while remaining an LG product at retail.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Apple’s October 13 Event Is the First Big Test</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bloomberg previously reported that Apple plans to introduce its new smart-home hub on <strong>October 13</strong>, alongside the first major HomePod mini update since 2020 and a new Apple TV set-top box. The LG accessories are reportedly still at least a few months away.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That sequencing makes sense. Apple can first establish the hub, Siri and TV layer, then widen the ecosystem with third-party hardware designed closely enough with Apple to avoid the rough edges that often come with generic smart-home accessories.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Ring and Nest Should Pay Attention</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Ring and Nest have spent years building recognizable hardware families around security cameras, doorbells, thermostats and connected-home software. Apple has mostly approached the category from the platform side through HomeKit, Home, Siri, HomePod and Apple TV.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The LG partnership could change that balance. Instead of waiting for hundreds of accessory makers to define the experience, Apple can work closely with a major electronics manufacturer on a coordinated family of devices that all arrive ready for the same hub and software layer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For LG, the arrangement also expands the company beyond appliances and displays into a more complete connected-home stack. BitcoinVersus.Tech has already been following LG’s expansion into other infrastructure markets, including its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/25/lg-2-6mw-cdu-ai-data-center-cooling/">2.6 MW liquid-cooling CDU</a> and new U.S. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/lg-builds-its-first-u-s-chiller-factory-for-ai-data-centers/">AI-data-center chiller factory</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Bigger Strategy</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The interesting part is not any single doorbell or thermostat. It is the possibility that Apple is finally treating the home as a coordinated hardware platform instead of a collection of compatible accessories.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Apple can combine a new hub, updated HomePod mini, Apple TV, Siri intelligence and tightly integrated LG accessories, the company could enter the smart-home hardware fight with an ecosystem that looks much more complete overnight. The first confirmation should come with Apple’s October 13 launch; the LG hardware will show whether that strategy can extend beyond Apple’s own products.</p>
