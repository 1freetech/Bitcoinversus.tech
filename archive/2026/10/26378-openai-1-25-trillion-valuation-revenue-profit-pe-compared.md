<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>October 25, 2026.</strong> OpenAI is reportedly seeking fresh investment at a <strong>$1.25 trillion valuation</strong>, down from a $1.4 trillion figure discussed earlier in October. But is that worth defensible using the same earnings, revenue and cash-flow measures investors apply to public technology companies? The short answer: <strong>not on current profits alone.</strong> The price is a bet that future revenue and margins grow dramatically. <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/openai-shifts-value-to-1-25-trillion-as-funding-talks-finalize">Bloomberg’s October 22 report</a> says the roughly $30 billion round was still being negotiated; the $1.25 trillion figure is <strong>not a completed IPO market capitalization</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>The proposed $1.25T price is about <strong>25×</strong> OpenAI’s reported <strong>$50B annualized September sales run rate</strong>, not 25× audited full-year revenue.</li><li>OpenAI has not publicly supplied a verified positive net-income figure from which to calculate a traditional P/E.</li><li>At <strong>30× earnings</strong>, $1.25T would require about <strong>$41.7B of annual net profit</strong>.</li><li>The strongest bull case is rapid enterprise adoption and software revenue; the biggest uncertainty is whether those sales generate durable free cash flow after compute spending.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What The $1.25 Trillion Figure Actually Means</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>OpenAI <a href="https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/">announced on March 31</a> that it closed a $122 billion funding round at an <strong>$852 billion post-money valuation</strong>. The new $1.25 trillion figure is a <strong>reported fundraising target</strong>, described by <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-working-on-new-30b-raise-at-1-25t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/">TechCrunch as pre-money</a>. A completed round and a negotiated price are different forms of evidence; the eventual deal could change.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company reported roughly <strong>$2 billion a month</strong> in revenue in March. Later, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/openais-annualized-revenue-20-billion-less-than-previously-signaled-ft-reports-2026-10-08/">Reuters reported on October 8</a> that September's annualized run rate was near <strong>$50 billion</strong>. That reconciled figure was below earlier descriptions approaching $70 billion, partly because cloud-partner revenue was treated differently. Annualizing a recent month is not the same as recording $50 billion in a completed fiscal year. The site previously covered the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/finance-uae-funds-10-billion-openai-30-billion-round/">earlier UAE funding talks</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

Investors can calculate earnings multiples from public statements; for OpenAI, they cannot independently do the same yet.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The P/E Test: How Much Profit Would Be Needed?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A traditional P/E ratio divides equity value by annual net earnings. <strong>It is not meaningful when net profit is absent or not verified.</strong> We can, however, reverse the formula to find the profit a $1.25T valuation implies.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:table --><figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Assumed P/E</th><th>Required yearly net profit</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>20×</td><td>$62.5B</td></tr><tr><td>25×</td><td>$50.0B</td></tr><tr><td>30×</td><td>$41.7B</td></tr><tr><td>40×</td><td>$31.3B</td></tr></tbody></table><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Illustrative calculations only: $1.25 trillion divided by assumed P/E. These are not earnings forecasts.</figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>At a 30× earnings multiple and a hypothetical <strong>25% net margin</strong>, OpenAI would need about <strong>$167 billion in annual sales</strong>, more than three times September’s annualized pace. At a 20% margin the revenue requirement climbs to about <strong>$208 billion</strong>. Moreover, <a href="https://sacra.com/c/openai/">Sacra estimates</a> OpenAI’s recent gross margin around 33%; a hypothetical $50B sales base at that margin generates just $16.5B of gross profit <em>before</em> research, staff, sales and other operating costs. These are scenario calculations, not a forecast of future margins.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Revenue Multiple Scenarios</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Holding the <strong>$50B run rate</strong> constant, a 10× sales valuation would be <strong>$500B</strong>; 15× would be <strong>$750B</strong>; 20× would be <strong>$1T</strong>; and 25× would be <strong>$1.25T</strong>. Even applying NVIDIA’s approximate 16.8× sales multiple produces about <strong>$840B</strong> on that revenue base. But NVIDIA’s profitability and business model are different, so this is a comparison rather than an intrinsic appraisal. On the other hand, <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/6011d061-eee3-4193-b3b7-8ee4155f538c">reporting on an internal projection</a> put OpenAI’s full-calendar-2026 revenue at roughly $36B, which would make the proposed value around <strong>34.7× projected 2026 sales</strong>, not 25×.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Could Justify The Premium?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>OpenAI’s <a href="https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/">March operating update</a> reported more than 900 million weekly ChatGPT users, over 50 million subscribers and enterprise activity generating more than 40% of revenue. A large consumer distribution channel, growing paid coding and business products, and stronger revenue per unit of compute could eventually produce valuable recurring cash flow. Those are tangible commercial advantages; valuing them at $1.25T still requires assumptions about retention, future pricing and margins. For wider AI-sector context, see our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/anthropic-revenue-12x-compute-costs-7-3-billion-ai-profitability/">Anthropic revenue and compute-cost analysis</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Could Undermine It?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Inference, model training and electricity-intensive data centers remain expensive. <a href="https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/openai-expects-to-burn-through-almost-280-billion-by-2030-ft-reports-4907970">Reuters summarized a Financial Times report</a> describing a projection of roughly <strong>$278B in cumulative negative free cash flow across 2026–2030</strong>. That is a reported investor-document forecast, <strong>not an audited fact</strong>, and <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/openai-said-forecast-nearly-280-billion-cash-burn-end-2030">The Information noted a company finance employee disputed the figures</a>. Funding needs, competition, capital intensity, dilution and the future accounting treatment of cloud revenue all matter. Our earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/03/openai-to-remain-unprofitable-until-2029/">coverage of OpenAI profitability forecasts</a> provides historical context; our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/imf-ai-market-correction-global-financial-stability/">IMF AI-market-correction report</a> examines broader valuation risk.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Verdict</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Under traditional metrics, $1.25T is not presently demonstrated by published earnings or free cash flow.</strong> A 25× annualized sales multiple may be defensible to an investor who expects exceptional, lasting growth and much better margins; it is difficult to defend as a conservative present-day valuation. The more useful question is not whether artificial intelligence matters, but how fast OpenAI can turn usage into <em>repeatable profits after its computing costs</em>. A future audited public filing could materially change this assessment.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
