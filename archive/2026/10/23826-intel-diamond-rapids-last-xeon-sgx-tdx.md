<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Intel says its next-generation Xeon platform, code-named Diamond Rapids, will be the last Xeon to include Software Guard Extensions, or SGX.</strong> The company said on October 9 that its future confidential-computing roadmap will focus on <a href="https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/opinion/confidential-computing-growing-momentum-sharpened-focus.html">Intel TDX</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>SGX isolates selected application code and data inside protected enclaves. TDX instead protects an entire virtual machine. Intel says the VM-based approach has gained broader cloud and enterprise adoption because it is easier to scale and deploy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The shift is not an immediate SGX shutdown. Intel says it will continue support and security updates for SGX-enabled platforms into the early 2030s. <a href="https://www.igorslab.de/en/intel-ends-sgx-with-diamond-rapids-future-xeon-processors-use-tdx-instead-of-protected-enclaves/">Igor’sLAB</a> notes that SGX and TDX protect different security boundaries, so existing enclave-based software may require architectural changes rather than a simple switch.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The move lands as hardware isolation becomes more important for AI systems. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/microsoft-sandboxes-ai-agents-windows-11/">Microsoft sandboxing AI agents on Windows 11</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/28/nvidia-adds-a-hardware-watchdog-for-autonomous-ai-agents/">NVIDIA adding a hardware watchdog for autonomous agents</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/anthropic-cuts-internet-access-internal-ai-evals-claude-unintended-actions/">Anthropic restricting internet access during internal AI evaluations</a>.</p>
