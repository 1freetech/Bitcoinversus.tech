<p><a href="https://research.google/blog/research-2026-10-ways-were-amplifying-human-ingenuity/">Google Research says</a> it will reveal a new low-power Edge AI chip next week, co-designed with Synaptics and built using Google’s open-source AI hardware IP.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The chip is aimed at devices where power and thermal limits matter: smart glasses, watches, connected cameras, streaming hardware and industrial vision or voice systems. Google says the new design will improve efficiency and performance, but it has not yet published the chip’s full name, process node, TOPS rating, memory configuration or power envelope.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The partnership already has shipping development hardware. The <a href="https://developer.synaptics.com/docs/developer-kits/coralboard">Synaptics Coralboard</a> uses the Astra SL2619 processor and exposes Google Research’s Coral NPU through an open-source MLIR/IREE software stack. That gives developers a path to prototype on-device AI before moving toward production silicon.</p>
