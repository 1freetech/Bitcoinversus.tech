<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A peer-reviewed <em>BMC Urology</em> study published October 10, 2026, found that two early-career urologists outperformed GPT-5.2 Instant, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 3 Pro Preview on the same 100 synthetic urological-cancer cases.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Humans led the comparison</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12894-026-02399-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">open-access study</a> reported primary composite success of <strong>89% and 85%</strong> for the two urologists, compared with <strong>70% for GPT-5.2, 64% for Claude and 60% for Gemini</strong>. The cases covered five urological cancers and five decision categories, and all five answer sources were scored against the same 2025 European Association of Urology-based framework.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The safety gap was larger</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Safety-critical errors appeared in 25% of GPT-5.2 responses, 25% of Claude responses and 26% of Gemini responses, versus 2% and 1% for the two doctors. Exact three-run stability among the LLMs was also limited: only 28.3% of tested vignette-model combinations reproduced the same full four-domain score profile across all three runs.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The result does not establish that doctors will always outperform AI. The study used synthetic vignettes, only two early-career clinician comparators and no external resources. It also did not test real patient outcomes. The authors reported no competing interests and no financial support.</p>
