<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google Cloud is previewing a new security service designed to help banks safeguard and transfer cryptocurrencies and tokenized assets. Unveiled around its October 28 digital-assets symposium in New York, <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/multi-party-compute/overview">Multi-party Compute (MPC)</a> distributes cryptographic signing authority across separate parties rather than relying on one machine holding a complete private key. The service remains in <strong>private preview</strong>, with a commercial release targeted for <strong>early 2027</strong>, according to <a href="https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/28/google-to-preview-crypto-custody-security-system-as-ai-threats-grow">CoinDesk’s report</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google enters an institutional custody market involving <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/18/deutsche-bank-nears-bitcoin-ether-custody-launch-2026/">Deutsche Bank</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/ripple-meritz-crypto-custody-korea/">Meritz Securities</a>. It builds on cloud-security infrastructure rather than introducing another blockchain.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2>How Google’s Crypto Security Works</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google’s <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/multi-party-compute">official documentation</a> describes threshold signing: independent participants retain separate key shares and collectively produce a valid blockchain signature without assembling one complete private key. Institutions can distribute those shares between Google Cloud, their own infrastructure and other authorized environments.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A transaction can also be evaluated against approval requirements, spending limits and permitted destinations. One important qualification appears in the <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/multi-party-compute/overview">product overview</a>: an automated policy verdict guides authorized signers, but a transaction flagged by the policy may still proceed if the required signing participants approve it. MPC reduces particular risks; it does not eliminate fraud, compromised approvals or operational mistakes.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Bitcoin Support and the 2027 Rollout</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google’s <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/multi-party-compute/bitcoin-custody">Bitcoin custody guidance</a> discusses threshold ECDSA signing, key-share isolation and compatibility considerations for Bitcoin addresses. Its <a href="https://docs.cloud.google.com/multi-party-compute/release-notes">October release notes</a> describe additional Bitcoin transaction-policy checks, including destination classification and change-output detection. The overview lists Solana-oriented Ed25519 functionality as <strong>coming soon</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The product is not Google’s first MPC work. Its <a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/how-to-secure-digital-assets-with-multi-party-computation-and-confidential-space">2023 reference architecture</a> already explained distributed digital-asset signing in Confidential Space. The difference now is a dedicated managed service for institutions, rather than a design that customers assemble themselves.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google has not named the banks testing the service or announced pricing. CoinDesk reported that parent company Alphabet has begun applying the technology to digital assets on Ethereum and Solana, without identifying those holdings. For investors and institutions following <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/drw-marex-otc-swap-canton/">tokenized financial markets</a>, the next milestones are confirmed customer deployments, supported networks and whether the planned 2027 launch arrives on schedule.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
