<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wandercraft has acquired Ekso Bionics, bringing two of the best-known exoskeleton robotics companies under one organization and creating a broader platform spanning clinical rehabilitation, personal mobility and physical AI.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://www.wandercraft.eu/articles/wandercraft-acquires-ekso-bionics">October 1 announcement</a>, Wandercraft said the deal combines its self-balancing robotics and physical-AI platform with Ekso Bionics’ U.S. clinical footprint and commercial reach. Financial terms were not disclosed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The combined portfolio now spans hospital rehab and home mobility</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wandercraft says Atalante X and EksoNR are now used across more than 700 rehabilitation centers worldwide. The combined company also plans to continue supporting Wandercraft’s Eve personal exoskeleton and Ekso’s Indego Personal system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The important part is not just scale. The acquisition joins two different design philosophies: Wandercraft has focused heavily on self-balancing gait and physical AI, while Ekso built one of the longest-running clinical exoskeleton businesses in the United States.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://dealroom.co/news/158445-wandercraft-acquires-ekso-bionics-to-build-global-exoskeleton-leader/">Dealroom’s independent coverage</a> confirms that Wandercraft acquired Ekso Bionics from a ChronoScale subsidiary and that the transaction is aimed at building an end-to-end robotic-mobility portfolio from rehabilitation through personal use.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Exoskeletons are becoming a physical-AI platform</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Modern exoskeletons are not just powered braces. They combine sensors, motors, balance control, gait estimation, embedded computing and increasingly adaptive software that must react to a person’s movement in real time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus has covered that progression before through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/03/revolutionary-self-installing-exoskeleton-kaists-walkon-suit-f1/">KAIST’s WalkOn Suit F1 self-installing exoskeleton</a>, which showed how mobility robotics is moving toward systems that can do more of the setup and balancing work themselves.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The same trend is attracting broader capital into physical AI. BitcoinVersus recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/misumi-50-million-robotics-physical-ai-fund/">MISUMI’s robotics and physical-AI fund</a>, another sign that investors increasingly view intelligent machines as a hardware-software platform rather than a single product category.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Ekso brings two decades of wearable-robot experience</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Ekso Bionics traces its wearable-robot work back roughly two decades and has built systems for clinical rehabilitation, personal mobility and industrial support. Its current medical portfolio includes EksoNR and Indego Personal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The following official Ekso Bionics video is not about the acquisition itself, but it shows the company’s core technology category: wearable robotic systems designed to assist standing, walking and mobility.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The acquisition also expands Wandercraft’s U.S. footprint</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wandercraft has historically been strongest in Europe, while Ekso brings a deeper U.S. clinical network and commercial presence. The combination gives Wandercraft a faster route into American rehabilitation markets without having to build that installed base from scratch.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters because hardware distribution, clinician training, service and reimbursement can be just as important as the robot itself. A sophisticated exoskeleton that works in a lab still has to be supported across hospitals, rehabilitation centers and eventually homes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus has also followed the broader evolution of assistive robotics through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/06/26/open-bionics-unveils-3d-printed-marvel-themed-robot-arms/">Open Bionics’ 3D-printed robotic arms</a>, another example of robotic assistance becoming more personalized, manufacturable and accessible.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A bigger exoskeleton company could accelerate the category</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The acquisition does not automatically solve the hard problems in robotic mobility. Battery life, cost, weight, fit, training, reimbursement and long-term reliability still determine how widely these systems can be used.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But combining Wandercraft’s self-balancing physical-AI stack with Ekso’s clinical footprint gives the merged organization a larger base of users, therapists, field data and engineering experience. That could shorten the loop between rehabilitation robotics research and practical everyday deployment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deal also shows where physical AI is heading: away from isolated demonstrations and toward companies that own the full stack from sensing and control software to hardware manufacturing, service and real-world deployment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
