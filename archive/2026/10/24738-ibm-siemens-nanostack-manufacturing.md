<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>IBM and Siemens are teaming up to push IBM’s 3D nanostack transistor architecture closer to practical manufacturing.</strong> The companies announced October 14 that Siemens will contribute electronic-design automation, process simulation, lithography optimization and manufacturing software to help IBM model and refine future nanostack generations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IBM’s <a href="https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-10-14-ibm-and-siemens-collaborate-on-research-to-advance-nanostack-architecture"><strong>new collaboration announcement</strong></a> says nanostack vertically stacks and staggers nanosheet transistors rather than relying only on shrinking them laterally. IBM says its current 7-angstrom research node can place nearly 100 billion transistors in a fingernail-sized area.</p>

The new work with Siemens focuses on the harder next step: building virtual models, optimizing lithography and identifying fabrication problems before chips reach a manufacturing line.</p>

<p>The architecture builds on the same electrostatic ideas covered in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/ossec-009-gate-all-around-nanosheet-electrostatics/"><strong>gate-all-around nanosheet electrostatics</strong></a>, but moves further into vertical transistor stacking. It also fits the broader shift toward <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/semiconductors-asm-ai-angstrom-atomic-precision/"><strong>angstrom-scale process control</strong></a> and the race to commercialize <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/semiconductors-rapidus-17-company-2nm-foundry-ecosystem-japan-15-billion/"><strong>next-generation logic manufacturing</strong></a>. IBM has demonstrated the research architecture, but this announcement is not a commercial production launch.</p>
