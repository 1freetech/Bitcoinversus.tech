<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Anthropic launched <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/launching-opt-in-vuln-finding-service-for-open-source">OSS Scanner</a> on October 8, a free opt-in service that periodically scans eligible open-source projects for security vulnerabilities using its strongest models, including Claude Mythos.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Anthropic said its models found more than <strong>29,000 candidate vulnerabilities</strong> over six months, while people manually reviewed about 6,000. Nearly 5,000 unverified reports were also sent to maintainers who specifically asked to receive everything the models found.</p>

<p>That speed comes with a tradeoff. Reports are fully model-generated and arrive without human review. Anthropic says an early validation test found that <strong>85 of 97</strong> high- or critical-severity findings across 48 projects met its coordinated-disclosure bar. <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008521/anthropic-open-source-oss-scanner">The Verge</a> noted the same tension: AI bug hunting can uncover real flaws while creating more reports for already stretched maintainers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The launch fits a broader push toward controlled AI security tooling. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/anthropic-cuts-internet-access-internal-ai-evals-claude-unintended-actions/">Anthropic restricting internet access during internal evaluations</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/microsoft-sandboxes-ai-agents-windows-11/">Microsoft sandboxing AI agents on Windows 11</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/23/palo-alto-networks-launches-continuous-ai-security-testing/">Palo Alto Networks expanding continuous AI security testing</a>.</p>
