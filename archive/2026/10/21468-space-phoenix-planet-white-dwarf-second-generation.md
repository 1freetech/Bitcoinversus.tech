<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Astronomers have identified a strong candidate for the first <strong>second-generation planet</strong> found around a white dwarf—a world apparently formed from material expelled when its host star died.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/science/alien-phoenix-planet-was-born-ashes-dying-star-2026-10-06/"><strong>Reuters reported</strong></a> that the planet candidate orbits the white dwarf <strong>HS 0209+0832</strong> every <strong>4.4 days</strong>. It sits so close to the star that ultraviolet radiation is stripping away part of its atmosphere.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02983-7"><strong>Nature Astronomy study</strong></a> found unusual enrichments of zinc, copper and niobium in the white dwarf’s atmosphere. Those chemical fingerprints suggest the material came from gas expelled during the star’s giant phase rather than from a normal first-generation rocky planet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The discovery adds a new chapter to the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/category/space/"><strong>Space</strong></a> story: planets may form not only around young stars, but also from stellar debris after a star dies. It also gives future observatories another target class to study, alongside missions such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/nasa-prima-far-infrared-probe-explorer-beyond-webb/"><strong>NASA’s PRIMA far-infrared telescope</strong></a> and newer <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/space-nasa-smallsats-propulsion-uv-satellite-inspection/"><strong>UV astronomy missions</strong></a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The result matters because it shows planetary systems may get a second chance. A star can die, shed material, and potentially create an entirely new generation of worlds from its own remains.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
