<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><code>The Important Shift Is Where Payments Happen</code></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The bigger story is not simply that another company can send stablecoins. The important change is that payment execution is being pulled into <a href="https://www.sap.com/products/financial-management/digital-currency-hub.html"><strong>SAP ERP software</strong></a>, where invoices, business partners and accounting records already live. That can reduce the number of separate systems a finance team has to move through between approving an invoice and actually settling it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>SAP’s own digital-currency tooling already describes 24/7 stablecoin settlement, integration with core ERP processes and automated reconciliation. Tereina’s launch pushes the same idea toward a broader payment layer capable of handling both conventional money and digital assets instead of forcing companies to treat stablecoins as a completely separate treasury workflow.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><code>Stablecoins Are Becoming Payment Infrastructure</code></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>BitcoinVersus has been tracking this convergence across the financial stack. Stripe recently made <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-stripe-makes-open-usd-the-default-stablecoin-across-its-payment-stack/"><strong>Open USD the default stablecoin across its payment stack</strong></a>, while Citi and Coinbase have been working to put <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-citi-coinbase-stablecoin-payments-bank-rails/"><strong>stablecoin settlement inside bank rails</strong></a>. UK banks have also tested moving <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/finance-uk-banks-live-sterling-tokenized-deposits/"><strong>live sterling deposits across tokenized rails</strong></a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Tereina fits that same pattern from a different direction. Rather than beginning with a bank, exchange or crypto-native payment processor, it starts from enterprise accounting and treasury software. That matters because the interface used by corporate finance teams can determine whether new settlement technology becomes a specialist experiment or an ordinary button inside an existing workflow.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><code>Why Enterprise Integration Matters</code></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Corporate payments are more than the movement of money. Each transaction has to map back to invoices, counterparties, approval rules, accounting entries, reconciliation and reporting. A payment rail that moves funds quickly but creates manual accounting work can lose much of its practical advantage.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Embedding the payment step inside the same system that already knows the invoice amount, supplier identity and accounting context can make stablecoins look less like a separate crypto product and more like another settlement option. That is the direction enterprise adoption has been moving toward: abstract the blockchain mechanics away from the employee making the payment while preserving the speed and programmability underneath.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><code>Fiat And Stablecoins In The Same Workflow</code></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Tereina’s decision to support traditional currencies alongside stablecoins may be just as important as the blockchain piece. Most large companies are unlikely to replace every existing bank payment at once. A mixed system lets treasury teams choose a rail based on currency, geography, settlement time, counterparty preference and cost without moving into a separate application.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That hybrid approach is likely to be the practical bridge between conventional corporate banking and tokenized money. Stablecoins can compete where instant settlement, 24/7 availability or cross-border friction matters most, while ordinary bank rails remain available for payments where they already work well.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><code>What To Watch Next</code></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The next question is adoption rather than technical possibility. Enterprise finance departments will care about compliance, counterparty controls, accounting treatment, custody, supported currencies, transaction costs and how easily payment records reconcile with existing books.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>If those operational details become routine, the stablecoin story may stop being mainly about crypto exchanges and start being about invisible settlement infrastructure inside ordinary business software. Tereina’s launch is another sign that this transition is already moving from pilot projects toward production finance systems.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
