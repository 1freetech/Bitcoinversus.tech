<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Richard Carmona’s career is difficult to summarize with a single profession.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He began as a U.S. Army Special Forces medic, later became a registered nurse and paramedic, trained as a trauma surgeon, served as a deputy sheriff and SWAT physician, ran a county health system, and eventually became the 17th Surgeon General of the United States.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That puts Carmona directly inside the logic of our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">Top 10 Toughest Jobs in the World in 2026</a>. Trauma surgery alone is one of the highest-consequence medical careers. Carmona added emergency response, law enforcement, public health and executive leadership on top of it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to the <a href="https://publichealth.arizona.edu/directory/richard-carmona">University of Arizona</a>, Carmona completed training in general and vascular surgery plus an NIH-sponsored fellowship in trauma, burns and critical care. He helped establish Arizona’s first regional trauma care system and later served as a professor of surgery, public health and pharmacy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->

<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Richard Carmona discusses leadership and public health in a long-form interview recorded for the C. Everett Koop Awards.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His path into medicine was not conventional. Carmona dropped out of high school, joined the Army in 1967 and earned his GED while serving. After military service, he continued his education and ultimately graduated from medical school at UCSF at the top of his class.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The career kept expanding. He worked as a deputy sheriff and department surgeon, became a hospital and health-system executive, and in 2002 was unanimously confirmed by the U.S. Senate as Surgeon General.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He is still active in public health. In May 2026, Carmona delivered the University of Arizona’s Dalen Lecture on global health security, prevention and preparedness. The university described him as a Laureate Professor drawing on decades of clinical medicine, emergency preparedness and health diplomacy. <a href="https://publichealth.arizona.edu/news/2026/dr-richard-carmona-inspires-audience-find-solutions-global-health-challenges">The 2026 lecture recap</a> shows that this is not simply a historical résumé.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

The common thread is not celebrity. It is repeatedly mastering fields that normally require entire careers on their own.</p>
