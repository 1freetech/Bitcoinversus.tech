<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Brian May built one of the most extraordinary dual careers in modern culture: stadium-filling rock guitarist and working astrophysicist.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before Queen became one of the world’s most successful rock bands, May was already studying physics and astronomy at Imperial College London. His doctoral research focused on zodiacal dust — the cloud of tiny particles spread through the inner solar system. Music eventually took over, and his PhD work was put aside for more than three decades.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then he went back. In 2007, May completed and submitted his doctorate at Imperial College. <a href="https://www.imperial.ac.uk/events/114918/zodiacal-dust-and-stuff/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Imperial College records</a> that his thesis, <em>Radial Velocities in the Zodiacal Dust Cloud</em>, returned to observations he had begun in the early 1970s and examined how dust in the solar system moves around the Sun.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That was not simply an honorary connection to science. May later became directly involved in planetary research. He and stereoscopy specialist Claudia Manzoni joined NASA’s OSIRIS-REx science team and created 3D views of asteroid Bennu from spacecraft imagery.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The work had a practical purpose. <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/bennu-stereo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NASA says</a> May and Manzoni formally joined the OSIRIS-REx team as collaborators to produce stereoscopic data products while scientists were selecting a sample-collection site on Bennu. After the asteroid material returned to Earth in 2023, May also helped create stereo images of the sampling hardware and recovered material.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>May’s résumé belongs beside other Career News profiles including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/punk-rock-singer-phd-molecular-biology-dexter-holland/">Dexter Holland’s combination of punk rock, molecular biology and aviation</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-marine-mechanic-to-doctor-to-astronaut/">Story Musgrave’s path from Marine mechanic to doctor to astronaut</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-five-olympic-golds-to-orthopedic-surgery/">Eric Heiden’s journey from five Olympic gold medals to orthopedic surgery</a>. In May’s case, the remarkable part is that the second career was not a celebrity side project. He returned to unfinished scientific work decades later, earned the doctorate, and then contributed to an active NASA asteroid mission.</p>
