<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Intel is using OCP Global Summit 2026 to push a simple idea: AI systems should stop treating every task like it belongs on the same kind of processor.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/artificial-intelligence/intel-ocp-2026-open-ai-infrastructure-for-ai-era.html">official OCP briefing</a>, Intel says agentic AI infrastructure should route work across CPUs, GPUs and other accelerators, matching each task to the most appropriate compute resource. The goal is lower inference cost, more flexibility and better use of hardware companies already own.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The timing matters because <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/ai-agents-7x-more-tokens-openrouter-cache/">AI agents are consuming far more tokens</a>, while companies are also tightening isolation and security around autonomous workloads. BitcoinVersus recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/microsoft-sandboxes-ai-agents-windows-11/">Microsoft sandboxing AI agents</a> and Intel’s move to make <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/intel-diamond-rapids-last-xeon-sgx-tdx/">Diamond Rapids the last Xeon with SGX</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The broader <a href="https://www.opencompute.org/events/ocp-summit/2026-ocp-global-summit/">OCP summit</a>, running October 12–15 in San Jose, is centered on scaling open infrastructure for AI. Intel’s pitch is not that CPUs replace GPUs, but that heterogeneous systems place each workload where it makes the most sense.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
