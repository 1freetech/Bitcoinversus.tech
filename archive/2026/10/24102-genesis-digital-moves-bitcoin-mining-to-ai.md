<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of Bitcoin mining’s biggest private operators is becoming an AI-infrastructure company. <a href="https://swi.com/swi-group-announces-e800-million-capital-increase/">SWI Group said October 12</a> that it raised <strong>€800 million</strong> from institutional investors at a <strong>€6.1 billion valuation</strong>, bringing total equity raised since March above €1 billion.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The money will fund power infrastructure, data-center buildouts and AI compute in Europe and the United States. SWI already controls more than 70% of <strong>Genesis Digital Assets</strong>, now being renamed SWI Digital. In July, SWI said it would reposition GDA’s assets from digital mining to <strong>high-performance computing and AI workloads</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Confirmed:</strong> SWI says the former mining platform contributes roughly <strong>1.3 GW</strong> of data-center capacity. Combined with SWI’s European AiOnX platform, the group says it controls more than <strong>3.6 GW</strong> of AI-ready infrastructure. SWI also became an NVIDIA Cloud Partner in August.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p><strong>Reported:</strong> The Wall Street Journal says SWI CEO Max-Hervé George has no interest in continuing Bitcoin mining and plans to sell GDA’s remaining Kazakhstan mining sites. That detail is reporting, not part of today’s financing release.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The engineering logic is straightforward: mining campuses already have scarce assets AI developers need—land, substations, grid connections, cooling and operations teams. This does <strong>not</strong> prove Bitcoin mining is broadly uneconomic; it shows that some owners now believe contracted AI compute can earn more from the same powered real estate.</p>
