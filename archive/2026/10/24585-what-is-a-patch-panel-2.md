<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A patch panel is a passive piece of network hardware that gives permanent Ethernet or fiber cabling a clean, labeled termination point inside a rack or wiring closet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Instead of running every building cable directly into a network switch, technicians terminate those fixed cable runs at the back of a patch panel. Short patch cords then connect the front of the panel to switch ports. <a href="https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/definition/patch-panel">TechTarget</a> describes the panel as a mounted assembly used to organize and manage incoming and outgoing LAN cabling.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[tweet https://twitter.com/eastdakota/status/1250479501726760960]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>A 2020 Cloudflare incident became a memorable example of why physical cabling discipline matters: maintenance work disconnected critical data-center connections, forcing a failover and recovery effort.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The key point is that a patch panel does <strong>not</strong> route packets, assign IP addresses, or make switching decisions. The switch is the active device. The patch panel simply makes the physical cabling easier to label, trace, test, move and replace. Cisco’s <a href="https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/en/us/products/collateral/optical-networking/ons-15200-series-dwdm-systems/extended-bandwidth-unit.pdf">optical patch-panel documentation</a> also highlights practical benefits such as cable routing, bend-radius control and easier troubleshooting.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->
[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FfQui0mk85Q]
<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>PowerCert Animated Videos explains the basic purpose of a network patch panel and how it keeps rack cabling organized.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In a typical rack, the path looks like this: wall jack or device → permanent cable → patch panel → short patch cord → switch. That layout protects the fixed cabling from constant handling and makes moves, adds and changes much easier.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
