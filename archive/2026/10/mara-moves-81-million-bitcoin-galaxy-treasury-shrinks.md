<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>MARA Holdings moved approximately 996.105 BTC, worth about $81.13 million, to a wallet identified by blockchain analysts as belonging to Galaxy Digital.</strong> The October 8 transfer, reported October 9, comes as the Bitcoin miner uses its reserves to manage debt and invest in AI infrastructure. But there is an important distinction: <strong>the blockchain transfer does not itself prove a sale.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/09/mara-transfers-usd81-1-million-in-bitcoin-to-galaxy-digital-as-strategy-pivots-to-ai">CoinDesk reported</a> the movement following wallet analysis, while <a href="https://ir.mara.com/">MARA’s investor-relations filings</a> document its broader policy shift toward selective Bitcoin monetization.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>What the $81 Million Transfer Means</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The movement was from an address labeled “MARA Miner” to one labeled “Galaxy Digital” by on-chain tracking services. Galaxy provides both trading and custody services. A transfer to a trading firm may precede a sale, but it may also involve collateral, settlement, lending or custody. Neither the sender's intent nor a completed market sale can be inferred from wallet labels alone.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>MARA's Bitcoin Treasury by the Numbers</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>MARA reported <strong>53,822 BTC</strong> at the end of 2025 and <strong>35,577 BTC</strong> at June 30, 2026. The difference is <strong>18,245 BTC</strong>, or a <strong>33.9% decrease</strong> in reported holdings across those dates. This net change is not identical to coins sold, because production, lending, collateral and other movements also affect balances.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The October transfer equals <strong>2.80%</strong> of the June 30 balance (996.105 ÷ 35,577 × 100). This is a comparison to a historical reported balance, <em>not</em> a claim that MARA still held exactly that amount in October.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Why MARA Has Already Sold Bitcoin</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In a <a href="https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_dbb7dc5ed2f6f14efc7baa122446cc6d/marathondh/news/2026-03-26_MARA_Holdings_Inc_Announces_1_0_Billion_1418.pdf">March 26 company announcement</a>, MARA confirmed selling 15,133 BTC for roughly $1.1 billion to help repurchase approximately $1 billion of convertible notes. The transaction was a corporate financing decision, not necessarily a prediction about Bitcoin's future price.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Its first-half 2026 sales totaled 23,093 BTC for approximately $1.6 billion, an implied average realized price near <strong>$69,286 per BTC</strong> using rounded proceeds. Reported more precise figures put the average at approximately $70,631; the discrepancy reflects rounding and reporting scope, so the rounded calculation should not replace the filing-derived metric.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2>Whale Buying Is Not the Opposite of Miner Selling</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Separately, analysts reported accumulation of about 15,000 BTC by larger wallets over three days following the October 7 decline. At an illustrative $82,600 per BTC, that is roughly <strong>$1.239 billion</strong>. The reported 15,000 BTC accumulation is about <strong>15.1 times</strong> MARA's 996.105 BTC transfer by coin count. But these are different wallet cohorts, methodologies and time windows; they should not be netted together as a single market-flow statistic.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
