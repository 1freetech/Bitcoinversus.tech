<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The best <strong>fighting video games</strong> reward timing, creativity, matchup knowledge and the willingness to learn from defeat. This BitcoinVersus.Tech <strong>Top 7 Fighting Video Games of All Time</strong> weighs gameplay, lasting influence, character variety, competitive depth and accessibility. The rankings are editorial judgments, not objective tournament standings. We selected seven different franchises spanning one-on-one arcade fighters, weapon combat, tag-team anime battles and platform fighting.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">7. Guilty Gear -Strive- (2021) — Modern Anime Fighting</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Arc System Works' <a href="https://www.guiltygear.com/ggst/en/movie/">Guilty Gear -Strive-</a> blends striking anime-inspired presentation with Roman Cancels, explosive pressure, distinctive characters and rollback-based online competition. Stylish effects make the action readable while leaving serious room for mastery. It earns a place for showing how a technical fighting series can welcome new players without discarding its identity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><em>Official Guilty Gear -Strive- launch trailer, published by PlayStation.</em></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">6. Dragon Ball FighterZ (2018) — Tag-Team Spectacle</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Arc System Works translated Akira Toriyama's visual world into rapid three-on-three battles with assists, aerial movement and dramatic combo routes. Its animated presentation looks close to the source material while supporting a layered competitive game. The title demonstrates how anime licensing and strong combat design can reinforce each other.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m5mfUiDWYmE]<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><em>Bandai Namco's official Dragon Ball FighterZ Nintendo Switch launch trailer, featuring the same underlying fighting game.</em></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">5. Soulcalibur II (2002 Arcade; 2003 Consoles) — Weapon Combat</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Namco's 3D weapon fighter made attack reach, spacing, eight-way movement and ring-outs central to every round. The console releases also featured platform-specific guest characters, creating a crossover event before modern guest rosters became routine. The later HD Online version preserved much of the original's appeal.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:shortcode -->[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gMtjMvw7fLU]<!-- /wp:shortcode -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><em>GameSpot's Soulcalibur II HD Online launch trailer illustrates the updated re-release of the classic.</em></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">4. Mortal Kombat (2011) — A Competitive Rebirth</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>NetherRealm Studios revived its flagship series with a return to a two-dimensional fighting plane, cinematic story mode, tag battles and its recognizable finishers. Beneath the spectacle, meter management, spacing and matchup knowledge gave competitive players plenty to study. This is the <strong>2011 reboot, frequently called MK9</strong>, rather than the original 1992 title.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">3. Tekken 3 (1997 Arcade; 1998 PlayStation) — The 3D Landmark</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Tekken 3 combined sidesteps, fast animation, distinct martial-arts styles and memorable fighters including Jin, Xiaoyu, Eddy and Hwoarang. Its smooth PlayStation-era combat helped bring 3D fighters into living rooms. The arcade-to-console transition also makes it a useful companion to our coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/09/05/playstation-set-to-offer-1300-retro-games-on-september-6th/">the enduring appeal of retro PlayStation libraries</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">2. Super Smash Bros. Ultimate (2018) — The Crossover Champion</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Nintendo's platform fighter brings together characters and stages from gaming history, with knockback percentages, aerial positioning, recovery and edge-guarding replacing conventional health-bar play. Its enormous roster and flexible local multiplayer make it a different kind of fighting classic. The 2018 reveal's <strong>“Everyone is here”</strong> message captured the game's defining ambition.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">1. Street Fighter II (1991) — The Genre-Defining Classic</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Capcom's <em>Street Fighter II: The World Warrior</em> established a blueprint for head-to-head fighting: distinct characters, special moves, round-based competition, spacing, reversals and combo discovery. Its arcade success helped turn local competition into a lasting culture. Later editions added refinements, and that legacy continues through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/street-fighter-6-arjun-october-13/">our Street Fighter 6 character coverage</a> and modern engine technology such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/03/gaming-capcom-rex-re-engine-ai-generation-game-engine/">Capcom's RE Engine evolution</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why These Seven?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Our list favors titles that changed how players fight, learn and compete. <strong>Street Fighter II</strong> takes the overall position for long-term influence; <strong>Smash Ultimate</strong> is the standout party-and-platform fighter; <strong>Tekken 3</strong> represents the 3D breakthrough; <strong>Dragon Ball FighterZ</strong> stands out for anime adaptation. Honorable mentions include <em>Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike</em>, <em>Virtua Fighter 5</em>, <em>Marvel vs. Capcom 2</em>, <em>Super Smash Bros. Melee</em> and <em>Street Fighter 6</em>.
