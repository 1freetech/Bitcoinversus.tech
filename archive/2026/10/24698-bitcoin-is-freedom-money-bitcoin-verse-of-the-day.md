<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bitcoin Verse of the Day — October 14, 2026</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p>“With regards to the general idea of freedom. The moment we see any sort of counting down on human rights or individual rights either as a country or nation states, what we tend to see is people moving towards adopting bitcoin. And that's not a coincidence… I see it as a direct result of the fact that bitcoin is freedom money.”</p><cite>Abubakar Nur Khalil, Bitcoin Core contributor</cite></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The line comes from the <a href="https://www.podpage.com/citadeldispatch/freedom-money-abubakar-nur-khalil-nigeria/">Freedom Money conversation</a> with Khalil, recorded at the Oslo Freedom Forum. It earns a place in <em>Bitcoin Verses</em> because it compresses a large argument into three words: <strong>freedom money</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitcoin’s appeal is often reduced to price. Khalil’s framing points somewhere deeper: a monetary network that can matter when banking access, currency stability or financial speech becomes constrained. That idea connects naturally with BitcoinVersus coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/10/25/report-by-nydig-claims-bitcoin-is-technically-freedom-of-speech-protected-by-1st-amendment/">Bitcoin and freedom of speech</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/19/global-bitcoin-adoption-machankura-enables-bitcoin-transactions-without-internet/">Bitcoin access across Africa</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/02/07/bitcoin-dada-is-empowering-ugandan-women-with-bitcoin-education/">Bitcoin education in Uganda</a>.</p>
