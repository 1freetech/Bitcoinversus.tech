<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chris Lemons works in a profession most people will never see up close: deep-sea saturation diving.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ranked saturation diving No. 1 in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">Top 10 Toughest Jobs in the World in 2026</a>. Lemons is the kind of public career profile that explains why.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://www.chrislemons.co.uk/chris-lemons">Lemons’ public biography</a>, he has spent more than 20 years in commercial diving and now works as an IMCA diving supervisor. Saturation divers can live inside pressurized chambers for up to 28 days, travel to the seabed in a diving bell and work for hours at depths reaching hundreds of feet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div style="position:relative;padding-bottom:56.25%;height:0;overflow:hidden;max-width:100%">[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u8ksWlbq_J0]</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Lemons explains saturation diving and the accident in a long-form interview.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In September 2012, a dynamic-positioning failure caused the dive-support vessel above him to drift. Lemons’ umbilical—the line supplying breathing gas, heat, light and communications—snagged and severed while he was roughly 300 feet below the North Sea.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He had only minutes of emergency breathing gas. His rescuers took more than 40 minutes to reach him. Lemons was recovered unconscious and revived inside the diving bell.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The story later became the 2019 documentary <em>Last Breath</em> and then a 2025 feature film. <a href="https://time.com/7262779/">TIME’s account</a> explains the real saturation-diving system behind the movie and the extraordinary survival event it dramatized.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What makes Lemons a Career Journalist subject is not only that he survived. He returned to the profession and eventually moved into supervision, carrying experience from one of the most technically unforgiving workplaces on Earth.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His career belongs beside other high-consequence profiles we have documented, including physician-astronaut <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/scott-parazynski-doctor-astronaut-everest/">Scott Parazynski</a> and game designer-turned-explorer <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/richard-garriott-game-designer-space-deepest-ocean/">Richard Garriott</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lemons’ résumé is a reminder that some careers demand more than credentials. They require technical skill, trust, physical endurance and the ability to stay functional when the environment itself can become the emergency.</p>
