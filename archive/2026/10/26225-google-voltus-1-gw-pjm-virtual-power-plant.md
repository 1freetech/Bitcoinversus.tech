<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>October 23, 2026 —</strong> Google and distributed-energy company Voltus announced on October 22 an expanded agreement to assemble a <strong>gigawatt-scale virtual power plant</strong> across the PJM electricity region. Instead of constructing one new generating station, the program will coordinate batteries, smart thermostats and flexible electricity use at participating homes and businesses, paying customers to reduce demand when the grid needs relief.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">From 100 MW to More Than 1 GW</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/virtual-power-plant-1-gigawatt/">Google describes the arrangement</a> as a tenfold expansion from the 100-megawatt agreement first announced in June 2026. <a href="https://www.voltus.co/press/google-voltus-1gw">Voltus states more precisely</a> that the October agreement adds <strong>1,070 MW</strong> to the original 100 MW commitment. Those statements use different descriptions of the portfolio’s total size, so the defensible headline is <strong>gigawatt-scale contracted capacity</strong>, not one precise amount of operational output today.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The PJM Interconnection region serves approximately <strong>67 million people across 13 states and Washington, D.C.</strong> Capacity enrollment, qualification, and dispatch performance still matter: an announced contract is not proof that every promised megawatt can already be called upon.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">How a Virtual Power Plant Works</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Voltus aggregates many smaller distributed energy resources into a coordinated portfolio. During a high-demand period, participating industrial facilities might shift a process, commercial buildings might adjust cooling setpoints, and batteries might discharge or supply local loads. Taken together, those actions can reduce stress on the grid without requiring one centralized plant to deliver all the flexibility.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Google funds the arrangement; Voltus recruits and manages participating resources. The companies say businesses and households can earn bill savings or direct payments. Actual earnings and dispatch terms will depend on the program, location, assets and participation requirements.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Connection to Data Centers and Bitcoin Mining</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Large computing campuses increase demand for reliable grid capacity, but new transmission lines and generating plants can take years. Demand response offers a complementary way to make existing infrastructure work harder during peaks. It does <strong>not</strong> create continuous new electricity generation or independently guarantee that a data center can connect to the grid.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Next Test Is Delivery</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>What remains unresolved is how quickly Voltus can enroll and qualify the portfolio, how reliably the assets will respond to dispatch events, and what measured benefits PJM customers receive. Neither announcement supplies evidence that the full gigawatt-scale capacity is already live or that regional household rates will fall by a specific amount.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Google and Voltus announced an expanded gigawatt-scale virtual power plant in PJM on October 22, 2026.</li><li>Google describes a tenfold increase; Voltus specifies 1,070 MW of additional capacity beyond June’s 100 MW agreement.</li><li>Participating homes and businesses could receive payments for flexible electricity use, subject to enrollment and dispatch conditions.</li><li>The agreement addresses peak grid demand; it is not equivalent to building 1 GW of continuously operating generation.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->
