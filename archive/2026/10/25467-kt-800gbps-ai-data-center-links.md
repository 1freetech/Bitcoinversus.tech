## Final Gutenberg Source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>South Korea’s KT has launched dedicated links for AI infrastructure with bandwidth options up to <strong>800 Gbps</strong>, expanding from the roughly 100 Gbps ceiling of its earlier dedicated-line offerings. <a href="https://kt-corp.tistory.com/74">KT announced the service on October 14, 2026</a> for organizations moving large volumes of data between AI data centers and other computing sites.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The new service adds 400 Gbps and 800 Gbps options and uses KT’s nationwide optical network. The company says it can design shortest-path and redundant routes between sites across roughly <strong>840,000 kilometers of fiber coverage</strong>. That makes the launch a practical extension of the same 800G networking trend behind <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/broadcom-ocp-2026-tomahawk6-102-4t-800g-nic-cpo-ai-networking/">800G NICs and co-packaged optics</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>KT also says the service uses an <strong>All-Photonic Network, or APN</strong>, architecture intended to reduce intermediate optical-to-electrical conversions. <a href="https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/10/14/kt-launches-ai-dedicated-lines-with-speeds-up-to-800gbps">Seoul Economic Daily independently reported</a> the 800 Gbps launch and KT’s emphasis on lower-latency optical paths.</p>

<p>The broader engineering problem is becoming more important as AI clusters spread across multiple buildings and campuses. Higher line rates alone do not remove every bottleneck: optics, switching, error correction, fiber plant and route design still matter. BitcoinVersus.Tech has tracked that progression through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/oif-448g-1600zr-ai-networking-optics/">448G and 1.6T optical standards work</a> and the rising value of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/ai-data-center-cabling-more-valuable-expensive/">data-center fiber and cabling infrastructure</a>.</p>
