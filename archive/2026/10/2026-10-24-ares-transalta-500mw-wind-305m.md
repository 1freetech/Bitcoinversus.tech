<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul><li>Ares Infrastructure funds purchased 49% of TransAlta's operating 500 MW Oklahoma wind portfolio.</li><li>TransAlta received $305 million before customary closing adjustments.</li><li>TransAlta retains majority ownership and operational oversight.</li><li>The portfolio comprises three facilities with long-term contracts.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>On October 22, 2026, <a href="https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/22/3386478/0/en/transalta-advances-portfolio-rotation-through-sale-of-minority-interest-in-oklahoma-wind-portfolio-for-us-305-million-and-closes-acquisition-of-colorado-gas-assets-for-us-1-billion.html">TransAlta confirmed</a> it had completed the sale of a 49% minority interest in its <strong>500 MW Oklahoma wind portfolio</strong> to Ares Infrastructure funds for <strong>$305 million</strong> in cash, before closing adjustments. This is a sale of an ownership stake in existing generation, not construction of an additional 500 MW.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Three Oklahoma Wind Farms</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The portfolio comprises White Rock West (100 MW), White Rock East (200 MW), and Horizon Hill (200 MW). Together, their nameplate capacity totals 500 MW. The 49% ownership interest does not change the turbines' physical capacity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>TransAlta keeps its controlling 51% interest and will oversee operations through a long-term asset-management agreement. The transaction was also documented by <a href="https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/news/view/2026/10/22/transalta-sells-49-minority-interest-in-oklahoma-wind-portfolio-to-ares">the seller's transaction counsel</a>. For background on the difference between power capacity and actual output, see our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-capacity-factor-explained-nameplate-efficiency-reliability/">capacity-factor explainer</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Financial Details and Power Demand</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>TransAlta expects the three assets to produce approximately $60 million in adjusted EBITDA during 2026. At 49%, that represents about $29 million attributable to the minority interest. This is a company forecast, not a confirmed annual result. Long-term contracts support the portfolio, while production tax credits are scheduled to expire in 2034.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company said the proceeds support its balance sheet and future opportunities, including the Centralia conversion and Alberta data-center projects. The deal follows a wider pattern of investments in operating power assets, including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/24/enel-140-million-270mw-us-solar-acquisition-october-2026/">Enel's 270 MW solar acquisition</a>. Our older <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/01/16/bitcoin-mining-finds-a-friend-in-u-s-renewable-energy/">renewable-energy and Bitcoin-mining report</a> provides additional historical context.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Comes Next</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Ares gains a minority position in contracted wind generation while TransAlta retains day-to-day oversight. Investors will watch actual generation, contracted revenues and post-2034 tax-credit economics. For direct documentation, consult the <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144800/000117184326006780/exh_991.htm">SEC-filed company announcement</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
