<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Coinbase says Bitcoin should begin preparing now for a future in which quantum computers could threaten today’s digital signatures, even though the company is not claiming such a machine exists today.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an <a href="https://www.coinbase.com/blog/working-together-on-btc-post-quantum-readiness">October 13 technical note</a>, Coinbase argued that a Bitcoin migration would need to solve more than simply choosing a quantum-resistant signature algorithm. Any upgrade would also have to fit Bitcoin’s limited block space, remain practical for full nodes, work with ordinary wallets and institutional custody, and provide a path for coins that migrate late.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company favors flexible output designs that separate the Bitcoin output “container” from the signature system. It specifically pointed to P2MR, proposed in BIP-360, and P2TRv2 as possible architectures. Coinbase also suggested studying hybrid outputs that could be spent with either a classical ECDSA/Schnorr key or a post-quantum key during a staged transition.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">This Is Planning, Not Panic</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Coinbase described the transition as a multi-year engineering problem, not evidence that Bitcoin is currently broken. That distinction matches BitcoinVersus.Tech’s recent explainers on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/can-quantum-computers-hack-bitcoin/">whether quantum computers can hack Bitcoin</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/what-is-post-quantum-cryptography/">post-quantum cryptography</a>, and the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/bitcoin-developers-quantum-security-tabconf-2026/">developer debate over Bitcoin quantum security</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-10-13-coinbase-bitcoin-post-quantum-migration-418509">The Block</a> separately reported that Coinbase is prioritizing flexible designs, staged protection and Bitcoin-specific testing. The company also cautioned that stateful post-quantum signatures can create serious operational risks if signing state is accidentally reused.</p>
