<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Strategy’s latest filing reports 848,517 Bitcoin, while its capital allocation now includes buying Bitcoin, repurchasing preferred stock and maintaining dollar reserves.</li><li>Daily preferred-stock dividends remain conditional on a shareholder vote; the proposal changes payment frequency, not the stated annualized dividend rate.</li><li>Saylor’s Bitcoin thesis combines digital credit, tokenized securities, monetary-policy expectations and an AI-era scarcity argument. Those are executive views, not guarantees.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Strategy’s Capital Stack Has Changed</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Strategy’s <a href="https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/bltf8d808d9b8cebd37/blt075438553ed3641e/6ad5860b04e8526be47d2cab/form-8-k_10-19-2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">October 19 Form 8-K</a> reports 848,517 Bitcoin acquired for $64.02 billion at an average price of $75,446 per coin as of October 18. During the prior week, the company acquired 267 Bitcoin for $22.1 million and repurchased $193.1 million of STRC. The same filing lists a $4.83 billion U.S. dollar reserve and $447.3 million of USD cash.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Those numbers give context to Saylor’s on-air description of roughly 845,000 Bitcoin and more than $75 billion in total reserve assets. He argued that Strategy needs room to buy or sell across Bitcoin, credit and equity markets rather than only accumulate. His shortest explanation was also his clearest: <strong>“The credit is the product, the equity is the byproduct.”</strong> That is Strategy’s operating thesis, not a demonstrated outcome; its success still depends on Bitcoin prices, market liquidity and continuing access to capital.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Daily Dividends Are Still Conditional</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saylor said Strategy could create the <strong>“first securities in the world that pay dividends every calendar day.”</strong> The important qualifier is approval. Strategy’s <a href="https://www.strategy.com/it/mstr/vote" target="_blank" rel="noopener">shareholder materials</a> say voting on the amendment completes October 28. If it passes, STRC’s first daily record date would be November 1 with payment on November 2; STRF, STRK and STRD would follow in January 2027. The company says the total annualized dividend rate would not change—holders would receive smaller payments more frequently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saylor presented the change as a way to reduce timing friction around preferred securities and prepare for a 24/7 tokenized market. The company’s formal language is narrower: it says calendar-day accruals could support price stability, liquidity and future market-infrastructure developments, while payment remains on the next business day. In other words, expanded tokenization is the stated direction, not proof that U.S.-listed securities already settle continuously.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A Regulatory Argument and an AI Scarcity Thesis</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On policy, Saylor said a capitalist wants <strong>“a bill of rights, not a bill of restrictions,”</strong> then characterized the stalled Clarity Act as the latter. His argument is a policy preference, not a legal conclusion: he favors usable rights to custody, tokenize and trade rather than additional layers of constraint. He also said restrictive monetary, trade and foreign policy had weighed on Bitcoin, while a larger role for bank credit and digital securities could become tailwinds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His AI argument connects the interview to a theme BitcoinVersus.Tech covered on October 12, when <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/michael-saylor-ai-agents-digital-money-bitcoin/">Saylor said AI agents will need digital money</a>. He expects artificial intelligence to create enormous wealth for some firms while pressuring labor-based businesses. His reason for holding Bitcoin through that transition was simple: <strong>“The robots can’t make more Bitcoin.”</strong> He later added that a money-supply expansion would make <strong>“Bitcoin benefit squared.”</strong> Both statements are his long-term investment thesis, not forecasts readers should treat as certain.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A Thread BitcoinVersus Has Followed Since 2023</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The through-line is clear when the interview is set beside the site’s earlier coverage. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/12/11/michael-saylor-explains-how-bitcoin-will-reshape-corporate-balance-sheets/">2023 report on corporate balance sheets</a> captured Saylor’s original idea that Bitcoin could change how companies store capital. A <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/17/rumor-michael-saylor-wants-a-bitcoin-dividend-for-microstrategy-shareholdersvernment-sells-33m-btc-through-binance/">2024 report about a possible Bitcoin dividend</a> was explicitly a rumor. Today’s proposal is more concrete but fundamentally different: it concerns cash-dividend timing on preferred stock, not a distribution of Bitcoin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The daily-dividend plan also extends our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/10/20/michael-saylor-announces-10-25-dividend-on-strategys-preferred-stock-strc/">2025 STRC dividend coverage</a>, when Strategy announced a 10.25% annual rate. And it fits directly with Saylor’s October 16 <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/16/michael-saylor-bitcoin-digital-credit-xapo-summit/">Bitcoin credit-stack explanation</a>: Bitcoin as digital capital, credit written against that capital and digital money built on top. The newest interview adds an execution detail that matters: Strategy is using a real balance sheet to defend the credit layer while continuing to accumulate Bitcoin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Bottom Line</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saylor’s bullish quote will draw the headlines, but the more durable development is Strategy’s increasingly flexible capital structure. The latest filing shows a company with a large Bitcoin reserve, a larger authorized credit-repurchase program and material dollar reserves. It does not prove a new bull market or guarantee that daily dividends will be approved. It does show how Saylor’s corporate-Bitcoin story has moved from simple treasury accumulation toward Bitcoin-backed digital credit, preferred-income products and tokenization infrastructure.</p>
