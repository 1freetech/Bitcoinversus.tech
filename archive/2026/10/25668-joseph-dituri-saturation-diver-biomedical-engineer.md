<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Joseph Dituri built a career around pressure—first surviving it underwater, then studying what it does to the human body.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saturation diving ranked No. 1 in our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">Top 10 Toughest Jobs in the World in 2026</a>. Dituri took that experience into a second discipline that demanded an entirely different level of training: biomedical engineering.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to the <a href="https://www.usf.edu/engineering/bme/faculty-staff/joseph-dituri.aspx">University of South Florida</a>, Dituri earned a B.S. in computer science, an M.S. in astronautical engineering and a Ph.D. in biomedical engineering. His research areas include life-support systems, high-carbon-dioxide environments, hyperbaric and hypobaric medicine, traumatic brain injury and safety for underwater and space exploration.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before academia, Dituri spent 28 years in the U.S. Navy. His career included saturation diving, undersea systems, deep-submergence work and submarine-rescue technology before he retired as a commander.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div>[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BkKn1G8OaR4]</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>University of South Florida footage shows Dituri resurfacing after completing Project Neptune 100.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In 2023, Dituri pushed the career crossover even further. He spent 100 consecutive days living inside Jules’ Undersea Lodge in Key Largo while collecting data on the physiological and psychological effects of prolonged hyperbaric exposure. The mission set a Guinness World Record and became a research platform rather than simply an endurance stunt.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His work is still active. A <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41882764/">2026 peer-reviewed study protocol</a> describes a blinded randomized clinical trial evaluating hyperbaric oxygen treatment for veterans and service members with chronic symptoms after mild-to-moderate traumatic brain injury. The study is designed for 420 participants.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is what makes Dituri such a strong Career Journalist subject. He did not leave one extreme profession and simply start over. He took decades of first-hand knowledge about pressure, life-support systems and diving physiology and converted it into formal engineering research.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The path also puts him beside saturation diver <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/chris-lemons-saturation-diver-survived-seafloor/">Chris Lemons</a>, whose career shows the operational danger of the profession, and smokejumper-inventor <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/frank-derry-smokejumper-steerable-parachute/">Frank Derry</a>, who similarly turned high-risk field experience into technical innovation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Dituri’s résumé is a reminder that the most extraordinary careers are sometimes built by taking knowledge from one unforgiving environment and carrying it into another discipline where that experience can become science.</p>
