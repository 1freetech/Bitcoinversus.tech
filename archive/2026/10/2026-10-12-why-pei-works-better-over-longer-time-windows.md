# Why PEI Works Better Over Longer Time Windows

**Published:** 2026-10-12
**Live article:** https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/why-pei-works-better-over-longer-time-windows/

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/bitcoin-12-month-pei-reading-61600/">12-month PEI reading near $61.6K</a> is useful, but it also shows why the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/05/power-efficiency-index-explained/">Power Efficiency Index</a> is usually stronger over spacious windows such as 24 or 48 months.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>PEI multiplies network hashrate growth by improvement in mining efficiency: <strong>PEI = (H₂ ÷ H₁) × (E₁ ÷ E₂)</strong>. In a 12-month slice, hashrate can finish close to where it started even after large swings from curtailment, miner shutdowns, weather, difficulty changes or fleet migration. <a href="https://hashrateindex.com/blog/what-is-bitcoins-hashrate/">Hashrate Index notes that even raw hashrate estimates need smoothing</a> because short observation periods can be distorted by block-time variance. A newly announced 8.9 J/TH machine can also be technically real before it is widely deployed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Across 24–48 months, those short-term effects matter less. ASIC generations have more time to replace older fleets, hashrate growth has more time to compound, and the efficiency factor captures a clearer technology cycle. Multi-year mining studies, such as <a href="https://braiins.com/blog/economics-immersion-cooling-bitcoin-miners">Braiins’ 48-month operating scenarios</a>, also illustrate how differently mining economics can look over longer horizons.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is why our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/23/18-month-power-efficiency-analysis-suggests-bitcoin-still-undervalued/">18-month PEI analysis</a> and the 12-month follow-up can point in different directions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Longer is not automatically better. A four-year window can cross halvings, regulatory shocks, migration events or an unusually low Physical Price. A 48-month reading is therefore better viewed as a <strong>structural PEI reading</strong>, while 12 months is closer to a near-term snapshot.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most useful PEI dashboard may eventually show 12-, 24- and 48-month readings together. When all three agree, the signal is more robust. When they diverge, the disagreement itself suggests the Bitcoin mining network is between regimes.</p>
