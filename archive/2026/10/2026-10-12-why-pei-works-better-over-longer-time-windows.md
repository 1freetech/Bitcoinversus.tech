# Why PEI Works Better Over Longer Time Windows

**Published:** 2026-10-12
**Live article:** https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/why-pei-works-better-over-longer-time-windows/

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/bitcoin-12-month-pei-reading-61600/">12-month PEI follow-up</a> is useful precisely because it exposes a weakness of short Power Efficiency Index windows: one noisy endpoint can dominate the result. Re-running the comparison with the same Oct. 11 daily-hashrate convention across 12, 24 and 48 months makes the contrast much clearer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/05/power-efficiency-index-explained/">Power Efficiency Index</a> uses <strong>PEI = (H₂ ÷ H₁) × (E₁ ÷ E₂)</strong>, where hashrate growth is combined with improvement in leading ASIC efficiency. Relative Price is then <strong>Physical Price × PEI</strong>, with Physical Price defined as the lowest observed Bitcoin price inside the chosen interval.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Method update:</strong> these three charts standardize the endpoint to Oct. 11, 2026, when the daily network estimate was 1,029 EH/s. YCharts, using Blockchain.com data, also reports 1,102 EH/s one year earlier. Daily hashrate is inherently noisy, which is one reason the 12-month reading should not be over-read.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">12-Month PEI</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

Daily hashrate endpoints 1,102 → 1,029 EH/s and leading ASIC efficiency 9.5 → 8.9 J/TH produce PEI ≈ 0.997×. Physical Price $57,747.77 gives Relative Price ≈ $57.6K versus an actual-price snapshot near $83.45K. The nearly flat PEI occurs because the lower one-year daily-hashrate endpoint offsets the hardware-efficiency gain.</em></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the endpoint-sensitive reading. A single 12-month slice can capture curtailment, weather, miner shutdowns, migration or simple block-time variance. It can also compare an announced efficiency frontier with a network that has not yet broadly deployed that hardware.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">24-Month PEI</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":23894,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/bitcoin-pei-24-month-reading-oct-2026.png?w=1024" alt="Bitcoin 24-month Power Efficiency Index chart showing PEI 2.436x, relative price about $140.7K and actual price about $83.45K" class="wp-image-23894" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Figure 2 — 24-month PEI: medium structural reading. Hashrate 640.61 → 1,029 EH/s and leading ASIC efficiency 13.5 → 8.9 J/TH produce PEI ≈ 2.436×. With the same $57,747.77 Physical Price, Relative Price is ≈ $140.7K. The ~$83.45K actual-price snapshot sits about 40.7% below that PEI-relative level.</em></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At 24 months, the index spans more of an ASIC replacement cycle. Hashrate growth has more time to compound and temporary network conditions have less influence on the full comparison. This does not make the reading a guaranteed fair value; it makes it a more structural measurement of the physical network progression PEI is designed to capture.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">48-Month PEI</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":23896,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/bitcoin-pei-48-month-reading-oct-2026.png?w=1024" alt="Bitcoin 48-month Power Efficiency Index chart showing PEI 9.672x, relative price about $149.7K and actual price about $83.45K" class="wp-image-23896" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Figure 3 — 48-month PEI: long structural reading. Network hashrate ≈ 257 → 1,029 EH/s and leading ASIC efficiency 21.5 → 8.9 J/TH produce PEI ≈ 9.672×. Using the November 2022 exchange-level low near $15,479 as Physical Price gives Relative Price ≈ $149.7K. The ~$83.45K actual-price snapshot sits about 44.3% below that level. Exact intraday lows can vary slightly by exchange.</em></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The 48-month window captures several generations of SHA-256 hardware and roughly a fourfold increase in network hashrate. That makes it less sensitive to one unusual week or month. It is also why a long-window PEI should be described as a <strong>structural reading</strong>, not automatically as a more accurate Bitcoin price forecast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why the Windows Disagree</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The three readings are not contradictory. They answer different questions. The 12-month window asks what the network did recently. The 24-month window captures a broader technology cycle. The 48-month window captures several cycles of hashrate expansion and efficiency gains. When all three agree, the PEI signal is stronger. When they diverge this sharply, the disagreement itself is useful information.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This also helps explain why our earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/23/18-month-power-efficiency-analysis-suggests-bitcoin-still-undervalued/">18-month PEI analysis</a> can point in a different direction from a one-year snapshot. Longer windows reduce endpoint sensitivity, although they introduce their own complications: halvings, regulatory shocks, migration events and unusually deep Physical Price lows can all influence the result.</p>
