<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hayley Wickenheiser reached the top of international hockey. Then she started another demanding career from the beginning.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Canadian star won four Olympic hockey gold medals, seven world championships and retired as Canada’s all-time leading scorer in women’s international hockey. She also competed in softball at the 2000 Summer Olympics, making her an athlete who reached the Olympic Games in two different sports. Her <a href="https://olympic.ca/team-canada/hayley-wickenheiser/">Team Canada biography</a> records 168 goals, 211 assists and 379 points across 276 international hockey games.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">From the rink to medicine</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wickenheiser retired from competitive hockey in 2017. Instead of remaining only in broadcasting, coaching or sports administration, she pursued medicine. She graduated from the University of Calgary’s Cumming School of Medicine in 2021 and began working in family medicine.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The career shift put her in the same broader class of multi-field achievers as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/laurent-duvernay-tardif-the-nfl-player-who-became-a-doctor/">Laurent Duvernay-Tardif</a>, who combined an NFL career with medicine, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/shawna-pandya-the-doctor-training-for-space/">Shawna Pandya</a>, whose medical work extends into extreme-environment and space research.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Then came the NHL front office</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wickenheiser joined the Toronto Maple Leafs’ player-development department in 2018 and continued rising while completing her medical education. Toronto promoted her to assistant general manager for player development in 2022.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That chapter ended in July 2026. After eight seasons with the organization, Wickenheiser and the Maple Leafs parted ways during a restructuring under new general manager John Chayka. In her public statement, she said she had expected to remain in a position where she could make a significant impact but that the new leadership group envisioned a different path. <a href="https://toronto.citynews.ca/2026/07/09/hockey-legend-hayley-wickenheiser-parts-ways-with-maple-leafs-after-8-seasons/">The Canadian Press reported the departure on July 9, 2026</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Three demanding careers</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wickenheiser’s story is compelling because none of the careers is ornamental. She was one of the defining hockey players of her generation, completed medical school after retiring from elite competition, and then climbed into senior management in the NHL.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Her path also echoes <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/sian-proctor-the-geoscientist-who-became-a-spacecraft-pilot/">Sian Proctor</a>, whose career moved across geoscience, education and spacecraft operations: expertise in one field did not become a reason to stop building expertise in another.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As of October 2026, Wickenheiser’s next professional chapter has not been publicly defined. That makes the story unfinished. She has already been an Olympic champion, physician and NHL executive. Whatever comes next will follow a career built around repeatedly entering difficult arenas and becoming credible inside them.</p>
