<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Supercomputing has moved from millions of floating-point operations per second to more than two quintillion. The chart below tracks milestone machines from the <strong>CDC 6600 in 1964</strong> through <strong>LineShine in 2026</strong> and shows just how steep that climb became.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is a follow-up to BitcoinVersus.Tech’s <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/flops-vs-hashrate/">FLOPS vs Hashrate</a> explainer and our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/7-fastest-supercomputers-world-2026-2/">7 Fastest Supercomputers in the World</a> ranking. The early points below use widely cited historical peak figures, while the modern era increasingly uses standardized TOP500 HPL measurements. That means the percentage jumps are best read as <strong>generational progress</strong>, not as a perfectly identical benchmark across all 62 years.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Across the full chart, performance increases by roughly <strong>732.8 billion times</strong> from 3 MFLOP/s to 2.1984 EFLOP/s—equivalent to an increase of about <strong>73.28 trillion percent</strong>. The most famous middle milestone is the jump from petascale to exascale: Roadrunner became the first 1-PFLOP/s machine in 2008, and Frontier became the first true 1-EFLOP/s HPL machine in 2022, roughly 14 years later. TOP500 documents Roadrunner at 1.026 PFLOP/s and Frontier at 1.102 EFLOP/s. </p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">CDC 6600 — 1964</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Often described as the first true supercomputer, Seymour Cray’s CDC 6600 established the category in 1964 at roughly <strong>3 MFLOP/s</strong>. It is the baseline for this chart: a machine measured in millions of floating-point operations per second at a time when most computers were dramatically slower.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Cray-1 — 1976</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Cray-1 pushed the chart to roughly <strong>160 MFLOP/s</strong>, a <strong>5,233.3% increase</strong> over the CDC 6600 baseline. Its vector-processing architecture became synonymous with high-performance computing and established the visual language of the classic supercomputer.</p>

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Cray-2 — 1985</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Cray-2 moved the milestone line into gigaflops at roughly <strong>1.9 GFLOP/s</strong>. Compared with the Cray-1 figure on the chart, that is another <strong>1,087.5% increase</strong>, or nearly 11.9× more floating-point throughput.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Numerical Wind Tunnel — 1993</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Japan’s Numerical Wind Tunnel represents one of the biggest leaps on the chart: roughly <strong>124 GFLOP/s</strong>, a <strong>6,426.3% increase</strong> over the Cray-2 figure. The machine helped demonstrate that massively parallel computing could outrun the traditional vector-supercomputer model.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">ASCI Red — 1997</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ASCI Red at Sandia crossed the teraflop barrier. The chart uses its later <strong>1.338 TFLOP/s</strong> TOP500 result, which is <strong>979.0% above</strong> the 124-GFLOP/s Numerical Wind Tunnel point. TOP500 records ASCI Red as the first teraflop-class system and the No. 1 machine of its era.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Blue Gene/L — 2004</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IBM’s Blue Gene/L moved the chart to roughly <strong>70.72 TFLOP/s</strong>, a <strong>5,185.5% increase</strong> from ASCI Red’s 1.338-TFLOP/s point. Blue Gene showed how enormous numbers of relatively modest processors could be organized into an extremely powerful and comparatively energy-conscious machine.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Roadrunner — 2008</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IBM’s Roadrunner at Los Alamos became the first supercomputer to sustain more than one petaflop on Linpack, reaching <strong>1.026 PFLOP/s</strong>. Relative to the 70.72-TFLOP/s Blue Gene/L point plotted here, that is a <strong>1,350.8% increase</strong>. TOP500 identifies Roadrunner as the machine that officially opened the petascale era.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">K computer — 2011</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Japan’s K computer reached <strong>10.51 PFLOP/s</strong> in 2011, becoming the first system to exceed 10 petaflops. That represented a <strong>924.4% increase</strong> from Roadrunner’s 1.026-PFLOP/s result—about 10.2× more performance in only three years.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Tianhe-2 — 2013</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>China’s Tianhe-2 raised sustained Linpack performance to <strong>33.86 PFLOP/s</strong>. Compared with the K computer’s 10.51 PFLOP/s, that was a <strong>222.2% increase</strong>. Its arrival also marked China’s return to the No. 1 position on TOP500.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Sunway TaihuLight — 2016</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sunway TaihuLight reached <strong>93.01 PFLOP/s</strong> in 2016. That was a <strong>174.7% increase</strong> over Tianhe-2. The system was also strategically significant because it relied on Chinese-designed Sunway processors rather than the accelerator-heavy architecture used by several other contemporary leaders.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Summit — 2018</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Oak Ridge’s Summit delivered roughly <strong>148.6 PFLOP/s</strong>, a <strong>59.8% increase</strong> over Sunway TaihuLight. The percentage jump was smaller than many earlier transitions, but Summit was important because its IBM POWER9 CPUs and NVIDIA GPUs anticipated the increasingly accelerator-heavy architecture used in both scientific computing and AI.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Fugaku — 2020</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>RIKEN and Fujitsu’s Fugaku pushed HPL performance to <strong>442.01 PFLOP/s</strong>. That was a <strong>197.4% increase</strong> over Summit and nearly tripled the plotted milestone in two years. Fugaku’s Arm-based A64FX architecture also proved that leadership did not require copying the dominant CPU-plus-discrete-GPU formula.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Frontier — 2022</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Frontier at Oak Ridge became the first true exascale machine on the TOP500 with an HPL score of <strong>1.102 EFLOP/s</strong>. That was a <strong>149.3% increase</strong> over Fugaku’s 442.01 PFLOP/s result. Measured from Roadrunner’s first petaflop in 2008, reaching Frontier’s exaflop required roughly <strong>1,074×</strong> more performance in about 14 years.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">LineShine — 2026</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>LineShine at the National Supercomputing Centre in Shenzhen reached <strong>2.1984 EFLOP/s</strong> on the June 2026 TOP500. That is a <strong>99.5% increase</strong> over Frontier’s 2022 milestone—essentially doubling the first exascale result. TOP500 says LineShine is the first listed machine to exceed two exaflops of sustained double-precision HPL performance using CPUs only.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the curve tells us</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The curve is not smooth because supercomputing progresses through architectural jumps. Vector processors gave way to massive parallelism; commodity processors became viable at enormous scale; accelerators changed the performance-per-watt equation; and modern systems increasingly combine specialized CPUs, GPUs, memory systems and high-speed interconnects. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-7-ai-accelerators-tokens-per-second-joules-per-token/">AI accelerator ranking</a> shows the same specialization now reshaping inference infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The historical milestones are documented across the <a href="https://www.top500.org/25years/">TOP500 historical archive</a>, while the June 2026 list records <a href="https://top500.org/news/lineshine-debuts-no-1-top500-enters-new-global-exascale-era/">LineShine’s arrival at No. 1</a>. The next frontier will not be raw FLOPS alone. Power efficiency, memory bandwidth, networking, cooling and useful performance per watt will determine how far the curve can continue rising.</p>
