<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Aaron Hale built one career around handling explosives and another around pushing his body through some of the hardest endurance events on Earth.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before becoming known as an ultramarathoner and entrepreneur, Hale spent 14 years in the military. He first served as a Navy chef, then moved into the U.S. Army and became an Explosive Ordnance Disposal technician and team leader. EOD is one of the professions we ranked among the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/top-10-toughest-jobs-world-2026/">toughest jobs in the world</a> because the work demands technical precision under immediate physical risk.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In 2011, Hale was injured by an improvised explosive device in Afghanistan and lost his eyesight. Years later, bacterial meningitis also took his hearing. His own biography now lists a second set of careers that would be demanding even without those injuries: entrepreneur, real-estate investor, speaker, podcaster and ultra-endurance athlete. <a href="https://www.pointofimpactpod.com/about">Point of Impact</a> documents that progression in his own words.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->

<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Hale discusses the path from Navy cook and Army EOD technician to endurance athlete, entrepreneur and real-estate investor.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of his most striking achievements came at the 2023 Badwater 135. <a href="https://www.badwater.com/adventurecorps-presents-the-2023-badwater-135/">Badwater’s official race record</a> identifies Hale as the first blind and deaf finisher of the 135-mile event through California’s Death Valley region.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He kept going. In February 2026, Hale completed a roughly 215-mile run beginning at the Indian Ocean in Kenya and then climbed Mount Kilimanjaro. That gives his résumé another unusual combination: bomb disposal, ultra-endurance racing, high-altitude climbing and business ownership.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That multi-discipline pattern is exactly what makes a Career Journalist profile worth documenting. Like saturation diver <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/chris-lemons-saturation-diver-survived-seafloor/">Chris Lemons</a> and veterinarian-mountaineer <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/ed-viesturs-veterinarian-high-altitude-mountaineer/">Ed Viesturs</a>, Hale moved from one high-consequence environment into another field that required a completely different kind of skill.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>His career is not remarkable because one job led neatly to the next. It is remarkable because the disciplines barely resemble one another, yet each demands the same underlying traits: preparation, tolerance for discomfort, judgment under pressure and the willingness to keep learning.</p>
