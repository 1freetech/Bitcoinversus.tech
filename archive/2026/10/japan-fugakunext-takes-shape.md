<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/09/hardware-japan-plans-to-build-worlds-most-powerful-supercomputer/">BitcoinVersus.Tech reported in January 2025</a> on Japan’s plan for a successor to Fugaku. The project is now far more concrete: RIKEN has published the basic design for <strong>FugakuNEXT</strong> and is targeting operation around 2030.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.r-ccs.riken.jp/en/outreach/topics/20260529-1/index.html">RIKEN’s May 29, 2026 technical report</a> says the system is being designed with Fujitsu and NVIDIA as an integrated AI-HPC platform. Fujitsu leads the overall system, compute-node and CPU design, while NVIDIA leads the GPU infrastructure. It will be the first Japanese flagship supercomputer built around GPU accelerators.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The updated primary-source target is more precise than early reports: RIKEN is pursuing up to <strong>100× application performance</strong> versus Fugaku, not a blanket 1,000× raw-compute increase. The design targets more than 600 EFLOPS of sparse FP8 AI performance while staying within an approximately 40 MW power constraint.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>The architecture also connects directly to BitcoinVersus.Tech’s coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/fujitsu-monaka-separate-5nm-cache-dies/">Fujitsu’s MONAKA processor work</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/nvidia-1-billion-us-science-supercomputers/">NVIDIA-backed scientific supercomputing</a>. FugakuNEXT remains a planned system, so its final delivered performance will not be known until hardware is built and independently benchmarked.</p>
