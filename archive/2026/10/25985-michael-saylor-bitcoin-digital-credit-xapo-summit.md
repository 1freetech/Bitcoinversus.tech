<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Michael Saylor used a fireside conversation at the inaugural Xapo Bank Summit in London to lay out a three-layer financial thesis: Bitcoin as digital capital, credit written against that capital, and digital money built on the resulting credit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Speaking with Laura K. Inamedinova, the Strategy executive chairman argued that the next phase of Bitcoin adoption may depend less on spot ownership and more on financial products built around it. <a href="https://www.xapobank.com/en/blog/the-inaugural-xapo-bank-summit-bitcoins-next-phase">Xapo Bank’s official summit recap</a> describes the same framework and notes that Saylor presented credit as a bridge between volatile Bitcoin capital and investors seeking income or lower volatility.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">From Bitcoin ownership to Bitcoin infrastructure</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saylor said during the interview that “Bitcoin has already won.” That remains his assessment rather than an established market fact, but it explains the rest of his argument. He is increasingly describing Bitcoin as the base collateral for a broader capital-market system rather than only as an asset to hold.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That view also connects with his recent argument that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/michael-saylor-ai-agents-digital-money-bitcoin/">AI agents will eventually need digital money</a>. In both cases, Saylor is focused on financial infrastructure that could sit above Bitcoin rather than on short-term price movement alone.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The drawdown stress test</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of the interview’s most useful sections deals with a severe downside scenario. Saylor discusses how Strategy evaluates its credit structure if Bitcoin were to fall to $20,000. Instead of arguing that another deep drawdown is impossible, he explains the importance of modeling whether obligations remain serviceable when the collateral loses substantial value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Strategy has formalized much of this framework in its explanation of <a href="https://www.strategy.com/media/articles/bitcoin-digital-credit-and-digital-money">Bitcoin, digital credit and digital money</a>. The company describes Bitcoin as the capital layer and its preferred securities as an effort to translate that volatile capital into instruments with different risk and income characteristics.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus has been tracking related forms of financialization elsewhere. Bitcoin mining <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/bitcoin-mining-hashrate-tradable-financial-asset-futures-tokenized-note/">hashrate itself is becoming a tradable financial asset</a>, while our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/proof-of-work-awards-21-most-influential-bitcoin-figures-2026/">2026 Proof of Work Awards</a> highlighted the growing influence of builders, miners and capital-market operators around Bitcoin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What gets built next</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The broader takeaway is not that Saylor’s model is guaranteed to succeed. Credit introduces counterparty, liquidity, legal and structural risks, and Bitcoin’s volatility remains material. The interview is significant because it shows where one of Bitcoin’s most prominent corporate advocates is directing attention next: from acquiring Bitcoin toward engineering financial products around it.</p>
