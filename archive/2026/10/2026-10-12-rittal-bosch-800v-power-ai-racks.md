<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Rittal and Bosch are bringing an 800-volt DC power architecture into AI data centers, combining racks, direct liquid cooling and power delivery in one compact system.</strong> Rittal unveiled the design October 12 at the 2026 OCP Global Summit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.rittal.com/com-en/Company/Presse/Pressemeldungen/OCP-Global-Summit-2026">Rittal says</a> its RiMatrix HPC architecture can place more than 1 MW of IT power within a few square meters. The system pairs OCP racks with a central coolant distribution unit and a Power Sidecar using Bosch power electronics. The sidecar is designed to scale to 2 MW.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The engineering logic is straightforward: higher voltage can move more power at the same current, which can reduce conductor size and losses while supporting denser racks. <a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/800-vdc-power-architecture-ai-factory/">NVIDIA has separately been pushing the industry toward 800 VDC power</a> for future AI factories, putting Rittal and Bosch inside a broader shift away from low-voltage rack distribution.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rittal says its water-based CDU delivers more than 1 MW of cooling and uses modular units that can be replaced during operation. Bosch says the power electronics build on 800-volt technology it has produced at scale for electric vehicles since 2023.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The important distinction is that this is an infrastructure architecture, not a claim that every AI rack is suddenly moving to 800 VDC. Rittal is showing a development demonstrator at OCP, while the industry is still standardizing the power path from grid to rack to accelerator.</p>
