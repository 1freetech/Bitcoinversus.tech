<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Arm said on October 13, 2026, that its AGI CPU averaged <strong>1.99× modeled performance per rack</strong> against AMD EPYC 9755 across seven agentic-AI workloads. The underlying server measurements were independently tested by Signal65; the rack-level comparison was then modeled from those measured results.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the tests showed</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://newsroom.arm.com/news/arm-agi-cpu-agentic-benchmarks" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Arm’s October 13 release</a>, modeled rack performance averaged 1.7× for tooling, 2.0× for agentic workflows and orchestration, and 2.2× for reasoning and planning. Arm also reported up to 45% lower sandbox-creation latency against AMD EPYC 9965.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://signal65.com/research/ai/arm-agi-cpu-performance-rack-scale/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Signal65’s independent analysis</a> says the same benchmark scripts were run on the AGI CPU and AMD systems, with individual modeled rack results ranging from 1.43× to 3.19×. The distinction matters: server performance was measured directly, while rack performance depends on modeled density and power assumptions.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Context</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The results extend Arm’s push into production data-center silicon. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/semiconductors-arm-neoverse-css-n4-128-cores-agentic-ai-silicon/">Neoverse CSS N4 for agentic AI</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/01/ibm-dual-isa-arm-zarchitecture-same-core/">IBM’s dual-ISA Arm work</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/nuvacore-2-5-billion-valuation-cpu-ai-data-center-core-first/">new competition around AI data-center CPUs</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Arm also introduced a new agentic-CPU evaluation methodology with support from Fujitsu, Meta and OpenAI. The methodology is still new, so broader independent replication will be needed before treating it as an industry-standard benchmark.</p>
