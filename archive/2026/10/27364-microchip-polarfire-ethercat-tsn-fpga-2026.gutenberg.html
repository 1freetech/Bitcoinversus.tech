Microchip Technology expanded its PolarFire FPGA networking platform on October 29 with new support for EtherCAT and Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN), giving designers of industrial robots and automation equipment more ways to coordinate devices over Ethernet. The announcement concerns intellectual-property blocks, software and evaluation tools for existing programmable chips—not the launch of a new processor or a newly fabricated ASIC.

The addition brings Beckhoff’s EtherCAT technology to PolarFire FPGAs and PolarFire system-on-chip devices, with 10, 100 and 1,000 Mbps connectivity. Microchip’s TSN IP targets 1 Gbps deterministic Ethernet on PolarFire SoC FPGAs. Unlike ordinary Ethernet, which generally delivers data on a best-effort basis, deterministic networking can reserve transmission opportunities for time-critical control traffic.

Why factory robots need predictable network timing

A robot arm, machine-vision camera and motor controller may share a network with diagnostic and monitoring traffic. Control instructions must arrive in the correct sequence and within predictable timing windows, not simply at a high average speed. EtherCAT is commonly used for distributed industrial motion control; TSN describes a family of IEEE Ethernet features that can schedule and prioritize traffic. They are complementary approaches, not interchangeable protocols.

Microchip’s industrial edge design documentation describes time-aware scheduling, precision time synchronization, traffic filtering and frame preemption. Its published PolarFire SoC TSN demonstration assigns scheduled traffic to a motor-control task while other packets compete for bandwidth, showing how network timing can be tested before equipment reaches the factory floor.

The development fits a broader move toward more capable industrial control silicon. PolarFire devices use reconfigurable FPGA fabric, while SoC versions combine programmable logic with embedded processing. That flexibility matters to equipment makers adapting robotics or networking designs without waiting for a custom fixed-function chip.

What developers can test now

The company lists its Libero SoC toolchain, reference designs, a dual-port Gigabit Ethernet FMC card and the PolarFire SoC Video Kit as evaluation tools. Existing developers can explore the interfaces before committing to an embedded product, but Microchip has not released independent power-consumption benchmarks or guaranteed factory deployment timelines for this specific networking update.

The practical challenge is interoperability across controllers, switches and software stacks. Microchip’s new IP broadens the choices for those systems; real-world latency, reliability and integration costs still need to be validated in each application.
