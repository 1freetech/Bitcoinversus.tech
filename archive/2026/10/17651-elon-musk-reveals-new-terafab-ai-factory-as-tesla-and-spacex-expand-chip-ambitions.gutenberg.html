Texas, October 28, 2026 — Elon Musk's Tesla and SpaceX are pushing ahead with Terafab, a proposed semiconductor manufacturing complex in Grimes County, Texas, as demand for AI chips grows across autonomous vehicles, robotics and space computing. The companies announced the location in August; Musk provided a more specific, but uncertain, manufacturing timetable on October 20.

The project's first phase represents more than $16.8 billion in planned investment and at least 3,000 projected jobs, according to the Texas governor's office. SpaceX has described a proposed campus with more than 100 million square feet of manufacturing space. Those numbers describe the planned buildout, not money already spent or a completed factory.

Tesla and SpaceX want more control over chip production

In its August project announcement, SpaceX said Terafab would combine logic-chip manufacturing, memory production, advanced packaging and testing at one site. Tesla wants chips for autonomous driving and its Optimus robots; SpaceX has outlined ambitions for computing systems operating in space. The companies say their future demand may exceed what conventional suppliers can deliver.

At SpaceX's October 20 earnings call, Musk said the smaller Austin research fab is aiming to produce a functional SRAM test chip in May or June 2027. He hopes volume production at the main Grimes County site could begin in the third or fourth quarter of 2028, but stressed that the timetable is highly ambitious. Reuters also reported the dates as targets, not assured outcomes.

Equipment, partnerships and power remain hurdles

Building advanced chips requires lithography tools, cleanroom infrastructure, specialist staff and years of process and yield qualification. Musk said suppliers were working to accelerate equipment deliveries, but did not confirm that a complete leading-edge production line had been installed. BitcoinVersus.Tech has separately covered TSMC's discussions about possible Terafab involvement; no finalized manufacturing partnership has been announced.

The companies have also described an eventual goal of producing chips representing one terawatt of computing electrical-power capacity per year. That figure is an aspirational rating for potential annual chip output, not the plant's confirmed electricity demand or an achieved FLOPS benchmark. For now, the story is a major Texas industrial commitment with important milestones still ahead, rather than an operating AI chip factory.
