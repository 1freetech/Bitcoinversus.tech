<p>AI developer Killian Lukas highlighted Xbox’s unusually open response to the recent wave of game decompilation, porting and remixing. In his <a href="https://twitter.com/hellokillian/status/2110120915774423112">X post</a>, Lukas pointed to Xbox CEO Asha Sharma’s message that player-friendly experimentation can be worth exploring even when it challenges an existing business model.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an <a href="https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/10/13/introducing-xbox-labs/">official Xbox Wire memo</a>, Sharma said Xbox has recently seen games decompiled and rebuilt for hardware they were never designed to support, Windows PC builds translated to Apple Silicon or browsers, and major games remixed into other games’ source code.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/asha_shar/status/2110083127741702241","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"x","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-rich is-provider-x wp-block-embed-x wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Xbox Labs will be a small group of game makers, engineers and scientists tasked with turning emerging ideas into experiments players and developers can try. Sharma said the team will build in the open and share tools with developers, while Xbox remains committed to its hardware and responsible stewardship of intellectual property. <a href="https://www.theverge.com/news/1010406/xbox-labs-game-decompiling-emulation-exploration">The Verge</a> notes that questions remain about how the company will handle unauthorized uses of its games and IP.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Xbox is not treating AI-assisted compatibility hacks and game remixing only as a threat. It is creating a lab to study what those experiments could become for players and developers.</p>
