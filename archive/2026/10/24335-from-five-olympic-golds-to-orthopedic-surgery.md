<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Eric Heiden built one of the strangest elite résumés in American sports: Olympic speed-skating champion, professional cyclist, Tour de France rider, Stanford-trained physician and orthopedic surgeon.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the 1980 Winter Olympics in Lake Placid, the 21-year-old Heiden entered every men’s speed-skating event and won all five. He took gold in the 500, 1,000, 1,500, 5,000 and 10,000 meters — a sweep stretching from the shortest sprint to the longest race on the program. <a href="https://www.teamusa.com/hall-of-fame/hall-of-fame-members/eric-heiden" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Team USA records</a> that he also set 15 world records during his speed-skating career.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then he switched sports. Heiden moved into professional cycling, won a U.S. national championship and rode for the 7-Eleven team in the 1986 Tour de France. Instead of turning that into a permanent second career, he changed direction again.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>By 1991, Heiden had earned his medical degree from Stanford. He became a board-certified orthopedic surgeon specializing in knees, shoulders and sports medicine. <a href="https://heidenortho.com/doctors/eric-heiden/page/3/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">His orthopedic practice describes medicine as his third career and his life’s work</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>He did not entirely leave the Olympics behind. He later returned as a physician for U.S. speed skaters, including at multiple Winter Games. That meant the athlete who once needed a medical team to keep him competing eventually became part of the medical team responsible for keeping later Olympians healthy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Heiden’s 1980 record gained new attention in 2026. Johannes Høsflot Klæbo won six gold medals in cross-country skiing at Milano Cortina, moving past Heiden for the most golds at one Winter Games. Two of Klæbo’s six came in team events, however, so Heiden’s five individual gold medals in one Winter Olympics still stand as a separate record.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>Heiden’s résumé fits the same rare category as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-navy-seal-to-doctor-to-astronaut/">Jonny Kim’s move from Navy SEAL to doctor to astronaut</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-nfl-draft-pick-to-astronaut/">Leland Melvin’s path from NFL draft pick to engineer to astronaut</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/punk-rock-singer-phd-molecular-biology-dexter-holland/">Dexter Holland’s combination of punk rock, molecular biology and aviation</a>. The common thread is not fame. It is reaching an unusually high level in several unrelated fields.</p>
