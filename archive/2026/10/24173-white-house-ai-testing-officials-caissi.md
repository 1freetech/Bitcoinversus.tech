<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Trump administration reportedly plans to move three senior officials from the Commerce Department’s federal AI testing center to the White House, a change that could put advanced-model evaluation closer to the administration’s central technology and national-security decision making.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-administration-plans-move-ai-testing-agency-officials-white-house-2026-10-12/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reuters reported on October 12</a>, citing The Information and a White House official, that three officials from the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence, or CAISSI, are expected to report to White House technology and cyber officials. Reuters said the names of the three officials were not disclosed and that the White House and Commerce Department had not immediately commented.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The move would come shortly after the administration elevated its broader AI coordination structure. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/politics-jay-clayton-white-house-super-intelligence-force-120-day-ai-review/">Jay Clayton’s appointment to lead a 120-day federal “super intelligence” review</a>, another sign that model capability, security and national competitiveness are being pulled into a more centralized policy process.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.nist.gov/caissi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NIST describes CAISSI</a> as the government’s primary industry contact for testing and collaborative research involving advanced commercial AI systems. Its work includes security and capability evaluations, voluntary standards, and assessments of risks involving cybersecurity, biosecurity and other national-security concerns.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The organizational question matters because AI evaluations increasingly involve systems that can take actions rather than simply answer prompts. Recent BitcoinVersus.Tech coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/anthropic-cuts-internet-access-internal-ai-evals-claude-unintended-actions/">Anthropic restricting internet access during internal evaluations</a> showed how testing conditions can affect whether unexpected model behavior reaches real websites or services.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The reported personnel move also follows the federal government’s recent shift toward the term “super intelligence.” BitcoinVersus.Tech documented <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/politics-white-house-ai-super-intelligence-executive-order/">the executive order directing agencies to adopt that terminology</a> as part of a broader policy reset around advanced AI.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For now, the reported transfer should be treated as an organizational change, not proof of a new testing mandate. No public order identifying the three officials or formally changing CAISSI’s statutory or technical responsibilities was available at publication time. The practical significance will depend on whether the move changes who sets evaluation priorities, receives model-test results, or decides how those findings influence federal policy.</p>
