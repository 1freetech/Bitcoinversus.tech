<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>SpaceX is moving Starlink Mobile closer to becoming a full U.S. wireless carrier.</strong> The company says a new nationwide 800 MHz spectrum deal could help it build a hybrid satellite-and-ground network that works indoors, outdoors and in traditional cellular dead zones.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>Fox Business</em> frames the move as Elon Musk trying to create a <strong>fourth major U.S. phone company</strong> alongside AT&amp;T, Verizon and T-Mobile. That is a useful shorthand, but SpaceX itself has been more precise: it says Starlink Mobile could become a <a href="https://www.spacex.com/updates/2035/starship-hls-9-tranquility-telemetry">“major mobile carrier in the US”</a>. It has not formally declared itself the fourth national carrier.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The technical shift is significant. SpaceX agreed to acquire up to <strong>14 MHz of paired 800 MHz spectrum</strong>, subject to FCC approval. Lower-band spectrum generally travels farther and penetrates buildings better than higher-frequency signals. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacex-acquire-spectrum-that-enables-starlink-mobile-services-2026-10-08/">Reuters reported</a> that the move is aimed directly at expanding Starlink Mobile beyond satellite-only coverage.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/starlink-mobile-800-mhz-spectrum-indoor-phone-coverage/">why the 800 MHz deal matters indoors</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/networking-fcc-october-29-direct-to-device-25mhz-spectrum-auction/">the FCC’s direct-to-device spectrum push</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/29/starship-reaches-orbit-and-deploys-26-starlink-v3-satellites/">Starlink V3 deployment</a>.
