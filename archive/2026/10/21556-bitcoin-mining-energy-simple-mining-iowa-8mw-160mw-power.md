<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.simplemining.io/"><strong>Simple Mining</strong></a> has acquired an approximately <strong>8 MW</strong> Bitcoin mining site in Iowa from <strong>Sphere 3D</strong>, adding an already-energized block of electrical capacity to a platform the company says now approaches <strong>160 MW</strong> across Iowa.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The transaction is an energy story as much as a mining story. The site supports about <strong>2,200 latest-generation miners</strong>, but the scarce asset underneath them is the power connection: transformers, utility service, containers, distribution equipment and an operating site that can already turn grid electricity into Bitcoin hashrate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">The Site Was Already Energized</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sphere 3D disclosed in an <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1591956/000121390026098043/ea0304863-8k_sphere.htm"><strong>SEC filing</strong></a> that the Iowa site had been energized in March 2025. Simple Mining had already been managing the facility under a services agreement before buying it outright, so this is not a greenfield build that still needs years of interconnection work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters because energized capacity has become one of the most valuable parts of the digital-infrastructure stack. BitcoinVersus has already covered how <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/energy-us-power-demand-record-highs-2026-2027-ai-crypto-data-centers/"><strong>AI and crypto data centers are pushing U.S. power demand toward record highs</strong></a>. A site with operating utility service can therefore be worth more strategically than an undeveloped parcel with a large theoretical megawatt number.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Sphere 3D Sold the Site for $1.5 Million</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The September agreement valued the Iowa property at <strong>$1.5 million</strong>. Sphere 3D said it received <strong>$300,000 in cash</strong> and a <strong>$1.2 million interest-free promissory note</strong> payable in equal monthly installments from December 2026 through November 2027.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The deal also assigned Simple Mining the mining containers, transformers and related equipment at the site. Sphere 3D separately retained the right to recover an approximately $300,000 utility prepayment and about $225,000 in security deposits, so the $1.5 million purchase price should not be treated as a simple replacement-cost estimate for 8 MW of new infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Eight Megawatts Can Support Thousands of ASICs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Simple Mining says the facility currently supports about 2,200 newer-generation Bitcoin miners. Spread evenly across 8 MW, that is roughly <strong>3.6 kW of electrical capacity per miner</strong> before accounting for site overhead, curtailment margins and the fact that individual ASIC models draw different amounts of power.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That relationship between power draw and useful hashrate is why operators track both machine efficiency and facility efficiency. BitcoinVersus recently explained why <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/bitcoin-mining-hardware-nameplate-wall-facility-joules-per-terahash/"><strong>nameplate J/TH and wall J/TH are not the same</strong></a>: transformers, fans, pumps, networking and electrical losses all consume energy that does not directly become SHA-256 work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Simple Mining Is Building Around Power Ownership</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/simple-mining-acquires-sphere-3d-bitcoin-mining-site-in-iowa-growing-platform-to-nearly-160-mw-302900603.html"><strong>October 7 announcement</strong></a>, Simple Mining said the acquisition brings its Iowa platform to nearly 160 MW. Its website currently describes more than 150 MW of power capacity, more than 40,000 miners under management and over 9 EH/s of hosted hashrate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company’s business model is vertically integrated around that electrical footprint: customers buy miners, deploy them into Simple Mining facilities and use the company for hosting and repair. That turns each energized megawatt into both a power asset and a service platform.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Energy Cost Still Decides the Economics</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Owning capacity does not automatically make a mine profitable. Bitcoin revenue per unit of hashrate changes with network difficulty, block rewards, fees and Bitcoin price, while power cost continues every hour a miner is running. That is why <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/bitcoin-mining-what-is-hashprice-revenue-per-phs/"><strong>hashprice</strong></a> and electricity cost have to be read together.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Simple Mining’s own site currently advertises hosted mining around a reference rate of $0.065/kWh for one S23 Hydro example. The company also says customers can pause miners, an important capability when electricity prices, weather or mining economics make continuous operation unattractive.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Monaco,Consolas,'Liberation Mono','Courier New',monospace">Why This 8 MW Deal Matters</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Eight megawatts is small compared with the gigawatt-scale campuses now being discussed for AI, but Bitcoin mining scales site by site. The acquisition gives Simple Mining another operating power block without waiting for a new interconnection, while Sphere 3D converts a mining asset into cash and a note as it shifts attention toward AI and high-performance computing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The larger trend is clear: in modern Bitcoin mining, the miners themselves are increasingly replaceable. <strong>Interconnected electrical capacity is the durable asset.</strong> ASIC fleets can be refreshed every generation; an energized site with utility service, transformers, land and operating infrastructure can take far longer to reproduce.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
