<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Social media often markets strings like <strong>/human</strong> or <strong>X10THINK</strong> as secret ChatGPT commands. They are not. ChatGPT does not have a magic vocabulary where typing a made-up label automatically unlocks hidden behavior. What <em>does</em> work is using a short label as a reusable prompt macro: define the instruction behind it, then paste that instruction in front of a task.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936848-how-do-i-create-a-good-prompt-for-an-ai-model">OpenAI’s prompting guidance</a> emphasizes clear goals, useful context, priorities, desired tone and format, and iterative refinement. <a href="https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/work-users-ynjqu/resources/prompting">OpenAI Academy’s prompting resource</a> makes the same broader point: there is no single perfect prompt. Better instructions usually beat mythical shortcuts.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters as ChatGPT expands beyond basic chat. BitcoinVersus.Tech has previously covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/artificial-intelligence-chatgpt-space-shared-ai-office/">ChatGPT Space</a>, explained <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/07/28/what-happens-inside-an-ai-data-center-when-you-ask-chatgpt-a-question/">what happens inside an AI data center when you ask ChatGPT a question</a>, and tracked <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/04/chatgpt-adoption-passes-1-billion-active-users/">ChatGPT adoption passing one billion active users</a>. Here are seven reusable prompt commands worth saving.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#7 — VOICELOCK</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “VOICELOCK — Write for [audience] in [tone]. Use varied sentence lengths, concrete language and natural transitions. Remove canned filler. Preserve every important fact and uncertainty.” The label is just a mnemonic. The instruction after it is what controls the result.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#6 — TEACHBACK</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “TEACHBACK — Explain this in plain English. Then ask me three short questions to test whether I understood it. After I answer, correct only what I missed.” This is stronger than simply asking for a child-level explanation because it turns the response into a learning loop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#5 — ASSUMPTIONCHECK</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “ASSUMPTIONCHECK — Before answering, identify the assumptions that could materially change the conclusion. State the most important uncertainty. Ask a question only if the missing information is essential; otherwise make the most reasonable assumption and label it.” This is useful for planning, troubleshooting and research.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#4 — REDTEAM</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “REDTEAM — Challenge my idea before helping me improve it. Give the strongest opposing case, the three most likely failure modes, the evidence I am missing and what new information would change your conclusion.” This is a better way to request pushback than telling ChatGPT to stop agreeing with you.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#3 — OPTIONGRID</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “OPTIONGRID — Give me three materially different approaches, not three rewrites of the same idea. Compare each on speed, cost, risk, upside and ideal use case. Then recommend one in two sentences.” The comparison criteria force the alternatives to separate in a useful way.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#2 — SOURCECHECK</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “SOURCECHECK — Separate verified facts, reasonable inferences and unknowns. For claims that can change over time, use current sources. If a reliable source is unavailable, say that clearly instead of filling the gap.” This is especially useful for news, prices, product specifications, laws and current events.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#1 — FINALPASS</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Paste this:</strong> “FINALPASS — Audit your answer against my original goal and every constraint I gave you. Find contradictions, unsupported claims, repetition and missed requirements. Correct them, then return only the revised final answer.” This turns quality control into an explicit final step instead of assuming the first draft is finished.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The important lesson is that these names are homemade. Rename them, combine them or save the ones you repeatedly use. A word like VOICELOCK or REDTEAM has no special power by itself; the detailed instruction attached to it creates the behavior. For harder work, clearly state the outcome, constraints and verification standard—and ask ChatGPT to think carefully when deeper reasoning would help.</p>
