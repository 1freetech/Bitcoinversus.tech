<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Taiwan is planning a new industrial park in Texas that could cost between <strong>$2 billion and $4 billion</strong> and is intended to help smaller Taiwanese companies establish U.S. operations around the fast-growing AI hardware supply chain. The project would support suppliers tied to companies such as NVIDIA, TSMC and Foxconn, but it is important to be precise: this is <strong>not</strong> a $4 billion NVIDIA factory, a new TSMC fab, or a fully government-funded Taiwanese plant.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The story accelerated after <a href="https://twitter.com/Polymarket/status/2111054798896198039">Polymarket highlighted the reported Texas plan on X</a>. Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs has since said the broader U.S. industrial-park strategy would use private funding while the government provides support, including loan guarantees and coordination with U.S. officials. The first project is expected to be in Texas, and Taiwan is considering more than one U.S. site.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Texas is becoming a Taiwanese AI supply-chain hub</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The plan fits a strategy Taiwan has been building for months. In an <a href="https://mnscdn.moea.gov.tw/MNS/english/news/News.aspx?kind=6&amp;menu_id=176&amp;news_id=124161">official Ministry of Economic Affairs release</a>, Taiwan described Texas as a strategic hub for AI server supply chains and said it had opened a Taiwan Trade and Investment Center in Dallas while working with the Taiwan Electrical and Electronic Manufacturers' Association, or TEEMA, to help Taiwanese companies form overseas industrial clusters.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That ecosystem is already taking shape. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently reported that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/tsmc-confirms-texas-team-terafab-talks/">TSMC has teams evaluating Texas</a>, while <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/nvidia-us-ai-manufacturing-plan-taking-shape/">NVIDIA's U.S. AI manufacturing network is expanding</a>. We also began tracking this reshoring push in 2025 when <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/14/nvidia-to-manufacture-500-billion-in-ai-supercomputers-across-texas-and-arizona/">NVIDIA outlined plans for large-scale AI-system production in Texas and Arizona</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the $4 billion number actually means</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to <a href="https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610160010">Focus Taiwan, citing the Ministry of Economic Affairs</a>, each proposed U.S. industrial park would cost roughly $2 billion to $4 billion. TEEMA would work with private companies to raise the development funding, while the government would help participating suppliers with financing support, visas, land coordination and communication with local and federal authorities.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ministry also acknowledged reporting that land has already been secured in Texas, but said it would provide details later. No final city, construction timetable, tenant roster or committed $4 billion spending package has yet been publicly announced. That distinction matters: the current number is an <strong>upper estimate for a planned industrial complex</strong>, not a completed transaction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why smaller suppliers matter</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AI hardware is not built by chip designers alone. Advanced systems depend on substrates, cooling modules, power equipment, connectors, circuit boards, chassis, optics, packaging and precision manufacturing. Giants such as TSMC and Foxconn can finance their own U.S. expansions; smaller companies in their supplier networks may need shared infrastructure and government coordination to move overseas economically.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Taiwan's Texas park moves from planning into construction, the bigger story will not simply be a $4 billion campus. It will be the transplantation of part of Taiwan's tightly integrated manufacturing ecosystem into the United States — closer to NVIDIA's AI-system production, Texas server assembly, U.S. data centers and potentially future semiconductor capacity.</p>
