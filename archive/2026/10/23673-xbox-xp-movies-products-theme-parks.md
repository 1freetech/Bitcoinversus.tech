<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Xbox has created a new division called XP to expand its game franchises into film and television, consumer products, partnerships, live events and physical attractions. The division is led by Kayleen Walters, who is moving from Mojang into the new role after helping oversee Minecraft’s expansion beyond games.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Xbox announced XP on October 8, 2026.</strong></li><li><strong>XP has four areas: Xbox Pictures, Xbox Products, Xbox Places and Xbox Partnerships.</strong></li><li><strong>Kayleen Walters will lead XP as president.</strong></li><li><strong>Xbox says games will remain at the center of the franchises XP expands.</strong></li><li><strong>Microsoft has not yet detailed XP’s budget, staffing level or a complete project slate.</strong></li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Xbox Chief Strategy Officer Matthew Ball introduced the structure in an <a href="https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/10/08/introducing-xp/"><strong>official Xbox announcement</strong></a>. The company describes XP as a way to consolidate work that had already existed across different teams and give it one organization focused on extending Xbox properties beyond gameplay.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://bsky.app/profile/wario64.bsky.social/post/3mxeib25wpc2j","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"bluesky","responsive":true} --><figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-rich is-provider-bluesky wp-block-embed-bluesky"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">https://bsky.app/profile/wario64.bsky.social/post/3mxeib25wpc2j</div><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Wario64 shared Xbox’s official XP announcement as the new division was unveiled on October 8.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:embed -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">XP Has Four Parts</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The name is a play on experience points, but Microsoft is using XP as an umbrella for four distinct businesses around its game franchises.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Xbox Pictures:</strong> film, television and other linear storytelling based on Xbox worlds and characters.</li><li><strong>Xbox Products:</strong> licensing and consumer merchandise tied to Xbox franchises.</li><li><strong>Xbox Places:</strong> live events, broadcasts, immersive attractions, theme parks and other location-based experiences.</li><li><strong>Xbox Partnerships:</strong> work with creators, brands, sponsors and communities.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is broader than simply launching a movie studio. Xbox says XP will work with outside creative partners and its existing game studios. <a href="https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/xbox-xp-film-tv-products-experiences-division-kayleen-walters-1236904912/"><strong>Variety independently reported</strong></a> the same four-part structure and Walters’ appointment.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Minecraft Became the Model</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Walters’ move matters because Microsoft is explicitly pointing to Minecraft as evidence that a game franchise can become a much larger entertainment business. Xbox says Minecraft sales rose from roughly 350 million units to more than 425 million during Walters’ tenure, while its merchandising operation expanded and <em>A Minecraft Movie</em> became a major theatrical hit.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gbNzxvz2_H4","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} --><figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-video is-provider-youtube wp-block-embed-youtube wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gbNzxvz2_H4</div><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Minecraft’s official channel shows the film adaptation that Xbox now points to as part of Walters’ record expanding the franchise beyond games.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:embed -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Walters is leaving Mojang as Microsoft makes a separate leadership change there. BitcoinVersus.Tech covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/microsoft-maria-angelidou-smith-mojang-minecraft-ceo/"><strong>Maria Angelidou-Smith becoming CEO of Mojang and Minecraft on October 19</strong></a>. Splitting those jobs puts operation of Minecraft itself under one leader while Walters concentrates on how Xbox franchises move into other forms of entertainment.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Players Are Already Debating the Strategy</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The announcement has produced both enthusiasm and skepticism among Xbox players. Some see obvious opportunities for franchises such as Halo, Fallout, Gears, Fable and The Elder Scrolls outside games. Others question whether Microsoft should put more attention into movies, merchandise and attractions while Xbox is also restructuring its core gaming business.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Games Still Have to Carry the Strategy</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Xbox is trying to make one distinction clear: XP is not supposed to replace investment in games. Ball wrote that great games remain at the center of these franchises and at the heart of Xbox. That matters because films, merchandise and attractions only have durable value if players continue to care about the underlying worlds.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Microsoft is still investing in the software side of that ecosystem. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/29/gaming-microsoft-releases-a-complete-xbox-multiplayer-sample-for-godot/"><strong>Microsoft’s complete Xbox multiplayer sample for Godot</strong></a>, which gives developers a reference implementation for Xbox services, networking and multiplayer. Mojang is also continuing to build out its own games, including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/29/gaming-minecraft-dungeons-ii-launches-sift-new-dimension/"><strong>Minecraft Dungeons II and the Sift</strong></a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Xbox Has Not Said Yet</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The announcement defines XP’s scope, but several practical questions remain open. Microsoft has not disclosed a separate XP budget, how large the organization will be, which franchises will receive projects first, or how much production will happen inside Xbox versus through licensing and external partners.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Those details will determine whether XP becomes a major operating unit or primarily a coordinating structure around work Xbox was already doing. The distinction is important, especially after a year in which Microsoft has also reorganized studios and cut gaming roles.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Bottom Line</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Xbox XP formalizes Microsoft’s attempt to turn successful game worlds into broader entertainment franchises. The structure now exists, the four business areas are defined and Walters has the job of leading them. What is not yet known is how much capital, staffing and new production Microsoft will put behind the idea.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>For now, the most defensible reading is simple: Xbox wants more of the economic life of its franchises to happen outside the console and PC screen, while publicly insisting that the games themselves remain the foundation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
