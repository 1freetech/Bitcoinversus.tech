<p>Oracle and Yucca Growth Infrastructure have revised the proposed power system for <strong>Project Jupiter</strong>, cutting the AI data center campus’s projected permitted greenhouse-gas emissions by about <strong>35%</strong>. The companies say the updated design lowers the annual greenhouse-gas figure from roughly <strong>8.8 million tons to 5.7 million tons</strong>, a reduction of more than 3 million tons per year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The distinction matters: these numbers are <strong>permit projections</strong>, not measured emissions from an operating facility. Project Jupiter’s fuel-cell microgrid is still going through New Mexico’s air-quality permitting process. Oracle’s <a href="https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/project-jupiter-microgrid-cuts-permitted-emissions-by-35-percent-2026-10-15/">October 15 announcement</a> says YGI submitted the revised design to the New Mexico Environment Department after technical questions from regulators and public feedback.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The revised proposal keeps the same basic fuel-cell approach while lowering permitted levels for greenhouse gases, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, particulate matter and hazardous air pollutants. Oracle says the changes do not reduce the IT capacity planned for the data center.</p>

<p>The project sits at the intersection of two increasingly important data-center constraints: access to dense computing capacity and access to dependable power. BitcoinVersus.Tech has been tracking the same infrastructure shift through <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/nidec-nir3-3-2mw-800vdc-cdu/">3.2 MW liquid cooling for 800 VDC AI facilities</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/intel-46000-xeon-cores-133kw-ai-rack/">133 kW rack-scale CPU density</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/frore-liquidjet-diamond-ai-gpu-cooling/">cooling systems aimed at 2,300W AI accelerators</a>.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Local reporting shows that the permitting process is not finished. <a href="https://sourcenm.com/2026/10/15/oracle-says-it-will-cut-project-jupiter-emissions-after-new-mexico-org-seeks-to-toss-permit-case/">Source New Mexico reported</a> that a hearing officer is considering a motion connected to the pending air-quality case later this month. That means the revised emissions figures should be understood as part of an active regulatory proposal rather than a final operating result.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The engineering direction is still significant. Instead of abandoning the onsite fuel-cell concept, the project team is trying to reduce the emissions envelope while preserving the computing capacity the campus was designed to support. Oracle has also committed to a public environmental dashboard for the site once it is operational.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For now, the strongest conclusion is narrow: Project Jupiter’s proposed microgrid has a materially lower permitted-emissions profile than the version previously submitted, but the facility still has to clear the remaining regulatory process before those design changes can be judged against real-world operation.</p>
