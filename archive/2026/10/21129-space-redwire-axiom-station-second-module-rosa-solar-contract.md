<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Redwire is turning a first-module space-station contract into repeat business.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The roughly $2.6 billion public space-and-defense company has won a follow-on award from Axiom Space to build two Roll-Out Solar Array wings for Axiom Station’s second module. Redwire is already supplying ROSA hardware for the station’s first module, so the new order extends the company’s role deeper into the commercial low-Earth-orbit buildout.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Redwire Moves From One Axiom Module to Two</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://ir.rdw.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/245/redwire-awarded-follow-on-contract-to-provide-roll-out">Redwire said on October 5</a> that it will develop and deliver two ROSA wings for Axiom Station’s second space-station module. The company is already building the same class of solar-array hardware for the first module.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That matters because repeat orders are often more revealing than first wins. The first contract proves a supplier can get through selection. The second suggests the customer is willing to keep that supplier embedded as the platform expands.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Redwire says it now has more than 65 solar-array wings delivered or in development across missions ranging from the International Space Station to national-security and deep-space programs.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why ROSA Matters</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Traditional rigid solar panels take up valuable launch volume. ROSA is designed to launch compact and then unroll in orbit, giving spacecraft operators a way to deploy large power-generating surfaces without requiring the same folded-panel architecture used by older systems.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is especially important for commercial stations. Habitation modules, research equipment, communications systems, life support and future manufacturing payloads all require dependable electrical power. As stations become larger and more capable, their power systems become core infrastructure rather than an accessory.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Axiom’s First Two Modules Are Intended to Become a Free-Flying Station</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.axiomspace.com/release/redwire-follow-on-contract-second-module">Axiom Space says</a> its first module is in final assembly at Thales Alenia Space in Turin, Italy, before integration and testing in Houston and eventual transfer to Kennedy Space Center. Axiom currently plans to launch that module in 2028.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The second module is also preparing for final assembly and is planned to launch less than a year later. Axiom says the first two modules together are intended to form an operational, four-crew-capable free-flying station.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>For a company the size of Redwire, the strategic value is not only the disclosed hardware order. The bigger opportunity is becoming a recurring infrastructure supplier as commercial stations add modules, customers and power demand.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Space Power Is Becoming Its Own Infrastructure Market</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Power is becoming one of the most important bottlenecks in space just as it is on Earth. More capable satellites, orbital manufacturing and space-based AI all require larger and more reliable electrical systems.</p>

<p>BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/cowboy-space-reason-1-laser-power-orbit-earth/">Cowboy Space testing orbital solar power and laser transmission</a>. Redwire is attacking the same broader power problem from the hardware side: make large arrays lighter, more compact at launch and proven enough to become standard infrastructure.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What to Watch Next</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<p>The contract value was not disclosed, so the near-term financial impact is difficult to quantify from the announcement alone.
