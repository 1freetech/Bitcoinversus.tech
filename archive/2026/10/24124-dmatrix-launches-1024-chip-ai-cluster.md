<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/d-matrix-announces-squadron-compute-clusters-for-ai-inference-at-data-center-scale-302906041.html">d-Matrix launched Squadron on October 12</a>, a multi-rack AI inference system that scales to <strong>16 racks and 1,024 Corsair XPUs</strong>. The company says Squadron is already running in its Arizona data center and is now shipping to priority customers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The unusual part is cooling: Squadron is <strong>air-cooled</strong>, not liquid-cooled. d-Matrix says that lets customers deploy it inside existing industry-standard data centers rather than rebuild facilities around new cooling infrastructure. The system combines Corsair XPUs, JetStream NICs and Aviator software, with Arista, Broadcom and Supermicro named as development partners.</p>

<p><strong>Company-reported performance:</strong> d-Matrix says a heterogeneous setup pairing Squadron with GPU servers reached <strong>845 tokens per second per user</strong> and <strong>20,415 tokens per second across 64 concurrent users</strong> using Attention-FFN Disaggregation. Those figures come from d-Matrix’s own demonstration and are not yet an independent industry benchmark.</p>

<p>The broader shift is important: AI infrastructure is starting to split workloads across different accelerators instead of forcing every task onto one GPU architecture. <a href="https://runtimewire.com/article/d-matrix-squadron-inference-clusters">RuntimeWire</a> notes that Squadron moves d-Matrix from selling accelerators toward complete data-center infrastructure.</p>
