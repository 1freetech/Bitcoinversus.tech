## Final Gutenberg Source

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>More than <a href="https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/backgrounders/Pages/submarine-cable-resilience.aspx">170 submarine cable repairs</a> were reported worldwide in 2025, according to the International Telecommunication Union. Building the cables is only part of the challenge. Fixing them at sea takes specialized ships and crews.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In an <a href="https://www.iscpc.org/publications/icpc-global-comparison-of-repair-commencement-times/">August 28, 2026 update</a>, the International Cable Protection Committee described a new global comparison of repair-start times, based on anonymized industry data. The research is intended to identify permitting and operational delays. Its public summary does not provide average waiting times; the full report is available by request.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Repair crews locate a fault, raise the cable from the seabed, remove the damaged section, splice in replacement fiber and lower it again. Weather, ship availability and access permissions can affect how quickly work begins.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Most breaks are accidental, often involving fishing equipment or anchors. Networks with several cable routes can redirect traffic, but regions with limited connections face greater risk of disruption.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Following a Baltic Sea cable incident, <a href="https://twitter.com/Tendar/status/2007601279691067624">a January 2026 post shared footage</a> of Finnish authorities boarding a ship suspected of involvement. The post documents an investigation, not proof of what caused the damage.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

So do repair vessels, backup routes and the skills behind <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/11/16/fiber-optic-training-outside-plant-osp/">fiber-optic cable work</a>.
