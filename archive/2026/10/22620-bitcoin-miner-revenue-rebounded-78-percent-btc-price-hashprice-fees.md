<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitcoin miners have staged a sharp revenue recovery from the summer lows, but the underlying data shows something more specific than a broad return to easy profitability. According to <a href="https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-miners-escape-months-of-financial-distress-as-daily-revenue-surges-by-78/">CryptoSlate’s October 9 report citing CryptoQuant</a>, total daily mining revenue climbed from roughly <strong>$27 million at July’s low to as much as $48 million</strong>—a gain of about <strong>78%</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The recovery is real. But it is still mostly a <strong>Bitcoin-price story</strong>. BTC rebounded from roughly $58,000 in July to above $83,000 during the recovery, while transaction fees remain a very small portion of miner income and network difficulty is already pointing higher again.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The 78% Revenue Jump Is a Point-to-Low Comparison</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The headline number compares roughly <strong>$27 million</strong> of daily industry revenue at July’s low with a later peak near <strong>$48 million</strong>. That works out to about <strong>77.8%</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That does not mean every miner’s revenue rose 78%, and it does not mean industry profit rose 78%. Total network revenue is distributed across an enormous and changing amount of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitcoin-hashrate-nears-1-zhs-difficulty-higher-hashprice-39/">Bitcoin hashrate</a>, while each operator faces different electricity prices, machine efficiencies, pool fees, financing costs, cooling loads, and downtime.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The more useful unit-level metric is <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/bitcoin-mining-what-is-hashprice-revenue-per-phs/">hashprice</a>: expected miner revenue per petahash per second per day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Hashprice Recovered About 41%, Not 78%</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CoinShares previously placed the June hashprice low near <strong>$27.70 per PH/s/day</strong>. As of October 9, <a href="https://data.hashrateindex.com/bitcoin-compute-data/bitcoin-hashprice-index">Hashrate Index</a> shows spot hashprice at <strong>$39.14 per PH/s/day</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is a recovery of roughly <strong>41.3%</strong>. The difference between a 78% rise in total daily revenue and a roughly 41% rise in revenue per unit of hashrate is important. Total industry revenue can rise while more mining capacity returns to the network and competes for that revenue.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hashrate Index currently estimates Bitcoin’s seven-day network hashrate at about <strong>997.03 EH/s</strong>. That is essentially the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitcoin-hashrate-nears-1-zhs-difficulty-higher-hashprice-39/">one-zettahash threshold</a>, meaning more compute is again chasing the same 3.125 BTC block subsidy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Transaction Fees Are Not Driving the Recovery</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CryptoQuant’s figures show seven-day average daily transaction-fee revenue improving from roughly <strong>$195,000 to $275,000</strong>. That is another healthy increase—about <strong>41%</strong>—but the absolute numbers remain small relative to total miner revenue.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A $275,000 daily fee run-rate is only about <strong>0.6%</strong> of a $48 million mining-revenue day. The comparison uses different reporting windows, so it should be read as scale rather than an exact accounting reconciliation. Hashrate Index’s current block data independently points in the same direction: transaction fees represent only about <strong>0.68% of block rewards</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That means the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/bitcoin-what-is-block-subsidy-halving-210000-blocks/">block subsidy</a> and Bitcoin’s dollar price are still doing almost all of the revenue work. Miners have not been rescued by a durable fee market.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Miner Selling Pressure Has Eased, but That Is Not the Same as Accumulation</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CryptoQuant’s Miner Profit/Loss Sustainability model classified miners as “extremely underpaid” through much of May to August. The model moved into its “fairly paid” regime around August 21, when Bitcoin reached roughly $76,000.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CryptoQuant also says no subsequent miner outflow has exceeded its “extreme” threshold since the roughly <strong>29,000 BTC</strong> movement recorded on August 21. That is consistent with reduced financial stress, but it should not be overstated. A transfer from a wallet identified as miner-associated can represent a sale, custody movement, collateral transfer, treasury reorganization, or another transaction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So the more defensible conclusion is that <strong>extreme miner-associated outflows have eased</strong>, not that every miner has stopped selling Bitcoin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Difficulty Could Take Back About 4.6% of Hashprice</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The recovery is already meeting its next pressure point. Hashrate Index currently estimates the next Bitcoin difficulty adjustment at about <strong>+4.83%</strong> around October 16.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If that adjustment occurred exactly as estimated while Bitcoin price, transaction fees, and other variables stayed unchanged, the inverse relationship between difficulty and hashprice would imply roughly a <strong>4.6% reduction in hashprice</strong>. A $39.14 hashprice would mechanically fall toward about <strong>$37.34 per PH/s/day</strong> under that simplified scenario.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That is not a forecast. Difficulty estimates change until the retarget occurs, Bitcoin price moves continuously, and transaction fees vary block by block. It does show how quickly new hashrate can absorb part of a price-driven recovery.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Revenue Recovery Does Not Mean Every ASIC Is Profitable Again</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The same $39.14 hashprice produces very different outcomes across hardware generations. BitcoinVersus just showed how a <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/canaan-a1366-a1346-five-joules-efficiency-kept-hashing/">five-joule efficiency gap between two Canaan Avalons</a> was enough for one machine to remain online while the other spent months curtailed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the site level, the gap becomes even wider because <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/asic-jth-not-site-jth-bitcoin-mining-efficiency/">ASIC J/TH is not total facility J/TH</a>. Pumps, fans, transformers, networking, containers, lighting, and auxiliary cooling all consume power without producing hashes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The industry can therefore move from distress toward stability without making every older machine economic. Efficient fleets at low-cost power sites recover first. High-J/TH hardware and expensive hosting remain closest to the shutdown line.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the Data Actually Says</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Total daily miner revenue rebounded from roughly $27 million to as much as $48 million: about +78%.</li><li>Spot hashprice recovered from roughly $27.70 in June to $39.14 today: about +41%.</li><li>Bitcoin’s price recovery remains the main driver of the improvement.</li><li>Transaction fees improved but still account for less than 1% of block rewards at current levels.</li><li>Extreme miner-associated outflows have eased since August 21 under CryptoQuant’s methodology.</li><li>A projected +4.83% difficulty increase could absorb part of the hashprice recovery if other variables do not rise with it.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The mining industry is healthier than it was at the summer low, but the numbers do not describe a new fee-driven golden age. They describe a network receiving a powerful dollar-revenue boost from Bitcoin’s price while hashrate returns, difficulty follows, and efficient machines capture the recovery first.</p>
