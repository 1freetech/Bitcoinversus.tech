<p>Longevity futurist Rand says <a href="https://twitter.com/rand_longevity/status/2110188843680858590">“80 will be the new 40 by 2035.”</a> It is a striking prediction. As a literal scientific forecast, though, it is not supported by current evidence.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There is real progress behind the optimism. Life Biosciences recently reported the first human data from its Phase 1 ER-100 epigenetic-restoration trial. The treatment was well tolerated through 56 days in three glaucoma participants, with preliminary visual-field improvements in two. But <a href="https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-data-from-ongoing-phase-1-trial-evaluating-er-100-in-optic-neuropathies/">the company itself stresses</a> that the trial is primarily testing safety and tolerability—not proving whole-body age reversal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Population evidence is much more cautious. A major <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-024-00702-3">Nature Aging analysis</a> found that gains in life expectancy have slowed and concluded that radical human life extension is implausible this century unless biological aging can be substantially slowed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For an 80-year-old to be biologically comparable to a typical 40-year-old, medicine would need to preserve or restore multiple systems at once: cardiovascular function, muscle, bone, immunity, metabolism and cognition. No human therapy has demonstrated that.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>BitcoinVersus.Tech has previously examined <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/how-close-are-we-to-solving-aging/">how close science is to solving aging</a>, the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/follow-up-first-human-er100-age-reversal-data-october-8/">first ER-100 human trial</a>, and earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/18/biotechnology-could-this-jellyvish-be-the-answer-to-human-longevity/">longevity biology research</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Verdict:</strong> possible as a long-term aspiration, but <strong>not currently a valid evidence-based prediction for 2035</strong>. The next nine years could produce major breakthroughs, but Rand’s timeline remains speculative.</p>
