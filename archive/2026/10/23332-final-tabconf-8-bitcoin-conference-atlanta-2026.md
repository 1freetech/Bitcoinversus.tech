<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bitcoin conference season is about to get unusually technical. <strong>TABConf 8 starts Monday, October 12, at Georgia Tech in Atlanta</strong>, and organizers say this will be the final TABConf after a run that began in 2018.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The four-day event opens with two Builder Days before shifting into conference sessions on October 14 and 15. The schedule reads less like a traditional crypto expo and more like an open-source engineering backlog: Bitcoin development with AI agents, Rust tooling, quantum-safe cryptography, mining-pool difficulty algorithms, wallet hardware, privacy, Lightning and hands-on projects.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Mining actually made the technical agenda</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>One session stands out for BitcoinVersus.Tech readers: <strong>“Recent Advances in Difficulty Adjustment for Mining Pools.”</strong> The talk is scheduled for Tuesday and focuses on how pools estimate a miner’s hashrate and adjust share difficulty from incoming work.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That sounds narrow, but it sits directly in the operating path between ASIC fleets and pool infrastructure. Share difficulty determines how frequently a miner submits valid shares to prove work to the pool. Anyone who wants the foundation first can start with our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/bitcoin-mining-it-what-is-stratum-asic-miners-pools/"><strong>Stratum explainer</strong></a>, which covers how miners and pools exchange jobs and shares.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The conference also includes an all-event <strong>Proof Of Print</strong> project that integrates Bitcoin mining into a 3D printer and filament dryer so the machine’s waste heat can perform useful thermal work. That fits a broader engineering trend we have been following, from home heat reuse to commercial heat-recovery systems.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>AI is showing up inside Bitcoin development</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Monday begins with a workshop called <strong>“Agentic Engineering for Bitcoin Developers.”</strong> Another session, <strong>“Agentic Autoresearch for Bitcoin,”</strong> focuses on tools that let AI agents conduct Bitcoin research. Later in the week, a BitLab “Acceleration” track asks what small teams can build when coding agents become part of the development workflow.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That makes the timing interesting. The Linux Foundation’s Open Source Summit Europe wrapped up in Prague on Friday after a week centered on Linux, open AI, software security and open infrastructure. Now a Bitcoin conference is picking up many of the same questions—but inside Bitcoin’s protocol and application ecosystem.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Rust, open source and the next generation of contributors</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>TABConf’s schedule is itself organized in the open, and several sessions are explicitly about open-source development. Monday includes a talk on exposing Rust Bitcoin libraries across Kotlin, Swift, Python and Dart, plus a session on the growing center of Bitcoin open-source development in the Global South.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Thursday is scheduled to open with <strong>“rust-bitcoin: The Road to 1.0,”</strong> covering the push toward a production-ready 1.0 release. If Rust is unfamiliar, our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/osrust-001-what-is-rust-rustup-rustc-cargo-ownership-first-program/"><strong>Rust introduction</strong></a> explains why memory safety and strong tooling have made the language increasingly important in infrastructure software.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The open-source funding side matters too. Bitcoin development is not only about conferences and GitHub commits; long-lived infrastructure needs maintainers and grants. We recently covered how <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/btrust-stratum-v2-braidpool-bitcoin-core-mining-grants/"><strong>Btrust funded Stratum V2, Braidpool and Bitcoin Core mining infrastructure</strong></a>, another example of open-source work moving deeper into the mining stack.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Quantum risk is back on the schedule</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Quantum computing is not treated as a single keynote topic. TABConf 8 lists sessions on isogeny-based signatures, post-quantum bridges and quantum-safe Bitcoin designs. That does not mean Bitcoin faces an immediate quantum break; it means developers are already debating what cryptographic migration paths could look like before the problem becomes urgent.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>Why this conference is worth watching</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>TABConf is small compared with giant finance-oriented Bitcoin events, but that may be exactly why its schedule is useful. The agenda exposes what builders are arguing about before those ideas become product announcements: AI-assisted development, Rust libraries, mining-pool algorithms, privacy, post-quantum cryptography, open hardware and new Layer 2 designs.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>SmartNews takeaway:</strong> the final TABConf starts Monday, and the most interesting part may be how little of its agenda resembles a price conference. Bitcoin’s developer community is spending its time on code, cryptography, mining infrastructure and open-source tooling—and that is often where the next wave of Bitcoin technology appears first.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
