<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>NASA’s Perseverance rover has moved beyond the ancient lakebeds that made Jezero Crater famous and into terrain that may reach much deeper into Mars’ earliest history.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In a September 28 mission update, NASA said Perseverance has spent much of 2026 exploring a rugged area west of Jezero’s rim called Lac de Charmes. The mission team describes it as a “wild west frontier” of valleys, ridges and ancient rocks formed long before the younger sedimentary layers inside the crater.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>According to <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/blog/mars-has-its-charmes/">NASA’s latest Perseverance field update</a>, the rover has already created 13 abrasion patches across the region while scientists map how its rocks formed. Early observations indicate a mixture of igneous material produced by cooling lava or magma, impact-generated rock, and targets that may record both processes.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Perseverance is now reading an older chapter of Mars</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Jezero Crater gave Perseverance access to rocks shaped by rivers, a delta and an ancient lake. Lac de Charmes is different. Many of its rocks formed when Mars itself was younger and the inner solar system was experiencing far more frequent asteroid impacts.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><a href="https://www.space.com/astronomy/mars/mars-rover-explores-wild-west-on-the-red-planet-space-photo-of-the-day-for-oct-1-2026">Space.com’s October 1 exploration update</a> notes that the terrain contains some of the oldest rocks Perseverance has encountered so far. That gives the rover a chance to investigate questions about the early Martian crust that cannot be answered only from the younger lake sediments inside Jezero.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The mission team has deliberately followed a looping path through Lac de Charmes rather than racing toward a single destination. Each abrasion exposes fresh rock beneath the weathered surface, giving instruments a cleaner look at mineral chemistry before scientists decide which targets deserve deeper study or sampling.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The campaign began with a difficult climb out of Jezero</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The shift into this terrain began when Perseverance finished climbing the western rim of Jezero Crater. In <a href="https://twitter.com/NASAPersevere/status/1867245095260742030">its December 12, 2024 status update</a>, the rover reported reaching the rim after roughly 3.5 months of climbing and about 1,640 feet of vertical elevation gain.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That climb changed the mission from studying one ancient lake system to comparing several kinds of Martian crust across a much broader geologic setting.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Ingenuity’s old hardware is helping Perseverance drive farther</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>One of the most interesting engineering details in the new update is that Perseverance is using a repurposed processor that originally supported communications with the Ingenuity helicopter. NASA says the processor is now helping a new navigation algorithm deliver longer and more accurate drives across the difficult terrain.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is a useful example of the same autonomy trend BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/02/nasa-astra-ai-robot-fleet-autonomous-science/">NASA’s AI robot fleet experiments</a>: spacecraft are gradually being given more onboard ability to decide how to move, navigate and prioritize science without waiting for every detail to come from Earth.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Lac de Charmes matters</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The scientific value of this region is not that the rover has already found evidence of life there. It has not. The value is that these rocks may preserve a period of Martian history older than the lake environments Perseverance spent its first years studying.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>If researchers can determine whether different outcrops formed from ancient magma, giant impacts or later alteration, they can reconstruct how Mars’ crust developed during the violent early history of the solar system.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That knowledge also feeds into the much longer-term question of human activity on Mars. BitcoinVersus.Tech has examined both <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/28/plants-on-mars-how-interstellar-labs-biopods-are-changing-agriculture-2/">closed-environment agriculture concepts for Mars</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/21/russian-scientists-unveil-plasma-engine-cutting-mars-travel-to-30-days/">advanced propulsion concepts aimed at shortening Mars travel</a>. Perseverance is working several steps earlier in that chain: figuring out what the planet is actually made of, where its most ancient materials are, and how its surface evolved.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The rover is still opening new terrain</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Perseverance is no longer operating only as the rover that explored Jezero’s delta. Its current campaign is turning it into a mobile survey of some of the oldest accessible terrain in the region around the crater.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That makes the next sampling decisions especially important. Every abrasion patch helps narrow the map, but a core sample from the right ancient outcrop could preserve a piece of Mars’ earliest geologic history for future laboratory study.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
