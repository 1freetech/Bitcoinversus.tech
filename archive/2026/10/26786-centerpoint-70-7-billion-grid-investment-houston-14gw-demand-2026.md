<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>CenterPoint Energy increased its 10-year capital investment plan by $4 billion to $70.7 billion on October 27, 2026, as Greater Houston prepares for potentially 14 gigawatts of additional electric demand by 2030.</strong> The Houston-based utility disclosed the changes alongside its <a href="https://investors.centerpointenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/centerpoint-energy-reports-strong-q3-2026-results-raises">third-quarter earnings</a>. The 14 GW figure is a company demand forecast—not 14 GW of electricity generation or connected data-center capacity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Key Takeaways</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>The 2026–2035 investment plan increased from $66.7 billion to $70.7 billion.</li><li>CenterPoint moved another $1.5 billion in planned projects into 2026–2030.</li><li>Large-load interconnections remain subject to ERCOT review, construction and regulatory conditions.</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Where the $70.7 billion goes</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to the <a href="https://investors.centerpointenergy.com/events">investor presentation</a>, about $51.5 billion of the spending is allocated to electric infrastructure, $19 billion to natural gas and roughly $0.2 billion to corporate needs. The utility also identifies at least $10 billion of additional investment opportunities beyond its current plan. Neither figure should be mistaken for a fully committed data-center construction budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The additional spending reflects pressure on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/24/nucor-165-million-electrical-grid-utility-structures-indiana-alabama-2026/">transmission equipment supply chains</a> and growing requests from industrial facilities and AI campuses. CenterPoint says roughly 10 GW of base demand and about 2 GW of studied demand remain in the first phase of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/ercot-pushes-bitcoin-mines-toward-hardware-tested-grid-models/">ERCOT's large-load review</a>. The utility cautions that approval, actual consumption and energization dates are uncertain.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Grid reliability is part of the investment case</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The utility's separate <a href="https://investors.centerpointenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/greater-houston-resiliency-initiative-ghri-update-centerpoint">October 6 resiliency update</a> reported more than 83,500 storm-resilient poles installed and 850 automation devices deployed. Those are completed upgrades, while the 14 GW load projection remains prospective. CenterPoint also projects approximately $5 billion in customer savings over a decade; that is a company estimate, not a realized reduction in bills.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/nerc-ferc-computational-load-50mw-100kv-owners-operators/">large AI and Bitcoin mining loads</a>, the immediate question is when transmission and distribution capacity becomes available—not merely how much capital is announced. The plan signals major grid investment, but each proposed connection still requires engineering, financing and regulatory milestones.</p>
