<p><a href="https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/artificial-intelligence/intel-accelerates-open-infrastructure-agentic-ai-era.html">Intel says</a> select Xeon 6+ rack configurations can scale beyond <strong>46,000 CPU cores</strong> while drawing <strong>133.45 kW</strong> at the rack level, giving agentic-AI systems a dense x86 layer for orchestration, data movement and tool execution.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The design was shown at OCP Global Summit 2026 alongside Intel’s GPU D-series. Many of the Xeon systems follow Open Rack v3, which matters because operators can combine high-density CPUs with accelerators while keeping the surrounding rack, power and management architecture modular.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Intel’s D-series adds the accelerator side of the stack: up to 480 GB of LPDDR5X in partner designs, 1.5 TB/s memory bandwidth and 1.3 PFLOPS of FP8 compute within a 350 W envelope. <a href="https://qz.com/intel-gpu-d-series-ai-inference-chip-ocp-summit-101326">Quartz noted</a> that a single card can support models up to 300 billion parameters, while an eight-GPU system can move beyond one trillion parameters.</p>

<p>The rack announcement builds on BitcoinVersus.Tech coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/intels-new-ai-gpu-supports-up-to-480gb-of-memory/">Intel’s 480 GB AI GPU</a>, the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/open-compute-project-nexus-ai-engineers/">Open Compute Project’s AI engineering work</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/12/cisco-silicon-one-g302-51-2tbps-ai-racks/">Cisco’s 51.2 Tb/s AI-rack silicon</a>. The larger trend is clear: AI infrastructure is being optimized at rack scale, not component by component.</p>
