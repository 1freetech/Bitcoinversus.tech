<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>GPS is a satellite system that helps a device figure out where it is on Earth.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Your phone, car, smartwatch, hiking GPS, and many other devices can listen to GPS satellites and use their signals to estimate your location.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Does GPS Mean?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>GPS means Global Positioning System.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>It is a U.S.-owned system of satellites, ground stations, and receivers. The satellites circle Earth and continuously send radio signals containing very accurate time and position information.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The U.S. government's <a href="https://www.gps.gov/gps"><strong>GPS.gov</strong></a> explains that GPS provides positioning, navigation, and timing services around the world.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ArABLo8Mog","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true} --><figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-video is-provider-youtube wp-block-embed-youtube"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ArABLo8Mog]</div><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>The Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum gives a short visual explanation of how GPS works.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Does Your Phone Know Where You Are?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Your phone listens for signals from several GPS satellites above Earth.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Each signal includes the satellite's position and a very precise time. Your phone compares when those signals were sent with when they arrived.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That tiny difference in time tells the phone roughly how far away each satellite is.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Does GPS Need Several Satellites?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Knowing your distance from one satellite is not enough to tell exactly where you are.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>With signals from several satellites, your device can compare multiple distances and narrow your position down to one place.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>GPS.gov explains that a receiver can use signals from at least four satellites to determine its three-dimensional position and time.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Does GPS Need the Internet?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>GPS itself comes from satellite signals, not from Wi-Fi or cellular internet.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>But the map on your screen may still need internet access to download streets, traffic, businesses, or directions. That is why a phone can sometimes know its location even when the map around it has not loaded.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Uses GPS?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>GPS is used for much more than driving directions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Phones and smartwatches</li><li>Cars and delivery vehicles</li><li>Airplanes and ships</li><li>Hiking and outdoor navigation</li><li>Farming and surveying</li><li>Emergency services</li><li>Precise timing for networks and other systems</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Satellites are also becoming more connected to everyday phones in other ways. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/starlink-mobile-800-mhz-spectrum-indoor-phone-coverage/"><strong>satellite-to-phone coverage</strong></a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Can GPS Be Wrong?</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Yes. Tall buildings, mountains, heavy tree cover, indoor spaces, radio interference, and other conditions can make satellite signals harder to receive accurately.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is why your location dot sometimes jumps around or takes a few seconds to settle.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Easy Way to Remember It</strong></h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>GPS is a group of satellites that helps your device figure out where it is.</strong></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The satellites send precise timing signals. Your device listens to several of them, compares the signals, and calculates its location.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That is the basic idea.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
