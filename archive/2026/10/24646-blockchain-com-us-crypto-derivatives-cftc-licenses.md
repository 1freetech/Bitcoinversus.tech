<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Blockchain.com says it has filed two applications with the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission that could expand its American business into regulated crypto derivatives and prediction markets.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/blockchaincom-files-for-cftc-licenses-to-offer-crypto-derivatives-and-prediction-markets-in-the-us-302905076.html">October 13 company announcement</a>, Blockchain.com said it is seeking registration as a Futures Commission Merchant, or FCM, and designation as a Designated Contract Market, or DCM. If approved, the combination could allow the company to offer U.S. retail and institutional customers crypto futures, options and event contracts.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The distinction matters. A <a href="https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/TradingOrganizations/DCMs/index.htm">CFTC-designated contract market</a> is an exchange operating under federal derivatives oversight, while an FCM is the intermediary that can accept customer orders and assets for futures-related trading. Blockchain.com has announced applications, not approvals, and has not provided a decision date.</p>

<p>The filing lands as regulated crypto products become more varied.
