# Finland’s 30 Bitcoin Heating Systems Face a 2028 Halving Test

*Complete canonical WordPress Gutenberg body retained following publication. The volunteer footer, original 5,000-satoshi QR code, media URLs, and original source citations are preserved.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Thirty buildings now use Finnish startup SunBit’s Bitcoin mining heat systems, according to founder Elmeri Suomi’s <a href="https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/11/08/these-finland-homes-get-free-heat-from-bitcoin-miners-as-long-as-btc-stays-above-usd70-000">November 8, 2026 interview with CoinDesk</a>. One 180-square-meter home in Nokia disconnected from district heating entirely, replacing it with a liquid-cooled Bitcoin Boiler and separate hot-water tank. The installations offer an appealing engineering proposition: mine Bitcoin while recovering heat that conventional ASIC operations would normally reject outdoors.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A more difficult question is whether the economics survive Bitcoin’s next block-subsidy halving, expected in 2028. Even when the heat is useful, running mining hardware consumes electricity, and mining income fluctuates with Bitcoin’s price, network difficulty and fees. SunBit’s 30-system figure is a company-reported deployment count, not an independently audited census.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">How a Bitcoin boiler turns electricity into hot water</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>According to <a href="https://sunbit.fi/en/articles/how-bitcoin-heating-works">SunBit’s technical explanation</a>, one liquid-cooled mining unit consumes approximately <strong>5.3 kilowatts</strong>. A boiler holds two units, providing up to 10.6 kW of heat. Its water loop can reach 60°C, connecting to hydronic radiators, floor heating or domestic hot-water storage. Remote controls adjust mining activity to electricity prices and equipment conditions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>At a constant 5.3 kW, a single unit would consume about <strong>46,428 kWh annually</strong> (5.3 × 8,760 hours). Nearly all electricity eventually becomes heat, but not all heat necessarily enters the building. Excess output can be expelled through an outdoor dry cooler. A conventional heat pump can deliver approximately 2.5–3 kWh of heat per kWh of electricity, so the mining boiler competes through cryptocurrency revenue rather than superior thermal efficiency.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A 180 m² house, an industrial hall and the limits of free heat</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://sunbit.fi/en/cases">manufacturer’s case studies</a> show different deployment models. In Kotka, a 200 m² home retained its oil boiler as backup. In Vimpeli, a 3,000 m² industrial hall combines mining heat, an oil system and an air-to-water heat pump, while a 30 kW solar array can supply surplus electricity. The company also describes an installation in the Netherlands.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CoinDesk reported a modeled annual electricity expense near <strong>$5,200</strong> for one 5.3 kW unit, against approximately <strong>$6,100 in annual Bitcoin receipts</strong> after SunBit’s service charge under the company’s assumptions. The roughly $900 difference comes before equipment depreciation, maintenance and financing. A quoted installed system starts around $20,000, while SunBit advertises three to six years of payback under favorable conditions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The 2028 halving changes the projected heating bill</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bitcoin’s current subsidy of 3.125 BTC per block is scheduled to fall to 1.5625 BTC at the next halving. At unchanged network hashrate, transaction fees, hardware output and market conditions, a miner would receive substantially fewer new coins. CoinDesk’s sensitivity analysis estimated that SunBit’s modeled electricity-offset economics would require Bitcoin above roughly <strong>$131,000</strong> following the halving. The threshold is a conditional scenario, not a price forecast or universal break-even point.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>SunBit’s publicly available calculator does not incorporate the 2028 event into its standard payback estimate. Bitcoin’s market price might rise, but competitors can also add more efficient ASICs, reducing each machine’s share of rewards. Our earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/06/finland-uses-bitcoin-mining-to-heat-homes/">Finland mining-heat coverage</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/top-7-bitcoin-mining-heat-reuse-2026/">2026 heat-reuse roundup</a> documented the broader opportunity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Can heating become part of the mining business model?</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Heat reuse offers a useful outlet for some energy that would otherwise be wasted, particularly where buildings already have hydronic plumbing and substantial winter demand. Competing concepts include <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/22/heatbit-bitwater-bitcoin-mining-water-heater-34-ths/">Heatbit’s household water heater</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/bitmain-antrack-ar31-20-hydro-miners-47u-heat-recovery/">BITMAIN’s larger hydro-mining racks</a>. Heating requirements, local electricity tariffs, coolant maintenance and summer heat rejection all affect viability.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>For homeowners, the appropriate comparison is total lifetime heating expense, including mining income, electricity, installation, repairs and alternative equipment. The boilers keep producing warmth even when Bitcoin mining becomes unprofitable. Whether that warmth is affordable remains a separate, measurable question.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
