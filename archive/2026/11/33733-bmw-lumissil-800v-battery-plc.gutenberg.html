BMW Group and semiconductor supplier Lumissil Microsystems have demonstrated a way for an electric-car battery to send monitoring information across the electrical connections already carrying power. Their November 10, 2026 announcement describes two-way power-line communication, or PLC, across a complete 800-volt Gen6 battery system. The potential reward is fewer dedicated communication wires and connectors inside future battery packs.

The experiment used BMW's sixth-generation hardware, already associated with the new iX3, to connect a central battery-management controller with multiple cell-supervisory circuits. Lumissil says the system transmitted cell measurements and control commands under varying electrical conditions. The company does not provide quantitative packet-error rates, data throughput, saved wiring mass or production cost figures.

How can electricity and data share the same conductors?

In conventional packs, monitoring circuits sample cell voltages and temperatures and pass those readings through dedicated data connections to the battery management system. Power-line communication instead superimposes a modulated signal on existing conductive paths. Receiver electronics recover the signal while the conductors continue serving their primary electrical function.

BMW and Lumissil call the approach “No New Wire.” The phrase describes the communication network. The high-voltage conductors, battery sensors, protective devices and the electrical insulation needed for vehicle safety do not disappear. PLC is also not the same as wireless communication; the information still travels through a physical electrical connection.

The test connected a central controller and several cell-monitoring nodes. According to Lumissil's November 10 release, bidirectional exchanges worked at individual-cell and complete-pack levels over representative voltage and current conditions. The results establish feasibility on the tested architecture, not certification for all driving, crash or aging environments.

Why a simpler battery harness matters

Each dedicated connector adds an assembly step and another interface to inspect. A communication scheme requiring fewer parts could make battery construction simpler and potentially reduce mass, cost and service complexity. The companies present those as expected benefits, rather than measured reductions.

BMW's Gen6 pack uses a cell-to-pack arrangement that places cylindrical cells directly into the high-voltage battery housing. BMW's central Energy Master consolidates essential control functions. The company has separately expanded battery manufacturing, including the new Woodruff plant in South Carolina. The experimental PLC architecture should not be confused with a confirmed wiring change in every BMW vehicle already leaving those factories.

The harder problem is electrical noise and safety

An 800-volt traction system is not a quiet communication channel. Switching inverters, charging hardware and changing battery impedances can interfere with a modulated signal. A peer-reviewed study of automotive high-voltage PLC channels describes noise and coexistence problems that any real implementation must manage.

Production approval would also require robust isolation, diagnostics, fault handling, electromagnetic compatibility and dependable communication when components age or fail. A successful laboratory exchange does not automatically establish automotive functional-safety compliance. The November announcement gives no commercial integration schedule, production pricing or independent endurance data.

BMW's broader EV strategy has already been covered in our reporting on its electric-vehicle sales mix. Parallel innovations include 800-volt DC power equipment for AI facilities and a graphene sensor powered by nearby electrical fields. Neither uses the same battery PLC system, but both illustrate efforts to get more functionality from existing electrical infrastructure.

The next test is a production-ready battery

The practical questions are measurable: how many connectors could be removed, what weight would be saved, how often do messages fail under electromagnetic stress and how safely does the system handle missing data? BMW and Lumissil plan to continue development. For now, the confirmed milestone is a complete-pack communication demonstration, not a wire-free vehicle or a production launch.
