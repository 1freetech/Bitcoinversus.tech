# Wells Fargo Nearly Doubled Its Bitcoin ETF Holdings

*Verbatim published WordPress Gutenberg body including the canonical volunteer footer, original donation QR, verified media source image and repaired YouTube blocks.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Wells Fargo nearly doubled its reported holdings of U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds during the third quarter. <a href="https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/bitcoin-etp-13f-institutional-holdings-q3-2026-wealth-channel-wells-fargo">Galaxy Research's November 19, 2026 analysis</a> found holdings rising from <strong>15,931 to 30,553 BTC-equivalent</strong> between June 30 and September 30, an increase of approximately <strong>91.8%</strong>. At September prices, the resulting position was valued near $2.55 billion.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The report arrives alongside <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/20/blackrock-ibit-record-541-million-bitcoin-etf-outflow-november-19-2026/">BlackRock's record $541.08 million IBIT net outflow</a> on November 19. Different dates matter: Wells Fargo's filing covers September holdings, while the record fund withdrawal occurred seven weeks later. Neither dataset proves what each Wells Fargo-managed account did during November's selling.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Wells Fargo passes Jane Street</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Galaxy examined filings from 2,166 managers. Wells Fargo overtook trading firm Jane Street, which held 23,167 BTC-equivalent, becoming the largest reported Bitcoin fund holder in the 13F dataset. The bank's position spans fund shares managed for various accounts; it is <strong>not evidence of Bitcoin held directly on Wells Fargo's corporate balance sheet</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Form 13F captures certain securities over which investment managers exercise discretion. Galaxy converts reported ETF shares into the corresponding underlying Bitcoin exposure. Client assets, dealer positions and hedge-fund inventories have different economic purposes. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/10/blackrock-bitcoin-self-custody-ibit-swaps-record-shares-fall-november-2026/">IBIT custody report</a> explains the distinction between fund shares and personally held Bitcoin.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A record quarter for professional ownership</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Reporting managers collectively held <strong>356,491 BTC-equivalent</strong> across 13 spot Bitcoin products at September 30, worth $29.78 billion. Their net quarterly addition was 49,229 BTC-equivalent, approximately $4.11 billion at quarter-end prices. The number of reporting managers increased by 191 to 2,166, reversing two quarters of declining participation.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The increase was uneven. The 50 largest managers added 59,578 BTC-equivalent, but other filers collectively reduced their positions by 10,349. Advisors and wealth platforms accounted for <strong>40.5%</strong> of 13F-reported exposure, up from 24.3% a year earlier. The largest firms drove most buying, even while the number of smaller holders expanded.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">November flows tell a different story</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Galaxy estimated that U.S. spot Bitcoin funds held 1,290,670 BTC at September's end, falling by 38,531 BTC to 1,252,139 by November 16. The November 19 IBIT record outflow occurred later still. A strong third quarter cannot establish how institutional investors behaved during the subsequent downturn.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the SEC filings leave out</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Form 13F generally reports eligible long securities positions within 45 days of quarter-end. It omits short positions and most derivatives, meaning some positions may be hedged. Client-directed brokerage holdings can also be absent. Late filings, amendments and overlapping parent-company reporting create further limits. Galaxy's figures measure professional fund exposure, not every Bitcoin owner.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The next meaningful comparison will be fourth-quarter filings, expected in February 2027. They should clarify how institutional holdings changed after the November selloff. For now, the verified development is a substantial September increase in Wells Fargo-managed Bitcoin ETF positions, followed by weaker fund flows later in the year.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
