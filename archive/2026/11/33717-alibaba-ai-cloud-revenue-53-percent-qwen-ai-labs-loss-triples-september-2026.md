# Alibaba’s AI Cloud Grows 53% as AI Labs Losses Triple

*Complete published Gutenberg body, including exact canonical volunteer footer and original donation QR. Accounting figures compare September quarters only.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Alibaba's AI cloud business is expanding rapidly, yet its spending on Qwen applications is rising even faster. In <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926130115/tm2630263d1_ex99-1.htm">financial results published November 19, 2026</a>, the Chinese technology group reported <strong>RMB60.97 billion ($9.09 billion)</strong> in AI Cloud and Compute Services revenue for the September quarter, up <strong>53%</strong> from a year earlier. Separately, AI Labs and Applications recorded a <strong>RMB10.50 billion adjusted EBITA loss</strong>, roughly three times its year-earlier loss.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The figures describe two distinct businesses, not an accounting contradiction. Alibaba's cloud division earns money selling compute, cloud services and associated infrastructure, while its AI Labs division spends heavily on models, agents and inference for consumer applications. The distinction matters as companies across the world try to turn expensive AI research into recurring revenue.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Cloud customers are spending more on AI</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Alibaba's cloud revenue from outside customers grew <strong>64%</strong> year over year; its recurring external-customer revenue, excluding compute equipment solutions, grew <strong>67%</strong>. The segment's adjusted EBITA increased 121% to RMB8.84 billion, reflecting higher revenue and better operating efficiency. The recurring category includes usage-based and subscription services, not a guarantee of long-term contracts.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company combines cloud infrastructure with its T-Head semiconductor operation. Its September <a href="https://www.alibabacloud.com/en/press-room/alibaba-unveils-roadmap-on-full-stack-ai-strategy">Apsara Conference roadmap</a> outlined new models, AI chips and an agent-oriented cloud. Alibaba listed the Zhenwu V900 chip for a planned first-quarter 2027 commercial release, not a completed mass deployment. For hardware context, see our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/19/nvidia-fy27-q3-revenue-treemap-hyperscale-enterprise-edge-2026/">NVIDIA cloud and enterprise revenue analysis</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Qwen inference costs overwhelm AI Labs revenue</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>AI Labs and Applications revenue rose 51% to RMB4.44 billion, but its adjusted EBITA loss widened to RMB10.50 billion from RMB3.44 billion. Alibaba attributed the deeper shortfall mainly to increased AI investment and higher Qwen app inference expenses. Serving responses to users consumes processing resources even when the underlying model has already been trained.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A comparison of adjusted EBITA shows cloud's RMB8.84 billion positive contribution against the labs' RMB10.50 billion loss. The figures cannot determine group profitability on their own because Alibaba reports other segments, shared costs and accounting adjustments. The company's total September-quarter revenue grew 10% to RMB271.52 billion, while reported net income benefited substantially from investment gains.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">AI spending is also pressuring cash flow</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Group free cash flow was a <strong>RMB34.47 billion outflow</strong>, versus RMB21.84 billion a year earlier. Alibaba attributed the change largely to cloud infrastructure expenditure, partly offset by better quick-commerce performance and customer prepayments. It nevertheless reported RMB487.93 billion in cash and other liquid investments at September 30, so negative free cash flow does not mean the company has exhausted its reserves.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Large AI platforms increasingly face a similar investment trade-off. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/blackstone-schwarzman-jack-ma-ai-data-centers-qts-bloomberg-interview/">Blackstone data-center investment coverage</a> examines infrastructure capital requirements. Alibaba's <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/himax-always-on-vision-qwen-glasses/">Qwen-powered wearables</a> and its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/31/brooklyn-nets-alibaba-360-degree-ai-replays-barclays-center-2026/">basketball replay deployment</a> illustrate potential uses beyond general chatbots.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the next results must prove</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Alibaba has demonstrated both growing demand for paid AI infrastructure and widening costs in AI applications. Investors will need to watch recurring customer revenue, cloud margins, inference economics, free cash flow and the pace of capital spending. The announced chip roadmap may support future efficiency, but neither a product launch nor new hardware automatically guarantees lower costs or higher returns.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
