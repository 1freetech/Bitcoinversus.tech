The New York Stock Exchange announced November 2, 2026, that Broadridge Financial Solutions, Computershare, Equiniti and Superstate have joined the group of transfer-agent partners for its planned tokenized-securities platform. The additions follow earlier agreements with Securitize and tZERO, bringing the roster to six companies. The new trading venue remains subject to regulatory approval and has not begun operating.

NYSE says the partners will connect blockchain-based ownership records with shareholder services that public companies already require, including entitlement verification, investor communications, dividend administration and proxy voting. Computershare, Equiniti and Broadridge together serve nearly 5,000 publicly listed companies, including almost 99% of the S&P 500, according to the exchange. Those client relationships do not imply that the companies have committed to issue tokenized shares.

Why transfer agents matter for tokenized shares

A conventional transfer agent maintains shareholder ownership records and helps public companies distribute information and administer corporate actions. Tokenization represents an asset using a digital token recorded on a distributed ledger. Issuers still need to establish who owns the underlying security, who may vote, and how economic rights survive transfers among wallets, brokers and custody systems.

Broadridge’s November 2 announcement says its governance smart contracts will connect tokenized securities to meeting agendas, voting rules and verifiable voting results. The proposed service includes wallet authentication linked to verified entitlements. Investors would be able to participate through compatible digital wallets, brokerage interfaces or Broadridge’s existing ProxyVote platform.

The governance arrangement differs from Morgan Stanley’s use of DTCC tokenization infrastructure, which concerns securities already held by the Depository Trust Company. It also differs from Canton Strategic’s tokenized-share trades. Both are related developments, but neither establishes that the planned NYSE-affiliated trading venue is operational.

The exchange’s trading plan

NYSE proposes combining its Pillar matching engine with blockchain-enabled post-trade systems supporting multiple settlement and custody networks. The future venue is designed for continuous trading, settlement described as instantaneous, orders denominated in dollar amounts and stablecoin-based funding. Operational details, supported chains, production volumes, venue launch date and approval conditions have not been finalized publicly.

The exchange says issuer-sponsored and third-party custodial tokens would remain interchangeable with traditional securities and preserve dividend and governance rights. Existing rules, custody arrangements, reconciliation procedures and regulatory obligations will determine whether such promises hold in commercial practice. BitcoinVersus.Tech previously covered the SEC’s conditional framework for tokenized-stock trading; the regulatory pathways and projects should not be treated as identical.

Governance rights remain the hard problem

Transfer agents must reconcile share ownership, beneficial holders, wallet permissions and votes when the same economic interest can exist in conventional and tokenized forms. The NYSE says the new partners combine established issuer relationships with blockchain-native infrastructure. However, a partner announcement alone cannot prove reliable instant settlement, error-free voting or lower operating costs.

The growing market has also included tokenized equities and ETFs on BNB Chain and issuer objections to competing stock-tokenization proposals. Ownership rights, issuer consent and the legal structure of the tokens can differ significantly among products, even when they use similar blockchain terminology.

The next measurable milestones are regulatory permission, the first eligible issuers and brokers, and actual transaction and shareholder-voting records on the NYSE-affiliated venue. Until then, the partnership expands planned market infrastructure rather than proving a live 24-hour stock exchange.
