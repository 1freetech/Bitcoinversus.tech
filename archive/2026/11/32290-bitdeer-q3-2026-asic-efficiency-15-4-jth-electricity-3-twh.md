<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bitdeer’s Bitcoin mining hardware became 23.4% more efficient per unit of hashing in Q3 2026, yet the company used 87.1% more electricity than a year earlier.</strong> The contrast, disclosed in Bitdeer’s November 10 earnings report, reflects a rapidly expanding mining fleet rather than a reversal in ASIC engineering progress.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitdeer reported average miner efficiency of <strong>15.4 joules per terahash (J/TH)</strong>, down from <strong>20.1 J/TH</strong> in Q3 2025. Total quarterly power usage nevertheless climbed from <strong>1.656 million megawatt-hours to 3.098 million MWh</strong>—equivalent to 1.656 and 3.098 terawatt-hours (TWh). Those figures are company-reported and unaudited.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A smaller energy cost per hash, a larger electricity footprint</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>J/TH measures the electricity required to sustain a given hashing rate: lower values mean less power is needed for identical computational output. At a constant <strong>1 EH/s</strong>, 20.1 J/TH implies roughly <strong>20.1 MW</strong> of miner load, while 15.4 J/TH implies <strong>15.4 MW</strong>. The theoretical reduction is 4.7 MW, or 23.4%, before changes in utilization, site overhead and other loads.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitdeer simultaneously expanded its mining capacity. The company reported average self-mining hashrate of <strong>79.6 EH/s</strong> during Q3 2026, compared with <strong>29.1 EH/s</strong> a year earlier. A new co-mining operation contributed another 23.0 EH/s of average quarterly hashrate. Combined mining output rose to <strong>3,855 BTC</strong>, versus 1,109 BTC in Q3 2025. More hashing across a much larger deployment can outweigh equipment-level efficiency gains.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">SEALMINER chips improve efficiency, but fleet averages differ</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitdeer’s <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/22/bitdeer-seal04-6-7-jth-chip-claim-vs-a4-9-45-jth/">SEAL04 and SEALMINER A4 hardware</a> offer a useful engineering comparison. Its A4 Ultra Hydro carries a manufacturer specification of <strong>9.45 J/TH at 886 TH/s</strong>, substantially below the fleet average. However, a product specification, an individual chip demonstration and a weighted fleet figure describe different measurement boundaries. The 9.45 J/TH figure must not be treated as a measured average across Bitdeer’s operating sites.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Earlier BitcoinVersus.Tech coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/13/canaan-bitdeer-bitcoin-asic-hardware-sales-decline-q3-2026-industry/">Bitdeer’s falling external ASIC sales</a> documented another side of its strategy: redirecting manufacturing output into proprietary and co-mining operations. The resulting fleet expansion helps explain rising company electricity demand.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Output gains do not guarantee stronger financial results</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Average electricity prices also rose from <strong>$43 to $47 per MWh</strong>. Bitdeer recorded <strong>$320.7 million</strong> in total Q3 revenue, up from $169.7 million, while its net loss widened to <strong>$125.5 million</strong> from $30.1 million. Higher power and depreciation expenses were among the factors cited.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Equipment efficiency still matters for miners facing volatile Bitcoin prices and network difficulty. Recent <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/14/powercompute-600-antminer-s21plusplus-85-phs-bitcoin-mining-november-2026/">PowerCompute fleet-replacement plans</a> illustrate a different strategy: adding hashpower without increasing contracted electrical capacity. The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/top-10-most-efficient-bitcoin-mining-machines-2026-j-th/">latest ASIC efficiency comparison</a> and our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/compute-per-joule-bitcoin-mining-supercomputers-singularity-power-efficiency-theory/">compute-per-joule analysis</a> provide further context.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bitdeer’s Q3 figures show why <strong>efficiency and total consumption must be reported separately</strong>. A lower J/TH rating establishes less miner power per hash—not lower aggregate electricity demand, lower grid impacts, or lower emissions. Those conclusions require information about fleet scale, operating hours, cooling overhead and each site’s actual electricity supply.</p>
