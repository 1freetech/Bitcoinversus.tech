# Laser-Made Plasma Accelerates Electrons in 95% of Tests

## Unique editorial angle and previous coverage
- Original Berkeley Lab 420nm FEL >90% *lasing-shot gain* and >1000 optical gain, WP 34142 (2025 experiment, DOE November 2026 retrospective).
- Berkeley Lab >8 hours *unattended FEL operation*, WP 34218 (April 2026 publication).
- HHU/Strathclyde modeled 2.3–4.4nm *water-window X-ray FEL*, WP 34277 (November 5, 2026 computational study, not hardware).
- Current WP 34299: *experimental plasma-filament witness acceleration* at INFN Frascati, a distinct mechanism and different performance metrics; no paper measuring FEL optical gain.
- WordPress searches for Galletti, "plasma filament," "250 MV/m," "0.5% energy jitter" found no duplicates before post creation.

## Source chronology and checked facts
- Galletti et al., Physical Review E 114 055201, published November 2, 2026: https://doi.org/10.1103/qjd9-p6dy
- Hebrew University institutional peer-review record: https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/experimental-demonstration-of-beam-driven-wakefield-acceleration-/
- Advanced Accelerator Concepts Conference July 2026 abstract reporting approximate 8 MeV in 3cm: https://indico.global/event/14577/contributions/155665/
- INFN Frascati March 2 2026 EuPRAXIA@SPARC_LAB TDR: https://w3.lnf.infn.it/eupraxiasparc_lab-technical-design-report-published/?lang=en
- Preprint February 26, accepted September 29, journal published November 2, 2026. November publication is a newly peer-reviewed record, not claim first performed in November.
- Direct experimental results: >250 MV/m field, witness acceleration observed in 95% of events, RMS energy jitter below 0.5%, preservation of witness charge and relative energy spread. Plasma formed via self-guided femtosecond laser in low pressure nitrogen; main acceleration is beam-driven wakefield.
- Approximate 8 MeV in 3 cm from conference. Units: 250 MV/m * 0.03 m = 7.5 MeV, consistent within approximation; NOT entire accelerator length.
- 95% is witness acceleration success, not >90% previous FEL lasing success; 0.5% is shot-to-shot beam energy jitter, not system electric efficiency or facility uptime.
