Which universities have the longest educational histories while still teaching students in 2026? This countdown ranks seven surviving higher-learning lineages by traditional founding or earliest documented teaching dates. These are not equivalent university-charter dates. Our oldest schools countdown similarly distinguishes institutional ancestry from uninterrupted modern form.

#7 University of Padua, Italy — 1222

Padua's own history traces its university to scholars who left Bologna seeking academic freedom. Its medical and astronomy traditions later attracted Galileo, while Elena Cornaro Piscopia earned a degree here in 1678. The university now teaches and researches across numerous fields.

The University of Padua's official eight-century heritage film, hosted through the university's own website.

#6 University of Salamanca, Spain — 1218

King Alfonso IX of León established Salamanca's Studium Generale in 1218, according to the university. Royal regulations followed in 1254; papal recognition came in 1255. Its legal and language scholarship shaped generations of Spanish-speaking academics.

Salamanca campus video linked by Queen Mary University of London's exchange-programme page.

#5 University of Cambridge, England — 1209

Cambridge records describe scholars arriving from Oxford in 1209 after conflict with townspeople. Their teaching community grew into colleges and research institutions. Today's laboratories and the early history of computing connect to our history of engineering education.

The University of Cambridge's official Welcome to Cambridge video, showing its modern colleges and student life.

#4 University of Oxford, England — teaching by 1096

Oxford says teaching existed by 1096, though there was no single founding charter. The university expanded after 1167 and developed colleges in the thirteenth century. Its historic libraries illustrate the value of preserving scholarship across generations.

The University of Oxford's own This is Oxford institutional video.

#3 University of Bologna, Italy — c. 1088

Bologna conventionally dates its Studium to 1088. Students organized teaching and hired professors, helping develop the university model familiar in Europe. Its legal scholarship underpins the long transformation of commerce and medieval contracts.

University of Bologna official film Nine Centuries of History, linked directly from its historical archive page.

#2 Al-Azhar University, Cairo, Egypt — c. 970–972

Al-Azhar began as a Fatimid-era mosque and teaching center. Its institutional history describes reforms culminating in a 1961 law that added medicine, engineering and other modern faculties. Its historic learning tradition predates those modern university structures.

Association of African Universities documentary on Al-Azhar University and its North Africa office.

#1 Al-Qarawiyyin, Fez, Morocco — 859

Guinness World Records recognizes Al-Qarawiyyin, founded in 859, as the oldest surviving continuously operating educational institution. Traditional accounts credit Fatima al-Fihri. Its origins were mosque-based; its later university status must not be confused with a ninth-century European-style charter.

NU Online's visual explainer of Al-Qarawiyyin's historic complex in Fez; traditional founding claims require scholarly context.

The distinction matters: a historian using the medieval European corporate university definition may rank Bologna first, while Guinness includes older African institutions of learning.
