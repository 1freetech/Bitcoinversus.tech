<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Hasbro is moving its fictional characters toward physical robotics.</strong> On November 10, 2026, Hasbro’s Sixth Wall Studio and Dynamic Creatures announced a partnership to develop expressive robotic characters for theme parks, resorts and entertainment destinations. The proposed systems would pair authorized voices and personalities with mobile hardware capable of moving, gesturing and responding to guests. No venue, specific licensed character or commercial opening date was announced.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">CharacterOS and SnowJay provide different pieces</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://newsroom.hasbro.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hasbro-launches-sixth-wall-new-ai-studio-building-next">Sixth Wall Studio</a>, which Hasbro launched in June, supplies CharacterOS and its Behavioral Licensing framework. The goal is to let rights holders authorize a fictional character’s voice, conversational conduct and personality rules while preserving licensing controls. Unlike an unrestricted character chatbot, an approved experience would need behavior boundaries that follow the character’s established identity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Dynamic Creatures contributes the physical layer. Its <a href="https://www.dynamiccreatures.com/snowjay">SnowJay robotics platform</a> coordinates movement, real-time control, perception, gesture execution and operational safety functions. The partners propose encounters in which a character greets a resort guest, assigns a story-related adventure and refers to the encounter during a later visit. Such continuity remains a planned capability rather than a documented customer installation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A mobile robot could turn toward visitors, match speech to body movement and react to changing surroundings. Delivering those behaviors reliably requires much more than a language model: sensors, actuation, control loops, animation timing, navigation, remote supervision and recovery from unexpected physical contact all matter.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Hugo is real, but its autonomy remains unfinished</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Dynamic Creatures revealed <strong>Hugo</strong>, a furry bipedal character prototype, in October. The company identifies <strong>43 actuators</strong>, while contemporary reporting cites 57 joints. The <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/13/business/dynamic-creatures-robotic-mascots/">Boston Globe reported that Hugo’s filmed interactions were teleoperated</a> and that independent physical interaction was still under development. A carefully staged demonstration does not establish unsupervised operation among visitors.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":32410,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The startup was founded by former Boston Dynamics and robotics-research leaders and serves as <a href="https://bostondynamics.com/news/dynamic-creatures-launches-mobile-interactive-character-robots-for-hospitality-and-entertainment/">Boston Dynamics’ official entertainment and hospitality partner</a>. It remains a separate business. The companies have not identified the precise underlying robot platform for any future Hasbro installation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Remembering visitors raises privacy and safety questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hasbro says proposed memory features would depend on <strong>guest choice and permissions</strong>, with controls determined for each location. Its initial Sixth Wall applications target audiences aged <strong>13 and older</strong>. Neither partner has established public specifications for visitor identification, memory retention, consent withdrawal or autonomous operation at a named park.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Physical safety will also shape deployment. Actuated robots moving near guests need reliable stopping, collision management, navigation and tested human oversight. An expressive prototype that dances, hugs or waves under remote control cannot alone demonstrate safe behavior in crowded venues.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> The Hasbro–Dynamic Creatures partnership is confirmed, and Hugo demonstrates existing expressive robot hardware. Named deployments, autonomous guest-facing safety, implementation of visitor memory and commercial launch schedules remain unverified.</p>
