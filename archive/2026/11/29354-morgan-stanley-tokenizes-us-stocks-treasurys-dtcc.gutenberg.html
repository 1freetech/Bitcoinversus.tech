Morgan Stanley confirmed on November 3, 2026, that it is among the first banks converting U.S. equities and Treasury securities into blockchain-based representations through the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation’s new Tokenization Service. The disclosure moves the firm beyond announcing an intention to participate: it says the bank is actively using infrastructure that launched October 28. The bank has not identified its tokenized securities, transaction volumes or the blockchain used.

The Depository Trust Company, or DTC, holds securities for market participants and supports the infrastructure behind ownership transfers. Its parent DTCC reported more than $114 trillion in total assets under custody when the service went live. That enormous custody figure is not the amount Morgan Stanley has tokenized. At launch, 20 institutions signed access agreements, including JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America, Fidelity and Morgan Stanley; signing an access agreement alone does not prove each participant completed transactions.

What changes when an existing security becomes a token

The service, called DTS, creates a digital entitlement representing a security already held at DTC. Eligible participants register approved digital wallets and instruct DTC to mint tokens or burn them when returning an entitlement to conventional form. DTCC says the blockchain representation preserves the underlying economic rights and investor protections. The token is not a newly issued share created from nothing, and conversion should not multiply the number of beneficial claims to the same underlying asset.

The permitted starting categories include selected Russell 1000 equities, exchange-traded funds following major indexes and U.S. Treasury bills, notes and bonds. DTCC initially connected the service to the Canton Network and a private Besu network. Besu is Ethereum-compatible blockchain software used in permissioned configurations. Additional network connectivity is planned, subject to infrastructure and regulatory checks.

The distinction between actual custody and a digital claim is important. A security's legal ownership records, corporate actions, settlement obligations and custody controls still require reconciliation. Institutional tokenization is therefore different from price-tracking crypto instruments that provide no enforceable interest in the referenced stock. Earlier BitcoinVersus.Tech reporting on the SEC’s tokenized-stock framework explains a separate market structure; the two regulatory arrangements should not be conflated.

Production service, not a new public stock exchange

DTCC tested live-production workflows in July 2026, including collateral pledges, securities lending, Treasury repurchase agreements, equity transfers and delivery-versus-payment settlement. The October 28 launch establishes an operating service for approved DTC participants. It does not mean ordinary retail investors can trade Morgan Stanley-issued tokens around the clock on an unrestricted exchange. DTC wallets, entitlement rules and institutional access controls still determine participation.

Morgan Stanley’s digital-assets strategy head Amy Oldenburg said practical integration experience would inform the bank’s future capabilities. Separately, Morgan Stanley is targeting the first quarter of 2027 for tokenized-equity support through its own alternative trading system, according to The Block’s October reporting. That trading-system objective remains a plan, not a live November rollout.

Bank adoption and the unanswered figures

Other institutions are developing related systems. BitcoinVersus.Tech has documented a Canton-network OTC swap between DRW and Marex, tokenized sterling deposits among British banks, and the NYSE’s planned tokenized-equity transfer-agent network. Deposits, securities and derivatives represent different legal claims, even when their electronic records use distributed ledgers.

Benefits proposed by DTCC include more flexible collateral, extended transfer windows and faster movement of securities between approved systems. Real-world outcomes still depend on wallet security, operational recovery, settlement finality, network interoperability and willingness of counterparties to transact. The launch does not prove lower trading costs or greater liquidity for Morgan Stanley’s customers.

The strongest next measures will be disclosed tokenized asset volumes, identifiable securities, use-case transaction data and verified progress toward Morgan Stanley’s planned 2027 trading functionality. As of November 5, the bank’s announcement establishes early use of an operating depository service, but it does not quantify the economic scale.
