Opera is moving the technology behind its MiniPay stablecoin wallet to Privy, the wallet infrastructure company acquired by Stripe in 2025. Announced November 19, 2026, the upgrade covers a service with more than 20 million cumulative wallet activations and over 600 million transactions since launch. Opera says the migration will begin rolling out during 2026. It has not said every wallet has already been migrated.

MiniPay launched inside Opera Mini in 2023 and expanded to Android and iOS. Its balances reside on the Celo blockchain. Privy will handle wallet creation, key security and sign-in, while users retain an option to export their keys. The migration is more than a new login screen because recovering access to a financial wallet is a security-critical operation.

One wallet across two phone platforms

Opera says MiniPay users will be able to sign in through email, a social identity, a passkey or an external wallet and access the same balance and transaction history on Android or iOS. Details saved once may be shared with participating services such as Noah and Gnosis Pay when a user elects to do so. Exportable keys are central to its self-custodial claim.

The arrangement combines two goals that can conflict: easier account recovery and user control of funds. Underlying private-key protection, authentication recovery, phishing resistance and provider outages still matter. It does not turn a stablecoin into a bank deposit or eliminate the risks of compromised credentials and faulty third-party applications.

Stripe handles deposits as well as wallet infrastructure

The news follows Opera’s November 12 announcement of Link by Stripe deposits in the United States and European Union. Eligible users can add stablecoins using a card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, subject to local availability and verification. Link concerns funding a wallet; Privy concerns authentication and its underlying wallet infrastructure. Neither arrangement means Stripe guarantees the stablecoin’s value.

Competitors are pursuing related infrastructure. Privy has also expanded embedded wallets on XRP Ledger, while Rain supplies embedded stablecoin wallets and BVNK distributes stablecoin payment infrastructure across multiple networks. Integration details and custody models differ across products.

Wallet activations more than doubled

Opera reported over 8 million activated wallets in July 2025, 10 million in September, 11 million in December, 12.6 million in February 2026 and more than 14 million by March. The November announcement placed the total above 20 million, alongside more than 600 million lifetime transactions in over 70 countries and 77 Mini Apps.

Activations are not the same as monthly active people. Lifetime transactions are not revenue, and a growth curve drawn between irregular company-reported milestones would imply precision not available from the disclosed data. The statistics illustrate the scale of the proposed wallet transition, not independent proof of retention or profitability.

What must work next

Opera says rollout will start in markets where MiniPay operates. Cross-device login, preservation of balances and history, reliable key exports, consistent recovery procedures and transparent incident response are the practical tests. The company has not supplied independent migration success rates or new security performance benchmarks.

For users, the significant question is whether a convenient new sign-in system preserves meaningful control over funds. Stablecoin settlement partnerships and Stripe’s broader stablecoin expansion show how quickly payment infrastructure is changing, but a successful wallet migration will require operational evidence beyond partner announcements.
