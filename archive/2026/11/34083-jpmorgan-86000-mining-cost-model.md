# JPMorgan Puts Bitcoin Mining Cost at $86K. What Does It Mean?

The [exact published Gutenberg editor source](./34083-jpmorgan-86000-mining-cost-model.gutenberg.html) accompanies this report. It was read back from live WordPress post **#34083** and contains the precisely approved synced editorial footer.

## Primary research, sourcing and calculations

- The Block article, **November 20, 2026**, covering JPMorgan analyst Nikolaos Panigirtzoglou's report: https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-20-jpmorgan-bitcoin-price-correction-gap-86000-production-cost-444763 . JPMorgan's central modeled cost **$86,000/BTC** is attributed to the bank and **not independently reconstructed**.
- At the time of the Wednesday JPMorgan report, BTC traded around **$65,000**, **$21,000** under the central estimate; on Friday November 20 the article reported BTC **below $60,000**, widening the gap beyond **$26,000**. The article does **not** imply a continuous spot value on all subsequent dates.
- June **$78,000** JPMorgan estimate confirmed from CoinDesk June 19: https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/06/19/live-markets-bitcoin-has-traded-below-its-mining-cost-for-five-months-squeezing-miners
- September **$85,000** estimate confirmed from The Block September 24: https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-09-24-jpmorgan-bitcoin-production-cost-miners-relief-416283
- Independent primary methodological comparison: Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance, *Bitcoin Security Budget Report*, **October 2026**, figure 11 and page 22: https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/ccaf-2026-funding-bitcoins-future.pdf . The Cambridge CCAF electricity-only estimate assumes **$0.05/kWh** and excludes ASIC hardware and non-electricity operating expenses; Glassnode uses separate difficulty and market capitalization regression. These outputs **are not equivalent** to JPMorgan's central cost estimate.
- Cambridge survey: electricity **81.1%** of reported cash operating expenditures excluding noncash costs; electricity tariffs **$0.020–0.065/kWh**, median **$0.045/kWh**, median all-in cash operating cost **$0.0555/kWh**. Survey does not meter every Bitcoin mining operation globally.
- Normalized illustrative ASIC cost calculation: **1 PH/s = 1,000 TH/s**, **12 J/TH** at the ASIC level gives **12 kW** and **288 kWh/day**. At **$0.05/kWh**, machine electricity is **$14.40/day**. Using Nov 20 sourced hashprice **$27.79 per PH/s-day** from https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/20/bitcoin-mining-hashprice-2779-asic-electricity-breakeven-november-2026/ produces electricity/gross-revenue ratio **$14.40/$27.79 = 51.8%**. Power only; cooling, downtime, pool fees, capex, financing and staff excluded. **Not JPMorgan's proprietary methodology.**
- BTC price below bank central modeled cost is not a guarantee of automatic shutdown, miner cash losses, or a rigid BTC price floor. Operators consider incremental power expenses and curtailment opportunities separately from sunk depreciation and equipment purchase costs.
