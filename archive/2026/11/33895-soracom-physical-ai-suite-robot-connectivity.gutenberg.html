Soracom is packaging the networking and security tools needed to operate autonomous machines at scale. On November 18, 2026, the Japanese connectivity company announced global self-service availability of its Physical AI Suite, bringing cellular access, private traffic routes, device identity, remote maintenance and AI workflows under one console. The company had introduced the package in Japan on November 10.

The launch is not a new robot or a new artificial intelligence model. It groups existing services for teams deploying warehouse machines, industrial equipment and mobile robots. Soracom says the platform reaches more than 500 mobile carriers across over 200 countries and territories, although actual coverage depends on local networks. Its November 18 announcement describes three operating areas: Connect, Protect and Manage.

A robot needs more than one type of connection

The Connect group starts with Soracom Air cellular access. An IMEI lock can bind a SIM to its intended machine, limiting reuse when a removable SIM is transferred. Soracom Arc extends encrypted connectivity to devices using Wi-Fi, wired internet or satellite links. Operators can apply common networking policies without assuming every machine runs on a single radio connection. NXP’s modular robotics development kit illustrates another essential layer: real-time controllers working with communication and autonomy software.

The challenge resembles operations behind Serve Robotics’ network of more than 2,000 deployed sidewalk delivery robots. Deployment requires ongoing software support, not merely a hardware shipment. Soracom did not announce an independently verified robot deployment count for the new suite.

Network controls can isolate machines

The Protect group includes Soracom Virtual Private Gateway, which can restrict network destinations and keep traffic on private routes, while Krypton automates certificate provisioning using SIM authentication. Peek and Junction provide packet capture, inspection and traffic mirroring for customer monitoring. Soracom can then disable a SIM if the customer’s detection system identifies suspicious activity.

The distinction matters: built-in network controls do not automatically guarantee malicious traffic detection. Customers remain responsible for monitoring rules, endpoint security, software integrity and machine safety. Soracom separately announced a professional assessment and hardening service on November 17, with pricing determined by each engagement.

Maintenance continues after installation

The Manage group offers Napter for time-limited maintenance sessions, alongside Flux for low-code automation of device data and operational decisions. Private, authenticated connectivity can carry firmware and AI model updates after a machine is deployed. An operator might diagnose a stalled robot, update its software and inspect telemetry without keeping a public management port permanently exposed.

Such infrastructure complements FORT Robotics’ work on resilient wireless links and Intrinsic’s open industrial robotics software. Connectivity, motion planning and machine safety are separate engineering problems, even when combined into one automated system.

What companies still need to measure

Soracom says customers can activate services through the console or API at standard rates, with no separate suite fee. Actual costs depend on subscriptions, usage and network conditions. The announcement provides no independently measured robot uptime, end-to-end latency, fleet productivity improvement or cybersecurity incident reduction. Evidence from production deployments will be necessary before claiming performance advantages.

Robotic safety additionally depends on emergency stops, loss-of-connectivity behavior and access controls. Cloud links should support supervision and updates without becoming the sole protection against dangerous motion. Soracom’s announcement matters because connected machines require continuous management long after delivery, but operational tests will determine how well the combined service works.
