# CBI Relocates 180 Bitcoin Miners as October Yield Tops 9%

*Verbatim published WordPress Gutenberg body with original Blockware-related source videos and unchanged canonical volunteer footer.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>French mining company <a href="https://www.actusnews.com/en/crypto-blockchain-industries/pr/2026/11/16/bitcoin-mining-activity-for-the-month-of-october-2026">Crypto Blockchain Industries (CBI) reported November 16</a> that <strong>180 Bitcoin ASICs are being relocated</strong> after a Missouri equipment shutdown. Its October mining fleet delivered an estimated annualized cash yield <strong>above 9%</strong>. Excluding capital invested in the inactive machines, CBI calculated an annualized yield <strong>above 12%</strong> for the remaining operating equipment.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Those numbers do not mean individual miners gained three percentage points in efficiency. The calculations use different equipment investment denominators, and both are lower bounds. They refer to October cash mining margins with Bitcoin near $78,000 at month-end, not realized investor profit or guaranteed annual returns.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why 180 machines are offline</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A September technical problem stopped <strong>150 Antminer S21+</strong> units at CBI's Missouri host. The company has added <strong>30 Antminer S21 XP</strong> units to the transfer. They were not earning Bitcoin during October, although no normal electricity expense accrued while shut down. CBI expects the relocated fleet back online by the end of 2026, a target that remains unverified.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/08/cbi-150-antminer-s21-plus-missouri-offline-relocation/">September outage coverage</a> documented the initial 150 units. The new disclosure adds 30 machines and reports the resulting impact on the company's operating calculations.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why positive mining yield can disguise downtime</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CBI defines annualized cash yield by valuing Bitcoin mined over the month, subtracting the electricity bill, dividing by the invested server cost and extrapolating the monthly result across a year. The method excludes equipment depreciation and resale values. A machine that sits idle remains part of the investment base even when it consumes no electricity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Moving ASICs involves transportation, power compatibility, ventilation, network access, firmware configuration and validation of pool payouts. Restarting on schedule can limit forgone revenue, but the company has not released site-specific acceptance tests, actual post-relocation hashrate or a completed restart date.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Grid limits add another source of lost production</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CBI separately attributed lower Bitcoin-denominated mining returns versus September to temporary electricity-grid access restrictions. The relocation and power limitations are distinct causes of lost mining time. The reported yield also depends on a <strong>preliminary Blockware Solutions electricity invoice</strong>; subsequent consumption reconciliation may change the estimate.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Comparable pressures affect other miners. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/19/texas-wind-power-collapse-bitcoin-mining-45-percent-uneconomic-hours-november-2026/">West Texas analysis</a> describes costly grid hours, while <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/14/bitcoin-mining-difficulty-drops-9-13-percent-block-975744-november-2026/">Bitcoin's November 14 difficulty adjustment</a> occurred after CBI's October measurement period and must not be treated as an October return driver.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">CBI still targets a 20% annualized return</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>CBI calls October's result insufficient against its ambition to approach a 20% annualized yield as Bitcoin prices improve. The company also allocates <strong>25% of mining profits</strong> to a share buyback program capped at €1 million. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/cbi-mining-profits-share-buyback/">share buyback coverage</a> distinguishes an allocation policy from completed repurchases.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A higher Bitcoin price could improve mining revenue, but network difficulty, uptime, hosting charges and equipment age can offset the benefit. An encouraging monthly cash figure should not be confused with a forecast that equipment will recover its full capital cost.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The next useful evidence is straightforward: return the 180 rigs to service, publish their measured hashrate and operating hours, reconcile the electricity bill and report results including all invested machines. Until then, October illustrates how downtime can erode the economics of an ASIC fleet.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
