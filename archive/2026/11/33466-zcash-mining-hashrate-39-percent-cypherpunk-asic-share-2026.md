# Zcash Mining Hashrate Surges 39% as Cypherpunk’s ASIC Share Falls to 13.1%

*Canonical WordPress Gutenberg body, preserved following publication. Original featured cover and source-based line chart retained in WordPress.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Zcash mining competition accelerated during the autumn as a surge in Equihash ASIC capacity reduced one major operator’s share of the network. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1509745/000110465926127625/tm2629799d1_ex99-1.htm">Cypherpunk Technologies reported November 16</a> that Zcash network hashrate rose from approximately <strong>23 GSol/s on August 18 to 32 GSol/s on November 13, 2026</strong>. The increase represents 39.1% growth over 87 days, calculated as (32 ÷ 23 − 1) × 100.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Despite operating roughly 4.2 GSol/s of Bitmain Antminer Z15 Pro equipment, Cypherpunk’s estimated network share fell from around 18% at launch to 13.1%. The contrast demonstrates a fundamental proof-of-work reality: a mining fleet can perform reliably while becoming less competitive relative to the entire network. Unlike Bitcoin’s SHA-256 ASICs, Zcash miners execute Equihash solution calculations, measured in solutions per second.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Network hashrate rose faster than Cypherpunk's deployed fleet</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company’s <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1509745/000110465926127609/cyph-20260930x10q.htm">Form 10-Q</a> records approximately 28 GSol/s by September 30, with Cypherpunk holding about 15% of the network. By November 13, the network reached 32 GSol/s while the company’s reported fleet remained approximately 4.2 GSol/s. The decline to 13.1% equals about <strong>4.9 percentage points</strong>, not a 4.9% fall in raw hashrate.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Cypherpunk launched its fleet in August after acquiring Z15 Pro machines and hosting rights for $33.33 million through an equity-linked transaction involving an affiliate of Winklevoss Capital. The ASICs are located at third-party facilities in Texas, Tennessee and West Virginia. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/cypherpunk-zcash-mining-fleet-reaches-125-million-revenue-run-rate/">September fleet report</a> covered the initial mining expansion, before the latest network-share figures became available.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Z15 Pro efficiency does not guarantee the same rewards</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>According to <a href="https://support.bitmain.com/hc/en-us/articles/19259431024281-Z15-Pro-Specifications">BITMAIN’s official specifications</a>, a standard Z15 Pro is rated at 840 KSol/s, draws 2,780 watts at the wall at 25°C, and achieves approximately 3.31 joules per kilosolution. The dedicated Equihash chips cannot simply be switched into GPU AI rentals or Bitcoin SHA-256 mining when economics change. Operators must account for electricity, cooling, repairs, pool performance and network difficulty.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A $30 million ASIC plan still has no binding purchase agreement</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Cypherpunk entered a <strong>nonbinding</strong> letter of intent with Bitmain for up to $30 million of next-generation Zcash machines, paying a refundable $6 million deposit. The company expects possible deliveries in mid-2027, but no definitive purchase is signed. Specifications, unit pricing and final delivery conditions remain unannounced. Its aspirational target of about 25% of network hashrate would require 8 GSol/s at today’s 32 GSol/s network size, and more if competitors keep growing.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Equipment buying pressure extends beyond one company. Our earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/06/mining-hardware-fortitude-bitmain-next-gen-zcash-asic-100-million/">Fortitude report</a> examined another prospective Bitmain ASIC arrangement. Firmware remains an operational constraint; <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/luxos-z15-pro-whatsminer-vh-bt1968-fan-control-fixes/">LuxOS support for the Z15 Pro</a> illustrates the ongoing maintenance requirements.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Production was strong, but revenue was not cash</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Across 44 operating days from August 18 through September 30, Cypherpunk mined 8,967.29 ZEC, valued at $9.9 million in accounting revenue using an average fair value of $1,105.46 per coin. The company retained the mined coins rather than selling them for cash. It reported roughly 95% fleet uptime and $104.33 cost per ZEC excluding depreciation, rising to $237.46 including equipment depreciation; neither figure is a guaranteed future cost.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>An additional protocol change complicates comparisons. Zcash activated NU7 on November 5, reducing its target block interval from 75 to 25 seconds without increasing the intended daily ZEC subsidy to miners. Faster blocks can affect stale shares and pool software. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/07/zcash-nu7-mainnet-activation-25-second-blocks-fee-reserve-nov-2026/">NU7 engineering coverage</a> explains the new interval. Larger fleets and faster hardware may produce more hashes, but total block issuance and operating economics still impose limits.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The broader lesson applies across ASIC mining: equipment efficiency is only one side of an equation shaped by network competition, fixed block rewards, energy costs and capital spending. Cypherpunk’s 2027 purchases remain proposals, and neither future hashrate nor profitability can be assumed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
