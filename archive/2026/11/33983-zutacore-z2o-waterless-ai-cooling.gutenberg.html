AI data centers could eventually cool powerful GPUs without a chilled-water plant. On November 16, 2026, ZutaCore and cooling manufacturer Munters unveiled Z₂O at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago. The proposed architecture transfers heat from processor cold plates to outdoor dry coolers through one sealed, two-phase liquid circuit, dispensing with several conventional facility components. Its headline efficiency is modeled, not an independently measured production result.

How a chip can cool itself without water

Z₂O circulates an electrically insulating dielectric fluid through cold plates attached to hot processors. Heat boils part of the fluid. The vapor carries thermal energy to the outdoor heat-rejection system, condenses, and returns toward the servers. Gravity and buoyancy do much of the circulation, with pumps retained for assistance and redundancy. No water-based cooling tower, separate facility-water loop, liquid-to-liquid heat exchanger or coolant distribution unit is specified in the reference design.

ZutaCore describes a repeatable 625 kW IT block, with 500 kW cooled through the two-phase loop and 125 kW through air. The 80/20 thermal split is a design assumption, not a claim that every GPU rack always maintains identical load distribution. Redundant pumps and spare rooftop heat-rejection capacity are part of the reliability plan.

What does a pPUE of 1.03 mean?

The manufacturers report a modeled annualized cooling-specific partial power usage effectiveness, or pPUE, of 1.030, compared with 1.115 for a high-efficiency chilled-water baseline. Under identical assumptions, every megawatt consumed by IT equipment would add approximately 30 kW of cooling electricity for Z₂O versus 115 kW for the baseline. The difference is 85 kW, equivalent to 73.9% lower modeled cooling electricity.

If that 85 kW advantage persisted around the clock for an entire year, the arithmetic would imply 744.6 MWh less electricity per megawatt of IT capacity. Neither an annual operating saving nor a customer bill has been independently verified. The model uses hourly Dulles, Virginia weather data, 43°C fluid saturation temperature, an 80/20 liquid-to-air split and continuous full IT loading. Actual performance depends on climate, service levels, component availability and other facility overhead.

Waterless cooling still comes with trade-offs

The claim of zero water use concerns the specified cooling process. It does not measure water consumed in electricity production, equipment manufacturing or the wider supply chain. Dielectric fluid selection, long-term leak management, maintenance procedures and mechanical reliability also require evaluation. Cooling-specific pPUE cannot substitute for a measured facility-wide PUE.

ZutaCore says an initial integrated system uses an ASRock Rack server based on NVIDIA HGX B300 hardware. A public launch announcement does not establish long-duration site performance across varied climates. The proposed reduction in plumbing may simplify deployment, although facility engineers still must verify power, rooftop space, electrical redundancy and heat-rejection capacity.
