# Cooling Perovskite Films Helps Solar Modules Hold 23.9% Efficiency

- Primary peer-reviewed: Buyi Yan and colleagues, "Retarding efficiency loss upon scaling in perovskite photovoltaics as fill factor becomes the bottleneck", Nature Communications, published October 29, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78867-1

## Recorded technical measurements and limitations

- 19.48 cm² aperture, eight scribed subcells, 23.9% certified steady-state conversion efficiency, 83.3% fill factor, 97% geometric fill factor.
- Wet precursor film cooled to 0–10°C for 15 seconds before vacuum treatment; uniform grain development and slowed crystallization.
- Separate open-circuit continuous one-sun aging studies showed fill-factor decay under one-third of control cases, not proof of 25-year panel lifetime.
- Shapley-value attribution suggests fill factor is dominant source of cell-to-module efficiency loss after accounting for inactive scribed area.
- Crucial units and tests: 19.48 cm² NOT m²; 97% geometric fill factor NOT power conversion efficiency; 23.9% certified steady-state NOT directly interchangeable with the company’s higher scan-mode claim.
- Research minimodule not an industrial-scale 2.88m² product. No unsupported claims about module commercial service life, actual costs or plant output.
