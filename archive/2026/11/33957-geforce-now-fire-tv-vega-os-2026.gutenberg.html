NVIDIA expanded its GeForce NOW cloud gaming app to three more Amazon Fire TV Stick models on November 19, 2026, making PC games accessible on additional living room televisions without a gaming console or expensive local graphics card. The launch covers Fire TV Stick HD, Fire TV Stick 4K Select, and the 2026 Fire TV Stick 4K running Vega OS, according to NVIDIA’s official announcement.

The new compatibility arrives alongside limited-time membership discounts and expanded access to Bethesda games purchased through GOG. Importantly, GeForce NOW already supported selected older Fire TV devices running Fire OS earlier in 2026. The November update extends the device list rather than introducing cloud gaming to Fire TV for the first time.

A small streaming stick, a powerful remote GPU

GeForce NOW renders supported PC games on NVIDIA servers, compresses the resulting video and streams it over an internet connection. A connected controller sends player inputs back to the server. The Fire TV Stick primarily decodes video and handles input, rather than running a demanding Windows game locally.

To begin, users install the GeForce NOW app on a supported Fire TV device, sign in and pair a compatible controller. GeForce NOW also requires access to supported games through linked stores or eligible subscriptions. The service is not an unlimited library where every PC title automatically appears.

NVIDIA lists 30% off the first six-month term of eligible Performance or Ultimate memberships through November 30, 2026, in select regions. A separate Amazon promotion offers 90% off the first month of a qualifying membership purchased directly on supported Fire TV devices through December 31 in participating regions. The discounts concern different offers and eligibility requirements; neither is a permanent reduction in subscription prices.

What about 5K and 360 FPS?

NVIDIA advertises GeForce NOW Ultimate streaming up to 5K at 120 frames per second, or as much as 360 frames per second at 1080p on supported systems. Both are different operating modes. The November announcement does not establish that the newly supported Fire TV Sticks can output either premium PC mode. An earlier Fire TV implementation was limited to 1080p at 60 FPS, according to NVIDIA’s February rollout notes.

Official NVIDIA GeForce video introducing Blackwell-powered GeForce NOW streaming in August 2025. Its performance discussion is for supported Ultimate configurations, not a technical demonstration of Vega OS Fire TV playback.

NVIDIA’s published connection requirements recommend approximately 25 Mbps for 1080p/60 FPS, 48 Mbps for 1080p/360 FPS and 65 Mbps for 5K/120 FPS on compatible PC clients. A display’s refresh rate, compatible software, local decoding capability, server distance and internet stability also determine the experience. Higher advertised frame rates cannot overcome long network latency or severe packet loss.

Five more Bethesda games and the bigger picture

NVIDIA says five Bethesda titles gain GOG support on GeForce NOW: Fallout 4, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Anniversary Edition, DOOM (2016), DOOM Eternal, and Indiana Jones and the Great Circle. The company also lists Graveyard Keeper 2 through Epic Games Store and GOG, among other weekly additions. Cloud availability may follow individual store launch dates.

The strategy expands gaming beyond traditional hardware ownership, but cloud gaming still depends on a reliable network, publisher participation and ongoing subscription terms.
