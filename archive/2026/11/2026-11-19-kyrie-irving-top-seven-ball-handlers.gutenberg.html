Who has the best handles in NBA history? Kyrie Irving takes the top position in our seven-player countdown, ahead of crossover pioneers, creative passers and modern shot creators. A great handle involves more than flashy dribbles. Players must protect the ball, manipulate a defender’s feet, change speed without losing balance and turn a live dribble into a shot or pass. Our ranking weighs control in traffic, deception, either-hand versatility, counter moves, effectiveness and influence. Emerging smart-ball sensor technology could eventually expand movement tracking, but cannot measure historical eras retroactively. There is no official NBA all-time dribbling metric; the order is editorial.

#7. Jason Williams: Creativity Without a Warning

Jason “White Chocolate” Williams treated ball movement like improvisation. His rapid hand switches, wraparounds, hesitation dribbles and disguised passing windows made defenders guard several possibilities at once. Much of his reputation comes from passing rather than repeated isolation scoring, an important distinction. But his ability to disguise the next action earns a place among the NBA’s most imaginative guards. His flair belongs in a specialist debate distinct from our broader Top 25 NBA players ranking, which considers total on-court value.

#6. Tim Hardaway: The Crossover Blueprint

Hardaway’s “UTEP Two-Step” remains a masterclass in shifting a defender’s weight. The sequence sells one direction with a low between-the-legs dribble, then reverses explosively before the defender can reset. The NBA’s technical retrospective details its mechanics and lasting influence. Hardaway did not invent the crossover; he helped transform it into a signature NBA weapon.

#5. Jamal Crawford: The Countermove Specialist

Crawford combined loose-looking rhythm with exceptional recovery and direction changes. His shake-and-bake and behind-the-back variations forced defenders to commit before he attacked the opening. His advantage was not only spectacle: the dribble created separation for pull-up jumpers and drives. Los Angeles Times reporting describes how Crawford studied earlier crossover specialists.

#4. Stephen Curry: The Shot Changes the Dribble

Curry’s handle is inseparable from his shooting range. A defender stepping forward to deny a deep three exposes a driving lane; retreating opens the pull-up. His repeated escape dribbles, quick resets and low changes of direction exploit the smallest gaps. The NBA’s 2023–24 Curry handles mix offers a recent view. Unlike a pure isolation dribbler, Curry often connects his handle to rapid off-ball movement.

#3. Isiah Thomas: Control in Heavy Traffic

Detroit’s Isiah Thomas attacked crowded lanes with quick redirection, a compact dribble and superb passing awareness. His handle supported championship-level offense rather than existing as a highlight exhibition. The NBA’s historical profile describes his rise as the engine of Detroit’s back-to-back title teams. Older-era footage and different enforcement of carrying rules make direct comparisons imperfect, but Thomas’s control under pressure remains extraordinary.

#2. Allen Iverson: The Most Feared First Crossover

Iverson made the wide, explosive crossover a cultural event. His shoulder and head sold the initial direction, and his rapid opposite-hand attack punished a defender’s first step. What makes the move legendary is the functional result: space for a jumper or a burst into the lane. Iverson’s scoring responsibility demanded repeated creation against set defenses, not merely occasional highlight opportunities. His influence also frames broader debates about individual greatness in our Jordan versus LeBron comparison.

#1. Kyrie Irving: The Complete Dribbling Arsenal

Irving has the broadest combination of controlled, game-speed options on our list. His low stance keeps the ball protected; either hand can launch a crossover, in-and-out, spin or behind-the-back move. The key is the second and third decision. When a defender survives an initial fake, Irving changes rhythm again without surrendering balance or his dribble. His footwork allows the same sequence to become a floater, reverse layup, pull-up jumper or pass.

Irving told the Dallas Mavericks that hand size, foot placement and attacking defenders’ hips contribute to his control. In a detailed ball-handling interview, he described preparing counters for his moves. Andscape reported on his low-dribble drills and work with Cleveland’s player development staff. His execution under confined space, with either hand and multiple finish options, separates Irving even from other elite creators.

Why Kyrie Wins the Ball-Handling Debate

Iverson has the iconic single crossover, Curry combines elite handling with unprecedented shooting pressure, Thomas owns remarkable traffic control, and Hardaway helped popularize a move that changed generations. Irving wins on the depth and continuity of his counters. When one move is stopped, the next can arrive immediately.
