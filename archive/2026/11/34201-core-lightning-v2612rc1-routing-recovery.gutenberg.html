Bitcoin's Lightning Network is getting a substantial software upgrade while developers work through a difficult security year. On November 18, 2026, the Core Lightning team published v26.12 release candidate 1, combining 832 commits over 169 days from 41 contributors. The pre-release improves payment routing, channel recovery and splicing stability. It is not a finalized production release, and operators should test changes before trusting live channel funds.

Why Core Lightning skipped an entire release cycle

The developers say a large volume of vulnerability reports diverted attention from the planned September v26.09 release. Security fixes instead went into the v26.06.x point-release series, while longer-term development continued. Consequently, v26.12rc1 contains roughly two cycles of work. A larger commit count does not automatically mean twice the performance or twice the security.

The project credits 19 first-time contributors, equivalent to about 46.3% of the 41 people named in the release. The remaining 22 previously contributed. The numbers describe participation, not a breakdown of how many commits any developer authored.

Smarter payments and clearer failures

Lightning payments travel through off-chain channels, where available liquidity and routing fees influence whether a payment succeeds. Core Lightning's askrene routing engine now remembers channels that successfully carried payments, called impressions, and factors fees into routing probabilities. Its xpay component also stops retrying every channel through nodes that repeatedly report an unknown next peer.

When a forwarding attempt fails, new failure_reason fields can distinguish insufficient outgoing liquidity, too many concurrent HTLCs, or exceeded in-flight limits. Better diagnostics may help operators tune liquidity and identify recurring bottlenecks. Neither the release notes nor independent tests establish a specific percentage improvement in payment success.

Recovery and splicing stability

Hashed timelock contracts, or HTLCs, coordinate conditional transfers between channel peers. The lightning-hsmtool utility can now recover HTLC outputs after channel-state loss, improving an important recovery path. That feature is not a guarantee that lost backups, every force-close scenario or stolen keys can always be recovered.

Developers also fixed a crash when splicing a channel before it is publicly announced, and corrected a stray channel_ready message after reconnection. Splicing changes a channel's funding transaction without necessarily requiring an entirely new channel, but implementations must coordinate precisely to avoid settlement mistakes.

The upgrade comes with compatibility work

The team says pay is scheduled to give way to xpay in v27.03. Several JSON-RPC and notification fields have changed, while released gRPC wire field numbers and types are now frozen for compatibility checks. An experimental bwatch wallet tracker and an optional simplified channel close remain explicitly experimental; they are not defaults that ordinary operators should enable without testing.
