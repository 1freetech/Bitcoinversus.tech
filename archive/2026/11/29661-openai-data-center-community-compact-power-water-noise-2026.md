<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>OpenAI said Thursday it will publish electricity and water use, construction plans, and independent noise studies for future data centers it owns and operates. The company’s <a href="https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/community-compact.pdf">National Community Compact</a> also commits it to giving each site a local point of contact and reporting annually on its progress. The company released a separate <a href="https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/compute-campus-policy-blueprint.pdf">policy blueprint</a> asking governments to apply similar protections across the industry.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>AI campuses concentrate thousands of servers, their cooling equipment, and electrical distribution in one place. Connecting a large campus can require new substations or transmission capacity. As BitcoinVersus.Tech reported in its coverage of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/05/aep-jacobs-wsp-37-billion-transmission-engineering-11-states-2026/">AEP’s transmission expansion</a>, utilities plan for new customer connections alongside regional reliability work. Where a campus prompts an upgrade, the utility must determine who pays and what happens if the customer needs less power than planned.</p>

<p>The electricity pledge predates Thursday’s compact. OpenAI’s <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/02/openai-grid-protectors-georgia-ai-data-center-1gw-demand-response-2026/">Grid Protectors announcement</a> earlier in the week described an agreement allowing Georgia Power to cut delivery to a planned Effingham County campus by up to 1,000 megawatts during periods of high demand. That figure describes how much power the utility could withhold at a given time. The energy saved would depend on the size and duration of each reduction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The compact adds a commitment to provide financial assurances before major spending begins so other customers are protected if a campus is delayed, reduced, or canceled. The structure matters because utilities can spend on infrastructure years before the expected load arrives. Earlier BitcoinVersus.Tech reporting on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/03/solar-power-construction-grid-lead-times-ai-2030/">transmission construction lead times</a> examined how long grid upgrades can take relative to new generation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>OpenAI also says it will stop requiring public officials to sign nondisclosure agreements as a condition of negotiations, with narrow protections for information such as trade secrets and legitimate security details. Independent noise studies would measure sound at property boundaries and in neighboring areas before operations begin and annually afterward. The <a href="https://openai.com/index/setting-new-standard-responsible-ai-infrastructure/">company’s announcement</a> also calls for early resident input and site-level reporting on hiring and apprenticeships.</p>

<p>Ownership limits the pledge’s reach. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-kicks-off-charm-offensive-change-voters-view-data-centers-2026-11-05/">Reuters reported</a> that partners including Oracle own much of the infrastructure dedicated to OpenAI. Site-specific agreements and permit conditions will show which protections apply at partner-owned facilities. A previous <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/senate-probe-questions-ai-data-center-costs-and-jobs/">Senate inquiry into data center costs and jobs</a> illustrates why local officials need measurable obligations when evaluating developers’ projections.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The blueprint asks state utility regulators to make large customers cover the costs they add to the grid. It also asks governments that offer tax incentives to publish the terms and recover benefits when developers miss agreed targets. The first local compacts will show how much residents can learn about individual projects and whether utility agreements spell out who carries the cost if projected demand fails to materialize.</p>
