Bitcoin owners can now divide an existing COLDCARD wallet backup across multiple locations without generating a new wallet or moving the coins. Coinkite’s firmware 5.6.3 for Mk4/Mk5 and 1.5.3Q for Q adds Codex32 and Shamir secret-sharing support. The releases arrived September 30, 2026, were described by Coinkite in October, and appeared in Bitcoin Optech’s November 20 roundup. The timing matters: the capability is established firmware, not a newly announced November release.

Under Shamir secret sharing, a single wallet secret becomes several different recovery shares. A 2-of-3 arrangement produces three shares, any two of which can reconstruct the original secret. One share alone cannot recover the wallet. COLDCARD allows two to nine shares and a configurable threshold of at least two. The wallet’s existing addresses and balances remain unchanged.

Why dividing a seed can be useful

A conventional seed-phrase backup may be sufficient for an attacker who finds it. With a threshold scheme, holders can distribute shares between physically separate secure locations while retaining recovery after losing one or more shares. For a 2-of-3 setup, two shares are necessary to unlock the secret and any single stored share is insufficient. The arrangement reduces one form of backup exposure but adds organizational complexity and opportunities for transcription mistakes.

Codex32 is specified in BIP-93. Its strings include checksums for detecting some recording errors. COLDCARD supports MS1 for BIP-32 master-seed material, plus vendor-specific CW1 and CX1 formats for BIP-39 entropy and passphrase-wallet material. Other recovery software must explicitly support the extensions. Mixing shares generated during separate split sessions will not work.

The security catch: an old weak seed stays weak

Coinkite’s security status advisory documents a separate July 2026 seed-generation flaw affecting older firmware. Updating a device fixes future generation behavior, but does not repair a previously weakened seed. Dividing such a secret into shares does not make the underlying key strong. Affected holders need to review the manufacturer’s migration instructions, including its narrowly defined independent-dice exception, rather than treat new Shamir shares as a substitute.

BitcoinVersus.Tech previously reported on the COLDCARD security incident, a separate phishing incident, and automated attacks on exposed wallet secrets. A separate Trezor supply-chain security story illustrates the broader trust problem. Physical custody, software integrity, secret quality and recovery planning require different safeguards.

Recovery matters more than the share count

The vendor advises recording every share accurately before leaving the split screen: COLDCARD does not retain a generated share set. QR, NFC and text-file exports reveal shares in plaintext, so an exposed export counts as an exposed share. Recovery should be rehearsed with the intended stored copies, confirming a known receive address rather than relying only on a short wallet fingerprint.

The firmware also strengthens PSBTv2 handling, including individual-input locktime requirements and stricter parsing. Those changes concern transaction signing and are distinct from backup redundancy. Owners should obtain firmware through official downloads, verify the published signature and hash, and confirm the installed version. Bitcoin’s monetary protocol does not change when a wallet improves its storage practices.
