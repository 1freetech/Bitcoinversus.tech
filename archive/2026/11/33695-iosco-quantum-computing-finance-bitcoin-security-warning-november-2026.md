# Regulators Urge Finance to Prepare for Quantum Attacks

*Verbatim WordPress Gutenberg body, including the unchanged BitcoinVersus.Tech volunteer editor note, original donation QR, source links and disclaimer.*

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Financial regulators are telling banks, exchanges and trading firms to prepare for a future in which quantum computers can undermine widely used digital signatures and encryption. On <strong>November 19, 2026</strong>, the <a href="https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD833.pdf">International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) published a final report</a> finding that much of the financial sector remains at an early stage of preparation. The warning extends to public blockchains, but does not mean Bitcoin or bank encryption has already been broken.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The report covers regulators across more than 130 jurisdictions. <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/global-securities-watchdog-urges-immediate-action-quantum-risks-finance-2026-11-19/">Reuters reported on November 19</a> that some consulted industry specialists believe a cryptographically relevant quantum computer could arrive within five years. That is a contingency discussed by experts, <strong>not a confirmed deadline or an IOSCO prediction</strong>. No computer capable of cracking commonly deployed financial public-key cryptography is known to exist.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why a future quantum machine threatens financial signatures</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Banks rely on public-key cryptography for secure connections, identity verification and authorizing transactions. A sufficiently powerful error-corrected quantum computer could use algorithms such as Shor's to attack widely used RSA and elliptic-curve systems. Breaking an authentication signature could threaten payment integrity, while decrypting intercepted confidential messages years later creates a separate <strong>harvest-now, decrypt-later</strong> risk.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Britain's <a href="https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2026/november/effective-practices-cyber-risk-and-resilience-management-relating-to-quantum-computing">Bank of England, Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority</a> issued complementary practices on November 19. Their advice covers cryptographic inventories, service-provider dependencies, migration plans and testing. The publication explicitly says its suggested practices are not new formal regulatory expectations.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why upgrading Bitcoin could be harder</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Bitcoin uses elliptic-curve signatures to authorize spending. Future quantum attacks on exposed public keys present a different security problem from disrupting SHA-256 proof-of-work mining. Wallets, nodes and custodians would need compatible signature rules, and old coins may require their owners to move funds into protected addresses. Inactive wallets complicate migration even after any protocol change is agreed.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/can-quantum-computers-hack-bitcoin/">Bitcoin quantum-security analysis</a> explains the distinction between signatures and mining. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/07/blockstream-shrincs-draft-quantum-bitcoin-wallet-benchmarks-2026/">Blockstream's experimental SHRINCS signatures</a> illustrate ongoing research, not a completed consensus upgrade. Quantum-safe signatures can increase transaction size and verification demands, influencing block space, fees and wallet compatibility.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">A migration plan is more realistic than a countdown</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology finalized its first three major post-quantum standards in August 2024: <a href="https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography">FIPS 203, 204 and 205</a>. They cover key encapsulation and digital signatures, including ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA. Applying those methods requires testing at the application, hardware and supplier level, not merely swapping one setting.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The British guidance also emphasizes <strong>crypto-agility</strong>, the ability to change algorithms again as security research advances. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/08/openssl-4-1-dtls13-grease-postquantum-cryptography-2026/">OpenSSL post-quantum coverage</a> illustrates software migration, while <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/lattice-machxo5-nx-tdq-post-quantum-fpga-ml-kem-ml-dsa/">Lattice's security FPGA work</a> shows the hardware side. Neither development proves a bank or blockchain is fully migrated.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Regulators also warn investors about quantum hype</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>IOSCO flags <strong>quantum washing</strong>, where firms exaggerate the maturity or financial benefit of quantum products. Portfolio optimization, derivatives pricing and market surveillance are research areas, but commercial advantage remains largely unproved. Regulators must assess claims without confusing experimental demonstrations with dependable production systems.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The practical message is preparedness rather than panic. Banks can inventory vulnerable cryptography and schedule staged upgrades today; Bitcoin developers and users face additional coordination questions. Neither an imminent universal cryptographic collapse nor immunity from future quantum attacks follows from the available evidence.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
