<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rice University engineers have found a way to carve densely packed diamond nanowires across an entire <strong>2-inch (5.1-centimeter) wafer</strong> without defining each microscopic feature through a separate lithography pattern. The process begins with a thin silver coating and continues inside a plasma etching chamber, where protective particles develop automatically as diamond is removed. The university announced the advance on November 23, 2026, following publication of the peer-reviewed research in <em>Advanced Materials</em> on October 28.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The demonstration also produced diamond nanowires approaching <strong>30 micrometers in length</strong> on polycrystalline substrates. In a separate lab test, textured boron-doped diamond electrodes degraded water pollutants <strong>2.5 times faster</strong> than flat counterparts. Neither result establishes mass-production readiness or equivalent performance in commercial treatment equipment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How silver becomes its own microscopic mask</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Conventional nanofabrication frequently requires resist deposition, optical or electron-beam patterning, development, and transfer of the pattern into the underlying material. Diamond is particularly challenging to process because of its chemical resistance. Rice avoided a separately patterned mask by covering the diamond surface with an ultrathin silver film before reactive-ion etching.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>During the opening minutes of plasma exposure, silver reorganized into nanoscale clusters that shielded tiny regions of the diamond. Reactive species etched surrounding exposed areas downward, leaving protected material protruding like an array of needles. As the silver diminished, inorganic material redeposited from the carrier wafer and chamber formed secondary protective spots that prolonged directional etching.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The process is therefore <strong>lithography-free, not mask-free</strong>. The essential masks form in place instead of being drawn through an external pattern. Rice’s technique uses reactive-ion etching, a fabrication method explained in the BitcoinVersus.Tech guide to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/semiconductors-what-is-etching-plasma-dry-wet-rie/">wet and dry semiconductor etching</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">One process, three kinds of diamond</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Researchers demonstrated the method on polycrystalline diamond, single-crystal diamond, and electrically conductive boron-doped diamond. The full-wafer fabrication test matters because growing a handful of nanowires in a small sample is different from producing a dense structure over a larger substrate. The process still needs independent reproducibility, yield, and uniformity measurements before industrial scalability is established.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Different substrates enable different applications. Boron-doped diamond can serve as an electrochemical electrode; a textured surface exposes additional active area to water. In Rice’s laboratory comparison, the nanowire electrodes accelerated pollutant breakdown by a factor of 2.5 against flat diamond electrodes. The finding resembles a different materials strategy covered in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/19/electrified-carbon-cloth-phosphate-water-recovery-2-6x-northwestern-2026/">Northwestern’s electrically driven water-recovery research</a>, although the pollutants, tests, and electrode mechanisms differ.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Diamond structures for light, sensors, and computing</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On single-crystal diamond, investigators measured brighter light emission in localized regions associated with atomic defects. Simulations additionally indicated improved photon extraction may be possible; no commercial optical-sensor efficiency gain was demonstrated. Diamond already attracts research interest for photonics and extreme environments, including <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/22/oist-diamond-maser-quantum-microwave-amplifier-1000x-2026/">quantum microwave amplification</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Its heat-conduction properties are also relevant to chip packages. BitcoinVersus.Tech has compared <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/08/lab-grown-diamond-vs-silicon-ai-chip-cooling-thermal-cost-wafer-2026/">lab-grown diamond with silicon for chip cooling</a>. However, an array of microscopic wires is not automatically an improved processor heat spreader, and Rice did not demonstrate a cooled AI chip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The immediate achievement is a simpler route to shaping hard diamond materials over wafer-scale areas. Future evaluations will need to establish cost, placement control, electrical contacts, durability and process compatibility before the approach becomes an industrial photonic or electrochemical product.</p>
