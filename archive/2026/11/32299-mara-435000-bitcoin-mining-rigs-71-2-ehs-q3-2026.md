<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>MARA Holdings reported ownership of approximately <strong>435,000 Bitcoin mining rigs worldwide</strong> as of September 30, 2026, including its share of equipment held through an equity-method investment. Yet Q3 Bitcoin production increased only <strong>4.8% year over year</strong>, despite an <strong>17.9% rise in energized hashrate</strong>. The November 9 SEC filing highlights the difference between deploying more computing equipment and earning a proportional share of Bitcoin’s block rewards.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What 435,000 rigs actually means</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company reported <strong>71.2 exahashes per second (EH/s)</strong> in energized capacity, versus 60.4 EH/s at the end of Q3 2025. MARA defines energized hashrate as manufacturer-rated capacity from installed equipment that could run at full output, including machines temporarily offline. Therefore, 71.2 EH/s is <strong>not a direct measurement of average live hashing</strong> or a guarantee that every reported rig was operating continuously.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The 10-Q lists average miner efficiency of <strong>16.9 joules per terahash</strong>, improved from 18.6 J/TH one year earlier. The 1.7 J/TH reduction equals approximately <strong>9.1% less miner energy per unit of hashing</strong> under equivalent operating conditions. It does not establish the same percentage decrease in facility electricity consumption, which also reflects fleet utilization and cooling overhead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Hashrate grew almost four times faster than Bitcoin production</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>MARA mined <strong>2,247 BTC in Q3 2026</strong>, compared with 2,144 BTC a year earlier. Its mining pool won 644 blocks versus 633. While installed capacity rose sharply, network difficulty, competing hashrate and operational factors limited the production increase. The company also reported a 7% sequential decline from Q2, when it produced 2,422 BTC.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The contrast resembles the challenge examined in BitcoinVersus.Tech’s <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/27/top-7-public-bitcoin-miners-by-btc-produced-q2-2026/">public-miner production rankings</a>. An operator must earn a growing share of network computation merely to maintain its relative reward share as competitors upgrade. Our <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/top-10-most-efficient-bitcoin-mining-machines-2026-j-th/">ASIC efficiency comparison</a> likewise distinguishes a machine’s rated efficiency from the economics of running an entire fleet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Power infrastructure and costs remain decisive</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>MARA reported <strong>1.9 gigawatts</strong> of total energy capacity, up from 1.7 GW, while purchased energy cost per Bitcoin at its owned sites rose slightly from $39,235 to <strong>$39,674</strong>. Its reported $0.04/kWh energy rate does not include all depreciation, overhead or financing costs. Revenue fell 35% to $164.2 million despite higher BTC production; the company reported $632.1 million in GAAP net income, but the income figure should not be interpreted as profit from mining alone.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Expansion gives MARA a stronger base of electrical infrastructure for other compute customers. Its <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/07/mara-ferc-approves-long-ridge-505mw-ohio-power-plant-acquisition-november-2026/">505 MW Ohio power-plant transaction</a> and earlier <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/09/mara-nebraska-bitcoin-mine-sale-rumor-kearney-100mw-decommission-project-horizon/">Nebraska facility transition</a> illustrate the move toward owning or controlling power assets. The previous <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/07/bitcoin-mining-mara-sold-23093-btc-1-6b-power-ai-infrastructure/">MARA Bitcoin sales analysis</a> provides financing context.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A separate <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/14/bitdeer-q3-2026-asic-efficiency-15-4-jth-electricity-3-twh/">Bitdeer fleet-energy analysis</a> underscores an important industry distinction: newer ASICs can increase compute per joule even as the total number of operating units and aggregate electricity use expand.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bottom line:</strong> MARA’s filing verifies a large, improving ASIC fleet and substantial energized capacity. Sustained operating hashrate, mining difficulty, electricity costs and asset utilization—not rig count alone—will determine whether greater scale produces stronger returns.</p>
