# California Tells Humans to Stop Fighting Robots in Cages

- Live URL: https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/california-stops-rek-human-robot-cage-fights/
- WordPress post ID: 19707
- Featured media ID: 19706
- Published: 2026-09-30T23:28:32
- Archive: final Gutenberg source

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>California has apparently discovered the sentence nobody expected to need in 2026: please stop putting people in cages with humanoid robots.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Prediction-market platform Polymarket <a href="https://twitter.com/Polymarket/status/2105488046699470864">flagged the surreal development</a> after California reportedly ordered San Francisco robotics startup REK to stop staging unsanctioned human-versus-humanoid cage fights. The timing is almost comedic. Humanoid robotics has spent years promising factories, warehouses and household assistants, and somehow we arrived at robot fight club first.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Yes, the robot actually fought back</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The spectacle traces back to a September 18 REK event in San Francisco, where internet personality Frankie LaPenna stepped into a cage opposite an EngineAI T800 humanoid. <a href="https://www.t3.com/entertainment/when-ufc-and-robot-wars-collide-human-versus-robot-cage-fighting-is-no-longer-confined-to-sci-fi">Reporting on the bout</a> describes the machine as roughly six feet tall and shows it throwing kicks after LaPenna landed punches. REK promoted the event as its first human-versus-robot fight.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/REK/status/2101771246962938155","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"x","responsive":true} -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>There is one important reality check before Skynet gets blamed: the robot was not autonomously choosing whom to punch. A human operator remotely controlled the machine while its onboard systems handled lower-level movement and balance. That makes the event closer to physical teleoperation esports than an autonomous robot deciding it has had enough of humanity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Robot fighting is becoming a real business</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>REK, short for Robot Entertainment Kombat, has been building a live entertainment format around human-controlled humanoids. Earlier this year, the <a href="https://www.sfchronicle.com/tech/article/buy-fighting-robots-sf-22231111.php">San Francisco Chronicle reported</a> that the company was establishing a permanent San Francisco location where visitors could watch, rent and even buy humanoid robots.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The story lands during a rapid expansion of physical AI. BitcoinVersus.Tech recently covered <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/30/berkeley-humanoid-lite-under-5000-open-source-robot/">Berkeley’s sub-$5,000 open-source humanoid</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/apptronik-us-humanoid-robot-hardware-supply-chain/">Apptronik’s warning about the U.S. humanoid hardware supply chain</a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/nvidia-isaac-ros-5-0-brings-ai-agents-into-robot-development/">NVIDIA Isaac ROS 5.0 bringing AI agents deeper into robot development</a>. REK is taking many of the same advances in actuators, balance, teleoperation and control systems and pointing them toward entertainment.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The funniest robotics regulatory problem yet</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The regulatory issue is less funny than the headline sounds. Once a human participant is trading blows with a heavy electromechanical machine, questions about sanctioning, operator responsibility, emergency stops, medical supervision and equipment inspection become very real. The novelty is that combat-sports rules were written for two humans, not one human and one remotely operated robot.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Still, there is something wonderfully 2026 about regulators having to confront the phrase “human-versus-humanoid cage fight” as an actual administrative problem. We were promised robots that would fold laundry. San Francisco apparently skipped ahead to the boss battle.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
