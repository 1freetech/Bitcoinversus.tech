# PowerCompute Gets 39% More Hashrate Per Miner Swap

Published: September 25, 2026  
Live: https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/25/powercompute-gets-39-more-hashrate-per-miner-swap/

PowerCompute is demonstrating one of the simplest ways a Bitcoin miner can expand without first securing a much larger electrical footprint: replace older ASICs with machines that produce substantially more hashrate at comparable machine-level power.

The company said September 23 that its current miner-refresh program is expected to deliver nearly **39% more hashrate per machine ultimately replaced**. The plan uses Bitmain Antminer S19 XP hardware to displace older S19-generation equipment across PowerCompute's Oklahoma and Mississippi operations.

## 1,940 S19 XP miners are moving into the fleet

According to [PowerCompute's September 23 operational update](https://www.power-compute.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/201/powercompute-miner-refresh-expected-to-deliver-nearly-39), the company expects to install 1,000 additional S19 XP miners at its Oklahoma facility by September 30. Those machines are averaging approximately **132 TH/s**.

The Oklahoma deployment replaces S19j Pro units rated at 100 TH/s or below. PowerCompute plans to move those machines to Mississippi, where they will replace older S19 units averaging about 95 TH/s.

PowerCompute also ordered another **940 S19 XP miners** on September 22 for delivery and installation in October.

## 771 PH/s could become 862 PH/s

PowerCompute reported 771 PH/s as of June 30. After the first phase and redeployment it expects approximately 822 PH/s. With the additional 940 miners online it forecasts approximately **862 PH/s**, an approximately **11.8% increase** from the June baseline.

At the machine level, replacing approximately 95 TH/s units with machines averaging 132 TH/s produces roughly **38.9% more hashrate per replaced machine**.

## More SHA-256 compute from comparable power

PowerCompute reports the incoming miners at approximately **21.5 J/TH**, compared with approximately **34 J/TH** for the machines ultimately being retired, while drawing comparable total power.

This matters because electrical capacity is often the scarcer industrial asset. Utility service, transformers, switchgear, conductors, cooling and energized rack positions can constrain expansion.

## $400,000 refresh targets $1.2 million in incremental annual capacity

The total cost of the 1,000 September machines and 940-machine order is approximately **$400,000**. PowerCompute says the purchases are funded from cash on hand and Bitcoin holdings.

Using a hashprice of $40 per PH/s per day as of September 21, PowerCompute estimates approximately **$1.2 million per year in incremental revenue capacity**, before curtailment and downtime.

## Why an S19 XP refresh can still make sense in the S21 era

The purchase is an interesting counterpoint to the industry's move toward S21-generation machines. As [BitcoinVersus.tech reported](https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/25/antminer-s21-is-the-new-s19/), customer demand and modern industrial deployments increasingly favor S21 hardware.

But acquisition cost matters. PowerCompute's stated approximately $400,000 cost for 1,940 machines works out to roughly **$206 per miner** before other transaction or deployment costs. For an operator with energized infrastructure and older 34 J/TH equipment, inexpensive S19 XP hardware can still create a large efficiency and density improvement.

## Another 3 MW remains available

PowerCompute says it also has up to **3 MW of additional energized capacity** in Mississippi. Using it for further Bitcoin mining or AI infrastructure would require additional capital and infrastructure.

The refresh is a useful mining-density case study: PowerCompute is targeting an 11.8% company-wide hashrate increase largely by putting more efficient silicon behind electrical infrastructure it already controls.
