About 7,000 humanoid robots were sold worldwide in 2025 for industrial and professional service applications, according to data compiled by the International Federation of Robotics. The figure provides an early industry benchmark for a robotics category attracting major investment while remaining small compared with established automation markets.

The IFR defines a humanoid robot as a machine with a human like appearance that can operate autonomously in environments designed for people. Legs are not required under the definition. The new sales tally covers industrial and professional service machines while excluding consumer and military humanoids, and medical robots are classified separately.

Humanoid sales remain small beside conventional robotics. IFR data show roughly 542,000 industrial robots were installed worldwide in 2024, while about 199,000 professional service robots were sold for applications including transportation, hospitality and cleaning.

Many humanoid robots sold during 2025 were not yet performing routine productive labor. IFR Secretary General Susanne Bieller told Reuters that research institutions and companies bought many units to generate data for improving artificial intelligence models.

The data highlight a major distinction between technical demonstrations and commercial deployment. Humanoid robots have demonstrated increasingly sophisticated locomotion and manipulation, but factory use requires repeatability, safety, uptime, integration with production systems and an economic advantage over conventional automation.

Forecasts nevertheless anticipate substantial growth. Reuters reported that Bank of America Global Research estimates about 90,000 humanoid robots could ship in 2026 and 1.2 million units in 2030. Forecasts are not measured sales, and the IFR figure establishes a much smaller observed baseline.
