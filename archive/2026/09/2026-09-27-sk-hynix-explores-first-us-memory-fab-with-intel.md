# SK hynix Explores First U.S. Memory Fab With Intel

SK hynix is exploring options that could bring wafer-stage memory manufacturing to the United States, including possible cooperation with Intel. The development is significant because SK hynix has become one of the central suppliers in the artificial-intelligence memory boom, while the United States is attempting to expand domestic semiconductor manufacturing capacity.

Reuters reported on September 16, 2026 that SK hynix and Intel were in discussions involving possible U.S. memory-chip manufacturing. SK hynix subsequently said it was considering multiple options while emphasizing that no agreement or manufacturing plan had been finalized.

That distinction matters. The signal is not a completed factory transaction. It is that one of the world's most important memory manufacturers is evaluating whether more of the semiconductor production chain should move onto U.S. soil.

## Why U.S. Memory Manufacturing Matters

AI infrastructure has pushed memory closer to the center of semiconductor strategy. Training and inference accelerators depend on large quantities of high-bandwidth memory and other advanced memory products. As accelerator deployments grow, the physical manufacturing capacity behind memory becomes part of the AI infrastructure equation.

SK hynix already has a major U.S. investment underway in Indiana focused on advanced packaging and research. A wafer-stage manufacturing arrangement would represent a different layer of the supply chain.

Intel, meanwhile, has spent heavily expanding U.S. semiconductor manufacturing capacity. Cooperation with another large chip producer could increase utilization of domestic fabrication infrastructure while giving SK hynix another potential manufacturing route.

## The Intel Connection

Any potential collaboration would need to be understood in the context of Intel's broader manufacturing strategy. Intel has been opening its fabrication network to outside customers while investing in new process technology and U.S. production capacity.

For SK hynix, the attraction would not simply be geographic diversification. U.S.-based wafer manufacturing could shorten portions of the supply chain serving American AI infrastructure and reduce reliance on a manufacturing footprint concentrated in Asia.

However, neither company has announced a finalized manufacturing agreement. The reported discussions should therefore be treated as exploratory.

## AI Is Rewriting the Memory Supply Chain

The larger story is the accelerating importance of memory in AI systems.

GPUs and other accelerators receive most of the attention, but the performance of modern AI systems increasingly depends on moving enormous quantities of data between processors and memory. HBM places stacks of high-speed memory close to AI accelerators, increasing bandwidth while reducing the distance data must travel.

That demand has changed capital-allocation decisions across the semiconductor industry. Memory suppliers are expanding HBM capacity, advanced packaging is becoming more strategically important, and governments are competing to attract semiconductor production.

A possible SK hynix manufacturing footprint in the United States would fit that larger transition.

## What to Watch

The next meaningful signal would be a formal agreement identifying a manufacturing site, process technology, production volume, investment amount or construction timeline.

Until then, the most important fact is that discussions are taking place at all. AI demand is encouraging semiconductor companies to reconsider where memory, processors, packaging and supporting infrastructure should physically be built.

For the United States, attracting wafer-stage memory production would deepen a domestic semiconductor ecosystem that already includes expanding logic, packaging and AI infrastructure investments.

For SK hynix, a U.S. manufacturing option could provide geographic diversification and closer proximity to one of the world's largest concentrations of AI infrastructure investment.

The discussions remain preliminary, but they illustrate how the AI buildout is reshaping not only what chips are produced, but where the factories producing them may ultimately operate.
