# EdgeCortix Unveils 3.36-PFLOPS RAIDEN AI Chiplet

Japanese fabless semiconductor company EdgeCortix has unveiled RAIDEN, a scalable AI chiplet platform aimed at physical AI systems such as robots, autonomous machines, aerospace equipment and high-performance edge servers.

The flagship RAIDEN X4 is architected for up to **3.36 PFLOPS of FP4 AI compute**, up to **256 GB of memory**, 548 GB/s of memory bandwidth, up to 1.54 TB/s of aggregate die-to-die bandwidth and as much as 6.4 Tb/s of chip-to-chip scale-out connectivity. These are EdgeCortix's announced specifications rather than independent benchmark results.

## Four dies operate as one AI system

RAIDEN scales from a single compute die to the four-die X4 while preserving a common hardware and software environment. EdgeCortix says X4 is designed to operate its dies as a tightly integrated system using the DNA-X accelerator architecture and MERA software stack.

The approach fits the broader semiconductor shift toward chiplet and heterogeneous integration. Designers can scale compute and connectivity across multiple dies inside one package, creating tradeoffs involving yield, packaging, thermal density, memory bandwidth and interconnect latency.

## RAIDEN targets physical AI outside the data center

EdgeCortix calls its target market the “thick edge,” where high-performance AI runs close to sensors, machines and operational environments. Robotics, autonomous systems, smart manufacturing, telecommunications, aerospace and edge AI infrastructure are among the intended applications.

Power efficiency and memory movement are especially important in these systems because compute, memory, interconnects and cooling have to fit practical electrical and thermal envelopes.

## Kawasaki Heavy Industries is an early customer

EdgeCortix says Kawasaki Heavy Industries selected RAIDEN-based solutions for multiple next-generation aerospace and defense products, while Unigen is developing RAIDEN-based server platforms. Customer sampling is expected in early 2027, with volume production planned for the second half of 2027.

The next meaningful test will come when production silicon reaches customers and independent workloads can measure sustained performance, power consumption, latency and software compatibility.

## AI hardware is becoming a system problem

RAIDEN reinforces a broader trend: raw arithmetic throughput matters, but memory capacity, bandwidth, packaging, interconnects, software and power efficiency increasingly determine whether that compute can be used effectively.
