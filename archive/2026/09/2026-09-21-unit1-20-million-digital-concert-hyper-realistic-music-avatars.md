# Digital Concert Startup Unit1 Raises $20 Million to Build Hyper-Realistic Music Avatars

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Digital performers are moving beyond one-off concert experiments and toward a potentially repeatable live-entertainment format. UK music-technology company Unit1 Studio has secured $20 million in new financing as it develops mixed-reality concerts built around lifelike digital avatars, live musicians, LED displays and physical stage production.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The funding follows a practical test of the company's central idea: making an avatar concert portable. <a href="https://unit1.studio/">Unit1 describes its work</a> as combining avatar production, creative direction, staging, venue operations and other production services into an end-to-end system. Industry reporting published September 20 and 21 says the new financing includes an equity round led by Balderton Capital plus production financing for concerts now in development.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">KT Tunstall Became the Test Case</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Unit1 has tested its approach with Scottish singer-songwriter KT Tunstall. Tunstall was recorded with motion-capture cameras while recreating a performance from roughly two decades earlier. The production used that performance data to build a digital version of the artist representing her current appearance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The company then moved the KT Tunstall avatar showcase from its earlier production location to a purpose-built studio at Tileyard London. Unit1 says the transfer demonstrated that an avatar concert can be relocated rather than permanently tied to one specialized venue. The company's commercial argument is that reducing the cost and difficulty of replication could make this type of show available to more artists and potentially allow versions of a production to operate in multiple locations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Stage Is Both Physical and Digital</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Unit1's proposed concerts are not simply prerecorded videos. The company says real musicians can perform live on stage while the digital performer appears through high-definition LED technology. Lighting, sound and physical props are then coordinated with the screen content to make the boundary between the physical stage and virtual performer less obvious to the audience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Digital Resurrection Creates a Rights Question</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The technology becomes more complicated when a production involves an artist who has died. Unit1 says it intends to work with artists, families, estates and rights holders and plans to use part of its financing for intellectual-property and licensing agreements. That distinction is important: the technical ability to recreate someone's appearance or performance does not by itself establish permission to use it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The success of ABBA Voyage has already demonstrated that audiences can accept a concert centered on digital representations of performers. Unit1's proposed difference is portability. Instead of requiring every production to remain in a dedicated venue, it is trying to build a model that can be reproduced and moved more like conventional touring entertainment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Whether audiences ultimately consider these performances authentic will depend on more than graphics quality. Consent, rights ownership, the involvement of the original artist or estate, live musicianship and transparency about what is prerecorded or digitally generated may become as important as the underlying motion-capture and display technology.</p>
