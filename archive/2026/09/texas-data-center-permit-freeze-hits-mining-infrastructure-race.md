# Texas Data Center Permit Freeze Hits Mining Infrastructure Race

Published: September 25, 2026
Live: https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/25/texas-data-center-permit-freeze-hits-mining-infrastructure-race/

Texas has paused new state permits for data centers while regulators audit the industry's impact on electricity and water, putting a new constraint around one of the most important power markets for Bitcoin mining and AI infrastructure.

On September 21, Governor Greg Abbott directed TCEQ to halt permits sought by data centers until ERCOT and state water regulators complete their audits. Reuters reports more than 470 GW of proposed projects are seeking grid connections in Texas.

## Why Bitcoin miners should care

Texas became a major Bitcoin mining market because large flexible loads can locate near abundant generation and participate in ERCOT demand-response and curtailment programs. The same grid access is now being pursued by enormous AI and hyperscale data-center projects.

The action does not erase existing mining operations, but it changes the development environment for new large-load campuses and expansions requiring state permits.

## The queue is far larger than the Texas grid

Texas has accumulated more than 470 GW of data-center projects seeking grid access. Not every project will be built, but the queue demonstrates competition for substations, transmission and generation.

For miners, the practical bottleneck is increasingly the energized megawatt: interconnection, transformers, switchgear, protection, distribution, networking and cooling.

## Flexible mining loads remain technically different

Bitcoin mining can shut down large blocks of compute rapidly when power prices spike or ERCOT needs load reduction. That differs from many AI workloads targeting much higher continuous utilization.

## Power infrastructure is becoming the scarce asset

ASICs and GPUs can be purchased on commercial timelines. New substations, transmission upgrades and interconnections can take years. For Texas mining expansions, permitting and interconnection risk now deserve the same attention as hashprice, efficiency and electricity price.
