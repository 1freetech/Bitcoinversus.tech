# Soluna’s 100 MW Bitcoin Campus Opens Path to 300 MW AI Build

Soluna Holdings’ wind-powered Project Dorothy in Briscoe County, Texas is becoming a useful case study in how Bitcoin mining infrastructure can secure power today while creating a path toward much larger AI data-center development.

Recent industry coverage highlighted ERCOT’s conditional Base Load classification for the 100 MW Dorothy campus. Soluna says Dorothy 1 and Dorothy 2 already account for 98 MW of energized capacity, while an additional 50 MW interconnection request is under study. The company is separately developing Dorothy 3 as a planned 300 MW AI and high-performance computing expansion.

## 98 MW is already energized for Bitcoin infrastructure

Project Dorothy sits behind the meter at the 150 MW Briscoe Wind Farm, which Soluna acquired in April 2026. Soluna’s current project inventory identifies Dorothy 1 as a 50 MW Bitcoin hosting and mining campus and Dorothy 2 as another 48 MW of Bitcoin hosting and mining capacity.

Soluna’s project page lists more than 15,000 operational miners at Dorothy 1 and more than 13,000 at Dorothy 2 in its latest displayed site statistics. The company uses excess and curtailed wind generation as an input for compute rather than treating the data center as a conventional grid-only load.

## Another 50 MW is under study

Soluna says it has submitted a load interconnection request for an additional 50 MW at Dorothy. The local transmission and distribution provider is studying that request directly. The company says the existing 100 MW campus has been conditionally classified as Base Load in ERCOT’s Batch Zero process.

Soluna cited more than 438 GW of large-load requests in ERCOT’s queue as of mid-2026, with data centers representing nearly 90% of those requests.

## Bitcoin mining is the bridge to a 300 MW AI campus

Soluna is developing Dorothy 3 for AI and HPC workloads on land adjacent to the existing mining campuses and the company-owned wind farm.

Across its portfolio, Soluna says it has approximately 206 MW of operating capacity and a development pipeline of roughly 6.3 GW.

## Why this matters for Bitcoin mining

Land, generation, substations, transformers, interconnection rights, cooling and operating experience can determine whether a mining site can expand or support another form of compute. Project Dorothy shows how an operating Bitcoin campus can sit at the center of that transition.
