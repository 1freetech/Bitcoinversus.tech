The European Commission has proposed a common sustainability rating system for large data centers as Europe prepares for rapid growth in artificial intelligence infrastructure. Facilities with installed information technology capacity of at least 500 kW already report operational data under EU energy efficiency rules, and the proposed rating system will turn reported information into comparable electronic labels covering energy, water and integration with the electricity system.

The rating scheme is designed to make resource efficiency easier to compare between facilities. The Commission says labels will cover energy and water use along with contributions to the local energy system, including waste heat reuse, clean energy generation and operational flexibility. Reuters reports that the proposal does not impose limits on total energy or water consumption and does not require operators to publish total power use through the label.

Data center electricity demand is becoming a larger infrastructure issue as AI computing expands. An EU energy efficiency report says European data centers consumed about 68 TWh of electricity in 2024 and projects consumption of roughly 114 TWh by 2030, equal to about 3.2 percent of EU electricity demand.

Cooling architecture is especially important for water efficiency. The Commission defines Water Usage Effectiveness as total water input divided by the energy consumed by information technology equipment. EU reporting for 2024 produced an average WUE of 0.58 among reporting data centers.

Waste heat is another metric with potential value outside a server campus. The Commission estimates that recovering around half of the waste heat produced by European data centers could provide an amount of heat comparable to the heating demand of four million households.

The proposal now faces a two month scrutiny period by the European Parliament and the Council. The Commission expects the first individual sustainability labels to appear in 2027. A separate 12 week consultation is examining possible minimum performance standards for European data centers, with a legislative proposal planned for the second quarter of 2027.
