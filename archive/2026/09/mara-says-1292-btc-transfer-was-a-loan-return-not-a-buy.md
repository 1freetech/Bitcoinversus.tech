# MARA Says 1,292 BTC Transfer Was a Loan Return, Not a Buy

Published: September 27, 2026
Live: https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/27/mara-says-1292-btc-transfer-was-a-loan-return-not-a-buy/

A 1,292 BTC transfer into wallets associated with MARA Holdings was widely reported as a roughly $98.6 million Bitcoin purchase. MARA says that interpretation was wrong: the coins were returning from a loan, not newly purchased Bitcoin.

## MARA says the Bitcoin was returned from a loan

MARA investor-relations vice president Robert Samuels publicly corrected reports about the transfer, saying MARA did not purchase additional Bitcoin and instead received the Bitcoin back from a loan.

The distinction is material. A new purchase represents deployment of fresh capital. A loan return moves an existing company asset back from a counterparty and does not by itself increase economic Bitcoin ownership.

## MARA already disclosed thousands of BTC in active treasury strategies

MARA's SEC-filed Q2 report said it held 35,577 BTC as of June 30, including 9,270 BTC under its digital-asset-management strategy: 4,742 BTC loaned and 4,528 BTC pledged as collateral.

## Why on-chain attribution needs corporate context

Blockchain data can show amount, timing and destination patterns, but identifying economic purpose requires additional evidence. A transfer can represent a purchase, collateral movement, custody migration or repayment of previously loaned Bitcoin.

MARA's filing says Bitcoin lending generated about $4.3 million of interest income during Q2.
