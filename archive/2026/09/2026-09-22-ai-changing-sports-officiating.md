# AI Is Changing Sports Officiating

**Published:** September 22, 2026  
**Primary topic:** Sports  
**Live article:** https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/09/22/ai-changing-sports-officiating/

Professional sports increasingly use computer vision, tracking cameras, sensors and automated analysis to help officials evaluate events that happen faster—or at angles more difficult—than humans can consistently judge.

## FIFA Uses Technology to Track Offside

FIFA developed semi-automated offside technology to help video match officials identify potential offside situations. Dedicated tracking cameras and computer processing estimate player positions and provide officials with measurements that can support a decision. The technology assists rather than simply replaces the referee: officials still evaluate the situation and apply the rules.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MxpI9GKizZE

## Cameras Are Becoming Sensors

A modern stadium can function as a data-collection environment. Multiple synchronized cameras observe players and the ball while software converts movement into machine-readable coordinates. The same infrastructure can support refereeing, tactical analysis, broadcast graphics, performance measurement and statistical models.

## Baseball Is Following the Same Path

Major League Baseball has also tested automated ball-strike technology. Tracking systems can estimate where a pitch crosses the strike zone, while challenge-based approaches preserve an initial human call and allow technology to review close decisions. That model illustrates a broader direction: humans manage the game while computers measure difficult events.

## AI Still Has Limits

More technology does not eliminate controversy. A tracking system can precisely measure an event while the sporting rule still requires interpretation. Automated systems also introduce engineering requirements involving calibration, sensor accuracy, network latency, software reliability and redundancy.
