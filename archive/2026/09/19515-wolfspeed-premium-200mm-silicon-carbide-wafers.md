# Wolfspeed Launches Premium 200 mm Silicon Carbide Wafers

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><em>AI-generated conceptual illustration of wafer inspection, not a photograph of Wolfspeed’s Premium substrate or equipment.</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Wolfspeed announced a new Premium 200 mm n-type silicon-carbide substrate on September 27, 2026. The product targets a manufacturing problem behind advanced power devices: improving the quality and consistency of the wafer before customers build devices on it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In its <a href="https://www.tmcnet.com/usubmit/2026/09/27/10452417.htm">published announcement</a>, the company says the substrate is available to order with dedicated capacity. It reports fewer than 0.01 micropipes per square centimeter and fewer than three per wafer, validated through X-ray topography. Those figures are supplier-reported specifications.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A materials upgrade within the 200 mm transition</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The launch adds a premium grade to an existing wafer platform. Wolfspeed points to continuous 200 mm production since 2022 and says improvements in defects and wafer shape can help customers obtain better epitaxial results within their existing processes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Earlier in September, the company’s <a href="https://twitter.com/Wolfspeed/status/2097686883123753053">public update</a> highlighted its transition to 200 mm n-type wafers ahead of ICSCRM in Yokohama. The post below provides background on that manufacturing direction; it predates the Premium product announcement.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the specifications establish</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As <a href="https://semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2026/sep/wolfspeed-280926.shtml">industry coverage explains</a>, the company is emphasizing defect density, micropipe count and wafer-shape stability as device requirements grow. The announcement does not disclose a customer-specific yield improvement percentage, substrate price or independent comparison against rival wafers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a buyer, the unanswered question is the economic result after qualification: how many acceptable devices emerge from each lot, at what cost, and with what repeatability? A lower reported defect count is useful evidence to evaluate, but it does not by itself establish the cost of a finished device.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
