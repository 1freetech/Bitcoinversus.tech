<p><strong>The CPI-Bitcoin Connection</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/09/04/bitcoins-september-challenge-benjamin-cohen-discusses-bearish-data-trends/">Bitcoin</a> aficionados, inflation has often been perceived as a favorable factor. Historical data underscores this sentiment, showcasing two of Bitcoin's all-time highs of $65,000 and $69,000 during a phase of robust monetary expansion and escalating inflation in 2021. Furthermore, as governments and colossal institutional investors warm up to Bitcoin, the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/03/26/bitcoin-vs-property-the-return-of-the-final-boss/">monthly CPI</a> data's impact on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/07/23/decoding-bitcoins-price-a-technical-review/">BTC prices</a> becomes more evident. To underscore this, a mere 48 hours post the last CPI release on July 12 led to a 4% surge in Bitcoin's price, establishing a new zenith for 2023 at $31,500.</p>

<p><strong>The Ripple Effect on Disposable Income</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>An inverse relationship exists between consumer prices and Bitcoin's price. When consumer prices soar, Bitcoin<a href="https://www.skrill.com/en/skrill-news/crypto/what-is-the-relationship-between-crypto-and-inflation/"> tends to see a downturn</a>. This pattern isn't isolated to Bitcoin alone but spans the entirety of the digital asset spectrum. Simplified, rising prices on essentials like food and utilities leave consumers with less disposable income. Since priorities like sustenance and utility bills supersede investment in Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, there's a palpable reduction in expenditure on digital assets.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Crypto's Affinity with Monetary Inflation</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Monetary inflation, characterized by the swelling money supply, usually results in a corresponding climb in the CPI. However, it's crucial to delineate between monetary inflation and the prices consumers and manufacturers deal with. The former encapsulates the volume of money in circulation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A noticeable trend is that when the M2 money supply augments, Bitcoin and the crypto realm bask in substantial gains. Conversely, when the M2 contracts, the digital assets realm grapples. Drawing parallels with historical graphs, it becomes apparent that the M2 growth rate began its descent towards the end of 2021 and the commencement of 2022. This dovetailed with the apex of the crypto market the preceding year, suggesting that this wasn't a mere coincidence.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bitcoin: The Monetary Inflation Hedge</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Time and again, history has spotlighted Bitcoin's market cap evolution in tandem with the M2 supply's growth, making it evident that cryptocurrency stands as a bulwark against monetary inflation. Thus, with the anticipation of the money supply burgeoning anew, the crypto market cap is likely to emulate its trajectory.</p>
