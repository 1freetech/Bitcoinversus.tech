<p style="text-transform:none">An <strong>Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)</strong> is a device that provides <strong>emergency backup power</strong> to a computer or network system during a power outage or voltage fluctuation. It acts as a <strong>buffer</strong> between the computer and the power source, ensuring that critical systems remain operational long enough to safely shut down or switch to an alternative power source. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">A UPS typically includes a <strong>battery</strong> that takes over when the main power supply is interrupted, preventing data loss and hardware damage caused by sudden shutdowns. It also protects against <strong>power surges</strong>, <strong>brownouts</strong>, and <strong>overvoltage</strong>, safeguarding sensitive components like servers, workstations, and network devices.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">There are several types of UPS systems, including <strong>standby (offline)</strong>, <strong>line-interactive</strong>, and <strong>online (double-conversion)</strong>, each designed for different levels of power protection. <strong>Standby UPS</strong> is the most basic type, commonly used for home or small office setups, while <strong>online UPS</strong> offers continuous power conditioning for mission-critical systems. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">UPS devices also provide <strong>surge protection</strong> and <strong>automatic voltage regulation (AVR)</strong>, which stabilizes the incoming voltage to prevent damage from power fluctuations. Many UPS systems come with <strong>management software</strong> that can initiate a graceful shutdown of the connected system when the battery is low. In IT environments, a UPS is essential for <strong>server rooms</strong>, <strong>data centers</strong>, and <strong>critical infrastructure</strong> to ensure <strong>business continuity</strong> and protect valuable data during unexpected power interruptions.</p>
