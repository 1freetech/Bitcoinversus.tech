<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">In a recent statement, the<a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/26/michael-saylor-anticipates-pro-bitcoin-policies-following-u-s-elections/"> U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission</a> (SEC) clarified that Proof-of-Work (PoW) <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/03/understanding-mining-algorithms-and-their-role-in-securing-cryptocurrency-networks/">cryptocurrency mining</a> activities, such as those involving <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/08/heres-how-scarce-bitcoin-actually-is/">Bitcoin</a>, do not fall under federal securities laws. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance noted that both solo and pooled mining operations do not meet the criteria of a securities transaction under the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/27/gary-gensler-declares-most-crypto-projects-are-securities/">Howey Test</a>, as miners' profits are derived from their own computational efforts rather than the managerial efforts of others.</p>
