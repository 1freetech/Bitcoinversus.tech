<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <em>Proof-of-Power Wall</em> concept from Jason P. Lowery’s <em><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/12/04/power-is-money-the-economical-physics-of-bitcoin/">SOFTWAR</a></em> introduces a cybersecurity model where control signals must carry proof of computational work to be accepted. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">By rejecting signals that lack this proof, the system ensures that every authorized command comes with a cost, reducing the risk of exploitation. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Adjusting the difficulty of the proof-of-power process allows the system to maintain a secure operating environment by dynamically increasing or decreasing the computational burden as needed. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">If threats rise, the system can require more proof, making unauthorized access increasingly expensive and impractical.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":11149,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/image-19.png?w=588" alt="" class="wp-image-11149" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This idea follows a fundamental principle seen in nature—survival requires continuous investment. Organisms, organizations, and now digital systems must expend resources to maintain security. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Just as The <em>Proof-of-Power Wall</em> concept presents a cybersecurity model where only control signals that bear computational proof-of-power are accepted, effectively filtering out unauthorized or malicious attempts to influence a system. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This approach directly applies to securing critical infrastructure, such as power grids, against cyber-physical threats. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">By requiring a cost to send control signals, the system prevents low-cost, high-frequency attacks that could otherwise compromise essential operations. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The difficulty of the proof-of-power process can be adjusted dynamically, increasing security as threats evolve while maintaining a functional level of efficiency for legitimate operations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The broader implications of this model align with recent U.S. policy shifts. On March 7, 2025, the White House issued an order establishing a <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/08/03/senator-cynthia-lummis-introduces-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-bill/">Strategic Bitcoin Reserve</a>, recognizing Bitcoin’s role as a scarce and secure asset similar to "digital gold." </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This move signals a growing acknowledgment of Bitcoin’s underlying proof-of-work mechanism as a tool for national security, reinforcing the idea that computationally expensive security models can provide a higher level of systemic resilience. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <em>Proof-of-Power Wall</em> builds on these principles by applying the same economic constraints that make Bitcoin secure to the digital defense of critical infrastructure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The diagram emphasizes the contrast between traditional control systems—where low-cost, easily exploitable signals can manipulate power grids—and a proof-of-power model that demands a computational investment. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">In this framework, Bitcoin mining itself can serve as a protective mechanism, ensuring that only signals backed by verifiable work can interact with the system. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">By making attacks prohibitively expensive while keeping legitimate operations efficient, proof-of-power creates a self-regulating security layer that aligns with broader national efforts to reinforce digital and physical security.</p>
