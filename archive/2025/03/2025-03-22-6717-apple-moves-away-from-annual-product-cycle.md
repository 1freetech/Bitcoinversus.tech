<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/09/25/apple-opens-iphone-nfc-chip-to-third-parties-paving-way-for-bitcoin-payments/">Apple</a> is gradually <a href="https://www.phonearena.com/news/apple-moving-away-from-yearly-product-releases_id163431">moving away</a> from its long-standing tradition of annual product releases, signaling a major change in its approach to innovation and consumer expectations. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Historically, the company adhered to a predictable schedule, launching updated iPhones, iPads, and Macs each year. This <a href="https://www.macrumors.com/2024/10/08/apple-release-schedule-changes/">strategy</a> benefited investors and aligned product releases with key sales periods, such as the holidays. However, Apple’s expanding product lineup has made it difficult to sustain yearly upgrades across all devices​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Mark Gurman from Bloomberg <a href="https://www.phonearena.com/news/apple-moving-away-from-yearly-product-releases_id163431">reports</a> that Apple is now favoring a more flexible release schedule, focusing on launching products when they are fully ready rather than adhering to a rigid annual cycle. This shift allows the company to prioritize quality over speed, reducing the risk of incomplete software or hardware features, which have plagued recent releases​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">While iPhones are <a href="https://www.macrumors.com/2024/10/08/apple-release-schedule-changes/">expected</a> to continue their yearly updates due to competitive pressures, other devices like the Apple Watch Ultra and the iPhone SE are now set to follow a two-year refresh cycle. This more staggered approach could foster greater innovation by giving Apple’s teams more time to develop substantial upgrades, rather than incremental improvements​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This strategic <a href="https://thedroidguy.com/apple-is-moving-away-from-yearly-iphone-product-upgrade-cycle-1265702">pivot</a> could benefit consumers, providing more meaningful upgrades when they do occur. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">For businesses, the flexible schedule may better align with corporate upgrade cycles, allowing for more impactful device refreshes. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Financially, the shift away from annual releases might cause short-term complications in revenue comparisons but could lead to long-term gains as Apple strengthens its product lineup with game-changing features​.</p>
