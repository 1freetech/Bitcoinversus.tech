<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>Definition and Functionality</strong><br>Serial communication refers to the process of transmitting data <strong>one bit at a time</strong> over a communication channel or computer bus. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>Unlike parallel communication</strong>, which sends multiple bits simultaneously, serial communication reduces signal interference and supports <strong>long-distance data transfer</strong> with fewer wires. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>It is widely used in telecommunications, industrial automation, and computer networking</strong> to facilitate reliable and efficient communication between devices.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p><strong>Types of Serial Communication</strong><br>There are two primary types of serial communication:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Synchronous Serial Communication</strong> – Data is transmitted in sync with a clock signal, ensuring precise timing. <strong>Common protocols include SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface) and I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)</strong>, often used in embedded systems and microcontrollers.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Asynchronous Serial Communication</strong> – Data is sent without a shared clock signal, relying on start and stop bits to define transmission boundaries. <strong>RS-232, RS-485, and UART (Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter) are widely used in computers, industrial equipment, and embedded systems.</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>Everyday Use of Serial Communication</strong><br>Serial communication plays a fundamental role in modern technology. <strong>Point-of-sale systems, industrial control systems, GPS receivers, and IoT devices rely on serial data transmission</strong> for real-time functionality. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>USB (Universal Serial Bus), which replaced older RS-232 serial ports, is now a standard for peripheral connectivity</strong>, enabling devices like keyboards, mice, and printers to communicate efficiently with computers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>Common Ports and Cables</strong><br>Several standard interfaces are used in serial communication:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>RS-232</strong> – Found in older computers and industrial systems, typically using <strong>DB9 or DB25 connectors</strong>.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>RS-485</strong> – Used for industrial automation and long-distance communication with <strong>twisted pair cables</strong>.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>USB (Universal Serial Bus)</strong> – The most common modern serial interface, supporting high-speed data transfer.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>UART (Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter)</strong> – Used in microcontrollers and embedded systems for simple serial communication.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
