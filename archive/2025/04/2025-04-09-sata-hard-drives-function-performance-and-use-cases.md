<p><strong>SATA (Serial ATA)</strong> hard drives are widely used for internal storage in desktops and laptops, offering a balance of performance and cost for general computing tasks. Introduced as a replacement for older <strong>PATA (Parallel ATA)</strong> drives, SATA provides faster data transfer speeds and thinner cables for improved airflow within the system chassis. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The most common version, <strong>SATA III</strong>, supports speeds up to <strong>6 Gb/s</strong> (roughly 600 MB/s), though mechanical hard drives (HDDs) typically operate well below that due to physical spinning limitations. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>SATA interfaces are also used by <strong>SATA SSDs</strong>, which take advantage of the interface’s bandwidth limits but still outperform mechanical drives significantly in speed and durability.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>SATA hard drives remain a reliable choice for mass storage due to their <strong>large capacities</strong>—often exceeding <strong>1 TB to 16 TB per drive</strong>—and affordability per gigabyte. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>They're commonly used for file servers, data archiving, backups, and extended multimedia storage. For CompTIA A+ candidates, understanding the role of SATA hard drives includes recognizing their connectors (15-pin power and 7-pin data), installation procedures, and performance limitations. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Technicians must also be aware of BIOS/UEFI drive detection, formatting standards (NTFS, exFAT), and how to differentiate SATA from faster storage interfaces like NVMe.</p>
