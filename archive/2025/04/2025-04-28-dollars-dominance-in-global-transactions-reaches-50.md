<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2023/11/28/bitcoins-rising-value-outpaces-traditional-wage-growth/">U.S. dollar's share</a> in global payments <a href="https://www.macrobond.com/insights/blogs/de-dollarisation-the-yuans-use-grows-but-the-greenbacks-global-role-is-proving-to-be-stubborn?utm_source=chatgpt.com">has reached</a> nearly 50%, marking its highest level in over 12 years, according to data from Macrobond. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>This resurgence underscores the greenback's enduring dominance in international finance, despite ongoing discussions about de-dollarization efforts. ​</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>In January 2025, <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-20/us-dollar-s-use-in-global-transactions-tops-50-swift-says?utm_source=chatgpt.com">the dollar accounted</a> for 50.2% of global transactions processed through the SWIFT network, up from 49.1% the previous month. This increase represents the highest proportion since SWIFT revised its data collection methods in mid-2023.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The euro, once a strong contender, has seen its share decline to approximately 20%, while the Chinese yuan's growth has plateaued. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Despite initiatives like China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS), the yuan's global payment share remains modest, highlighting the dollar's entrenched position. ​<a href="https://www.uobgroup.com/web-resources/uobgroup/pdf/research/MSN_230515.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Bank</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Analysts attribute the dollar's sustained dominance to its deep liquidity, widespread acceptance, and the absence of a comparable alternative. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Even amid geopolitical tensions and efforts by some nations to diversify away from the dollar, its role as the primary currency for global trade and finance remains unchallenged.'</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
