<p><strong>NFC (Near Field Communication)</strong> is a short-range wireless communication protocol that enables two electronic devices to exchange data when they are within approximately <strong>4 centimeters</strong> of each other. Operating at <strong>13.56 MHz</strong>, NFC is used primarily in <strong>mobile payments, identification badges, smart cards, and secure authentication systems</strong>. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Unlike <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/07/26/how-to-connect-your-ipad-with-a-uag-keyboard-bluetooth-lite-review/">Bluetooth</a>, which requires device pairing, NFC connections are instantaneous and rely on <strong>inductive coupling</strong> to transfer information. It supports three modes: <strong>peer-to-peer</strong>, <strong>reader/writer</strong>, and <strong>card emulation</strong>, allowing a smartphone, for example, to act as a payment card, a digital ID, or even a transit pass.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>NFC is essential in troubleshooting <strong>mobile device connectivity</strong>, configuring <strong>tap-to-pay services</strong>, and deploying <strong>device-based authentication methods</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Because it consumes little power and functions passively in some modes, NFC can be embedded in low-power devices like hotel keycards or wristbands. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>It is commonly found integrated into Android and iOS smartphones, and often works in conjunction with <strong>secure elements or trusted execution environments (TEE)</strong>. Technicians must also understand NFC's limitations, such as its short range, susceptibility to interference from metal objects, and the importance of ensuring <strong>firmware and OS-level security</strong> to avoid unauthorized data exchanges.</p>
