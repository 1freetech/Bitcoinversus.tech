<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>​In March 2025, U.S. inflation <a href="https://www.wsj.com/economy/cpi-inflation-march-2025-interest-rate-6327d1f8?utm_source=chatgpt.com">decreased</a> more than anticipated, with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rising 2.4% year-over-year, down from 2.8% in February and below the projected 2.6%. The Core CPI, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/inflation-march-cpi-consumer-price-index-federal-reserve-interest-rates-2025-4?utm_source=chatgpt.com">increased</a> by 2.8%, marking its lowest annual rise since March 2021. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>Key Highlights:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Monthly Decline:</strong> The CPI decreased by 0.1% in March, following a 0.2% increase in February, primarily due to a significant drop in energy prices.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Core Inflation:</strong> Core CPI rose 0.1% month-over-month, after a 0.2% increase in February. ​<a href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/us-consumer-prices-unexpectedly-fall-march-2025-04-10/?utm_source=chatgpt.com" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Reuters</a></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>Market Reactions:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Despite the favorable inflation data, financial markets <a href="https://nypost.com/2025/04/10/business/inflation-slows-more-than-expected-in-march-in-first-round-of-data-since-trumps-tariffs/?utm_source=chatgpt.com">exhibited volatility</a>. Futures tied to major indices declined following <a href="https://nypost.com/2025/04/10/business/inflation-slows-more-than-expected-in-march-in-first-round-of-data-since-trumps-tariffs/?utm_source=chatgpt.com">the report</a>:​</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Dow Jones Industrial Average (DIA):</strong> Futures dropped by 1%.​</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>S&amp;P 500 (SPY):</strong> Futures decreased by 1.3%.​</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Nasdaq 100 (QQQ):</strong> Futures fell by 1.7%. </li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>Policy Implications:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The Federal Reserve, which paused interest rate cuts in January, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/us-consumer-prices-unexpectedly-fall-march-2025-04-10/?utm_source=chatgpt.com">may consider</a> resuming easing in June, with the current rate at 4.25%-4.50%. Policymakers are concerned about the dual threat of rising inflation and slowing growth, especially in light of recent tariff escalations. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>Outlook:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>While the March data <a href="https://nypost.com/2025/04/10/business/inflation-slows-more-than-expected-in-march-in-first-round-of-data-since-trumps-tariffs/?utm_source=chatgpt.com">indicates</a> a temporary easing of inflation, the recent implementation of substantial tariffs by the Trump administration is expected to exert upward pressure on prices in the coming months. Economists caution that the full impact of these tariffs has yet to materialize in consumer prices.</p>
