<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/25/comparing-and-contrasting-common-network-configuration-concepts/">Static IP</a> addressing</strong> and <strong>DHCP (<a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/tag/dynamic-host-configuration-protocol/">Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol</a>)</strong> are two distinct methods of assigning IP addresses to devices on a network. <strong>Static IPs</strong> are manually configured by a technician and remain fixed, ensuring consistent identification—ideal for servers, printers, and devices requiring predictable access. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>However, they require manual configuration and can lead to IP conflicts if not properly managed. <strong>DHCP</strong>, on the other hand, automatically assigns IP addresses from a predefined pool, simplifying network management and reducing errors, especially in dynamic environments with many devices.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/25/summarizing-services-provided-by-networked-hosts/">DHCP</a> offers greater scalability and is widely used in home and enterprise networks. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>However, static IPs offer more control and reliability for critical systems. DHCP uses UDP ports 67 (server) and 68 (client), and if unavailable, devices may fall back to <strong>APIPA addresses (169.254.x.x)</strong>. The CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam emphasizes knowing when to apply static vs. dynamic IPs based on security, accessibility, and network structure.</p>
