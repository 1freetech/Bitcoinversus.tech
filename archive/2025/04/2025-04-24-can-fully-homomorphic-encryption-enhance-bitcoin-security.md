<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05904">represents</a> a significant advancement in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/02/29/who-is-satoshi-nakamoto-new-emails-with-adam-back-enter-public-domain/">cryptography</a>, enabling computations on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/09/26/california-to-join-states-supporting-digital-drivers-licenses-and-ids-in-apple-wallet/">encrypted data</a> without the need for decryption. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This capability ensures that data remains confidential throughout processing, addressing longstanding security challenges in various sectors.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Traditionally, data <a href="https://inpher.io/technology/what-is-fully-homomorphic-encryption/">had to be decrypted</a> before processing, exposing it to potential breaches. FHE allows analytical functions to operate directly on ciphertexts, producing encrypted results that, when decrypted, match those obtained from plaintext computations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homomorphic_encryption">concept</a> of homomorphic encryption was first proposed in 1978, but practical implementations remained elusive for decades. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">In 2009, IBM researcher Craig Gentry introduced the first plausible construction of a fully homomorphic encryption scheme, marking a breakthrough in the field. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">FHE has numerous applications. <a href="https://www.zama.ai/introduction-to-homomorphic-encryption">For example</a>, it enables private queries to a search engine—the user submits an encrypted query, and the search engine computes a succinct encrypted answer without ever looking at the query in the clear. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">It also enables searching on encrypted data—a user stores encrypted files on a remote file server and can later have the server retrieve only files that (when decrypted) satisfy certain criteria. ​</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GFptdPqqFI","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Despite its potential, FHE faces challenges, particularly in computational efficiency. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Processing encrypted data requires more resources than plaintext operations, leading to slower performance. However, <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/fully-homomorphic-encryption">ongoing research</a> aims to optimize these processes, making FHE more practical for widespread use. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Several open-source libraries and frameworks have emerged to facilitate the adoption of FHE. These tools aim to make FHE more accessible to developers and organizations, promoting the integration of secure data processing into various applications. ​</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Fully Homomorphic Encryption and Bitcoin: Potential Applications</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><strong>Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) could revolutionize Bitcoin by enabling secure computations on encrypted transaction data without exposing sensitive information.</strong> This cryptographic breakthrough would enhance Bitcoin's privacy features while preserving the integrity of its decentralized network.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Potential Uses of FHE in Bitcoin</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Confidential Transactions Without a Trusted Third Party</strong><br>Bitcoin currently relies on techniques like CoinJoin or confidential transactions to obscure amounts and addresses. <strong>FHE would allow nodes to verify transactions without ever seeing the actual transaction details, enhancing financial privacy.</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Off-Chain Computation Without Revealing Data</strong><br>Layer 2 solutions like the Lightning Network rely on <strong>off-chain transactions</strong> to scale Bitcoin. With FHE, smart contracts and multi-party computations could occur off-chain without revealing transaction amounts or contract terms to intermediaries.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Secure Bitcoin Custody and Multi-Signature Wallets</strong><br>Multi-signature wallets require participants to approve transactions collaboratively. <strong>FHE could allow participants to sign transactions in a fully encrypted manner, ensuring that private keys remain hidden even during the authorization process.</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Decentralized Finance (DeFi) on Bitcoin Without Compromising Privacy</strong><br>Ethereum's smart contracts enable DeFi applications, but <strong>FHE could introduce privacy-preserving DeFi on Bitcoin</strong>, allowing lending, borrowing, and decentralized exchanges (DEXs) without revealing individual transaction data.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Regulatory Compliance Without Data Exposure</strong><br>Governments and financial institutions often require compliance with <strong>anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) regulations.</strong> <strong>FHE could enable cryptographic proofs that transactions comply with legal requirements without disclosing user identities or amounts.</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Challenges of Using FHE in Bitcoin</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Computational Overhead</strong>: FHE is resource-intensive, requiring significantly more processing power than traditional cryptography.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Integration Complexity</strong>: Bitcoin's base layer was not designed for homomorphic encryption, meaning substantial protocol upgrades would be needed.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Scalability Concerns</strong>: Given Bitcoin's block size limits and decentralization requirements, implementing FHE efficiently would require optimization techniques or integration at Layer 2.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Future of FHE and Bitcoin</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Developments in FHE could bring <strong>privacy-preserving smart contracts, confidential transactions, and scalable off-chain computation</strong> to Bitcoin. As cryptographic research advances, integrating FHE into Bitcoin-related protocols could further decentralization and financial sovereignty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) could revolutionize Bitcoin by enabling computations on encrypted transaction data without exposing sensitive details. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This advancement would enhance financial privacy, allowing users to verify transactions while keeping amounts and addresses confidential. FHE could also improve smart contracts on Bitcoin, enabling more complex, privacy-preserving DeFi applications without revealing contract logic to the public. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">However, computational overhead and integration challenges make FHE impractical for immediate implementation on Bitcoin’s base layer, requiring further research and optimization. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">If scalability issues are resolved, FHE could strengthen Bitcoin’s privacy and security, making it a more robust financial system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">In summary, Fully Homomorphic Encryption offers a promising solution for secure data processing, allowing computations on encrypted data without compromising confidentiality. As research progresses and implementation becomes more efficient, FHE is poised to play a crucial role in enhancing data security across various industries.​</p>
