<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is reportedly planning to end most of its routine food safety inspections, <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fda-food-safety-inspections-plans/">according to</a> CBS News. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The proposed changes would shift oversight responsibilities to state and local authorities, a move that has sparked debate among public health officials and consumer advocacy groups.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The FDA currently contracts with 43 states and Puerto Rico to conduct some routine inspections, particularly those considered lower-risk. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The agency has stated that the proposed shift aims to reduce redundancy and free up resources for higher-priority inspections, such as those involving infant formula manufacturers and foreign food facilities. However, critics have raised concerns about the consistency and safety of inspections in states without existing FDA contracts.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Advocates for the change argue that states can often conduct inspections at a lower cost while maintaining federal standards. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>However, some experts warn that the transition could undermine oversight and transparency, potentially slowing responses to foodborne illness outbreaks.</p>
