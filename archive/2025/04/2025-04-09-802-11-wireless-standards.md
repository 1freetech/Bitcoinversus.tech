<p>The <strong>802.11 wireless standards</strong> represent a series of specifications developed by the IEEE for wireless local area networking (WLAN). </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The original <strong>802.11</strong> standard, established in 1997, supported data rates up to <strong>2 Mbps</strong> in the <strong>2.4 GHz frequency band</strong> but quickly became obsolete. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <strong>802.11a</strong> standard, released in 1999, introduced operation in the <strong>5 GHz frequency band</strong> and increased speeds to <strong>54 Mbps</strong>, though its range was limited. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Simultaneously, <strong>802.11b</strong> extended the range while operating at a maximum speed of <strong>11 Mbps</strong> in the <strong>2.4 GHz band</strong>, making it one of the first widely adopted Wi-Fi standards. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The subsequent <strong>802.11g</strong> standard (2003) combined the benefits of 802.11a's higher speed (<strong>54 Mbps</strong>) with 802.11b's range by using the <strong>2.4 GHz band</strong>, ensuring backward compatibility.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A significant advancement came with <strong>802.11n</strong>, also known as Wi-Fi 4, introduced in 2009. It supported speeds up to <strong>600 Mbps</strong> and operated on both <strong>2.4 GHz</strong> and <strong>5 GHz bands</strong>, using <strong>MIMO (Multiple Input, Multiple Output)</strong> technology to improve performance. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <strong>802.11ac</strong> standard, or Wi-Fi 5, followed in 2013, delivering data rates of up to <strong>3.5 Gbps</strong> over the <strong>5 GHz band</strong> with features like <strong>MU-MIMO (Multi-User MIMO)</strong> to enable simultaneous connections for multiple devices. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most recent iteration, <strong>802.11ax</strong> (Wi-Fi 6), launched in 2019, provides speeds up to <strong>10 Gbps</strong> and operates on both the <strong>2.4 GHz</strong> and <strong>5 GHz bands</strong>, using advanced technologies like <strong>OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access)</strong> to enhance efficiency in high-density environments. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Each standard represents the evolution of wireless networking, addressing the needs for higher speeds, broader coverage, and improved reliability.</p>
