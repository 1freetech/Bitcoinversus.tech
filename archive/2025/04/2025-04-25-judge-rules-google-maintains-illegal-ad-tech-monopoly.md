<p>​A U.S. federal judge <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/google-adtech-antitrust-federal-judge-ruling-mono2025-4?utm_source=chatgpt.com">has ruled</a> that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/28/google-rehires-ai-pioneer-noam-shazeer-for-2-7-billion/">Google</a> illegally monopolized key segments of the digital advertising market, marking a significant antitrust victory for the Department of Justice and 17 state attorneys general. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The court found that <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/29/doj-proposes-google-divest-chrome-browser-to-address-antitrust-concerns/">Google</a> willfully tied its publisher ad server and ad exchange, maintaining over 90% market share and limiting competition, which harmed publishers, the competitive process, and web users. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>However, the court sided with Google concerning advertiser ad networks, stating that the plaintiffs failed to prove monopoly in that area. Remedies, potentially including a breakup of Google's adtech business, will be determined in a future hearing. Google plans to appeal, claiming its ad tools are competitive and beneficial. ​</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>This ruling follows <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/google-adtech-antitrust-federal-judge-ruling-mono2025-4?utm_source=chatgpt.com">another antitrust decision</a> in August by a Washington, DC judge who also found Google to be acting monopolistically in its search business, with a hearing scheduled to address further consequences. ​</p>
