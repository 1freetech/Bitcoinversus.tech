<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>In an interview with Yahoo Finance, former Trump economic advisor Judy Shelton drew a striking parallel between Donald Trump’s economic strategy and Russia’s transformative reforms during the 1980s. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://twitter.com/VersusInstitute/status/1909961529375568256","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"twitter","responsive":true} -->
<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-rich is-provider-twitter wp-block-embed-twitter"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">
https://twitter.com/VersusInstitute/status/1909961529375568256
</div></figure>
<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Shelton, a longtime critic of centralized economic planning, <a href="https://x.com/VersusInstitute/status/1909961529375568256">recalled</a> her early career evaluating the financial collapse of the Soviet Union:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><em>"</em>I started my career analyzing the internal financial and monetary condition of the soviet union. That’s going back to the mid 80s.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Remember, they wanted to have perestroika, that means ‘rebuilding’ that was their transformation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There economy was failing because it was too much government.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was based on a stat-managed economy. and they knew they couldnt suvive if they didn’t rebuilld it. …</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And to the amazement of many people in the west – and this is when i was a research fellow at the hoover institution on a postdoctoral fellowship at stanford – this suprised people in the west.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>An entire country – probably the 2nd most powerful country in the world – was goin bankrupt. and they did go bankrupt.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It can happen. so we are tackling the same tendencies…</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is a huge challenge to try ot make this transistion and [give] domestic industries a chance to reempower itself and solidify our economy. I think its worth it to take this chance. It’s risky, but i think its justified and i think its an imperative<em>."</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Shelton’s analogy suggests that Trump’s push for economic “liberation” from globalized supply chains and top-heavy regulation mirrors the Soviet Union’s belated effort to escape a failing, state-managed economy. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>In both instances, the goal was to decentralize control and rebuild domestic strength—Russia through perestroika and Trump through deregulation and domestic industry revitalization. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>However, while Russia’s restructuring came from a position of systemic collapse, Trump's strategy was framed as preemptive, aiming to stave off stagnation and reinforce economic sovereignty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
