<p style="text-transform:none">Greg Jones was an All-American running back at Florida State, renowned for his punishing power and exceptional speed. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Put simply, Greg Jones was actually<em> built</em> different.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">I know the arguments: <br><br>"Jerome Bettis was stronger!" "Mike Alstott was tougher!" "Derrick Henry was faster!" "Jim Brown was quicker!" "TJ Duckett was smoother!" <br><br>No the fuck they weren't. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">He was 6 feet and 245 pounds in college. He gained another 10-15 pounds by the time he matured in the NFL. He was just as big as the biggest power backs, but <em>faster</em>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Shame on the Jacksonville Jaguars for moving him to fullback. If they kept him at running back, he may have been due for the highest paying contract for a professional at his position during that time. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zj-2WR_KuUE","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-4-3 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">His transition from college to the NFL saw him drafted by the Jacksonville Jaguars in the second round of the 2004 NFL Draft. The Jaguars recognized Jones’s potential and moved him to fullback, where he would make NFL history​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Wearing the iconic number 33, Jones became a cornerstone of the Jaguars' offense. His dense, muscular build made him a formidable blocker, and he played a crucial role in helping running backs like Fred Taylor and Maurice Jones-Drew achieve success.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":6410,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">In 2008, Jones’s skills were rewarded with a $17.4 million contract, making him the highest-paid fullback in NFL history at that time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Despite suffering an ACL injury that sidelined him for the 2006 season, Jones <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Jones_(fullback)">bounced back</a> with resilience. His ability to power through adversity and dominate the field once again solidified his legacy. After stints with the Houston Texans and New Orleans Saints, Jones retired in 2015 as a Jacksonville Jaguar​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Now, as a coach, Jones continues to influence the game, passing his wealth of knowledge to the next generation of players. His presence in the sport remains impactful, inspiring athletes to push their limits.</p>
