<p style="text-transform:none">The power law is a mathematical relationship where one quantity varies as a power of another. It is often expressed in the form y=axky = ax^ky=axk, where aaa is a constant, xxx is the independent variable, and kkk is the power or exponent. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Power laws describe many natural and social phenomena, such as income distribution, city populations, and the frequency of earthquakes, where smaller events occur more often than larger ones. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">This scaling behavior is significant in understanding systems with broad variability, highlighting how a small number of large occurrences dominate in many datasets.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The power law, as applied to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/11/15/fbi-confiscates-electronics-from-polymarket-ceo-shayne-coplan-following-trump-election-prediction/">Bitcoin</a>, describes how its price or adoption scales disproportionately with certain factors, such as network effects or investment inflows. For instance, as more participants join the Bitcoin network, the value of the network tends to grow exponentially rather than linearly. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none"><br>This relationship highlights Bitcoin's potential for extreme growth as it benefits from a compounding effect of adoption and use. </p>
