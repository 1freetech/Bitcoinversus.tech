<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The iconic "Endless Shrimp" promotion, long a staple at Red Lobster, has been shelved for good by the brand’s new CEO, Damola Adamolekun. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <a href="https://www.freep.com/story/news/local/michigan/2024/11/13/red-lobster-endless-shrimp-hush-puppies-michigan/76221187007/">decision</a> comes as the seafood chain confronts the financial fallout from past missteps, including high operating costs tied to the unlimited shrimp deal, which contributed to the company’s bankruptcy filing last year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":8902,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.tech/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/damola_adamolekun-1.webp?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-8902" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><sup>Photo Credit: Fortune</sup></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Adamolekun, a Harvard Business School graduate, minced no words: “I know how to do math. This wasn’t one decision that led us to bankruptcy… but there were certainly big mistakes made over the last few years." His pointed remarks highlight the stark reality of unsustainable spending. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">For Red Lobster, giving away seemingly unlimited shrimp proved as financially unfeasible as printing money without concern for deficits—a poignant parallel to the broader economic struggles of nations grappling with overspending.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">To revive Red Lobster, Adamolekun is investing $60 million in a strategic makeover, focusing on renovations, menu improvements, and better value propositions—shifting from endless shrimp to “the best deal for the best lobster.”</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Much like the U.S. government’s ballooning deficit, fueled by unchecked spending and debt accumulation, the shrimp promotion's unprofitability underscores the importance of balancing offerings with fiscal responsibility. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Adamolekun's pivot exemplifies how financial discipline and focused investments can restore health to a floundering enterprise—lessons that extend well beyond the world of seafood.<br></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
