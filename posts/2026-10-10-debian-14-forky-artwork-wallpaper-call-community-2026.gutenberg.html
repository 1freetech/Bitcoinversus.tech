Debian 14 needs a look—and the project is asking the community to design it.

The upcoming release is code-named Forky, and Debian has opened its official artwork call for wallpapers, login screens, installer graphics, and other visual pieces that could ship with the operating system.

The deadline is November 26, 2026. That means an artist working from a laptop today could end up designing the default desktop seen by Debian users around the world.

This is very Debian

Debian is one of the oldest major Linux distributions, but the project still treats something as visible as the default wallpaper as a community decision. The artwork needs to feel distinctly Debian, stay clean enough for everyday use, and integrate without requiring core software patches.

That community model is the same reason Debian keeps showing up far beyond hobby desktops. BitcoinVersus recently covered CERN moving more than 2,200 accelerator control systems to Debian 13, while our Linux distribution guide explains why Debian remains foundational across servers and desktops.

You do not need to be a Debian developer

This is the fun part: the artwork call is not just for kernel hackers or package maintainers. Artists can create a proposal, publish it through the Debian artwork process, and let the community evaluate it.

Ars Technica highlighted the call this week, noting that the winning design could appear on boot screens, login prompts, and desktop wallpapers for millions of installations.

SmartNews takeaway

If you make digital art and like Linux, Debian is giving you a real shot at putting your work inside a major operating system. The deadline is November 26. Open source is not only code; sometimes it is the wallpaper behind the terminal.

And once the desktop is up, Linux still has plenty of practical work to do—like the systemd timers we recently covered.
