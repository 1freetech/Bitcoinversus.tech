A 10 MWh vanadium flow battery at California’s Viejas Enterprise Microgrid has delivered more than 1 GWh of electricity since entering commercial operation. The October 1 milestone offers a practical look at energy storage doing daily work at a resort, beyond factory capacity announcements and proposed projects.

In its operating update, Invinity Energy Systems says customer Indian Energy’s installation was fully commissioned in late 2025. The battery charges from onsite solar and supplies electricity for Viejas Casino & Resort near San Diego. The company reports that it has met or exceeded its contracted performance parameters.

What does 1 GWh delivered actually mean?

The two headline numbers measure different things. The 10 MWh figure describes the battery’s storage capacity. More than 1 GWh describes cumulative electricity delivered over repeated use. It does not mean the site can hold a gigawatt-hour of electricity at once.

For scale, 1 GWh equals 1,000 MWh. Dividing that throughput by the reported 10 MWh capacity gives 100 nominal full-capacity equivalents. That is an arithmetic comparison, not a verified count of complete charge-discharge cycles: actual dispatch can involve partial cycles, changing operating limits and losses.

Invinity highlighted the result in its October 1 post congratulating Indian Energy and the California Energy Commission.

Solar generation becomes a dispatchable site resource

Yahoo Finance’s coverage identifies the associated solar array as 15 MW and describes the project as California’s largest tribal microgrid. The update concerns the Invinity battery within that installation, rather than a claim that the entire microgrid has only 10 MWh of storage.

A solar array’s MW rating describes generating power; a battery’s MWh rating describes stored energy. Neither number alone tells an operator how long every load at a facility can run. That calculation also needs the battery’s usable energy, its discharge power limit and the actual load profile.

The daily-use angle complements our coverage of an Xos mobile charging hub passing 1 GWh delivered. Throughput milestones help readers distinguish equipment being used from equipment merely being announced.

Why mining and data-center operators should pay attention

For a computing facility, the useful comparison is its own duty cycle: how often storage must charge, how quickly it must respond and how long it must support a load. A resort’s operating record does not establish that the same system can replace a data center’s UPS or meet a mining site’s economics. It does provide a real deployment to examine.

That makes this a useful companion to Energy Vault’s eight-hour storage project and Amazon’s agreement supporting additional nuclear generation. Generation capacity, storage duration and operating performance answer different infrastructure questions.

The next useful evidence would be a published operating dataset: availability, delivered energy by month, efficiency and maintenance requirements. Invinity’s milestone is a company-reported result; the announcement does not supply an independent performance audit.
