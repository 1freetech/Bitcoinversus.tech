The control board is the small computer that coordinates an ASIC miner. It does not perform most of the SHA-256 hashing itself. Instead, it runs firmware, connects the miner to the network, communicates with the pool and tells the hashboards what work to process.

That makes it the bridge between the miner’s hardware and software. The board handles settings, temperature and fan telemetry, pool credentials, firmware updates and communication with each hash chain. If it fails, a miner may power on but lose its web interface, network connection or ability to initialize its hashboards.

[tweet https://twitter.com/tylerkstevens/status/2099605393743774159]

Tyler Stevens’ 256 Foundation presentation shows why open-source mining projects treat the control board as its own layer alongside firmware, hashboards and pools.

A bad controller can also look like another failure. Before replacing one, technicians should rule out power, data cables, firmware and network configuration. Our guides to hashboards, miner PSUs and Stratum show how those layers interact.

[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sekb9DbCEw8]

VoskCoin demonstrates a physical ASIC control-board replacement, including removal, installation and testing.

Compatibility matters. Control-board architecture, firmware image and supported hashboard combinations can vary even inside the same miner family. Check the Braiins firmware documentation and BITMAIN support documentation for the exact model before servicing hardware.

Simple rule: the control board manages the miner; the hashboards do the hashing.
