<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>British Bitcoin treasury company B HODL reported that its Lightning payment-channel capacity reached 11.7 BTC across its nodes on October 12, approximately twice its June 30 position. The milestone was reiterated in a November <a href="https://www.aquis.eu/stock-exchange/announcements/6027875">Aquis regulatory announcement</a> that also disclosed changes to the company's leadership. B HODL is developing payment infrastructure intended to earn Bitcoin-denominated revenue from capital committed to network connectivity.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">From treasury holdings to network liquidity</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A treasury balance and a Lightning channel represent distinct operational uses of Bitcoin. Bitcoin held in an ordinary wallet can remain unspent until the owner chooses to transact. A Lightning funding output commits capital to a channel with another node, allowing the parties to exchange updated balances and eventually settle the channel on-chain. A node connected to multiple counterparties can forward payments between them.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>B HODL launched its Lightning service-provider platform in May 2026. Customers may obtain receiving capacity through the company's own service or through Amboss Magma. An inbound channel lets a customer receive funds when the remote peer has sufficient outgoing balance. Obtaining capacity does not guarantee a route to every potential payer, because additional channels and directional balances elsewhere in the network may constrain a transaction.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the disclosed figures establish</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The company's annual-results disclosure put channel capacity near 5.8 BTC on June 30 and above 11.7 BTC on October 12. Using the rounded values gives an increase of approximately 5.9 BTC, or 102 percent. The November regulatory announcement separately placed treasury holdings at 168.487 BTC on November 18. The figures measure different dates and different concepts; channel capacity is not necessarily identical to company-owned funds within channels, and should not simply be added to treasury holdings.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The announcement also described the first public node as fifth globally by availability on September 30, citing 1ML and Amboss. Availability is one aspect of operational reliability, rather than a direct measure of forwarded payment volume, revenue or transaction success rates.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Liquidity provision as infrastructure</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Running routing infrastructure requires reliable network connectivity, secure key management, monitoring and enough balance in the correct locations. Operators may earn forwarding fees or payments for opening and maintaining channels. Expenses can include hosting, monitoring, on-chain funding and closing transactions, rebalancing and staff time. A growing capacity figure alone does not establish a positive net return.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Related developments include <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/21/core-lightning-v26-12-release-candidate-832-commits-payment-routing-2026/">Core Lightning's upcoming routing upgrade</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/26/lightning-loop-036-beta-bitcoin-deposit-address-recovery-2026/">Lightning Loop's deposit recovery changes</a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/07/core-lightning-260610-bitcoin-channel-draining-5000sat-runes-security-update-2026/">channel security fixes</a> and the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/27/nucula-23-dollar-open-hardware-bitcoin-cashu-nfc-payment-terminal-2026/">low-cost payment terminal prototype</a>. Each addresses a different layer of the operational stack, from security and routing to user-facing payment access.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>B HODL also disclosed a nonbinding letter of intent with Atlas 21, the developer of ZEUS Wallet, in August. A potential relationship could connect its routing capital to additional wallet infrastructure, but a letter of intent is not evidence of a signed commercial rollout. Subsequent disclosures should be evaluated for active connections, routed volume, realized fees, and operating costs rather than capacity alone.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
