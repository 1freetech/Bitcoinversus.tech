## Verified primary reference sources
- #7 Damascus: UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/20/ dates city to third millennium BCE; Tell Ramad on outskirts may have earlier settlement 8000-10000 BCE but NOT evidence Damascus itself was an urban city at that point.
- #6 Erbil: World Monuments Fund https://www.wmf.org/projects/erbil-citadel documents 6000+ years; UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1437 describes elevated tell and discontinuity in social occupation of the citadel itself.
- #5 Aleppo: UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/document/222832/ says 'may have been inhabited since sixth millennium BCE'; uncertainty explicitly retained.
- #4 Plovdiv: local municipality https://www.plovdiv.bg/en/tourists/sight-seeing/ancient-plovdiv/ identifies sixth millennium BCE first settlement on Nebet Tepe, distinct from urban town (12th century BCE).
- #3 Byblos: UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/295/ dates early fishing community to approximately 8000 years ago; Phoenician town developed later.
- #2 Diyarbakır: Hurriyet https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/archaeologists-uncover-oldest-known-section-of-diyarbakir-city-walls-217008 reports excavations at Amida Höyük and ~8000 BCE occupation; UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1488/ verifies later city walls and gardens but does not independently verify 10,000-year continuity.
- #1 Jericho: UNESCO https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1687 documents permanent settlement 9th-8th millennium BCE, site northwest of modern Jericho.
- No invented annual precision; approximate earliest associated settlement dates explicitly separated from city founding and strictly continuous city occupation. Tie in approximate ages for Byblos/Plovdiv is not scientific rank precision.
