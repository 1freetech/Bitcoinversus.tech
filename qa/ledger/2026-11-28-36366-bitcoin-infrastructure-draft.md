The mean opening size reached 0.450 BTC, compared with 0.280 BTC in March. Larger individual openings do not necessarily indicate an expanding network: the same report measured a decline in aggregate public capacity during the final week of October.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Capacity and payment direction</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A Lightning channel is a Bitcoin-backed agreement between two participants. Opening a channel requires an on-chain funding transaction; subsequent transfers update balances between participants without recording every payment on the base blockchain. Other nodes can forward a payment along a sequence of channels, provided each connection has enough spendable balance in the required direction.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Channel capacity denotes the combined value available within a connection, not the amount immediately available to send from either endpoint. For example, a channel with 0.45 BTC of total capacity might have only 0.05 BTC on the side that needs to forward a payment. Channel placement, directional balances and the reliability of counterparties can therefore matter more than the headline capacity figure.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">More openings, less public capacity</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Amboss recorded 1,567 openings from October 26 through November 1, compared with 1,461 during the previous week. Funding committed to those new connections nevertheless fell from 758.3 BTC to 702.2 BTC. The published average opening size declined from 0.519 BTC to 0.448 BTC. Simultaneously, 1,925 channels closed cooperatively and 368 closed by force. The public network's measured capacity fell from 3,458.8 BTC to 3,246 BTC, roughly 6.2 percent.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The observations describe different quantities. New-channel averages measure funding flows into newly created connections. Total public capacity measures the outstanding stock at a particular time. A higher monthly average opening size can coexist with falling aggregate capacity when closures or other changes remove more funding than openings add. Private channels are absent from the public graph, so the report cannot establish total Lightning activity or transaction volume.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The engineering implications</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Payment operators must manage their channel balances as well as software security. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/26/lightning-loop-036-beta-bitcoin-deposit-address-recovery-2026/">Lightning Loop's deposit recovery changes</a> and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/21/core-lightning-v26-12-release-candidate-832-commits-payment-routing-2026/">Core Lightning's upcoming routing upgrade</a> illustrate separate operational improvements to Bitcoin payment infrastructure. <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/28/bitcoin-bip333-bip324-one-byte-message-aliases-2026/">Bitcoin peer-to-peer message negotiation</a> concerns the base network's peer protocol, while the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/27/nucula-23-dollar-open-hardware-bitcoin-cashu-nfc-payment-terminal-2026/">low-cost payment terminal prototype</a> illustrates how payment tools reach users.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>For a merchant or routing operator, relevant performance measures include payment success rates by amount, usable inbound capacity, routing fee revenue after operational expenses and the cost of on-chain channel management. The next network reports can establish whether larger new openings persist or merely reflect an October funding pattern. Without transaction-level performance measurements, the public channel statistics alone do not prove improved payment reliability.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
