## Crisis-event editorial fact-check

- Required core event: liquidation/failure of the Medici banking partnerships in Florence in **1494**, confirmed from Oxford Bibliographies (Medici Bank); historical banking scholarship by Raymond de Roover; PSL Quarterly Review (A. Sapori); and the Cambridge historical study of the bank's London branch.
- Distinction: a major bank/company failure amid political crisis and years of declining branch activity; **not** an evidenced Europe-wide stock-market crash.
- Chronology discussed: 1397 bank foundations, expansion into European trade and papal finance, peak around Cosimo's death in 1464, branch/lending difficulties in later decades, liquidation in 1494, reorganization in other firms.
- Accounting context: Medici-era double-entry bookkeeping existed before 1494; Pacioli published an influential printed exposition in 1494. ICAEW and the Library of Congress support this distinction.
- Caution: No unsupported aggregate crash percentage, dollar figure, bank-wide losses or daily stock-index metrics inserted.
